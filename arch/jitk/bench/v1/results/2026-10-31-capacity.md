# Sofort capacity for the real tasks (2026-10-31)

Sofort's memory classes were tuned on small synthetic payloads, and the real
bench v1 tasks did not fit them (`2026-10-30-fit-matrix.md`). This round
measures each program's working set and adds two larger classes, glue-8m and
glue-16m (ABI 12). On those classes, tool replies may exceed 1 MiB. Sizes,
costs and mechanism are in `../../../shard/README.md` "Limits (ABI 12)". The
per-program working sets are in `../tasks/README.md` "Working sets".

## Fit matrix, before and after

Both runs used S1, 100 samples per adapter over the 25 task programs (20 per
task), latency scale 0 and a 30 s timeout, on HVF (Apple M4 Pro, macOS 26.6.1,
Node v24.22.1). The Mac was at load 55–58. Correctness does not depend on load,
and no timings are quoted here. Cells are correct / attempts.

Before (`fit-matrix3`, ABI 11, after the structure-budget fix `11737397`):

| adapter | T1 | T2 | T3 | T4 | T5 | all |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| sofort-hvf-2m | 0/20 | 0/20 | 0/20 | 0/20 | 0/20 | 0/100 |
| sofort-hvf-4m | 20/20 | 4/20 | 16/20 | 0/20 | 16/20 | 56/100 |

After (`fit-matrix4`, ABI 12, commit `19afa64d`):

| adapter | T1 | T2 | T3 | T4 | T5 | all |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| sofort-hvf-2m | 0/20 | 0/20 | 0/20 | 0/20 | 0/20 | 0/100 |
| sofort-hvf-4m | 20/20 | 4/20 | 16/20 | 0/20 | 16/20 | 56/100 |
| sofort-hvf-8m | 20/20 | 16/20 | 20/20 | 16/20 | 20/20 | 92/100 |
| sofort-hvf-16m | 20/20 | 20/20 | 20/20 | 16/20 | 20/20 | 96/100 |

The remaining failures are all out of memory:

- **glue-8m:** T2 4 and T4 3.
- **glue-16m:** T4 3 only.

glue-2m and glue-4m fail exactly as before, so they did not regress:

- **glue-2m:** out of memory, plus the 4 T2 5 `guest_fatal` runs and 4 T4 3 runs refused at the 1 MiB reply frame.
- **glue-4m:** out of memory, plus the 4 T4 3 `FRAME_LIMIT` runs and 4 T4 5 guest errors.

T4 3's 1.28 MiB page now crosses the frame on glue-8m and glue-16m. What stops
T4 3 there is the heap: it keeps every point of every page, and its
high-water passes glue-16m's 14.5 MiB. QuickJS in Wasm completes it in
1.47 MiB. kvm-dev gives the same completion per class over one run of each
program, except T2 5 on glue-4m (below).

## What each class costs

One slot, measured as in `../../../shard/README.md` "Limits". The Linux
figures come from kvm-dev (nested KVM): its sizes are exact, its times are not
performance claims.

| class | charged per START | heap capacity | largest tool reply | real-task programs completed (of 25) | HVF slot footprint: idle / after the tasks | HVF reset p50 | Linux one-slot RSS: after `return 1` / after the tasks | KVM reset p50 (nested) |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| glue-2m | 2 MiB | 864 KiB | 107 KiB | 0 | 3.0 / 7.9 MiB | 104 µs | 11.6 / 14.0 MiB | 182 µs |
| glue-4m | 4 MiB | 2,256 KiB | 545 KiB | 14 (13 on kvm-dev) | 4.5 / 11.0 MiB | 218 µs | 15.0 / 17.6 MiB | 188 µs |
| glue-8m | 8 MiB | 6,168 KiB | 1,227 KiB | 23 | 8.5 / 17.0 MiB | 523 µs | 16.5 / 26.7 MiB | 230 µs |
| glue-16m | 16 MiB | 14,480 KiB | 2,759 KiB | 24 | 17.0 / 24.0 MiB | 1,184 µs | 14.5 / 44.9 MiB | 265 µs |

The two engines pay for a class differently:

- **HVF.** The copy reset rewrites the whole writable window, so idle
  footprint and reset time grow with the class (about the arena plus 2 MiB).
- **KVM.** The reset copies only the pages a run dirtied, so reset time barely
  grows and a slot holds what its recent runs used, until the 2 s idle trim.
  A large class costs memory on Linux only when jobs use it. The charge is
  reported in `Engine.limits.memoryMB`, but Node does not admit by it.

## Larger replies

The reply bound is a property of the class (`arena::Class::reply_bytes`):

| class | reply bound |
|---|---:|
| glue-2m | 1 MiB |
| glue-4m | 1 MiB |
| glue-8m | 2 MiB |
| glue-16m | 4 MiB |

Each bound is the power of two above the largest reply that class's heap
holds. Fragmenting replies further was the alternative. It would not have
helped: the heap, not the frame, is what refuses a large reply, and messages
already span records.

Host replies are trusted, so only they grow:

- CALL, DONE and CONSOLE keep the 1 MiB frame on every class.
- Node still reassembles the shard's messages to 1 MiB.
- Node checks each reply with `parseHostJson(s, replyBytes)`; the shard and
  the HVF zygote check it against the snapshot's class.

## Default class: recommendation, not implemented

**Recommendation:** make glue-8m the default for code-mode jobs.

- It completes 92 of 100 real-task runs (23 of 25 programs). glue-2m
  completes none, and glue-4m completes 56 of 100.
- glue-16m adds only T2 4, and costs twice glue-8m's HVF idle footprint and
  reset time.

**Costs against glue-2m, per slot:**

- **HVF:** about 5.5 MiB more idle footprint (8.5 vs 3.0 MiB) and about 0.4 ms
  more reset (523 vs 104 µs).
- **Linux:** a small job's memory is about unchanged. A large job holds what
  it used.

**Why it is left as `glue-2m`.** The switch is one line
(`executor/select-backend.ts` `shardClass()`, `tslib/deps.ts` `shardClass()`),
but it moves the published glue-2m density and reset figures:

- the Linux memory parity with Hyperlight;
- the HVF parking gate receipt, which is per snapshot, so it would be
  re-measured on the new default;
- the parity corpus cases that expect glue-2m's out-of-memory errors.

Whether code mode pays about 2.8× the HVF slot memory for real tasks is the
owner's decision. Without the switch, `JITK_SHARD_CLASS=glue-8m` selects the
class per deployment.

## Open: the guest allocator

The Sofort guest needs 2–10× the heap QuickJS needs for the same program
(`../tasks/README.md` "Working sets"). For example, T2 1 needs 352 KiB in
QuickJS and has a 2.5 MiB high-water in the guest; T4 3 needs 1.47 MiB and
the guest runs out of memory above 14.3 MiB.

- **Cause:** the kernel's mmap is first-fit over address-linear memory and has
  no `mremap`, so freed big blocks leave holes.
- **Why it matters most:** an allocator that reuses those holes would let
  glue-4m or glue-8m complete most of what glue-16m does. That is the larger
  density win.
