# Does movable JS relieve scarce compute?

Research date: **2026-10-28**. Scope: a QuickJS/wasm32 program paused at a quiescent tool `await`, resumed on another compatible host. **F** = sourced fact (including attributed vendor claims); **M-prior** = repository measurement, not reproduced here; **I** = inference; **E** = explicit estimate or arithmetic scenario. No new cloud, device, energy, or latency measurement was performed. Prices and product availability were checked for this date and can change. Dollar figures are USD; GB is decimal unless stated otherwise.

## 1. The scarce thing is usually not the movable thing

**F — demand and supply:** Anthropic reported in June 2025 that its agents used about **4×** the tokens of its chat interactions and multi-agent systems about **15×**. This is its workload mix, not a universal multiplier. The IEA's April 2026 update estimates **485 TWh** of data-centre electricity in 2025, up **17%**, and **50%** growth for AI-focused centres; it expects the HBM shortage to persist through at least **2027**. These are stronger evidence of accelerator, memory and power pressure than of a worldwide shortage of JS execution slots. [Anthropic, 2025-06-13](https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/multi-agent-research-system); [IEA, 2026-04-16](https://www.iea.org/reports/key-questions-on-energy-and-ai/executive-summary).

| Agent/app component | 2024–26 scale or price anchor | Scarcity and what moving this JS can change |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **GPU inference: prefill** | **F:** Prompt processing is the high-parallelism, commonly compute-bound phase; decode has different placement needs. An H100 SXM has **80 GB HBM**, **3.35 TB/s** bandwidth and up to **700 W** TDP. [MemHA, 2026 preprint](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.29986); [NVIDIA H100 specifications](https://www.nvidia.com/en-sg/data-center/h100/). | **I:** Scarce accelerator throughput. A JS continuation can decide *which warm model endpoint* to call, but cannot carry tensor-core work or prefill activations to an ordinary CPU/edge Worker. |
| **GPU inference: decode and KV** | **F:** Decode is often limited by memory bandwidth and hot memory capacity. **E:** An 8B BF16 model has ~**16 GB** weights before runtime overhead; an unbatched token's weight-streaming floor on H100's advertised bandwidth is **4.8 ms**. For an illustrative 32-layer, 8-KV-head, 128-dimension BF16 cache, **128 KiB/token** means **1 GiB at 8,192 tokens**. [NVIDIA](https://www.nvidia.com/en-sg/data-center/h100/); [repository derivation](../moonshots2/05-systems.md). | **I:** HBM and serving schedule are scarce. A ~100 KB–MB JS image does **not** include weights or KV. Routing a call can use spare *already warm* capacity; moving the JS image cannot free a parked provider KV cache without serving-engine cooperation. |
| **CPU glue and tool code** | **F:** Cloudflare Workers Standard charges **$0.02 per million CPU-ms** and **$0.30 per million requests** after included quantities. **E:** 10 ms of Worker CPU on one million requests is **$0.20** incremental CPU charge, versus $0.30 in request charges. [Workers pricing, updated 2026-10-02](https://developers.cloudflare.com/workers/platform/pricing/). | **I:** Usually plentiful and cheap relative to model calls. Placement can reduce wall time and the CPU/bandwidth burden at a remote origin. The portable interpreter may consume *more* CPU than direct V8. |
| **Network and origin/backend work** | **F:** AWS's S3 pricing example charges **$0.09/GB** for Internet egress from Ireland. Cloudflare's placement docs say a Worker can run by its upstream and reach a database/API with single-digit-ms latency. [AWS S3 pricing](https://aws.amazon.com/s3/pricing/); [Cloudflare Placement, updated 2026-10-12](https://developers.cloudflare.com/workers/configuration/placement/). | **I:** Often the movable program's best target: put selective joins, filters and sequential tool calls next to the owner. This can spare network bytes and origin round trips, provided the owner permits foreign code and the computation is not slower than a database query. |
| **Storage and checkpoints** | **F:** R2 Standard lists **$0.015/GB-month**, **$4.50/million writes**, **$0.36/million reads**, and free Internet egress. **M-prior:** a simple continuation was **99,854 B gzip**; an agent-shaped Zod state was **1.50 MB**, and a retained/freed 6 MiB buffer left a **6.39 MB** image. [R2 pricing, updated 2026-10-26](https://developers.cloudflare.com/r2/pricing/); [spike](../portability/02-wasm.md); [roadblocks](../v8/03-roadblocks.md). | **I:** Cheap per image, but images, retained versions and restore I/O multiply with migration/forks. Full-memory images can ship freed sensitive bytes. The model, data and host-side effect ledger are separate. |
| **Energy and power access** | **F:** Google estimated **0.24 Wh** for its *median Gemini Apps text prompt in May 2025*, with system overhead included; it explicitly says the estimate is point-in-time, provider-specific and not independently verified. H100 chip TDP is up to **700 W**; the IEA projects data-centre demand to **950 TWh in 2030**. [Google, 2025-08-21](https://cloud.google.com/blog/products/infrastructure/measuring-the-environmental-impact-of-ai-inference); [NVIDIA](https://www.nvidia.com/en-sg/data-center/h100/); [IEA](https://www.iea.org/reports/key-questions-on-energy-and-ai/executive-summary). | **I:** Grid connection and site power can be scarce. Placement changes *where/when* joules are consumed, or avoids joules only when it removes work or lets equipment sleep. A lower carbon grid does not imply fewer joules; the Gemini median is not an agent-query estimate. |

**E — an apples-to-apples bill ceiling:** For one million independent tasks at **2,000 input + 200 output tokens** each, the historical Sonnet 4 tariff of **$3/$15 per million input/output tokens** gives **$9,000** in model charges. Ten milliseconds of Worker glue per task costs **$0.20** of incremental CPU and **$0.30** of incremental request charges before the included allowance. These are list prices, not production margins or comparable physical joules. Removing *all* that Worker CPU could lower this modeled bill by only **0.0022%**. Even if CPU glue were 5% of a different workload's total cost, eliminating it entirely would improve total cost at most **1.053×** (`1/(1−0.05)`). [Anthropic Sonnet 4 list prices, 2025](https://www.anthropic.com/pricing?subjects=claude&type=product); [Workers pricing](https://developers.cloudflare.com/workers/platform/pricing/).

**M-prior — mobility has a CPU tax:** On matched warmed local glue tasks, QuickJS/Wasm took **3.993 ms versus 0.772 ms** for V8 on a 1,000-record JSON/filter/group task (**5.2×**), and **1.771 versus 0.155 ms** for handwritten validation (**11.5×**). A million-step scalar recurrence was **31.1×** slower. These are local microbenchmarks, not universal speed ratios. The minimal 100 KB image excludes the **332 KB gzip** interpreter artifact, module compatibility, host metadata, cold start and transfer. A 3 MB snapshot takes a **2.4 s payload-only floor at 10 Mbit/s**. [Repository roadblocks and methods](../v8/03-roadblocks.md); [portability spike](../portability/02-wasm.md).

## 2. Where usable spare capacity exists

| Pool | Size evidence and access | Useful interpretation |
| --- | --- | --- |
| User laptops/phones | **F:** GSMA estimates **54% of the world's population owned a smartphone in 2025**; ITU estimates **6.0 billion Internet users** in 2025. These are reach indicators, not idle CPU counts. Apple's 2024 M4 advertises a **38 TOPS** Neural Engine, but peak operations are neither available JS throughput nor LLM tokens/s. [GSMA, 2025](https://www.gsma.com/somic/somic-2025-overview-report/); [ITU, 2025](https://www.itu.int/en/ITU-D/Statistics/Pages/stat/default.aspx?code=678); [Apple M4, 2024-05-07](https://www.apple.com/newsroom/2024/05/apple-introduces-m4-chip/). | **I:** Enormous *installed* device base, small and unknown opt-in, eligible, plugged-in, foreground capacity. Device owners supply power, memory, thermal headroom and wear; a browser tab has no claim to spare NPU capacity. |
| Browser WebGPU / WebNN | **F:** Chrome shipped WebGPU on desktop and expanded Android support; Firefox 141 Windows and Safari 26 also shipped it. Hugging Face reported ~**85% global WebGPU browser support in March 2026** and demonstrates local embeddings, speech recognition and other models with Transformers.js. WebNN entered **Chrome/Edge 146 origin trials in March 2026** and was a W3C Candidate Recommendation Draft on **2026-10-21**. [Chrome WebGPU overview, updated 2025-08-11](https://developer.chrome.com/docs/web-platform/webgpu/overview/); [Transformers.js WebGPU guide, 2026](https://huggingface.co/docs/transformers.js/guides/webgpu); [W3C WebNN minutes, 2026-02-12](https://www.w3.org/2026/02/12-webmachinelearning-minutes.html); [W3C draft, 2026-10-21](https://www.w3.org/TR/webnn/). | **I:** Browser inference is real for compact models, but API support is not device/model eligibility, model download, quality or free GPU time. WebNN origin trial is not a universal shipping NPU API. QuickJS/Wasm needs a host import to call WebGPU/WebNN; its copied memory does not contain the browser's GPU objects. |
| Browser built-in model / native device model | **F:** Chrome's Prompt API page (updated **2026-10-21**) lists web support at **Chrome 148** and extension support at **138**; its foundation-model APIs currently exclude Chrome Android/iOS and require **22 GB free storage** plus either **>4 GB VRAM** or **16 GB RAM and ≥4 CPU cores**. The model is separately downloaded. Apple's Foundation Models framework exposed a roughly **3B-parameter, 2-bit** on-device model in 2025, optimized for summaries, extraction and classification; Apple updated it in 2026 and exposes it through native Swift/Python APIs. [Chrome Prompt API](https://developer.chrome.com/docs/ai/prompt-api); [Apple WWDC25](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2025/286/); [Apple 2026 updates](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/Updates/FoundationModels). | **I:** A moved JS orchestrator could call a *resident* local model through a host adapter. The Apple framework is not automatically callable from arbitrary web JS; Chrome's built-in model does not make phones a general inference fleet. A 3B model cannot be assumed to match frontier reasoning. |
| Idle server CPU and spot instances | **F:** Cast AI's 2026 report observes **8% CPU**, **20% memory**, and **5% GPU utilization** across its Kubernetes sample. The sample represents customers/clusters it sees, not all cloud servers or an available public pool. AWS advertises Spot up to **90%** below on-demand; Google advertises Spot VMs up to **91%** below default, with preemption. [Cast AI, 2026](https://cast.ai/reports/kubernetes-optimization-report/); [AWS Spot pricing](https://aws.amazon.com/ec2/spot/pricing/); [Google Spot pricing](https://cloud.google.com/spot-vms/pricing). | **I:** Spare CPU exists in poorly packed fleets and interruptible markets. A movable capsule helps recovery from preemption for stateful *CPU* work; normal queues/retries and application checkpoints capture much of this value. Low observed GPU utilization does not mean a stranger can access a compatible warm model or its HBM for free. |
| Edge PoPs | **F:** Cloudflare reports a network in **330+ cities** and GPUs for AI inference in **210+ cities** in its 2026 Security Signals report. These are vendor footprint claims, not equal GPU capacity in every city or a promise of same-host scheduling. [Cloudflare, 2026 report, p. 34](https://cf-assets.www.cloudflare.com/slt3lc6tev37/3cSPNEMGs9ayabiOocz06I/19bf4edac8cc1bd8a002b3129280cc40/2026_Cloudflare_Security_Signals_Report.pdf). | **I:** Edge CPUs are good for local glue and reducing origin round trips. Actual free inference capacity, model residency, quotas and placement control remain provider decisions. |
| Different power/carbon regions | **F:** Google's **2025 regional** table lists grid intensity **19 gCO₂e/kWh** in Stockholm, **30** in Finland, **366** in Singapore, and **588** in Jakarta; its CFE% is a different, procurement-aware metric. Cloudflare's October 2026 Container tariff shows a concrete regional cost spread: Internet egress **$0.025/GB** in North America/Europe versus **$0.05/GB** in Oceania/Korea/Taiwan, after the respective included allotments. These are *egress*, not CPU, rates. [Google Cloud regional carbon data, 2025](https://cloud.google.com/sustainability/region-carbon); [Cloudflare pricing, updated 2026-10-02](https://developers.cloudflare.com/workers/platform/pricing/). | **I:** The average-grid spread is substantial, but a migration's *marginal* emissions depend on time, actual powered equipment and grid dispatch. Regional cloud prices, egress and residency can erase the benefit. |

## 3. Five placement tests

| Scenario | What movement could save (**E**, unless cited) | Physics/economics limit; payer |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **A. Follow the data** | A program visiting an owner's 1 GB dataset, reducing it there to 1 MB, avoids **~1,000× cross-boundary payload bytes** versus exporting the entire dataset. A 10 KB answer is **~100,000×**; this is a bytes denominator, not a total-compute claim. At the S3 example's $0.09/GB, one avoided 1 GB Internet download saves about **$0.09** plus transfer time, while scan CPU still runs. Stateful multi-owner workflows are the most plausible need for a continuation. | **I:** The data owner pays local CPU/I/O and must authorize code; the application pays admission, checkpoint, result transfer and perhaps hosting fees. SQL pushdown, a stored procedure or a stateless Worker near one database usually achieves the same reduction. If the capsule retains the dataset, it becomes a large, potentially disallowed export. Test against those baselines, not only bulk download. [AWS S3 pricing](https://aws.amazon.com/s3/pricing/); [Cloudflare Placement](https://developers.cloudflare.com/workers/configuration/placement/). |
| **B. Follow free GPU/model capacity** | **F:** AWS Bedrock cross-region inference already routes a *request* to supported regional capacity, and its global option advertises approximately **10%** cost savings. **I:** A capsule can wait, change endpoint or carry non-model state when a region fails or is cheaper. [AWS cross-region inference docs, accessed 2026-10-28](https://docs.aws.amazon.com/bedrock/latest/userguide/cross-region-inference.html). | **I:** The model must already be loaded, licensed and quality-compatible there; prompts, data rules and KV affinity matter. Moving a 3 MB JS image often costs more latency than routing a prompt/result. It changes neither prefill FLOPs nor decode HBM demand. The provider pays GPU/energy; app pays tokens and migration. Useful if the *whole workflow* must resume by the selected capacity, otherwise route the model request. |
| **C. User device, cloud for hard calls** | **E:** If **90%** of one million qualified tasks can use local rules or an eligible resident small model with equal acceptance quality, central calls fall from 1 million to 100,000. Under the simple Sonnet example above, central model charges fall from **$9,000 to $900** before local costs/fallback overhead. **99%** qualified local resolution would make central calls **100× fewer**, but no source establishes such a hit rate. | **I:** This is a credible scarce-GPU lever **only because inference is replaced**, not because JS moved. The user pays device joules, memory and latency; provider/app pays hard calls and support. A 3B 2-bit weight array has a **0.75 GB ideal minimum** before scales, KV, tokenizer and runtime. Cold download, thermal throttling, OS suspension, privacy consent, model quality and lack of native API access narrow eligibility. A normal client app with a local-model fallback may do the same. [Apple WWDC25](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2025/286/); [Chrome Prompt API](https://developer.chrome.com/docs/ai/prompt-api). |
| **D. Follow the sun/green power** | **E:** For delay-tolerant jobs, choose a warm site/time with lower price or grid intensity. The Google regional *average* values above differ by **~31×** between Stockholm and Jakarta per kWh; this is **not** a measured job-emissions reduction. Spot CPU VMs may discount up to 90–91%; OpenAI's Batch API already gives **50%** off synchronous inference with a **24-hour** completion window, without shipping a JS heap. [Google carbon data](https://cloud.google.com/sustainability/region-carbon); [AWS Spot](https://aws.amazon.com/ec2/spot/pricing/); [Google Spot](https://cloud.google.com/spot-vms/pricing); [OpenAI Batch docs](https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/guides/batch). | **I:** The application pays for deadline slack, state transfer, regional egress and perhaps a worse latency tail. GPUs/weights must be warm, or boot/load costs dominate. Carbon benefit needs hourly *marginal* signals and evidence that one site's output falls as another rises; annual CFE% is insufficient. For a single waiting task, a delayed queue plus model-request routing usually wins. Mobility adds value if meaningful in-flight local state must survive a shift. |
| **E. Volunteer/idle-device agent work** | **E:** One million opt-in laptops contributing one CPU core-hour/day offer **one million core-hours/day** in principle. That is a scenario, not an observed accessible fleet. BOINC demonstrates pull-based independent work, checkpoint/retry and redundant validation; user devices are a huge potential CPU pool. [BOINC platform paper](https://boinc.berkeley.edu/boinc_a_platform_for_volunteer_computing.pdf); [GSMA smartphone ownership](https://www.gsma.com/somic/somic-2025-overview-report/). | **I:** Users pay electricity/battery, bandwidth, RAM and trust risk; an operator pays coordination, verification, incentives, abuse control and fallback. Chrome can freeze CPU-intensive background tabs under Energy Saver; iOS background runtime is discretionary and often suspends apps. Tool agents also need credentials and external side effects, which an untrusted volunteer cannot safely own. Best fit is consented, read-only, verifiable batch CPU work; for agent workflows, ordinary task chunks with explicit input/output are generally easier to audit than live-heap migration. [Chrome, 2025-01-20](https://developer.chrome.com/blog/freezing-on-energy-saver); [Apple WWDC25 background execution](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2025/227/). |

## 4. What already works without moving the program

| Conventional mechanism | What it achieves | Incremental question for a portable continuation |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Request/region routing** | **F:** Bedrock cross-region profiles route model *requests* to available regional compute; Cloudflare AI Gateway's October 2026 Auto Router selects eligible models by quality/cost with fallback and turn affinity for prompt caching. [AWS](https://docs.aws.amazon.com/bedrock/latest/userguide/cross-region-inference.html); [Cloudflare Auto Router](https://developers.cloudflare.com/ai-gateway/features/auto-router/). | **I:** Does keeping local algorithm state across model-site changes save more than sending the next API request to the chosen endpoint? Usually no. Moving mid-turn can lose cache affinity. |
| **Smart Placement / fixed region** | **F:** Cloudflare places a fetch Worker near a probed host or cloud region, or uses traffic analysis to choose a location. [Placement docs](https://developers.cloudflare.com/workers/configuration/placement/). | **I:** Does the *same live program* need to cross multiple owners/regions while preserving closures and pending calls? For one origin, fixed placement is enough. |
| **Batching and warm serving** | **F:** OpenAI Batch advertises **50%** lower cost and completion within 24 hours. Inference servers also batch requests and manage KV; the literature's prefill/decode separation addresses accelerator mismatch. [OpenAI Batch](https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/guides/batch); [MemHA, 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.29986). | **I:** Can the caller expose deadlines and readiness to the serving engine? A portable JS checkpoint is not required for the GPU-side gains. |
| **Model router / local model fallback** | **F:** Bedrock intelligent prompt routing chooses between supported models in a family for predicted quality and cost; Chrome and Apple's device models expose local inference for eligible tasks. [AWS router](https://docs.aws.amazon.com/bedrock/latest/userguide/prompt-routing.html); [Chrome](https://developer.chrome.com/docs/ai/prompt-api); [Apple](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2025/286/). | **I:** A normal client/server app can route calls. A continuation becomes useful when a task actually migrates between a user session, owner domain and edge host while retaining *non-model* state. |

**I — the specific product hypothesis:** Mobility is an orchestration primitive for **stateful, selective near-data work** and **hybrid device/cloud work**. It is not a new accelerator scheduler. The near-data buyer saves exported bytes, origin round trips or compliance friction; the hybrid buyer reduces central model demand on narrow, quality-checked tasks. For cheap-region or idle-CPU work, movement is mainly a durability/placement convenience. No public Workers API inspected here guarantees arbitrary same-host GPU placement, access to KV eviction, or execution on a random phone's NPU.

**I — the fair kill test:** Measure completed useful jobs at equal quality/freshness with (1) central export, (2) SQL/query pushdown plus a normal queue, (3) Cloudflare placement, (4) stateless source+small JSON state/replay, and (5) the movable continuation. Include interpreter/module, all checkpoint versions, host ledger, data/model downloads, CPU, GPU calls/tokens, egress, p50/p95 and device energy when measurable. For a two-owner selective data workflow, require **≥100× fewer cross-boundary bytes versus export** *and* a material gain versus the strongest pushdown/placement baseline; otherwise the 100× headline is only a bad-baseline comparison. For device inference, require a held-out quality gate and central-call reduction after cold loads and fallbacks. These are proposed thresholds, not observed results.

## 5-line TL;DR

1. **F/I:** GPU/HBM, site power and origin/network locality are scarce; cheap JS CPU generally is not.
2. **I:** A movable JS heap carries neither weights nor KV, so it does not directly add inference capacity.
3. **E/I:** Visiting a data owner can cut transfer bytes by 100–100,000× on selective jobs; SQL pushdown is the real baseline.
4. **E/I:** Eligible local models can cut central calls drastically on narrow tasks, but users pay device costs and quality must hold.
5. **I:** Request routing, Smart Placement, batching and model routers already solve most single-site placement cases.

**Verdict: GO-WITH-CHANGES for the compute-scarcity angle.** Sell movement where *stateful placement* unlocks data-local computation or a quality-checked device/cloud split. Do not claim that copying a JS continuation distributes frontier inference, creates GPU capacity, or yields 100× end-to-end agent savings. The defensible 100× version is **cross-boundary bytes avoided on selective data workflows**, or **central calls avoided on a highly qualified narrow workload**; neither has yet been measured against strong baselines.

**Three next steps**

1. **E:** Build one two-owner, multi-stage read-only workload with a 1 GiB fixture and small answer; compare the five baselines above, including full migration bytes and p95 latency.
2. **E:** Test 1,000 held-out extraction/classification tasks across browser WebGPU or supported built-in/native local models and a cloud fallback; report quality, eligibility, cold load, energy where instrumented, central calls and user-device cost.
3. **E:** Instrument a trace of real agent jobs by prefill/decode tokens, tool CPU, origin bytes/RTTs, storage and deadlines; use it to reject placements whose saved scarce resource is smaller than migration overhead.
