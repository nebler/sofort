### 1. P01 — A growing library of timed hardware abstractions — conditional investigation pick

**I:** Replace selected slow public bus/peripheral models with fast executable abstractions, preserving declared IO ordering and timing bounds and falling back to RTL when assumptions fail. **Q seed:** UC says adapting a SystemC/QEMU memory/bus model takes extra time and adapting gem5 to a new pipeline is worse (g2-40, full file in register, 06:00); he names Spike and full Verilog as distinct references at 02:00. The surprising opportunity is the gap between enormous instruction-only speed and modest whole-platform speed: models could grow the parts actually dominating simulation rather than another fixed CPU surrogate. The broad field is already occupied by Spike-RTL, Golden Gate and USF generation; only the CPU-hosted, contract-checked peripheral-library residual deserves investigation. This is neither a driver-translation project based on hardware traces nor a kernel/OS-growing project.

- **What grows / oracle / tailwind:** C++ or Rust component abstractions and their machine-checkable preconditions grow inside a fixed bus/event interface. Check instruction results with Spike, bounded component behavior and IO traces with independently run RTL, and resource/timing claims against held-out workloads. Stronger models may infer more compact state machines and harder timing invariants; frozen acceptance gates reject unsupported abstractions. Trace agreement alone is insufficient for unbounded correctness.
- **One-shot test:** A bounded UART model could be prompt-sized. The proposed value is a reusable, actually validated family of models, with compositional assumptions and retained counterexamples; it is not the generation loop. Whether that family remains valuable beyond a mechanical generator is unproven.
