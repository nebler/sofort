## Recurring themes across the share

All themes below are **I**, grounded in the cited Q rows. A repeated engineering pattern is not by itself an open market.

1. **A small stable semantic boundary can support much more generated structure.** Hardcaml derives a large library and several interpreters from a handful of primitives ([g2-23](../transcripts/g2-23-fossi-foundation-high-performance-hardware-design-with-hardcaml-rachit.txt), 05:00, 12:00–16:00). Chipyard composes cores, accelerators and protocols over existing interfaces ([g2-30](../transcripts/g2-30-hotchipsvideos-hc2025-s6-power-methodology.txt), 01:05:00–01:09:00). Programmable NICs grow policies over fixed packet, DMA and atomic machinery ([g2-28](../transcripts/g2-28-hotchipsvideos-hc2025-s4-networking.txt), 36:00–47:00). This is tailwind-shaped, but each substrate already exists.
2. **Parameterization and composition move the verification problem upward.** Nigam explicitly asks how to verify whole generators, not just instances (g2-23, 19:00–20:00). Condor cross-checks implementations while a shared compliance suite is still emerging at the talk's date ([g2-29](../transcripts/g2-29-hotchipsvideos-hc2025-s1-cpus.txt), 27:00–28:00). Berkeley distinguishes block testbenches from system/application testing (g2-30, 01:26:00). These are different assurance levels; they should not be collapsed into “tests pass.”
3. **Rare combinations defeat plausible-looking prototypes.** Missing diagonal road tiles required extensive generation testing ([g2-26](../transcripts/g2-26-gdc-festival-of-gaming-evolving-worlds-from-chaos-the-art-led-approach.txt), 22:00–25:00). Seasons, weather, GI paths and upscalers made Shadows very hard to QA ([g2-27](../transcripts/g2-27-gdc-festival-of-gaming-rendering-assassin-s-creed-shadows.txt), 56:00–57:00). Cache hazards and dynamic conflicts force CPU replay (g2-29, 15:00–18:00). The useful artifact would have to cover such interactions, not merely generate another happy path.
4. **Performance is a system property with a scarce resource budget.** A rendering schedule must fit memory, queue and image-quality constraints (g2-27, 48:00, 01:00:00–01:01:00); fabric algorithms must operate within microseconds (g2-28, 01:22:00); wider CPUs need matching memory throughput (g2-29, 01:45:00–01:55:00); harvesting firmware must respect a variable power floor (g2-30, 33:00–38:00). Functionally equivalent outputs can differ radically in usefulness.
5. **The strongest explicit effort signals are per-configuration or per-integration costs.** Generated HDL tooling is “a lot of work” (g2-23, 12:00); engine fork maintenance is “death by a thousand cuts” ([g2-25](../transcripts/g2-25-gdc-2025-unreal-engine-preempting-challenges-in-aaa-unreal-engine-deve.txt), 25:00); flat CPU physical design would take multiple weeks per iteration (g2-29, 24:00); NIC control is “mountains of software and hardware” (g2-28, 01:22:00). Those are stronger seeds than general enthusiasm for AI, although private implementations may block a solo entrant.
6. **Artist control and intent are not interchangeable with numerical optimization.** A weight of nine had no usable visual meaning to Fox (g2-26, 33:00–38:00); Graham argues generic engines constrain technical experimentation ([g2-24](../transcripts/g2-24-gdc-festival-of-gaming-independent-games-summit-a-case-for-making-your.txt), 08:00, 23:00); Murphy recommends programmer-defined scripting guardrails (g2-25, 29:00). Better models help only if the owner retains explicit decisions and can inspect outcomes. This does not justify re-pitching the rejected judgment compiler.
7. **Existing tools are often undiscovered; wrappers are especially vulnerable to the next model.** Murphy repeatedly says teams rebuild debuggers, asset databases and gameplay frameworks already present in UE (g2-25, 18:00–22:00). Nigam warns that a new HDL without the surrounding tools is insufficient (g2-23, 12:00). Berkeley's method succeeds partly through existing Chipyard/FireSim/Hammer infrastructure (g2-30, 01:05:00). Documentation/search or a simple wrapper may solve some pains, but does not establish a durable standalone project.
8. **Platform friction survives custom-domain code.** Graham's audience stays with Unity for mobile export and receives no good alternative (g2-24, 26:00–28:00); Ubisoft describes Xcode as a challenge and loses track of effective platform configurations (g2-27, 58:00–59:00); Berkeley's live chip demo encounters serial-monitor routing trouble (g2-30, 01:20:00–01:23:00). All are concrete pains, but a packaging adapter alone probably fails the one-shot test.
9. **Resource efficiency comes from not materializing unnecessary work or state.** UE's proximity PCG avoids storing/loading detail before needed (g2-25, 41:00–42:00); Ubisoft stores sparse lighting probes and only dirty GPU rows (g2-27, 14:00, 26:00–30:00); NICs offload and chain transforms without extra copies (g2-28, 18:00–22:00); Everactive disables unused domains (g2-30, 43:00–44:00). These are already demonstrated practices, not predictions of an untouched field.
10. **Reality supplies the difficult oracle.** Shadows' editor cannot predict exact console behavior (g2-27, 54:00–55:00). Cooling simulations need experiments and realistic nonuniform heaters (g2-30, 13:00, 25:00–26:00). PEZY clearly labels pre-silicon simulation/emulation results (g2-29, 34:00, 53:00–54:00). A software simulator can check its own model while still being wrong about hardware.
11. **Time scales matter in both observation and control.** Millisecond sampling misses microsecond switch behavior (g2-28, 01:57:00–01:59:00); a main-thread animation initialization creates a visible frame hitch (g2-25, 05:00–06:00); coarse duty cycling raises sensor response latency (g2-30, 35:00–39:00). A useful check must observe the time scale on which the claim is made.
12. **Backwards compatibility and inherited state constrain improvement.** Old project defaults survive engine upgrades (g2-25, 04:00–05:00, 39:00); compression offload keeps Deflate for existing data (g2-28, 25:00–26:00); IBM decouples DRAM generations from sockets (g2-29, 01:19:00–01:20:00). Growing implementations must keep working obligations, not only add features.
13. **Graceful transitions and emergency recovery require separate contracts.** Entity-to-actor promotion should preserve state (g2-25, 44:00–46:00); packet loss/reordering requires selective recovery (g2-28, 51:00–57:00); energy-aware nodes prefer cooperative peripheral shutdown but sometimes must cut power immediately (g2-30, 57:00–59:00). This is a repeated implementation problem, not a claim for another general transition-test product.
14. **Independent assurance is different from creating an assurance artifact.** Signatures without verification are ineffectual ([g2-21](../transcripts/g2-21-usenix-usenix-security-25-an-industry-interview-study-of-software-sign.txt), 12:00–16:00); e-classes assume valid transformations (g2-22, 06:00–08:00); a chip booting is insufficient without complete applications (g2-30, 01:17:00–01:18:00). More generated evidence does not automatically strengthen assurance.
15. **Several apparent disagreements are conditional, not universal contradictions.** Graham favors small custom engines (g2-24, 10:00–17:00), while Murphy cautions against replacing supplied UE systems (g2-25, 18:00–22:00): both ask whether a framework fits actual requirements. Nigam doubts naive software-to-hardware translation (g2-23, 19:00), whereas Berkeley reports useful programmable hardware generation (g2-30, 01:05:00–01:16:00): the latter still involves deliberate architecture. NVIDIA prefers buffering at the source (g2-28, 01:30:00), whereas Broadcom discusses deep-buffer inter-data-center products (02:03:00): link distance and workload differ. These are useful design tensions, not proof that one speaker is wrong.

### 1. P01 residual — A road-world engine that grows its missing geometric vocabulary

**Q seed:** Fox says finding the “minimal set of tiles” was “not easy” (g2-26, 22:00); Colin says they had “no guide book” (23:00) and only a “QA team of two people” (24:00). Their technical road connections initially worked while camera motion was intolerable (19:00–21:00). **I:** Build a specialized procedural road-world implementation whose accumulated artifact includes actual road pieces, connectors, drivable surfaces, camera envelopes and composition rules. Given a new junction, grade or camera requirement outside its vocabulary, models extend that vocabulary and implementation inside fixed geometric and route contracts. This is more specific than WFC: Tessera/DeBroglie already assemble constrained tiles, so assembly and statistical controls are dropped. The interesting residual is eliminating missing *realizable* pieces without breaking traversal or the camera. Art direction remains an owner-set acceptance criterion; a geometric test cannot certify beauty.

- **Shape / oracle / tailwind — I:** What grows is an executable family of road/tile realizations and their valid compositions. A separately implemented route-graph checker, swept vehicle/camera envelopes and deterministic traversal simulator check it; exhaustive checks apply only to the declared finite grammar. Better models extend the supported shapes and constraints faster while existing accepted pieces still run without them.
- **One-shot test — I:** A tiny road generator is one-prompt code. The proposed value must be a substantial interacting vocabulary with reproducible traversal coverage and explicit completeness boundaries. If a stronger model produces the same full validated vocabulary from a single prompt, this direction fails; elapsed development alone is no defense.
- **Value / ambition — I:** A game can request unfamiliar procedural road layouts without someone manually discovering missing tile shapes. **Rung:** product, potentially reusable game infrastructure; roughly 10× only if the grown realization library is central.
- **Six-week version — E:** Rust geometry/checking core plus a C# viewer, the talk's ten-shape topology as a starting reference, two vehicle envelopes and two cameras. Demo a requested junction that initially fails, its minimized geometric cause, and an added realization that passes old and new cases. No commercial assets or external participants required.
- **Kill — I:** A small amount of authored geometry plus existing Tessera constraints may already solve the problem. Reproduce a genuinely missing camera-safe shape before investing further. **Status:** Unclear narrow residual; strongest research pick here.

### 2. P02 residual — An energy-adaptive application library with safe peripheral behavior

**Q seed:** Calhoun wants to “gracefully reduce the capabilities” (g2-30, 37:00); his energy subsystem exposes firmware hooks for “different policies” (51:00). In Q&A he says faulty peripherals can require cutting power “without coordination” (57:00–58:00). **I:** Grow complete application/peripheral behaviors for battery-free nodes: what sensing, communication and update work remains useful at each energy level, how devices quiesce, and how work recovers when a peripheral is abruptly removed. The accumulated artifact is working behavior, not an energy dashboard or driver-translation archive. ETAP, constraint-based compilation, DIPS and Everactive already cover major portions; do not pitch intermittent execution, energy optimization or power-trace replay as novel. The residual would need to demonstrate useful service-preserving policies across ordinary publicly purchasable hardware that those systems do not already supply.

- **Shape / oracle / tailwind — I:** Models grow C/Rust application state machines and bounded peripheral policies. Fixed application obligations, a separate supply/peripheral model and measurements on an owner-controlled board check timing, recovery and energy use. Stronger models implement more device/application combinations; accepted firmware remains deployable without inference.
- **One-shot test — I:** A next model can write a generic adaptive scheduler. It cannot conjure this board's real cutoff/restart observations or turn an incorrect device model into physical truth. If the useful value reduces to a few known API calls and data-sheet power numbers, drop it.
- **Value / ambition — I:** A battery-free sensor keeps doing the most useful safe work instead of failing unpredictably as power changes. **Rung:** product with an expanding firmware library; a broad hardware platform would exceed solo scope.
