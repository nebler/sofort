**Triage consequence (I):** the corpus provides unusually strong practitioner signals, but it does not produce five verified empty markets. The strongest shortlist items below are research directions with explicit residuals. Prior art already has much of their plumbing and sometimes most of the proposed product. Phase 2 should try to disprove each residual before implementation. Public availability, broad adoption, and the proposed end-to-end property are different claims.

## Five most interesting candidates, with the ambition filter

### 1. P01 — A network that delivers physical projects, not just purchases

**I:** Take Lütke's Halloween street and Friday 3D-printing examples literally (65, 09:00–12:00): an agent commits to an accomplished physical objective involving design, fit, inventory, fabrication, delivery, and recovery when a dependency fails. At full scale, this is infrastructure through which households and small businesses commission new physical outcomes without first becoming project managers. The scarce asset is a growing set of measured project outcomes plus merchants/fabricators willing to make compatible commitments. **F:** [UCP already provides extensive transaction and fulfillment infrastructure](https://shopify.engineering/UCP); its existence eliminates a “new agent shopping protocol” pitch. **I:** the candidate survives only if real projects expose dependencies and failure ownership that those existing rails do not already handle. This is more surprising than another personal-shopping agent because the useful result is in the physical world, with an actual deadline and an accountable chain of suppliers.

**One-shot test — I:** Yes, a better model could build a convincing planner/shopper from one prompt. Its non-code value would be actual fit/material/delivery observations, reliable counterparties, and responsibility for recovery; a prompt cannot manufacture those facts or commitments.

**Ambition rung — I:** **Field-changing:** make small physical invention and execution available on demand. The supporting network would be **field infrastructure** for commerce and fabrication.

**Smallest first step — I, 2–4 weeks:** choose one tightly bounded printing/craft project class and recruit a small real supplier/fabricator cohort only in a separately authorized pilot. Record requirements, substitutions, arrival promises, measured fit, and successful completion for several projects. The demonstration target is a successful whole project with one deliberately unavailable dependency resolved using real supplier commitments, plus a reusable outcome record. It must add observable capability beyond a generated checklist or UCP checkout demo.

**Kill test — I:** stop if existing merchants or project services already deliver that class end to end, if no one values the completion commitment, or if the supposed reusable knowledge reduces to model-generated descriptions. Do not claim a general manufacturing breakthrough from a craft pilot.

### 2. P02 — Institutional meaning that remains attached to finished work

**I:** Vanessa's failure is precise: every operation succeeds while “downloads” becomes “user growth” (66, 18:00–20:00). Build toward a shared institutional memory whose meaning can be followed from source evidence through calculations, compositions, charts, and published conclusions. Definitions must have accountable domain owners, real examples, explicit disagreements, time windows, missingness rules, and change histories. SAP's runtime design knowledge (67, 09:00–16:00) suggests the same institution-to-execution problem beyond metrics. **F:** [Apache Ossie already targets semantic interoperability across tools and agents](https://github.com/apache/ossie/blob/main/README.md). **I:** the candidate's residual is empirical preservation and adjudication of meaning in completed work, not another schema standard or semantic layer. Its large version is a federation in which independent teams can trust what another team's generated artifact actually claims.

**One-shot test — I:** Yes, a model can implement typed envelopes and a report checker. The non-code asset is agreed business meaning, expert decisions about ambiguous cases, organizational adoption, and a history of actual corrections that future releases must preserve.

**Ambition rung — I:** **Field infrastructure** for organizational knowledge, potentially changing which decisions can safely cross team and tool boundaries. This ambition is conditional on real multi-owner adoption.

**Smallest first step — I, 2–4 weeks:** work with one real metric owner and two distinct artifact consumers. Collect examples of “download,” “install,” “user,” missing weeks, and changed periods. Use Ossie-compatible definitions where possible, then demonstrate an owner-adjudicated correction propagating from a metric's changed meaning into both a chart and a narrative report. The novel target is observed preservation of a real contested meaning through the whole chain, not a synthetic assertion passing its own test.

**Kill test — I:** compare the exact workflow with existing semantic-layer/lineage products and Ossie extensions. Stop or contribute upstream if they already preserve these properties, or if consumers only want an ordinary semantic-model integration. A static declaration of units does not establish real interpretive correctness.

### 3. P03 — A researcher-owned commons for questions whose answers are not already known

**I:** Kernighan's natural-language-to-SQL use case is unusually strong evidence because a renowned programmer is helping a real nonprogrammer colleague and still cannot identify the wrong answers (61, 1:13–1:15). The larger possibility is a consortium of research/data owners that continuously contributes real questions, domain interpretations, counterexamples, reproducible evidence, and legitimately unresolved cases. Agents could then perform analyses whose acceptance is grounded in the institution's observations and expert commitments rather than a second model's confidence. **F:** [BIRD/LiveSQLBench already pursue substantial real-world and semantic-context evaluation](https://bird-bench.github.io/), and [BEAVER has enterprise/domain-knowledge annotations](https://beaverbench.github.io/). **I:** a static SQL benchmark, automatic query fixer, or generic checker-of-checkers is already taken. The residual is a living scientific practice and data-governance network, not another leaderboard.

**One-shot test — I:** Yes, the query app, collaboration UI, and evaluation harness are one-shottable. Real dataset access, the researcher's intended question, domain-grounded observations, and independent adjudication of ambiguous/unknown answers cannot be generated from the product brief.

**Ambition rung — I:** **Field infrastructure**, with a possible **field-changing** outcome in one discipline if it enables trustworthy analyses that previously needed scarce specialist mediation. This is not a claim that all scientific truth becomes automatically decidable.

**Smallest first step — I, 2–4 weeks:** choose one accessible real dataset and a consenting domain owner in a separately authorized pilot. Capture a small set of actual questions, include cases where SQL executes but answers the wrong question, and record the evidence by which the owner distinguishes them. Publish a permitted, reproducible subset with at least one ambiguity resolved by new domain evidence rather than a model vote. Compare directly against existing BIRD/BEAVER-style tasks to identify the additional asset.

**Kill test — I:** stop if current benchmarks/services already supply the intended ongoing owner-grounded workflow, the owner cannot supply an independent acceptance basis, or the contribution becomes a synthetic test set. “Human reviewed” is not sufficient when the human also lacks the answer; explicitly preserve unknowns.

### 4. P04 — A shared collection of real customer failure worlds

**I:** Generalize Tailscale support's reconstruction of AWS/Synology/customer environments (59, 02:00–04:00) and Convex's difficulty reproducing production concurrency bugs (71, 05:00–07:00) into a maintained, permissioned collection of runnable failure worlds. The large objective is to make recurring integration failures reproducible across vendors before the next customer suffers them, with evidence that a repair helps the actual affected environment. Real device behavior, network policies, versions, anonymized incident histories, and continuing vendor participation are the asset. **F:** [Replicated already offers support-state inspection and matching-environment workflows](https://docs.replicated.com/vendor/support-inspecting-support-bundles). **I:** a customer-cloning tool alone is therefore not novel. The residual is shared, cross-vendor, continuously validated empirical coverage spanning kinds of systems outside one product's compatibility matrix.

**One-shot test — I:** Yes, a better model could generate a reconstruction harness. It cannot infer private customer topology, physical device behavior, permission to retain incidents, or whether the proposed world really reproduces an actual customer's failure without observations and participants.

**Ambition rung — I:** **Field infrastructure** for reliability across heterogeneous deployed systems, rather than an individual support assistant or another replay product.

**Smallest first step — I, 2–4 weeks:** start with a few permissioned real incidents in one mixed device/network/cloud stack. Preserve the minimum necessary state and define customer-approved success criteria. The demonstration target is the same real failure reproduced and then repaired under two independently maintained system/version combinations, with a portable, permitted case another maintainer can actually run. Clearly label physical or proprietary pieces that cannot be reproduced faithfully.

**Kill test — I:** first test whether Replicated, vendor support tooling, or existing compatibility programs already reproduce the selected cases. Stop if all worlds are synthetic, sanitization removes the bug, customers will not permit retention, or the corpus merely duplicates existing vendor test suites. A wider label on a support bundle is not a moat.

### 5. P05 — A network for resolving cross-company evidence exceptions

**I:** AWS's bank-onboarding vision (68, 17:00–18:00) is ambitious because separate companies must accept one another's evidence, not merely exchange messages. Lütke's regulator meetings over button wording (65, 19:00) identify the stubborn part: accepted judgment and authority. The proposed residual is a network that makes each institution's unresolved requirements explicit, routes them to authorized humans when necessary, and retains permitted resolutions so equivalent future cases do not restart from PDFs and meetings. **F:** [Swift's KYC Registry already supplies shared due-diligence documents, review, access control, and audit](https://www.swift.com/products/kyc-registry?AKredir=true); [GLEIF already supplies verifiable organizational role/authority infrastructure](https://www.gleif.org/en/organizational-identity/lei-vlei/the-verifiable-lei-vlei). **I:** those are major incumbents, so neither a new document registry nor “identity for agents” is a credible novelty claim. The interesting question is whether unresolved acceptance differences can become a reusable, institutionally recognized coordination asset.

**One-shot test — I:** Yes, a model can build agents that exchange credentials and draft responses. It cannot grant another bank's acceptance, create issuer credibility, establish authority to resolve an exception, or maintain real counterparty agreements.

**Ambition rung — I:** **Field infrastructure** for interorganizational work, with a field-changing long-term goal of reducing repeated institutional onboarding from months to a bounded evidence-resolution process. The transcript's days/weeks outcome is a prediction, not a demonstrated result.

**Smallest first step — I, 2–4 weeks:** obtain two cooperating authorized reviewers in one low-scope, nonproduction evidence process, subject to separate pilot authorization. Reconstruct one already-completed case with conflicting requirements. Demonstrate that a permitted prior resolution can be reused in a second equivalent case while each reviewer independently retains acceptance authority. The first novel result must be actual mutual recognition and eliminated repeated work, not two bots agreeing with each other.

**Kill test — I:** compare directly with Swift's existing review/communication/API features and the participants' current processes. Stop if the exception workflow is already covered, reviewers will not recognize reusable resolutions, or liability/privacy/authority prevents even a narrow pilot. This is the hardest shortlist item to validate without institutional access and the least suitable for a code-only solo demo.

## The most ambitious statement

**Q — Tobi Lütke, Shopify founder/CEO, video 65, 12:00–13:00:** “It's just like I think it's just computers to fulfill our wishes.”

**I:** I choose this over the software-factory claims because its immediate context joins generated designs, real 3D printing, and physical retail. It points beyond producing software toward accomplishing intentions in the world. It is a large possibility, not evidence that a particular proposed business is open.

Other strong ambition evidence retained in the extraction: Thompson's editor/assembler/OS in three weeks (Kernighan, 61, 28:00–29:00); Hamming's prediction that computing would perform more than half of research work (reported by Kernighan, 61, 33:00); Shopify's large renderer factory (65, 24:00–26:00); a project service coordinating real purchases and deadlines (65, 09:00–12:00); SAP's single interaction layer across its systems (67, 06:00–08:00); multinational agents coordinating onboarding (68, 17:00–18:00); company-wide creation by 150,000 employees (68, 18:00–19:00); and an engine serving many agent worktrees from shared state (63, 18:00–19:00).

**I — Enduring bottlenecks when code is cheap:** observed world state and data permission (P03/P04), physical fit/supply/delivery and capital (P01), institutional meaning and taste learned through use (P02/P06), participant trust/authority and cross-party acceptance (P05), independently grounded acceptance evidence (P03/P07/P09), actual compute and storage operations (60; P11/P13), and human attention/relationships (59; 61; 66; 68). These are reasons to investigate a project, not proof it is defensible. Better models can remove many reasoning and implementation failures while leaving these inputs unavailable.
