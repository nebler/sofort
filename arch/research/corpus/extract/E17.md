### Disagreements and unresolved tensions

- **Q → I, proof maintenance:** Beck finds changing proved systems a drag (p3-44 37:00–40:00); de Moura says AI removes that pain in his practice (p3-45 11:00–13:00). This is a valuable comparative research question, not evidence either speaker is universally wrong. Check language, proof granularity, statement size, integration and actual edit histories.
- **Q → I, autonomous factories:** Beck reports every fully unattended attempt going off the rails (p3-44 30:00–34:00); Dodds reports unattended maintenance shipping through explicit documentation, previews, reviewers and recovery layers (p3-49 00:00–18:00). These may concern different tasks and acceptance criteria. PR counts alone cannot adjudicate the tension.
- **Q → I, always-available leverage versus disengagement:** Dodds recommends prompting from a phone rather than merely recording an idea (p3-50 11:00–12:00; p3-53 04:00–07:00), then describes the same pattern harming family time, meals and sleep (p3-47 05:00–10:00). Quiet incoming notifications do not by themselves resolve self-initiated engagement.
- **Q → I, one-shot specification versus iterative discovery:** de Moura envisages checked synthesis from precise intent (p3-45 05:00–10:00); Beck emphasizes that requirements and deployed systems evolve (p3-44 35:00–43:00). These can coexist: a checked implementation can still implement an incomplete or subsequently changed goal.
- **Q → I, breadth versus significance:** Tao celebrates AI finding overlooked proofs but worries that humans cannot digest the flood (p3-46 11:00–13:00, 18:00–21:00). A system can improve solved-problem count while reducing the fraction understood or reused.
- **Q → I, full-stack ambition versus a narrow primitive:** invisible custom software suggests broad composition (p3-48 00:00–09:00), while Iroh's progress required discarding a full stack (p3-57 11:00–15:00). A large mission does not imply owning every implementation layer.
- **Q → I, competing protocol scopes:** Voss questions whether A2A and related protocols need distinct scope from MCP (p3-56 05:00–14:00). Fenner demonstrates an editor-specific ACP boundary (p3-55 12:00–16:00). The shared acronym is not evidence they are the same design. This corpus alone does not establish current protocol winners.

## Five most interesting candidates, through the ambition lens

### 1. C01 — Make machine-produced mathematics digestible as a public knowledge base

**I:** Build a sustained community process that turns checked research proofs into a living, teachable conceptual map: what the central idea is, which assumptions matter, where the technique transfers, and whether another researcher or learner actually understands it. The practitioner signal is unusually direct: **Q**, Tao calls the current flood “proof indigestion” (p3-46 20:00), and de Moura expects human-polished explanatory artifacts to remain valuable (p3-45 35:00–38:00). **F:** [Lean Blueprint](https://github.com/PatrickMassot/leanblueprint) already supplies exposition/formalization infrastructure; the [Mathlib roadmap](https://mathlib-initiative.org/roadmap/) overlaps in text companions, reuse and provenance. **I:** The residual hypothesis is a measurable, expert-run digestion pipeline for newly generated research, including conceptual simplification and successful transfer to neighboring questions. An automatically summarized proof page is insufficient evidence of that result. This is a potential change to how a field accumulates and teaches knowledge, with participation and explanatory quality as the difficult work.

**One-shot test — I:** A stronger model could build the reader, graph and draft explanations from one prompt. The value would be accepted mathematical organization, observed comprehension, researcher reuse, community credit and ongoing editorial stewardship. Better generated prose alone would not supply those outcomes; if experts find no value beyond the model's direct explanation, this candidate fails.

**Ambition rung — I:** **Infrastructure for a field → changes what the field can do**, by keeping communal understanding scalable with machine discovery.

**Smallest first step / novel observation sought — I:** In 3–4 weeks, recruit one subject expert and a small learner/researcher cohort around one newly available, awkward proof. Produce a checked conceptual decomposition and compare unaided comprehension and transfer-to-a-nearby-problem against the raw proof and a direct model explanation. Publish corrections, failure cases and the comparison. The sought new result is demonstrated transfer from a particular opaque proof, not a claim that the publishing interface is novel. No transfer gain or no expert willingness to maintain it is a stop signal.

### 2. C03 — Measure AI on live research, including the attempts that disappear

**I:** Establish a consented longitudinal observatory of researchers' real work: prospectively registered questions, all attempted runs, compute and human interventions, abandoned lines, expert judgments, and later scientific use. **Q:** Tao says firms' success denominators and resource use are unknown to him (p3-46 22:00–24:00); Beck objects to output counts detached from the mission (p3-44 45:00–49:00). **F:** [First Proof already publishes benchmark costs, logs and expert assessments](https://1stproof.org/third-batch-press-release.html). **I:** The remaining candidate concerns the sampling of day-to-day unscreened research and subsequent use over months. It should begin as an extension or collaboration wherever possible. The ambitious asset would be a trusted empirical account of which scientific work is genuinely advancing as models improve, rather than another leaderboard. Whether researchers will disclose enough failures and sensitive work is the first unresolved objection.

**One-shot test — I:** Instrumentation and a dashboard are one-shottable. Prospective task access, publication permission, expert review, complete denominators and evidence of eventual reuse require a network and elapsed time. A better model does not retroactively reveal unrecorded failed attempts.

**Ambition rung — I:** **Infrastructure for a field**, potentially changing research allocation and access to compute if the evidence becomes authoritative.

**Smallest first step / novel observation sought — I:** In 2–4 weeks, register a consenting small group's next 20 bounded research-assistance tasks before outcomes are known. Agree what counts as useful, record failures and human work, and independently adjudicate results. Publish the full sampling rule and a cost/usefulness distribution. Seek a newly observed difference between selected-success impressions and real task yield; do not claim a representative field estimate from 20 tasks. Stop if the sample cannot include honest failures or duplicate an existing First Proof/community initiative's scope without adding useful observations.

### 3. C04 — Preserve the production of scientists while automating scientific output

**I:** Create a research apprenticeship network where agents help students participate in real, checked research while mentors and funders explicitly reward growth in understanding, judgment and original question selection. **Q:** Tao warns that replacing graduate-student-level papers with AI may eliminate the process that produces the next generation of researchers (p3-46 27:00–28:00); Beck describes learning as the goal that leaves software behind (p3-44 32:00–34:00). **F:** [LeanTutor](https://ojs.aaai.org/index.php/AAAI/article/view/41514) demonstrates a verified tutoring approach; its reported proof dataset does not establish a long-term research apprenticeship outcome. **I:** The candidate's distinctive value is the institution and observed human trajectories, especially whether students can later reason and select problems without an agent. This is more ambitious than faster homework completion: it addresses whether the scientific community renews itself under abundant machine output. Mentor participation, genuine research access and funding incentives are difficult independent requirements.

**One-shot test — I:** A next-generation model could generate a tutoring platform and curricula. It cannot create the participants' elapsed learning, a mentor's trusted assessment, real research ownership, or the community's willingness to recognize that training. If a direct model tutor achieves the same durable transfer without the network, the additional institution needs a different justification.

**Ambition rung — I:** **Changes what a field can do**, conditional on demonstrating a viable way to scale expertise as well as papers.

**Smallest first step / novel observation sought — I:** In four weeks, run a tiny mentor-led cohort on one real formalization or proof-digestion contribution. Agree task allocation and use of agents in advance. Compare immediate completion with a delayed unaided explanation and a new related problem; retain unsuccessful cases. Seek a concrete observed training pattern that improves durable understanding without preventing contribution. This first study demonstrates feasibility and failure modes, not an effect size or proof that a new educational system works.

### 4. C05 — A public empirical basis for device-to-device computing

**I:** Organize an independent device/operator cohort that measures continuity of real applications across browser/native networking, Wi-Fi/carrier changes, NATs, restricted environments, relay fallback and upgrades. **Q:** Tokunaga's browser inference prototype cannot supply TURN fallback and may fail on restricted networks (p3-39 23:00–24:00); O'Brien describes custom simulators, packet tools and performance history needed for dependable networking (p3-57 16:00–18:00). **F:** [Iroh already offers diagnostic and monitoring services](https://www.iroh.computer/services/enterprise), and its [0.98 release documents field-relevant regressions and tests](https://www.iroh.computer/blog/iroh-0-98-0-getting-back-to-traversing-nats). **I:** The residual is a public, multi-stack observation commons calibrated to real participant environments, with data flowing back into existing implementations. Its large mission is to make user-owned distributed computing dependable enough to build on broadly. A single-vendor dashboard or generic tunnel would duplicate existing work. Participation, consent, topology diversity and recurring measurement are the durable assets.

**One-shot test — I:** Models can build probes and simulators. They cannot summon independent carriers, devices, operator cooperation, historical failure rates or permission to observe networks. A better networking stack can reduce failures; continuing measurements must prove they still reveal useful new behavior.

**Ambition rung — I:** **Infrastructure for a field**, contributing empirical stewardship to O'Brien's much larger Internet-upgrade ambition.

**Smallest first step / novel observation sought — I:** In three weeks, enlist a handful of volunteers and compare one browser WebRTC use case with an Iroh application under the same controlled Wi-Fi/mobile transitions. Collect only the agreed technical diagnostics, document failures and relay usage, and return one real reproducible finding upstream. The novel observation sought is a previously unrecorded cross-stack continuity difference, not a claim of creating the first network simulator. Stop if existing public Iroh/WebRTC observations cover the cases or volunteers will not sustain useful coverage.

### 5. C08 — Shared rules for the human capacity consumed by autonomous agents

**I:** Treat a person's decision time and interrupt tolerance as a resource that every connected agent must respect: a common owner-controlled schedule, urgency standard, batchable decision queue, delegation limits and feedback on actual attention costs. **Q:** Dodds says the remaining limit is himself, reports lost sleep and family attention, and asks how to stop interacting with tireless agents (p3-47 02:00–10:00). His recommendation to initiate new work from a phone in p3-50 11:00–12:00 reveals that suppressing incoming alerts is only part of the problem. **F:** [OpenClaw already has quiet hours and rate limits](https://docs.openclaw.ai/android); Dodds builds his own quiet-hours skill on camera. **I:** The large residual hypothesis is a cross-provider agreement with demonstrated reductions in unnecessary decision demand while preserving genuinely urgent contact and meaningful owner control. Taste, stopping behavior, missed urgency and independent vendor adoption make this more than a scheduling widget. Its route to field infrastructure is uncertain and should be judged strictly.

**One-shot test — I:** Quiet hours, prioritization and batching are one-shottable. Cross-agent enforcement, real owner preferences, observed urgency errors and changes in actual behavior require consent, use and institutional adoption. If improved models remove the decision demand or existing device controls suffice, this project should contract or stop.

**Ambition rung — I:** **Category → infrastructure for a field** if multiple independent agent systems honor shared owner limits; **tool** if it remains a notification filter. The latter fails the ambition filter.

**Smallest first step / novel observation sought — I:** In 2–3 weeks, work with a few people already running agents across at least two independent systems. Record a short baseline, then apply an owner-chosen shared decision window and exception rule. Measure unanswered critical events, unnecessary interruptions, self-initiated reentry and actual disengagement. Seek new evidence of whether coordinated limits outperform per-app quiet hours. Small samples establish feasibility and unintended behavior, not health effects or population-wide productivity gains.
