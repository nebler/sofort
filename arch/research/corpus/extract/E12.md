## Five most interesting candidates

### 1. A paid apprenticeship infrastructure for the post-coding profession — C02

**I:** Build the institution that can train and place 10,000 product engineers a year through supervised work with real businesses, rather than sell a course about using agents. The practitioner seed is unusually explicit: Voss says the old junior ladder is disappearing and asks who teaches spending a week with a baker and coming back with a spec (p3-08 19:00–22:00, 08.17–08.20). Stephenson's warning about tools that merely accelerate already exceptional learners supplies a valuable selection rule (p3-01 1:28:00–1:32:00, 01.50–01.52). Employers, local businesses, mentors and learners jointly supply access and evidence of competence. **F:** IBM already runs apprenticeship and entry-level programmes ([official programmes](https://www.ibm.com/careers/career-opportunities)); this is an attempted expansion and different training unit, not the invention of apprenticeships. **I:** One person can begin in weeks with three paid novices, one experienced mentor and three consenting businesses, recording a manual baseline, discovered constraints, acceptance decisions, delivered outcomes and transfer to a second unfamiliar business. The first distinctive demonstration should be novices producing verified operational value and acquiring transferable discovery judgement, rather than merely generating more code. Whether mentors and paid placements scale is the central kill test.

**One-shot test — I:** A stronger model can build the curriculum app and agents; it cannot conjure trusted customer access, paid placements, mentors' scarce attention or longitudinal evidence that a novice can independently discover and solve the next problem. The non-code value is **participants, trust, observed learning and situated taste**. Stronger agents could also reduce demand for the role, so the programme must repeatedly compete against agent-only baselines.

**Ambition rung — I:** **Infrastructure for a field**, potentially changing entry into software development; the first cohort tests an institution, not a new learning-management UI.

### 2. The high-altitude launch tower as shared physical infrastructure — C01

**I:** Take Stephenson's unusually literal “somebody ought to build” seriously: a tall structure whose shared economics include launch and additional high-altitude uses (p3-01 1:15:00–1:22:00, 01.42–01.46). This is the clearest 100–1000× ambition in the share because it changes a physical boundary condition for a whole field. His own account also warns that insurability and delayed revenue can defeat a brilliant launch idea. **F:** ASU/Stephenson/Hjelmstad already explored the concept and structural models ([project](https://hieroglyph.asu.edu/project/the-tall-tower/), [engineering account](https://hieroglyph.asu.edu/2014/08/structural-design-of-the-tall-tower/)); it is neither a new invention nor a proved feasible construction project. **I:** The first weeks should produce a reproducible coupled load/economics model reviewed by a structural specialist, plus an inexpensive instrumented structural-module experiment comparing measured wind-induced response with a predeclared prediction. The distinctive target is new measured behaviour that materially changes an envelope or financing assumption from the earlier concept work. A tabletop structure cannot validate a 20-km tower; no launch hardware is needed for this first step. Stop if the analysis finds no credible physical or multiuser economic path; publish the constraint instead of polishing a simulator.

**One-shot test — I:** A model can write simulation/control code and review old designs. It cannot produce **new physical measurements, site rights, material/fabrication capacity, committed anchor users, insurable reliability or capital** from a prompt. Better models help this programme rather than substitute for those assets.

**Ambition rung — I:** **Changes what a field can do**, conditional on engineering/economic feasibility; the first step is an independently checkable measurement, not a claim to build a megastructure alone in weeks.

### 3. A federation of real company task worlds — C03

**I:** Let organisations contribute authorised, refreshed task environments covering real service state, external dependencies, relevant human context and independently defined outcomes, so agents can train and be evaluated on the whole work rather than toy replicas. The seed is repeated by three people: Ali's dependency problem (p3-05 35:00–36:00, 05.24), Song's unanswered distributed/S3 question and manual data preparation (p3-06 29:00–31:00/55:00, 06.13–06.14/06.28), and Gupta's forward-deployed environment setup (p3-07 15:00, 07.20). **F:** [TheAgentCompany](https://the-agent-company.com/) already offers a simulated software company, and Harbor already runs realistic environments in this corpus. **I:** The proposed extension is a governed federation with service-owner participation and measured correspondence to live workflows. In weeks, recruit two organisations with the same narrow multistep task, obtain permission for one relevant external service/test tenant, and package snapshots plus held-out actual executions. Demonstrate a failure that passes the local mock but is predicted by the contributed world and confirmed in the authorised live workflow. Kill the idea if maintaining fidelity costs as much as doing the work manually or participants will not grant the necessary access. The contribution process and refresh economics matter more than the runner.

**One-shot test — I:** A model can generate the harness and synthetic company; it cannot invent **real task distributions, external-service permissions, participant commitments or longitudinal observations**. Better models can reduce packaging cost, but their simulations still need observations of the systems they claim to represent.

**Ambition rung — I:** **Infrastructure for a field**: a shared empirical substrate for autonomous economic work. A private benchmark directory alone is only a product and does not satisfy this ambition.

### 4. Experimental agent-organisation science that predicts software structure — C05

**I:** Treat Casey's prediction as a falsifiable scientific programme: communication and validation delays should cause separate pockets of improvement, even in organisations composed entirely of agents (p3-03 02:00–05:00, 03.01/03.05). Run long-lived controlled organisations against evolving software tasks and measure whether runtime/API/data boundaries emerge from communication constraints, not merely whether more agents score higher. **F:** The [scaling-agent-systems paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.08296) already compares fixed coordination architectures across many configurations; its existence rules out “first science of multi-agent scaling.” **I:** The specific extension is architectural emergence and adaptation after delays change. One person can begin in weeks with a small evolving application, equal total compute budgets, a few team sizes, controlled communication/compiler latency and independently scored user requirements. The distinctive demonstration would be a reproducible shift in the *software's* dependency/API structure caused by changing only a communication link, followed by a successful prediction on a held-out requirement. If the effect vanishes after controlling context, budget and task decomposition, publish that result. Do not escalate to a million agents without a measured effect and a reason scale helps.

**One-shot test — I:** The orchestration code is one-shot material. The potential value is **new empirical observations and scientific knowledge**, independently replicated across tasks, models and organisations, plus compute and a cumulative experimental record. A stronger model can run the science faster; it cannot make the claimed law true by writing it down.

**Ambition rung — I:** **Infrastructure for a field**, with a possible **field-changing** result if it predicts how huge autonomous engineering organisations should be structured. Without a new predictive result, it remains a research contribution rather than a company.

### 5. A participant market for useful compute, with task-level grades — C13

**I:** Build the missing shared agreement around what compute actually delivers: task success at defined quality, latency, availability, access scope and total cost, then contracts and eventual clearing around comparable service grades. Kennon explicitly says price per successful task is hard to standardise and that compute needs grades, while distinguishing them from ratings, price indexes and hedges (p3-10 01:00/12:00–15:00, 10.02/10.12–10.15). The panel's token-futures joke supplies a second, weak signal (p3-03 34:00, 03.25). **F:** [SemiAnalysis](https://gpu-index.semianalysis.com/) and [Computable](https://www.getcomputable.com/) already publish GPU rental-price indexes; ClusterMax/InferenceX occupy ratings and stack comparison in the talk. **I:** A new chart or commodity-price scraper is not the proposal. Begin in weeks with two willing operators, one paid buyer and one bounded workload; agree independently checked acceptance criteria and responsibility for failed/late work, execute the same paid batch across both, and publish participant-approved evidence of cost and settlement. The distinctive target is an actual buyer/operator agreement that remains comparable after a serving-stack update, rather than another laboratory throughput comparison. If participants cannot agree a grade without recreating bespoke consulting, the market thesis fails. No claim is made that these contracts or a resulting market would already be liquid.

**One-shot test — I:** A model can build the exchange UI and contracts-as-code. It cannot supply **actual transaction observations, scarce compute commitments, accepted grades, counterparties' trust, permissions or market liquidity**. Better models may make outcomes easier to verify, but the participant agreement and capital commitment remain real work.

**Ambition rung — I:** **Infrastructure for a field**: make quality-adjusted computational work comparable and contractible. It becomes field-changing only if the common grade survives changing models/tasks and earns real adoption.
