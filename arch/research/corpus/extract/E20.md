## Recurring themes and disagreements across the share

References in this section use the video IDs and source-file links in the ten sections above; times are the supplied transcript buckets. These are cross-video interpretations (**I**), not measured prevalence estimates.

1. **Integration is comparable to invention in effort.** Myreen says integrating a verified pass takes almost as much work as devising it (g1-01, 58:00–59:00); Minsky describes years of productionizing multicore and difficult mode propagation through libraries (g1-05, 04:00–06:00, 47:00); Google reports 13,000-line migration chunks forced by interop limitations (g1-10, 03:00). **I:** Better generators are most useful inside stable, independently checked integration boundaries.
2. **The definition of correct behavior is a separate asset from a capable search engine.** Su calls reliable oracles the central obstacle to generated transformations (g1-03, 45:00, 49:00); Primi says Antithesis checks whatever the user defines, not what the engine independently knows is right (g1-09, 56:00); Wayne distinguishes invariants from trace-level liveness and explicitly states fairness assumptions (g1-08, 20:00–23:00). **I:** Name the exact oracle and its assumptions before proposing autonomous growth.
3. **A proof can stop at the wrong boundary.** Myreen's FPGA story ends at the model/physical-device boundary and his liveness theorem needs memory bounds (g1-01, 04:00–05:00, 28:00–30:00). Primi finds failures outside the abstract algorithm, including operating-system connection behavior (g1-09, 35:00–37:00, 53:00–55:00). Rust references and C++ references have incompatible aliasing promises (g1-10, 17:00–19:00). **I:** An integration theorem must say what remains assumed; neither a formal model nor a test suite covers every surrounding system automatically.
4. **Real workloads and representative tasks overturn comforting benchmarks.** Minsky's small-program benchmarks missed large regressions (g1-05, 42:00–44:00); Rute questions near-duplicate theorem splits, competition training and incomparable compute budgets (g1-06, 07:00–09:00); Primi warns that pristine subsecond tests miss aging and maintenance behavior (g1-09, 21:00, 25:00). **I:** Keep exact test scope visible, and do not turn a generated-corpus percentage into a production failure rate.
5. **Memory and lifetime accounting survive stronger models.** CakeML liveness can fail through OOM (g1-01, 28:00); Eio continuations and async buffers need disciplined ownership (g1-04, 22:00, 36:00); off-heap GC pacing caused persistent pain and actual rollbacks (g1-05, 25:00–33:00); C++ aliasing, invalidation and relocation resist naïve Rust mappings (g1-10, 20:00–27:00). **I:** The promising artifact is an executable implementation with explicit resource semantics, not a diagram claiming universal safety.
6. **User-space extension points enable experimentation.** Eio uses effects to let schedulers evolve without rebuilding the language (g1-04, 34:00–41:00, 49:00); Minsky wants adaptive library schedulers (g1-05, 47:00–49:00); Mandry/Cramer propose typed extension points for foreign templates (g1-10, 36:00–38:00). **I:** An extensible substrate is useful only if the extension contract preserves the properties the host depends on.
7. **Version drift repeatedly recreates manual work.** GCC updates break translation validation (g1-01, 14:00–15:00); Lean/mathlib maintain adaptation notes and combined CI (g1-02, 52:00–57:00); AI proof tools and premise databases need updates (g1-06, 12:00–13:00); Rust ABI bindings need regeneration (g1-10, 32:00–34:00). **I:** Accumulated checked artifacts should be tied to concrete versions, with change detection outside the generating model's control.
8. **Usability is a technical bottleneck, not proof that models are too weak.** Rute disputes the capability-only explanation (g1-06, 02:00); Rust leaders discover enduring learning barriers (g1-07, 15:00–19:00); Antithesis calls onboarding its bottleneck despite strong search (g1-09, 58:00–59:00). **I:** These are real opportunities for useful tools, but a tutor, installer or generic agent wrapper usually fails the requested one-shot test.
9. **Most broad directions are spoken for by their seed's author.** FRO owns Lean parallelism, documentation, grind and monadic reasoning (g1-02, 25:00–26:00); Eio already provides portable native I/O (g1-04, 38:00–40:00); OxCaml owns modes, adaptive schedulers and library propagation (g1-05, 46:00–52:00); Antithesis ships agent skills (g1-09, 58:00–59:00); Google develops Crubit and richer interop (g1-10, 08:00–10:00, 32:00–39:00). **I:** A roadmap is an ownership signal, not a vacant project request.
10. **Preserve the disagreements rather than resolving them by assertion.** Myreen favors robust verified passes and warns about translation-validation brittleness; an audience researcher argues validation is more practical for LLVM-scale artifacts (g1-01, 14:00–15:00, 53:00–55:00). Su emphasizes optimization gaps, while an audience member challenges the compatibility of optimality and predictability (g1-03, 40:00–41:00). Madhavapeddy criticizes POSIX hot paths, then agrees it is useful for configuration after Stephen's challenge (g1-04, 50:00–52:00). An audience member proposes a larger benchmark community; Minsky instead prioritizes easier use of real programs (g1-05, 53:00–55:00). **I:** These tradeoffs are unresolved design inputs, not slogans to choose a side by.

## Five most interesting candidates, ranked under the tailwind lens

**I — Interpretation of this shortlist:** these are the five most interesting directions to investigate, not five claims of passing the filter. A full novelty/feasibility verdict would be dishonest after this small search. P02 and P01 are the strongest seeds; P06, P04 and P08 have substantial unresolved objections. In particular, P06/P08 may collapse into one project. ### 1. P02 — Compiler-certified space bounds that a source programmer can use

**I — Candidate/value:** grow a usable source-facing memory-proof layer over CakeML's existing compiler-sound cost model, so a developer can establish a concrete heap/stack budget and retain a liveness guarantee in the resulting machine code. The direct seed is **Magnus Myreen, CakeML researcher**, saying “we don't have a good way of actually proving anything ... beyond ... small programs” in [`g1-01`](../transcripts/g1-01-acm-sigplan-pldi24-much-still-to-do-in-compiler-verification-a-perspec.txt), **35:00–36:00**, together with the source-cost problem at **30:00–32:00** and the proposed logic integration at **44:00**. This is unusually specific: a verified lower-level oracle exists, a complementary high-level reasoning approach exists, and the practitioner says the join is missing. The interesting output is a growing body of executable, compiler-linked memory guarantees, usable without a model at runtime. **F:** the [POPL 2023 logic paper](https://iris-project.org/pdfs/2023-popl-diamonds.pdf), §2.1, explicitly identifies adaptation to DataLang as the bridge; [CakeML's current project list](https://cakeml.org/projects.html) does not establish that this integration has shipped.

- **Shape / Oracle / Effort (I):** models grow verified rules for closures, roots, containers and loops, plus certified source-to-DataLang explanations, inside a human-defined cost interface. HOL4/compiler theorems check memory claims; a Lean/Rocq-side logic cannot be treated as trusted merely because it resembles the HOL4 semantics. The compilation/version relation itself needs proof or an explicitly stated trusted boundary. Myreen's **35:00–36:00** limitation is the effort signal.
- **Tailwind (I):** stronger models can discharge larger root/liveness obligations and extend coverage while the same checker rejects unsound bounds. Each release increases the useful proved-program set rather than replacing the value with chat advice.
- **One-shot test (I):** a small bound calculator is plausibly prompt-sized and fails. The proposed value is the accumulated, replayable theorem chain tying nontrivial source patterns to exact compiled artifacts, including compiler-induced allocation changes. Its resistance to a future one-prompt build is a hypothesis to test, not a mathematical impossibility.
- **Ambition rung / solo / language (I/E):** **field infrastructure**, approached as a roughly **10× solo research-engineering project** by reusing CakeML rather than creating another language/compiler. Use Standard ML/HOL4, with an optional Rust interface. No partners or private traces are needed for a bounded core; a universal clean cost semantics would be much too broad.
- **Six-week version and demo (E):** one streaming parser or bounded-buffer service, plus a closure/container example, receives a proved budget linked to the compiled code. A deliberately retained root or changed allocation schedule makes the certificate fail. The demo must exceed the small “yes”/list examples already present in prior work.
- **Strongest objection / cheapest kill (I):** allocation motion makes source bounds either intractable or so conservative that they are useless. Spend a few days attempting one closure-capturing example against the actual DataLang semantics. Stop if the proposed user interface merely renames every compiler intermediate state or cannot handle one changed root set. Also stop if a current integrated implementation already provides the claimed guarantee.

### 2. P01 — A verified compiler that can join an ordinary OCaml program

**I — Candidate/value:** grow a verified native-object backend for one real OCaml toolchain, so useful verified components can be called by ordinary OCaml code without rewriting the whole application. **Magnus Myreen** names the adoption problem—“you have to write your entire program in [CakeML]”—in [`g1-01`](../transcripts/g1-01-acm-sigplan-pldi24-much-still-to-do-in-compiler-verification-a-perspec.txt), **37:00**, and asks for real object/interface specifications and verified functional separate compilation at **42:00–44:00**. This has a more concrete value than another compiler demo: remove the all-or-nothing boundary the implementer dislikes. Begin with one calling convention and constrained value representation; GHC, multiple OCaml releases and arbitrary concurrent foreign callbacks are later scope, not initial commitments. **F:** [Pyrosome's July 2026 account](https://www.csail.mit.edu/event/framework-modular-metatheory-and-verified-compilation) already covers general compositional verified-compilation theory. The residual must be a checked bridge to a real toolchain, not a renamed modularity framework.

- **Shape / Oracle / Effort (I):** models grow object emitters, representation adapters and proof-bearing calling/GC-root rules inside a fixed native interface specification. The oracle combines a mechanized machine/calling-convention model with tests against the pinned public OCaml implementation. Differential runs help discover spec mistakes but cannot prove every host-runtime behavior. The effort seed is repeated language-specific proofs and integration work at **41:00–42:00**, **58:00–59:00**.
- **Tailwind (I):** better models extend the supported value/call shapes and prove their composition faster. Already accepted native modules and interface theorems remain useful when proposal models change.
- **One-shot test (I):** a C wrapper for a pure scalar function is prompt-sized. The intended value is a growing verified emitter/adapter artifact with linked guarantees across native representation and lifetime boundaries. The human work is choosing a sound boundary and keeping the host assumptions explicit; proof scripts alone do not establish the spec matches the host.
- **Ambition rung / solo / language (I/E):** **field infrastructure**, approximately **10×** only when confined to one architecture/toolchain and a deliberately restricted component subset. Standard ML/HOL4 plus OCaml and limited C/Rust support code. Full GHC-and-OCaml heap/runtime interoperability is beyond the proposed solo scope.
- **Six-week version and demo (E):** an unmodified OCaml application links a separately compiled verified leaf module through a real native object interface. Exercise a bounded supported representation and an allocation/root boundary; show the proof assumption that prevents a corrupted call. A scalar-only bridge is an early feasibility result, not the finished value.
- **Strongest objection / cheapest kill (I):** interoperability requires verifying or trusting so much of the host GC/runtime that the end-to-end value disappears. Write the smallest honest boundary theorem before expanding the emitter. Stop if it reduces to assuming every interesting property of the foreign runtime, or if a current verified native-interface project already supplies this exact bridge. Status remains **Unclear**.

### 3. P06 — A small checked machine for asynchronous I/O operations

**I — Candidate/value:** grow an executable user-space I/O engine whose operation vocabulary includes dependency ordering, buffer loans, completions and cancellation, then add optimized native interpreters only when they preserve the engine's contracts. The seed is **Anil Madhavapeddy**, describing I/O as a “DSL ... interpreted by the back end” in [`g1-04`](../transcripts/g1-04-acm-sigplan-vmil-25-the-wild-west-of-post-posix-io-interfaces.txt), **37:00**, saying existing higher-level interfaces do not exploit every backend at **46:00**, and naming divergent metadata and missing shared semantics at **51:00–54:00**. The value would be portable fast I/O with a reviewable resource/lifecycle contract. **F:** [Eio already has resource APIs, multiple schedulers and a mock backend](https://github.com/ocaml-multicore/eio/blob/main/HACKING.md). This candidate survives only as a materially stronger checked execution artifact; a new portability layer or ordinary mock is taken.

- **Shape / Oracle / Effort (I):** models grow native batch interpreters and safe flow specializations inside a fixed finite operation semantics. A simple reference interpreter, bounded interleaving exploration and buffer-ownership proofs are the checks. Public native API documentation constrains mappings. Normal tests cannot justify a universal refinement claim, and a specification permitting premature buffer release is itself wrong. The engineering signal is “extremely hard to retrofit” at **22:00**, plus incomplete backend exploitation at **46:00**.
- **Tailwind (I):** each stronger model can implement more native operation families and propose more batching/zero-copy strategies, while independently checked lifecycle laws retain accepted work. The result runs as a normal library without model inference.
- **One-shot test (I):** a finite-state checker or io_uring wrapper alone is prompt-sized. The intended artifact is a working, progressively expanded engine with checked mappings and measured fast paths. If the strongest model can produce the complete narrow engine, mappings and independent acceptance evidence from one prompt, this scope fails and should be dropped rather than relabeled infrastructure.
- **Ambition rung / solo / language (I/E):** **product moving toward field infrastructure**, about **10×** only for a compact vocabulary and two public backends. Rust for the library and a mechanized logic for contracts, or an OCaml/HOL4 implementation. Keep every change in user space; no kernel or OS growth.
- **Six-week version and demo (E):** a stream-copy/echo workload runs through an intentionally simple interpreter and a Linux ring backend. Adversarial completion ordering, cancellation and close/reuse cases are checked against the same fixed contracts; one optimized batch avoids copies or transitions under an explicit equivalent-behavior argument. Measure against Eio/liburing baselines, including overhead.
- **Strongest objection / cheapest kill (I):** existing queue-verification work already provides the relevant semantics, or the common model loses platform-specific performance. Audit those systems before expansion, then implement one batch involving cancellation and a buffer loan. Stop if the only deliverable is another test harness, or if the optimized interpreter cannot retain a concrete performance benefit. Status remains **Unclear**.

### 4. P04 — A complete native-code correctness chain for reference-counted programs

**I — Candidate/value:** grow a bounded functional-language compiler whose theorem covers source behavior, reference-count insertion, reuse, allocation and final native execution together. **Magnus Myreen** explicitly requests an “end ... verified compiler ... with reference counting” in [`g1-01`](../transcripts/g1-01-acm-sigplan-pldi24-much-still-to-do-in-compiler-verification-a-perspec.txt), **45:00–46:00**, while acknowledging existing reference-counting work. The value is predictable reclamation in a useful compiled artifact with a clear correctness chain. **F:** [Koka](https://koka-lang.github.io/koka/doc/kokaspec.html), [Ori's 2026 AIMS work](https://ori-lang.com/blog/the-double-free-that-rebuilt-aims/) and [Prism's checked reference-count/reuse design](https://sdiehl.github.io/prism/compiler.html) already occupy much of the surrounding ground. Their existence is a strong warning: only the missing complete native-code theorem could justify a separate project, and this extraction has not proved that it is missing everywhere.

- **Shape / Oracle / Effort (I):** models grow a small compiler/runtime and proofs of count placement, reuse and allocator invariants inside a fixed source and heap semantics, composing with an existing verified backend where technically valid. A prover kernel checks the whole semantic chain; differential execution is supporting evidence. The effort seed is Myreen's framing as a PhD-sized missing stack, not a missing `dup`/`drop` algorithm.
- **Tailwind (I):** stronger models increase language/optimization coverage under stable proof obligations. The durable output is an executable compiler plus checked accumulated transformations, not a model deciding whether its own counts look plausible.
- **One-shot test (I):** an agent-generated RC interpreter is prompt-sized and fails. The full proposed value includes a replayable correctness theorem through real allocation and native execution. If completing this theorem is just assembling already available components, the project may be a useful integration patch rather than the requested ambitious undertaking.
- **Ambition rung / solo / language (I/E):** **field infrastructure**, approximately **10×** only with an acyclic, restricted language and a reused backend. Standard ML/HOL4 or Rocq with an OCaml/Rust implementation. Arbitrary cycles, threads and full existing-language compatibility would exceed scope.
- **Six-week version and demo (E):** compile lists/trees with sharing and reuse; prove the count/allocator simulation and connect one native run to the source result. Inject an invalid reuse or missing retain and show that the proof fails. Merely showing fewer allocations is already well-covered prior art.
- **Strongest objection / cheapest kill (I):** the exact complete guarantee is already being built, or its only missing work is a years-long semantic proof with little usable output. Audit the closest projects' theorem statements before implementing anything. Drop immediately if they cover the proposed chain. Status is **Being built in parts / exact residual Unclear**, so this is lower confidence than P02/P01.

### 5. P08 — A contract-preserving queue scheduler on shared rings

**I — Candidate/value:** investigate a user-space M:N scheduler that presents a precisely bounded queue interface while mapping work directly onto shared rings, reducing the scheduler conflicts Madhavapeddy describes. The direct seed is **Anil Madhavapeddy** asking about the gap between macOS shared queues and low-level rings and saying an M:N implementation “to my knowledge ... hasn't been done yet” in [`g1-04`](../transcripts/g1-04-acm-sigplan-vmil-25-the-wild-west-of-post-posix-io-interfaces.txt), **45:00**. **Yaron Minsky** independently names job granularity, thread budgets and combined concurrency/parallelism difficulties in [`g1-05`](../transcripts/g1-05-acm-sigplan-rebase-25-the-saga-of-multicore-ocaml.txt), **47:00–49:00**. The clear value would be letting ordinary queued programs use modern I/O without hidden competing pools. **F:** [IORingSwift](https://github.com/PADL/IORingSwift) already offers a ring-backed Swift executor, including global/preference policies and documented limitations. This eliminates the easy wrapper version and makes the remaining claim much narrower.

- **Shape / Oracle / Effort (I):** models grow queue policies and workload specializations inside an owner-written contract for serial ordering, dependencies, completion and cancellation. A simple queue interpreter and state exploration check semantics; measured throughput, latency and core budgets check performance. An exact claim about priorities needs an explicit contract rather than assuming the reference's unspecified scheduler choices are guaranteed. The source's unfinished mapping at **39:00**, **45:00** is the effort seed.
- **Tailwind (I):** stronger models could add checked policies for distinct workload classes and native backends. Accepted scheduler implementations and measured behavior remain artifacts that execute without a model.
- **One-shot test (I):** a thread pool or ring executor is prompt-sized and already shipped elsewhere. A complete contract-preserving scheduler with multiple proven mappings might accumulate value, but this is the weakest one-shot defense in the shortlist; a stronger model may dissolve the residual design work.
- **Ambition rung / solo / language (I/E):** **product**, potentially **field infrastructure** if the queue contract supports independent runtimes. Approximately **10×** only if a useful restricted queue model has an efficient implementation. Rust or OCaml, with a small mechanized checker. Full Grand Central Dispatch parity is not the proposed scope.
- **Six-week version and demo (E):** a bounded serial/concurrent/dependency queue program runs unchanged on a simple interpreter and ring-backed executor. Show ordering/cancellation checks, a reproducible reduction in pool/context-switch overhead, and behavior under CPU saturation. Compare directly with IORingSwift's executor policies and Eio rather than a deliberately weak baseline.
- **Strongest objection / cheapest kill (I):** this is already solved by current executors, or it is only P06 with a different frontend. Run one real queued-I/O workload on the closest existing executor before designing another. If there is no missing semantic guarantee or material scheduling benefit, drop it; otherwise merge with P06 unless the separate value is demonstrated. Status remains **Unclear**, not a recommendation to compete with a funded runtime team.
