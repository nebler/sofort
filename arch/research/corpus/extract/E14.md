## Five most interesting candidates after the ambition filter

All five are **I expansions of practitioner signals**. They are research directions to investigate with participating users, not five established open startup opportunities. The quick checks found meaningful prior art for each. They avoid re-pitching the kill list's agent loops, generic API migrations, hosting primitives, ordinary replay, schema generators, or generic proofs as new.

### 1. P01 — Cooperative, continuously observed infrastructure

**I — Candidate:** Make operational infrastructure inspectable through a shared, revisable record of what has actually been observed: geometry, assets, age, coverage, known uncertainty, proposed work, and accepted corrections across owners. **Q — Signal:** Leaman describes repeated expensive plan/reality collisions, 6–8-week-old surveys, 10 GB/km, and human review of detected problems (p3-26 07:00–13:00), then suggests imagery can show “how it looked last week” (25:00). **F — Closest attempt:** SNCF/Sopra Steria already reconcile field scans and plans in an operational railway twin, while Niantic offers capture and field-acquisition infrastructure. [NVIDIA/SNCF](https://www.nvidia.com/en-us/on-demand/session/gtc25-S71822/), [Niantic Capture](https://www.nianticspatial.com/products/capture). **I — Interesting residual:** An accepted cross-owner observation/correction network could change how infrastructure gets planned and maintained; another scene viewer does not supply that. Demand for the shared layer and its absence remain unverified.

**One-shot test — I:** Yes for a viewer and ingest prototype. The potential value is new physical observations, field access, accumulated temporal evidence, compute, and agreements about whose corrections count; a stronger model cannot observe an unvisited track or obtain consent by reasoning.

**Ambition rung — I:** **Field infrastructure**, with a **field-changing** destination if work can be planned against a fresh shared world record. Smallest first step in weeks: obtain permission for repeated captures of one accessible site from two independent observers, reconcile one real plan against observations, and have a responsible operator accept/reject a changed placement. The distinctive result to seek is a confirmed correction supported by a second observation plus explicit unknown/stale regions. Its novelty must be checked against existing twin workflows before scaling; no unmeasured accuracy or uniqueness claim.

### 2. P02 — A shared memory-safety boundary across LLVM ecosystems

**I — Candidate:** Extend the already working Fil-C approach into a common protected runtime/ABI and maintained dependency closure for more than one source-language ecosystem. **Q — Signal:** An audience member asks why it is not “Phil everything that targets LLVM IR?” and Pizlo replies “Send patches” (p3-23 46:00); he also reports 94 million lines of existing userland (31:00), incomplete optimization coverage (18:00), and mechanical but nontrivial pointer-representation changes (39:00–42:00). **F — Closest attempt:** Fil-C's GIMSO pass already treats incoming LLVM modules, with frontend and optimization adaptations around it. [GIMSO](https://fil-c.org/gimso), [compiler](https://fil-c.com/compiler). **I — Interesting residual:** The hard extension is common semantics, actual library behavior, and accepted reliability across a second language/runtime, rather than merely attaching an LLVM pass. This is most plausibly an upstream ecosystem project; general C/C++ safety itself is taken.

**One-shot test — I:** A better model may write the port. Non-code value must be new semantic/compatibility knowledge, independent runtime evidence, expensive workload coverage, and maintainer adoption. The invariants and accepted dependency closure must remain valid across toolchain changes; code generation alone does not establish that.

**Ambition rung — I:** **Field infrastructure**, potentially **field-changing** if unsafe interoperability ceases to be an unavoidable cross-language boundary. Smallest first step in weeks: coordinate with Fil-C maintainers, choose one small second-frontend module and a real C dependency, and demonstrate a useful mixed-language workload under one capability model with explicit unsupported cases and faults contained at the boundary. The distinctive target is a real workload crossing that shared boundary, not another standalone safe-language demo. First check whether an existing branch already does it. Do not promise binary compatibility with conventional unsafe libraries or formal proof of the trusted runtime.

### 3. P03 — Change authority that crosses organizational silos

**I — Candidate:** Build an adopted change institution in which a customer-journey owner can negotiate a time-bounded mandate spanning multiple code owners, reserve acceptance/review capacity, preserve each owner's constraints, and complete one end-user outcome. **Q — Signal:** Murphy's 100-repository/70-team Cantonese rollout took about two weeks once a PM and engineer could contribute everywhere and owners were directed to accept the change (p3-24 36:00–39:00); holidays and repository roadmaps are explicit blockers (35:00). **F — Closest attempt:** Sourcegraph supplies batch-change execution and approval ownership, and InnerSource supplies outside-contribution roles. [Sourcegraph](https://sourcegraph.com/docs/agentic-batch-changes/configuration), [InnerSource](https://innersourcecommons.org/learn/learning-path/trusted-committer/01/). **I — Interesting residual:** Authority and committed acceptance capacity are the scarce assets. Existing multi-repository automation substantially occupies the implementation. This candidate fails if ordinary leadership/InnerSource practice already solves the prospective customer's problem.

**One-shot test — I:** Yes for the coordination UI and automation. The remaining value would be authority, trust, negotiated commitments, organizational participation, and observed completion histories; a model cannot make another team prioritize or accept work on its own.

**Ambition rung — I:** **Field infrastructure** if many owners adopt transferable, auditable change mandates; initially an organizational experiment/service. Smallest first step in weeks: recruit three real code owners around one blocked customer-facing change, record their actual constraints and acceptance commitments, then finish it while separating generation time from permission/review wait. The distinctive evidence is the same change becoming possible through accepted cross-owner commitments. A fabricated multi-repo demo would not test the proposition. The source supports cross-team scope; cross-company federation is an additional I hypothesis.

### 4. P04 — A durable identity/history for real places and their occupants

**I — Candidate:** A shared authoritative continuity record that distinguishes establishment, operator, address, physical premises, and moving location, while preserving who asserted each change, what an agency accepted, and how old identities connect after moves or splits. **Q — Signal:** Johansen must check a national registry, join historical activities, handle restaurants on islands/airports/ships without ordinary addresses, and treat inspector statements differently from owner statements (p3-28 35:00–37:00); Leaman independently sees plans disagreeing with physical placement (p3-26 07:00–12:00). **F — Closest attempt:** Overture GERS provides stable IDs/history matching but documents identifier churn and the absence of published split/merge successor mappings. **One-shot test — I:** Yes for schema, matching, and UI. A model cannot invent authoritative historic occupancy, inspect a mobile venue, or decide which registry/inspector has jurisdiction; the value would be contributed observations, adjudication, trusted adoption, and a growing continuity network.

**Ambition rung — I:** **Field infrastructure** for public services, infrastructure operators, logistics, and location-dependent records. Smallest first step in weeks: with permission, assemble a small real case set containing a move, occupant change, addressless premise, and disputed correction in one jurisdiction; have a domain owner adjudicate links and preserve a contradictory observation rather than silently merging it. The distinctive result is accepted historical continuity that a current address/ID lookup loses. Stop expanding if an existing authoritative registry already delivers it; integrate there instead.
