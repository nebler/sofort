## Recurring themes across the share

All statements in this section are **I**, grounded in the cited **Q** rows above.

1. **Acceptance, not typing, limits useful throughput.** The Anthropic audience asks how to review the explosion of generated changes (p3-14 06:00–08:00); Uber and Faire independently call validation/review the cycle-time bottleneck (p3-18 11:00, 28:00–29:00); Omar has roughly 50 open PRs waiting on him (p3-19 0:40:00). This is strong demand evidence, but a generic AI reviewer is explicitly on the kill list and already in all these organizations.
2. **Good evaluation data is harder than an eval harness.** Anthropic reports meaningless 100% scores and advises high-quality historical cases (p3-14 03:00–04:00). Warp needs human-selected representative pools, subjective rubrics and costly repetitions (p3-16 24:00–30:00, 42:00–44:00). Faire wants missed human findings counted, and Uber calls those private labels valuable (p3-18 39:00–42:00). The lead is longitudinal, independently adjudicated observations and participation, not benchmark-running code.
3. **Self-improvement is implemented; knowing it improved is unresolved.** Anthropic's audience asks for alternative dreaming paths tested against evals and preservation of minority insights (p3-13 13:00–15:00). Warp ships instruction-changing PRs but primarily watches next week's task scores and acknowledges uncertain attribution (p3-17 0:51:00–0:57:00). Faire has not consistently evaluated divergent local skills; Warp has not established its with/without-spec effect (p3-18 17:00–18:00, 34:00). A prospective field experiment differs from another self-editing loop.
4. **Scaffolding expires with model improvements.** Context resets become dead weight (p3-11 03:00–04:00); skills may become thinner (p3-13 11:00–12:00); skill edits accrete constitutional addenda (p3-17 0:33:00–0:34:00); Uber's old worktree skill now misbehaves, and AGENTS.md needs deletion passes after model changes (p3-18 19:00, 38:00–39:00). Evidence for removing a rule may be as valuable as adding one.
5. **The scarce resources include compute, experimental budget and human attention.** A workshop lacks hard spending caps (p3-12 27:00); model trials multiply expensively (p3-16 42:00); self-improvement PR cadence must fit human review capacity (p3-17 0:47:00–0:48:00); Uber schedules maintenance off-peak and bounds repair diffs (p3-18 22:00–24:00); Hazel's instrumentation and planetary data exceed local memory (p3-19 0:19:00–0:20:00, 0:55:00–0:56:00). Better models do not create these finite resources from a prompt.
6. **Durable histories are useful only with provenance, time and scope.** Managed Agents separates immutable history from compacted context (p3-11 09:00–10:00) and layers user/org memories (p3-12 30:00–31:00); Warp cites failed runs in instruction PRs (p3-17 0:15:00); Faire/Warp distinguish dated design intent from current functionality (p3-18 35:00–39:00); Omar uses permanent versions to permit deliberate scientific/software upgrades (p3-19 1:04:00–1:05:00). A historical record should not silently become present authority.
7. **Partial work must remain useful while others continue.** Blocking custom tools interrupt steering, motivating coordinator patterns (p3-14 01:00, 16:00–18:00). Hazel preserves type/runtime services around incomplete code (p3-19 0:03:00–0:18:00) and proposes this as a requirement for a shared executing commons (0:47:00). Uber's company graph and skill reachability concern a different but related failure: context/capabilities can exist without being available to the agent that needs them (p3-17 0:49:00–0:50:00; p3-18 14:00–16:00).
8. **Coordination becomes expensive before writing code does.** Warp models a foreman, specialists and checkpoints (p3-16 04:00–10:00); Uber power users orchestrate hundreds of agents (p3-18 57:00–58:00); Omar describes duplicate work, low-hanging-fruit selection and recursive conflict repair, while qualifying the need for finer granularity (p3-19 0:41:00–0:44:00, 0:50:00–0:53:00). Generic agent hosting and git-at-agent-scale are killed ideas; real contention measurements and existing-community adoption are still research questions.
9. **Observation and implementation correspondence remain separate from elegant definitions.** A wrong Hazel test is immediately visible but still wrong (p3-19 0:25:00); Myers finds an alleged soundness counterexample in an implementation based on sound theory (p3-20 28:00–33:00); production traces/crashes guide maintenance at Uber and in Myers's reliability account (p3-18 23:00; p3-20 08:00, 40:00). A proof is only as relevant as its specification and relationship to the deployed system.
10. **Human intelligibility is not solved by another model's approval.** Warp scores reviewability and sometimes rejects a plausible instruction change (p3-17 0:04:00–0:08:00, 0:37:00); Faire wants legible evidence (p3-18 29:00); Omar builds change tours and live typed views (p3-19 0:26:00–0:28:00, 0:40:00–0:41:00); Myers wants toolchains that remain good for people as well as agents (p3-20 18:00–21:00). Taste must be observed with intended users.
11. **Authority and accountability survive automation.** Workspaces and application mappings enforce sharing decisions (p3-13 08:00, 18:00–20:00; p3-14 04:00–12:00). Uber/Faire retain a responsible human, while Warp assigns responsibility to the reviewer (p3-18 52:00–57:00). Omar says organizational release decisions still matter (p3-19 1:05:00); Myers asks whether software serves users, not only its employer's intent (p3-20 02:00–03:00). A model can implement a policy but cannot grant the authority or agreement behind it.
12. **Better tools can serve humans and agents together.** Omar extends live/incremental semantic feedback to agent loops (p3-19 0:29:00–0:39:00); Myers argues the engines beneath LSP and MCP are the same leverage point (p3-20 19:00–23:00). This is a research direction, not evidence that a new language-service wrapper is defensible.

## Five most interesting candidates, with the ambition filter applied

These are **I**, not novelty certifications. They deliberately move value away from generated code. They are **not** pitches for the killed generic reviewer, migration agent, verifier loop, agent host, git store or scheduler. Existing efforts are named because the likely first move is a contribution or partnership. The proposed first steps are falsifiable wedges for a few weeks, not promises of unprecedented results. A distinctive demo still needs Phase-2 prior-art and demand checking.

### 1. A continuously validated planetary computational commons

Omar's climate example is the strongest scale change in the share: connect ocean-temperature, fish/ecology and ice models into an executing knowledge environment, keep incomplete work local, and let scientists investigate current observations and counterfactuals (p3-19 0:47:00–0:49:00, 1:03:00–1:05:00). **I:** the opportunity is a jointly maintained scientific integration and acceptance network, not building another Hazel. Each participating model keeps explicit assumptions, versions, data origins and uncertainty; field experts authorize couplings and published snapshots. Hazel already owns the articulated vision and important foundations, so this is a potential contribution/application around that work. Scientific validity cannot be inferred from a neat typed graph, and this review has not established an open commercial market. **Smallest first step:** with an ocean-modeling and an ecology practitioner, couple two existing public computations for one small coastal question, ingest a newly arriving observation, show exactly which results change, preserve the old decision snapshot, and keep one deliberately incomplete branch from taking down the rest. The interesting demonstration is jointly accepted, observable scientific coupling with visible uncertainty and partial failure, rather than an AI-generated dashboard.

**One-shot test:** a stronger model could write the prototype; it cannot manufacture new environmental observations, scientific agreement about a coupling, or participating institutions' trust and authority. Without those, this collapses to a one-shot demo.  
**Ambition rung:** **field-changing** — potentially changes how several sciences maintain a shared executable account of the world; the first wedge is field infrastructure.

### 2. Experimental science for self-improving development organizations

Anthropic's audience asks which dreaming path actually improves outcomes (p3-13 13:00–15:00); Warp acknowledges weak trends, future-task confounding and uncertainty about instruction patches (p3-17 0:51:00–0:57:00); Faire has divergent, diffusely owned skills without consistent experiments (p3-18 17:00–18:00). **I:** build a participating network that runs prospective interventions on how agents work, publishes causal results where permitted, and maintains evidence about which rules still help across model generations. GEPA, Warp and FrontierMemory already cover optimization, replay and downstream memory evaluation; the proposed contribution is real longitudinal organizational experiments with independent endpoints. This could make “factory engineering” an empirical discipline rather than a sequence of score increases. **Smallest first step:** recruit two willing maintainers/teams, freeze one model and outcome definition, randomize one narrowly scoped skill revision across comparable new tasks for several weeks, and record accepted useful work, human intervention, cost and later discovered defects. Compare that conclusion with the ordinary before/after score dashboard. The distinctive output is a reproducible intervention history connecting changed rules to independently judged consequences, including a null or harmful result if that is what occurs.

**One-shot test:** the randomization and reporting code is one-shottable; permissioned longitudinal observations, actual team participation, independent outcome adjudication and accumulated evidence across model releases are not. If it only runs synthetic tasks with a self-selected judge, it loses the proposed advantage.  
**Ambition rung:** **field infrastructure** — an empirical foundation for deciding how increasingly autonomous organizations should operate.

### 3. A production-linked review and acceptance evidence commons

Faire asks to measure what reviewers missed, Uber calls human feedback valuable private data, and both report review as a bottleneck (p3-18 28:00–29:00, 39:00–43:00); Omar's own backlog reinforces the attention constraint (p3-19 0:40:00). **I:** organize independent maintainers around a continuously renewed corpus linking an actual change, intended behavior, executed evidence, review decisions, later defects and who accepted the risk. A human comment is a candidate label, not truth; attach a reproducer or other adjudication where possible. Qodo and other benchmark efforts already measure precision/recall, so novelty cannot be “a review benchmark.” The larger value would be trustworthy, permissioned field evidence that several vendors and users accept, including evidence about human comprehension and escaped failures. **Smallest first step:** have two unrelated maintainers independently adjudicate a small set of real past PRs and newly observed review misses, retain disagreement, and link each accepted defect to executed evidence and the resulting repair. Ask whether the common model-judge ranking changes when those independent labels and field consequences are used. That produces new observations a prompt cannot invent, without pretending a few PRs justify human-free merging.

**One-shot test:** collection software and score calculation are easy to generate; valid observations, reproducible field failures, independent judgment, rights to share them and confidence in the institution are not. The asset must accumulate and remain resistant to vendors redefining success.  
**Ambition rung:** **field infrastructure** — a common empirical basis for delegating software acceptance, with explicit limits rather than a claim of universal safety.

### 4. An API design laboratory run with real ecosystem consumers

The Warp presenter, drawing on Astro work, specifically says marketing-page benchmarks do not answer API-design questions (p3-16 16:00); Warp's qualitative judging and the panel's local skill disagreement show why an abstract “correctness” score is insufficient (p3-16 43:00–44:00; p3-18 17:00–18:00). **I:** build a long-lived collaboration of framework/library maintainers and consumers that can compare candidate API designs using authentic integration work, actual misunderstandings and future maintenance burden. Google's API studies and StopMotion show the measurement tradition already exists. The larger proposal is to make this evidence reusable across a whole ecosystem and across agent-generated design alternatives, not to generate API code or migrations. **Smallest first step:** with one library maintainer and a handful of independent consumers, test two plausible designs for one real upcoming change using the same integration tasks, record clarification requests and conceptual failures, and publish the maintainer's resulting decision with consumer consent. A useful new artifact is the observed decision trail and disagreement, not a leaderboard from invented tasks. Establish appetite for repeated participation before calling it infrastructure.

**One-shot test:** a better model can generate both API variants and the study UI; it cannot produce real consumers' behavior, long-term maintenance observations or an adopted community's taste from a prompt. Pure model-versus-model API scoring would fail this test.  
**Ambition rung:** **field infrastructure** if it becomes a shared mechanism for an ecosystem to choose interfaces; otherwise it is a useful but smaller research service.

### 5. A preventive-maintenance network bounded by maintainer attention

Uber's managed Sunday loops make the new bottlenecks unusually concrete: compute is saturated, and developers must not receive an unmanageable pile of repairs (p3-18 22:00–24:00). Myers's account suggests targeted defect work is often a question of priority rather than coding capability (p3-20 39:00–41:00), while human ownership remains explicit throughout the panel (p3-18 52:00–57:00). **I:** scale the idea beyond one company's factory into a permissioned network that allocates compute and repair effort across participating projects according to observed failure value and the maintainers' actual acceptance capacity. This is the most speculative extension: the transcript supports managed capacity, not demand for an external exchange. Renovate already schedules, groups and rate-limits updates, and Uber has an internal managed solution; a scheduler or repair bot would fail both filters. **Smallest first step:** recruit three consenting repositories, agree on one important maintenance class, a fixed compute allowance and a fixed human-review allowance, and deliver only the independently validated repairs that fit those limits. Record useful repairs accepted per unit of maintainer effort, displaced work, rejected proposals and any remaining backlog. The test is whether coordinated allocation creates value beyond each project's existing bot limits; stop treating it as an opportunity if maintainers prefer their existing controls.

**One-shot test:** a stronger model can build the scheduler and agents; it cannot grant merge authority, recruit maintainers, supply scarce compute, observe future operational failures or make attention unlimited. A network without actual permissions and accepted repairs is only a dashboard.  
**Ambition rung:** **field infrastructure** if it turns autonomous maintenance into a coordinated, sustainable capability across an ecosystem; otherwise it is a product already close to internal platforms and dependency bots.

## What this share does and does not establish

**I:** the clearest upper-rung practitioner vision is Omar's live scientific commons. It is already a published research direction, with a visible implementation base, so an honest next action is to find a scientific adoption/validation wedge with its builders. The strongest independently repeated near-term pain is acceptance and measurement after generation. Turning that pain into a durable project requires new observations, trusted participants and authority; adding an agent loop, score, skill PR, replay runner or configuration file does not pass the one-shot test on its own. The remaining candidates are provisional research programs, not verified vacant markets.
