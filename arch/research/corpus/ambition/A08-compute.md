# A08 — Compute, energy and networks as the binding constraint

Research: 2026-10-28–29. **Q** = supplied practitioner quotation or attributed claim; **F** = checked source; **I** = inference or proposal; **E** = estimate, scenario or experimental threshold. Caption times identify minute buckets. Vendor measurements remain attributed measurements, not reproductions.

**I — ONE pick: an open commons for local task models.** Accumulate permissioned, independently judged outcomes that turn recurring cloud-model tasks into small, portable local models. The field-scale ambition is that useful intelligence becomes a locally executable public asset, continuously improved by its users, rather than a fresh rental on every invocation. The initial wager is narrower: can multiple independent applications share enough outcome evidence to make reliable local specialization economical? A fine-tuning service or classifier SDK alone fails the ambition filter.

**I — Idle-device verdict: partial revival of the mechanism, no general economic reversal.** WebGPU and open weights make genuinely useful local inference much easier. They do not make stranger-owned devices dependable, private, cheap to coordinate, or equivalent to a tightly connected GPU cluster. The strongest surviving volunteer workload is public, asynchronous search with independently checkable results. Distributed conversational frontier inference across arbitrary phones remains a poor starting point.

## The numerical boundary

### What changed by 2026, and what did not

**F — Access improved:** Google's browser-platform account documents WebGPU across major browser families, with operating-system and device restrictions. Safari/iOS 26 and supported Android devices materially broaden access. This is not a claim that every phone exposes the same accelerator capabilities. [WebGPU availability](https://web.dev/blog/webgpu-supported-major-browsers).

**F — Execution is already available:** WebLLM supplies local WebGPU inference, structured output and worker integration. LLMlet implements llama.cpp/WebRTC browser sharding. Its documented configuration and the talk describe a 4 GB Wasm32 heap, serial layer distribution, experimental GPU support and relay limitations. The heap limit belongs to that configuration; it is not a universal 4 GB ceiling on all browser GPU memory. [WebLLM](https://github.com/mlc-ai/web-llm), [LLMlet](https://github.com/ktock/llmlet).

**F — Lifecycle remains conditional:** Chrome documents freezing eligible CPU-heavy background tabs under Energy Saver. Its exemptions include some active WebRTC use, so “every background inference tab is frozen” would overstate the source. An exemption is not an uptime contract, and deliberately circumventing owner power preferences is not a supply strategy. [Chrome lifecycle behavior](https://developer.chrome.com/blog/freezing-on-energy-saver).

**F — Abundance has competition:** Apple's 2026 foundation-model family includes a 3B dense on-device model and a larger sparse on-device variant. Its cloud fallback is an attested, controlled hardware fleet. Open browser inference cannot inherit those confidentiality guarantees. [Apple model announcement](https://machinelearning.apple.com/research/introducing-third-generation-of-apple-foundation-models), [PCC design](https://security.apple.com/documentation/private-cloud-compute/).

**F — Cloud is still inexpensive at the low end:** the checked Together price page lists Llama 3 8B Lite at **$0.14/M input and $0.14/M output tokens**, Qwen3.5 9B at **$0.17/$0.25**, and a much wider range for other models. These are retail rates, not provider production costs. Michelle's price-shock account does not justify pricing every local task against a premium frontier endpoint. [Together prices](https://www.together.ai/pricing).

### FLOPs, memory and WAN arithmetic

**E — Dense decoding approximation:** roughly `2 × active parameters` FLOPs per generated token, excluding attention/context overhead. An 8B dense model needs about **16 GFLOP/token**; 20 tokens/s is about **0.32 TFLOP/s** of this work. At batch one, reading approximately 4 GB of ideal 4-bit weights from 100 GB/s effective memory bandwidth already imposes an optimistic **25 tokens/s** ceiling. Actual quantization metadata, kernels, KV cache and memory pressure reduce it. Advertised phone NPU TOPS at a different precision are not interchangeable with sustained browser FLOPs.

**F/E — Datacenter comparison:** NVIDIA specifies **3.35 TB/s HBM bandwidth** and up to **700 W** for H100 SXM. Dividing that bandwidth by 4 GB gives an idealized 838 weight reads/s, before other limits; batching changes the comparison again. Consumer hardware being already purchased does not remove the datacenter's memory and batching advantage. [H100 specifications](https://www.nvidia.com/en-eu/data-center/h100/).

**E — WAN sharding:** with eight serial stages and seven handoffs of 20 ms each, communication alone costs 140 ms/token: at most **7.1 tokens/s** before computation. At 50 ms/handoff that ceiling falls to **2.9 tokens/s**. For hidden width 8,192 and FP16 activations, one 2,048-token prefill boundary transfers approximately **33.6 MB**. Seven boundaries move about **235 MB in aggregate**. A single 100 Mbit/s constrained boundary needs about 2.7 seconds merely to serialize its prefill payload. Streaming/pipelining changes end-to-end scheduling, not those bytes or propagation delay. A 70B 4-bit model's ideal **35 GB** of weights takes approximately **47 minutes** to download over 100 Mbit/s, before overhead.

**F/I — Homa's correct implication:** its Linux implementation and research target datacenter transport. Browser WebRTC does not become Homa, and changing congestion control cannot eliminate geographic propagation delay. Use WAN peers for independent requests or search branches; keep tightly coupled tensor parallelism inside a fast local fabric. [Homa implementation](https://github.com/PlatformLab/HomaModule). Exo's reported multi-device speedups use topology awareness and RDMA/Thunderbolt, illustrating the distinction rather than disproving it. [Exo](https://github.com/exo-explore/exo).

### Energy and the million-device illusion

**E — Electricity alone:** a laptop drawing 20 incremental W at 20 tokens/s uses **1 J/token**, or **0.278 kWh/M output tokens**. At an assumed $0.30/kWh this is **$0.083/M**. At 6 tokens/s it is **$0.278/M**, already above the $0.14/M example before paying for verification, network, hardware wear or coordination. A smaller phone model drawing 3 W at 20 tokens/s would use **0.15 J/token**, about **$0.0125/M**. That smaller model's task quality must be established separately; these are illustrative operating points, not device measurements. Measure incremental wall energy and report total wall energy too.

**E — Generous fleet scenario:** one million enrolled laptops, each supplying four hours/day at 20 tokens/s, produce **288 billion output tokens/day** before failures. They consume **80 MWh/day** at the assumed 20 W, costing **$24,000/day** at $0.30/kWh. At $0.14/M the cloud output equivalent is only **$40,320/day**. If electricity is reimbursed, just **$0.0041 per donated device-hour** remains for everything else. Even a $0.01/hour participation payment would exceed that margin. A 4 GB model rollout to the fleet moves **4 PB**; at an assumed $0.02/GB distribution charge that is another **$80,000 per rollout**. Cached weights help; frequent updates, churn and replication hurt. Input billing, quality and cloud batching must be matched in an actual comparison.

**I — Decision:** these numbers kill “free universal inference because billions of devices exist.” They do not kill owner-local private inference, donated public science, extremely reusable cached small models, or expensive search whose useful output is cheap to check. A network's enrollment count and nominal FLOPs are not delivered capacity.

## Idea 1 — A commons that refines rented intelligence into local capability

### 1. Seed

**Q:** Sunil's phone wish and Ree's “tiny” classifier trained on actual task data, v11 16:00–17:00. Eric Simons independently connects local electricity-cost execution, open weights, compute procurement and user-contributed training data, p3-64 3:50–3:52. **I:** the natural scarce asset is not another runtime; it is a shared body of real task outcomes and permission to learn from them.

### 2. Vision at full scale

**I:** millions of applications publish and reuse locally executable learned capabilities: intent selection, bounded extraction, ranking and other recurring decisions. Each capability comes with weights, supported input scope, actual cross-device costs, independently judged outcomes and an owner responsible for updates. Common bases and reusable cases amortize specialization across many adopters. Private raw inputs can remain on their owner's device; only explicitly approved cases or aggregate observations enter the commons. Difficult novel work goes to a large teacher; successful recurring work becomes durable local capability.

**I — Ambition rung:** infrastructure for a field, potentially changing what abundant generated applications can afford to do offline. The 100–1000× ambition is removing repeated cloud reasoning from a large portion of software's decision surface. It is not a promise that a tiny model becomes a generally interchangeable frontier agent. Finished capabilities and quiet maintenance matter more than inviting every user to continually retrain.

### 3. What was blocked, and why now

**I:** conventional classifiers and model distillation were possible long ago and fail this test by themselves. The new research hypothesis is broad, reusable specialization across continually generated applications: teachers can now propose training cases and candidate solutions for unfamiliar task semantics, small open models can execute on broad consumer hardware, and production outcomes can supply correction. Previously the per-task expert-data and deployment burden prevented that scope. The unresolved enabling result is cross-owner transfer of useful cases with little expert work. If every application still requires bespoke ML consulting, the field-changing claim fails.

**F:** Inference.net already demonstrates commercial specialization at meaningful scale; Apple, Knowledgator and browser runtimes supply major pieces. **I:** their existence strengthens feasibility while sharply reducing novelty. The proposed residual is an open, multi-owner, portable outcome-and-model commons whose benefits cross application/vendor boundaries. No search establishes that residual as empty territory.

### 4. One-shot test

**I — Non-code value:** permissioned production failures, owner-approved labels, real later outcomes, adoption and a continuing community willing to maintain them. A stronger model can write the compiler, exporters and registry and become a better teacher. It cannot synthesize future real-user errors, privately held application semantics, rights to share data, or independent acceptance. If the project consists only of an automated teacher/student training script, abandon it as one-shottable.

### 5. Physics, economics, data and law

**E:** a 33M-parameter INT8 model has approximately **33 MB of raw weights**, before runtime/tokenizer overhead. A rough `2PL` calculation for a 128-token encoder pass gives **8.4 GFLOP**; attention adds work. At an assumed effective 0.1 TFLOP/s and 2 W, that is about **84 ms and 0.17 J**. This is a measurement target, not an extrapolation from a datacenter GPU model card. Native acceleration may outperform WebGPU; browser execution must justify itself through reach and deployment cost.

**E — Cloud baseline:** a Qwen3.5 9B invocation with 500 input and 100 output tokens costs approximately **$0.00011**, or **$110 per million calls**, at the checked Together rates. A premium frontier call may cost more, but the experiment must also compare the cheapest cloud model that meets acceptance, deterministic code/rules, embeddings and an existing generic classifier. A small model's 100× arithmetic reduction is not automatically 100× total-system saving.

**E — Decisive equation:** let `C_T` be the measured cost per accepted cloud-baseline task; `C_L` local execution, device cost and distribution cost per task; `q` the fraction escalated; `a` the fraction additionally cloud-audited; `F` the entire specialization/refresh cost, including steward labor, over `N` uses; and `C_H` any additional per-task human intervention. Use the same cost boundary and accepted-task denominator for both paths. A simplified matched-quality ratio is:

`R = C_L/C_T + q + a + F/(N × C_T) + C_H/C_T`.

**E:** this assumes escalations/audits cost one baseline call each; actual retries or larger teachers increase their terms. Record owner minutes as well as cash and price them explicitly. **100× requires `R ≤ 0.01`; 1000× requires `R ≤ 0.001`.** A 5% fallback rate alone caps the saving at 20× before other costs. With an illustrative $50 fixed cost, `C_T=$0.00011`, and only one million uses, amortization alone is **45.5%**; at 100 million uses it is **0.455%**. Sharing demand and reusable models is central to the ambition, not a registry embellishment. A scenario with 0.2% local cost, 0.3% escalation, 0.04% audit and 0.455% amortization barely reaches 100× if additional per-task human intervention is effectively zero. None of those rates has been demonstrated here; $50 is not an estimate of a six-week project's full labor cost.

**I — Data and law:** obtain task-owner approval for data, labels and redistribution; inspect each base-model license and teacher's training/output terms. Local raw data does not make shared gradients, cases or weights automatically private. Processing personal data still requires a lawful, transparent, purpose-limited and minimized process. Use explicitly permissioned non-sensitive tasks in the pilot. [European Commission GDPR principles](https://commission.europa.eu/law/law-topic/data-protection/information-business-and-organisations/principles-gdpr_en). Do not assume that being able to download a scientific paper permits republishing its text or training artifacts; EU text/data-mining exceptions have conditions, including rights reservation. [Directive 2019/790, Article 4](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/eudr/2019/790/article/4?view=plain).

### 6. Closest attempts and the difficult residual

**F/I:** Inference.net's task-trained models and OSSAS are exceptionally close, already combining production data, specialization and distributed compute. Forge announces a user-data/open-model/compute flywheel. GLiClass and Apple's local models make the small-model mechanism ordinary. Generic agent JIT and skill compilation are also explicitly killed in the local research. The residual must therefore be demonstrated as **benefit from a common body of real outcomes across independent owners, portable to their devices**, with a lower continuing human cost than each owner's separate specialization effort. A public model zoo or exporter does not qualify. None of these competitors is established as stalled.

### 7. Six weeks for one person

**E — Scope: 60–90 hours, $100–300 experimental spend, borrowed devices.** Weeks 1–2: secure two cooperating OSS application owners and three recurring, non-sensitive decision tasks; collect genuinely occurring queries and owner-held acceptance labels. Pick bounded intent/tool selection and one structured extraction task, not autonomous end-to-end agents. Preserve time-separated held-out examples before teacher synthesis. If no owners provide real cases, stop.

**E:** weeks 3–4: reuse an existing small base, ordinary fine-tuning and ONNX/browser/native runtimes. Export merged models; do not create a new compiler or federated-learning protocol. Compare separately trained models with models benefiting from the other owner's approved difficult cases. Include rules/embeddings and an off-the-shelf classifier. Record abstention, hidden errors, latency, cold download, energy and owner minutes.

**E:** weeks 5–6: run on one actual phone and two laptops; introduce a real owner-approved task/schema change and a later time slice. Publish weights where licensed, the permitted cases, sealed-test results and measured refresh burden. The sought new artifact is a cross-owner, cross-device production transfer result: cases from one deployment improve another's locally accepted outcomes without increasing its private-data exposure or maintenance burden. It can be a negative result. A polished local chatbot would not answer the question.

### 8. Strongest objection and cheapest kill

**I:** specialized tasks may not share enough structure; rare mistakes and drift force enough escalation or review to erase the advantage. A confidence score can be confidently wrong under changed inputs. Conformal or calibrated abstention does not remove its distributional assumptions.

**E — Two-day screen:** obtain 300 real held-out cases from one owner; compare a cheap cloud baseline, rules/embeddings and the small model before building a registry. If local success/coverage is poor or there is no user value beyond cents saved, stop. The six-week research survives with a real 10× result and demonstrated cross-owner benefit; retain the 100× full-scale claim only if observed coverage, refresh costs and realistic demand make `R ≤ 1%` plausible. Zero failures in 300 independent cases only bounds an error rate near 1% at 95% confidence; roughly 3,000 are needed to bound it near 0.1%, and correlated/drifting production data weakens both statements. Do not certify rare-error performance from a tiny demo.

### 9. Builder fit

**I:** good systems/platform fit for portable execution, resource accounting, dataset boundaries and an open publication workflow. ML evaluation, participant acquisition and stewardship are the stretch; keep training conventional. The software is deliberately replaceable by better models. The continuing outcome network is the project. It does not require GrowOS, migrating agent heaps or building an agent-hosting business.

## Idea 2 — A planetary volunteer search engine whose useful results carry proofs

### 1. Seed

**Q:** Kohei's real browser inference demo, Eric's abundance ambition, and de Moura's code-plus-proof vision. **I:** take the volunteer exception seriously: use untrusted devices to propose answers for public mathematical or program-verification obligations, then accept only results independently checked against the pinned obligation. Do not ask a peer to prove that it spent FLOPs; ask it to return useful checkable work.

### 2. Vision at full scale

**I:** donated browsers, laptops and phones maintain an open queue of subgoals for formal mathematics and public software. Many independent local small-model searches operate without WAN synchronization on every token. Accepted lemmas become reusable public infrastructure; negative search observations inform future allocation without being mistaken for proofs of impossibility. A much smaller trusted checking layer validates successful proposals. A planetary network could expand formal reasoning available to public-interest projects without a central inference purchaser.

**I — Ambition rung:** changes what formalization can attempt if it brings a large new population of useful search resources and produces accepted new results. A browser worker backend for an existing proof queue is the six-week wedge, not the full ambition.

### 3. Why now, with the historical objection included

**F:** BOINC already made volunteer search possible. DeepSeek-Prover-V2 publicly describes a 7B model handling subgoal search under a larger-model decomposition pipeline. Its model family is released with weights. [DeepSeek prover model card](https://huggingface.co/deepseek-ai/DeepSeek-Prover-V2-7B).

**I:** the formerly blocked property is useful learned mathematical proposal generation on widely available owner hardware, at a rate that outgrows central checking cost. The whole construction was not impossible before: if small local provers contribute only toy results, this collapses to old volunteer computing and fails the ambition filter. Better local models and reusable formal libraries create a testable opening, not proof that it is already viable.

### 4. One-shot test

**I — Non-code value:** accumulated accepted proofs, expert-approved problem statements, donated hardware/time and a community that values the outputs. A future model can implement all clients and scheduling and improve proposal quality. It cannot retroactively donate devices or make an unsolved theorem's proof exist without doing the mathematical work. A better model might also make centralized solving so cheap that the network loses its purpose; that is a real failure mode.

### 5. Physics, economics, data and law

**E:** 7B at ideal 4-bit storage needs **3.5 GB of raw weights**, with context/runtime overhead beyond that. It fits selected laptops much more naturally than arbitrary phones or LLMlet's constrained heap. Begin on laptops; phones enter only when a measured smaller model is useful. Give each peer an entire independent search branch, cached model/library assets and a pinned statement. No per-token inter-device round trips.

**E:** a batch of 256 candidates averaging 512 generated tokens is **131,072 output tokens**. At $0.14/M that is only **$0.0184 of output billing** in the inexpensive 8B example, before repeated input billing and quality differences. Donation acquisition can easily cost more. The reason to proceed would be new useful formal results and participant interest, not an extravagant dollar value assigned to token counts. A laptop at 20 W and 10 tokens/s spends about **262 kJ / 72.8 Wh** on that batch, or approximately **$0.022 of electricity** at the assumed tariff, before checks. A cheap API may already win cash and energy economics.

**I/E — Useful throughput is the key:** measure distinct accepted obligations per incremental kWh and per dollar of complete cloud-equivalent work, including the unsuccessful branches, library downloads, checker CPU and retries. A useful experimental gate is at least **20× search-to-checking compute asymmetry** and some genuinely new accepted progress. That asymmetry alone is insufficient if a small central batch server produces many more accepted proofs per joule.

**I:** pin the proposition, imports, toolchain and axiom policy; verify centrally in a sandbox and reject unauthorized axioms or replaced definitions. A valid proof of the wrong statement remains the wrong result. Cap elaboration resources; a malicious candidate must not become a checker denial of service. Public, licensed theorem statements and libraries avoid sending customer secrets to peers; participants explicitly opt into bounded foreground or native execution. No cryptocurrency is required to test this.

### 6. Closest attempts

**F:** [Proof Commons](https://proofcommons.org/) already offers human-reviewed statements, agents, Lean checking and public failed attempts. [Open Proof Network](https://openproofnetwork.org/docs/architecture-decisions.html) describes a crowdsourced lemma graph and automated proof acceptance. [DeMath](https://demath.org/) and [Conjectures.io](https://conjectures.io/how-it-works) add proof/bounty workflows. These occupy the mathematical commons and reward concept; website claims alone do not establish their throughput or maturity.

**I:** contribute volunteer-device supply to an existing effort rather than invent another proof institution. The potentially new result is an economically and energetically useful heterogeneous inference supply layer, measured against a central prover on the same obligations. Public checking does not make poor small-model proposals valuable. No evidence establishes that the existing proof efforts stalled for lack of browser workers.

### 7. Six-week artifact

**E:** weeks 1–2: obtain a cooperating formal-library maintainer and 20–50 actual unresolved obligations at a pinned revision; establish the cheapest useful central baseline and reject obligations already settled at that revision. Weeks 3–4: add an opt-in WebLLM/native worker to an existing queue, with per-branch budgets, local caching, leases and cancellation. Keep Lean checking on ordinary trusted server hardware initially. Weeks 5–6: use 5–10 separately owned laptops, impose withdrawals and network loss, and publish accepted proofs, failed attempts, full energy/download accounting and the central comparison. The sought demo is an accepted maintainer-useful lemma produced by real donated inference despite device withdrawal, with measured net contribution. Claims of unprecedented mathematics require the maintainer's agreement.

### 8. Kill and cheapest experiment

**E:** before networking, run the chosen quantized small prover on 20 maintainer-supplied obligations for one capped evening. Kill the browser-supply project if it contributes no useful branch progress, if the central cheapest model dominates accepted output/kWh, or if no existing proof community wants the results. Do not hide behind total tokens generated. The strongest objection is that expert problem decomposition and human statement review, already scarce in S07, bind long before donated inference does.

### 9. Builder fit

**I:** excellent Rust/Wasm/distributed-systems fit; mathematics, Lean packaging and model usefulness require an external maintainer. Scope can remain one part-time person only by reusing a proof commons and central checker. This is the **most crazy-but-not-impossible** option: a public mathematical search utility funded in donated attention and electricity, with no assumption that unrelated devices can impersonate a datacenter fabric.

## Idea 3 — A compute exchange that settles on delivered work

### 1. Seed

**Q:** Kennon's successful-task metric and 50% operator-dependent output difference, p3-10 01:00–04:00; Michelle's scarce capacity and residency problem, v12 27:00/45:00. E12 explicitly proposes a participant market for useful compute. **I:** take the institutional version seriously, beyond a benchmark dashboard.

### 2. Vision at full scale

**I:** buyers procure independently measurable batches of work with defined model/precision or task-quality scope, deadlines, availability and remedies for failed delivery. Operators using different silicon can compete on equivalent service obligations. A nonprofit/open standards body accumulates delivered-job histories, agreed grades and dispute precedents; providers can implement the software independently. Eventually capacity commitments become financeable because the underlying delivery obligation is intelligible. Price indexes, ratings, model-execution evidence and buyer acceptance remain distinct facts.

**I — Ambition rung:** infrastructure for a field, comparable in institutional difficulty to creating accepted grades and settlement rules for a new commodity. A machine-readable contract without counterparties is not the project.

### 3. Why now

**I:** GPU rental contracts were always possible. The newer possibility is contracts over a changing multi-provider inference supply whose execution can be cheaply checked and whose application outcomes are automatically measurable in bounded domains. Open weights, reproducible execution and better validators reduce one barrier. They do not make arbitrary creative or scientific work fungible. If each buyer needs a unique human panel and bespoke negotiation, there is no common market grade and the ambition fails.

### 4. One-shot test

**I — Non-code value:** actual buyers/operators, shared grade definitions, transaction observations, reputations, authority to settle disputes and committed replacement capacity. A stronger model can write matching, invoicing and verification software. It cannot manufacture liquidity, counterparties' assent or capital that absorbs non-delivery. Open source can support these institutions without owning every transaction.

### 5. Physics, economics, data and law

**E — Operator denominator:** $1/GPU-hour at 1,000 accepted tokens/s is **$0.278/M**; 1,500/s is **$0.185/M**, a 33% unit-cost reduction from 50% more output. These are scenario input rates, not observed hardware results, and exclude other costs. At equal invoice rates, utilization and failed delivery matter materially.

**E — Viability gate:** all-in execution verification, buyer-quality checks, disputes and replacement reserve must cost less than approximately **5% of delivered-job value** for an illustrative 10% gross spread to leave useful room. A $100 batch allows a $5 checking/dispute budget; a $0.00011 individual task allows only $0.0000055. Batch settlement is necessary. A human spending ten minutes at an assumed $60/hour costs $10, exhausting the $100 batch's margin. Quality checks must therefore be objective and disputes exceptional, not merely assisted by a chatbot.

**F/I:** TOPLOC, LOGIC and Gensyn address parts of execution verification. They do not establish accuracy against a buyer's objective. Start with a frozen open model and input/output obligations, or a narrow task with an independently owned checker. A checked sample creates a bounded statistical claim, not evidence for every job; precision changes may preserve acceptance or violate the agreed grade. Privacy and data-residency restrictions can fragment supply. Modern silicon portability is useful only after reproducing the delivery grade.

**I — Law:** the six-week experiment is bilateral purchase and measurement of real services, with an agreed refund procedure. Transferable speculative contracts, margin, pooled risk and derivatives are a separate regulated question; do not launch them under the assumption that compute is exempt. The CFTC describes registration requirements for certain derivatives intermediaries; that is not a legal classification of this proposed contract. [CFTC registration scope](https://www.cftc.gov/check). Procurement, tax, privacy and the parties' jurisdictions still require their own review before actual operation.

### 6. Closest attempts

**F/I:** Akash, io.net, Together and Prime Intellect already sell or coordinate compute/inference. Prime's capital and verification work make it a direct competitor. [SemiAnalysis ClusterMAX](https://clustermax.semianalysis.com/) already rates operating capability; [Computable](https://www.getcomputable.com/) publishes a reproducible GPU-hour price index. Neither should be recreated. The residual hypothesis is an accepted cross-provider delivery grade with observed settlement after a failure or serving-stack change. This is an institutional opening only if real buyers and operators adopt it; search did not establish that no existing supplier already provides adequate contractual remedies.

### 7. Six weeks

**E:** weeks 1–2: find one actual buyer and two independent operators willing to use the same bounded batch specification and publish redacted results. No matching engine. Agree model identity, precision, context, deadline, successful-delivery denominator and responsibility for reruns. Weeks 3–4: execute purchased matched batches through existing APIs and existing verification, with $100–300 total experimental spend. Weeks 5–6: exercise one agreed non-delivery condition and one version/precision change; publish the parties' accepted bill, remedy and operational evidence. The new object sought is a real common obligation that survives a contested delivery, not an exchange screenshot. No real dispute needs to be manufactured: an agreed, disclosed fault drill suffices.

### 8. Kill

**E:** spend two days asking prospective participants whether one common grade would change a real procurement decision. If two providers will not accept it, an existing contract already suffices, checking/settlement exceeds 5%, or agreement requires ongoing consulting per task, stop. The strongest objection is that varying task quality prevents fungibility while established providers already sell reliability. More code cannot repair that.

### 9. Builder fit

**I:** strong platform and accounting fit, weak solo fit for commercial procurement, disputes, capital and legal operations. A six-week open contract/transaction experiment is feasible; operating a general exchange part-time is not. It ranks below the task commons because permission and counterparties dominate the initial work and direct funded competition is unusually close.

## Idea 4 — Agent demand as an aggregated, dispatchable grid resource

### 1. Seed

**Q:** Michelle's scarcity and compute-currency framing, Kennon's useful-work denominator and Lattner's performance-per-silicon framing. **I:** energy-aware scheduling is an extrapolation from these signals; the supplied practitioners do not explicitly ask for an electricity-market product. Keep that evidential weakness visible.

### 2. Vision at full scale

**I:** independent inference operators collectively promise a grid operator a verifiable amount of load reduction or absorption while still completing deadline-constrained agent work. Work with hours of slack follows available energy; urgent interactive work stays on its contracted service path. Model caches, job queues and checkpoint costs enter the dispatch decision. The commons is an interoperable pool of trusted flexibility and delivered-work evidence across owners, eventually making new compute capacity usable under constrained grid connections.

**I — Ambition rung:** infrastructure shared by the compute and electricity fields. The full-scale value is additional grid-compatible compute capacity and reliable flexibility, not a lower-carbon badge on an unchanged workload.

### 3. Why now

**I:** deferrable batch scheduling and demand response existed long ago. The hypothesized new supply is a very large volume of machine-generated agent/research jobs with explicit output deadlines, predictable checkpoints and less need for a person to wait. Better agents could expose usable slack rather than only generating additional peak demand. The open problem is whether that slack is real and accessible across independent owners. If these are merely ordinary nightly batch jobs, existing schedulers already handle them and the new ambition disappears.

### 4. One-shot test

**I — Non-code value:** measured electrical response, agreements with operators/utilities, approved baselines, workload owners' permission to delay, and repeated evidence of delivery through grid events. A future model can implement dispatch policy but cannot create a grid connection, meter record, utility agreement or spare deadline capacity from a prompt.

### 5. Physics, economics, data and law

**E:** 100 GPUs at 700 W represent **70 kW GPU power**, before hosts/cooling. A real 20% reduction for one hour saves/shifts **14 kWh**; at an assumed $0.10/kWh that is only **$1.40** of energy. If 20% reduced power also loses 20 GPU-hours at an assumed $2/hour rental value, the opportunity cost is **$40**. Power and throughput are not linear, so measure the actual curves. Simple electricity arbitrage is weak unless power limiting preserves most throughput or jobs have otherwise unused slack. Fixed rental commitments can keep billing while the machines wait.

**I/E:** the large value can instead be access to constrained capacity or contracted grid services, as the current industrial efforts demonstrate. Do not insert a speculative capacity payment into an otherwise losing equation. A 100 kW portfolio reducing 20% offers only 20 kW; market minimums, baseline methodology and aggregator rules are jurisdiction-specific. A software trace or GPU utilization counter is not a certified meter. Carbon-intensity forecasts, marginal emissions, cash price and grid constraints are different signals; optimizing one need not improve the others.

**I — Data/law:** obtain real job deadlines, restart costs, telemetry and the operator's tariff/contract. Price/carbon datasets may have reuse restrictions. Utility dispatch and settlement require the relevant operator's approved metering, baseline and agreement; the pilot cannot claim market qualification from a few GPUs. Private agent data should remain within its authorized region and operator. No migrating personal work to the cheapest unknown peer.

### 6. Closest attempts

**F:** Google already has the 1 GW contracted program described above. Emerald AI has commercial deployments and large 2026 funding, not merely a proposal. NVIDIA's DSX collaboration explicitly targets flexible AI factories. [NVIDIA/Emerald announcement](https://nvidianews.nvidia.com/news/nvidia-and-emerald-ai-join-leading-energy-companies-to-pioneer-flexible-ai-factories-as-grid-assets). The [Green Software Foundation Carbon Aware SDK](https://carbon-aware-sdk.greensoftware.foundation/) already supplies carbon-aware timing/location primitives.

**I:** the residual is multi-owner aggregation with credible delivered-work commitments for operators too small to build bespoke utility relationships. Evidence here does not establish that those operators need a new intermediary or can qualify economically. Existing aggregators and Emerald can extend downward. A generic energy-aware agent scheduler is killed by this prior art.

### 7. Six-week artifact

**E:** weeks 1–2: obtain one consenting small GPU operator with a real deferrable workload and electrical measurements; identify an aggregator's actual minimum qualification requirements. Weeks 3–4: use existing job scheduling and carbon/price feeds; model measured checkpoint/cache behavior rather than moving token streams between regions. Weeks 5–6: conduct a disclosed power-limiting or deferment drill, observe rebound and missed deadlines, and publish wall-power reduction versus accepted output. With borrowed access the software/pilot can stay under $300; renting 100 GPUs for the experiment is outside scope. The sought result is an actual work-preserving load response unavailable from simply delaying all jobs, plus an external assessment of whether aggregation would qualify. No utility payment or partnership is assumed.

### 8. Kill

**E:** a two-day workload-slack audit comes before control software. Stop if there is no operator access, less than 10% controllable load at the required deadlines, uncompensated lost output exceeds plausible value, or an aggregator says the potential portfolio cannot qualify. Even a promising energy result does not establish a business. The strongest objection is that expensive GPUs should remain productive, while energy is a small share of their total cost and existing industrial entrants already solve the valuable grid-access problem.

### 9. Builder fit

**I:** systems work is suitable; utility relationships, metering and deployment access are a poor fit for one part-time builder. Contribute measured workload-flexibility evidence to an existing effort if access exists. Do not begin by writing another scheduler. This has enormous field ambition but the weakest accessible wedge of the four.

## Ranking and decision

| Rank, I | Idea | Non-code asset that could compound | Present verdict |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Commons for local task models | Cross-owner outcomes, rights, useful local models and a maintenance community | **Conditional pick.** Must prove cross-owner benefit and a plausible sub-1% total cloud-dependence budget for the 100× claim. Generic distillation is occupied. |
| 2 | Proof-carrying volunteer search | Accepted formal results, expert-reviewed obligations and contributed hardware | **Most crazy-but-not-impossible.** Only revive idle-device compute where useful output is objectively checkable and small-model search contributes. Work with an existing commons. |
| 3 | Delivered-work compute exchange | Agreed grades, transactions, remedies, reputation and capacity commitments | Institutionally ambitious; strong seed, substantial funded competition, difficult solo acquisition. |
| 4 | Grid-flex agent compute pool | Verified electrical flexibility and operator/utility agreements | Physically feasible and industrially validated in adjacent deployments; access and existing competitors make a solo opening uncertain. |

**I — ONE pick and six-week commitment:** run Idea 1 as a cross-owner local-specialization experiment, not a platform launch. End with two cooperating application owners, three bounded real tasks, permitted reusable cases and weights, a phone/two-laptop comparison, a time-separated test, and one measured update. Seek a result showing that evidence from one owner improves another's locally accepted outcomes enough to reduce continuing cloud and expert work. If the common evidence adds nothing, fold useful artifacts upstream and stop the standalone ambition.

**E — The number that makes or breaks it:** **`R ≤ 1%`** for a credible 100× all-in saving at equal accepted-task quality. That is the combined budget for local cost, escalation, audits, amortized specialization/refresh and added human intervention, not permission for a 1% hidden error rate. A 5% escalation fraction already rules out 100×. At the cheap-cloud baseline, demand reuse and human maintenance are as consequential as FLOPs.

**I — What remains dead:** universal free browser inference, WAN tensor parallelism justified by aggregate FLOPs, private agent execution on stranger-owned plaintext hosts, another classifier service, generic distillation/JIT, a benchmark masquerading as an exchange, and a carbon-aware cron service. Model retirement is a real capacity problem, but cold loading/deprecation and provider scheduling are existing mechanisms; it does not by itself establish a new field-scale commons.

## Five-line TL;DR

1. **I:** WebGPU and open weights revive useful local execution; they do not rescue a universal idle-device compute market.
2. **E:** A generous million-laptop scenario leaves about $0.004/device-hour after electricity at a cheap 8B API comparison, before coordination or verification.
3. **I:** Pick shared real task outcomes that turn recurring cloud calls into maintained local capabilities; the code and fine-tuning mechanism are already available.
4. **E:** In six weeks, test cross-owner benefit on three actual tasks and three devices; 100× needs all-in cost below 1% of the accepted cloud baseline.
5. **I:** The wild bet is public proof-carrying volunteer search; cheap checking can remove the trust bottleneck, but only useful accepted proofs justify the electricity.
