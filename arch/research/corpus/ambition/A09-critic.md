## How the two tests are applied

**One-shot test.** “Yes” means a working bounded implementation is plausibly prompt-sized for a model one or two generations beyond the stipulated baseline. It does not mean a production institution, complete ecosystem migration, or physical installation is prompt-sized. Missing credentials and a customer's configuration are usually inputs to an implementation, not a durable asset. A reusable body of independently obtained observations, accepted obligations, or continuing permission is different. A model may also access new observations through tools; the claim is that obtaining and governing them remains necessary, not that AI can never participate.

**Ambition test.** A larger customer count does not move a product up the ladder. “Commons” does not move a database up the ladder. Field infrastructure must enable independent participants to rely on a shared asset; field-changing work must enable something qualitatively unavailable before. The credible new affordance is often the ability to build many alternative implementations or maintain a whole compatibility closure, with the scarce acceptance process outside the generator's control. Another consultancy, dashboard, queue, or registry does not acquire that affordance by being described at planetary scale.

**Release risk, E.** **H** = high risk that the builder finds the implementation 2–5× less exciting after the next model release; **M** = implementation erodes but real observations/relationships remain; **L** = the named external asset should remain valuable. These are directional judgments, not numerical valuations. A useful upstream patch can be H without becoming useless.

## The four directions worth developing, ranked

The estimates below assume **8–12 builder hours per week for six weeks**, existing development hardware, and no unsolicited access to organizations or people. Recruitment and domain review consume that allowance. Suggested participation, compensation and compute are future experiments, not actions taken during this audit. “New” means an unreported observation or accepted capability to seek and check against prior art, not a guaranteed discovery.

### 1. B20 — An executable, federated account of web compatibility — the ONE pick

**Seed, Q.** Giorgio describes testing “more than 12 frameworks” and private company repositories ([ViteConf, 4:26–4:27](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=PfyIEgLzNYI&t=15960)). Angular's speaker explains that “even those bugs have users” ([9:14](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=PfyIEgLzNYI&t=33240)). These are independent maintainers in the same event, not independent publishing channels. The real pain is dependence on behavior that no clean interface specification captures.

**Full scale, I.** Independent runtime, framework, library and application owners maintain a living map of what applications actually require. Candidate foundational replacements run against participant-owned cases where those cases live. The shared record distinguishes observed behavior, an owner-accepted obligation, a permitted incompatibility and unknown coverage. This makes wholesale implementation replacement a routine ecosystem operation instead of repeatedly rediscovering private compatibility edges. It is not a universal proof of correctness.

**Why now, I.** Cheap implementation produces many credible replacement engines and many more changes. It also lowers the cost of adapting downstream cases and minimizing failures. Previously the engineering effort needed to maintain this breadth was a binding cost; now externally owned acceptance becomes the scarce part. The 100× ambition is the breadth of replaceable infrastructure, not a claim that one CI run is 100× faster.

**One-shot value, I.** The runner is prompt-sized. The asset is the set of accepted obligations, real downstream observations and participants willing to keep checking them. Private source can remain local; participants can disclose a narrowly scoped result or authorized reduced repro. A signature establishes who supplied a result, not that the assertion is true. Conflicting observations and untested cases must remain visible. Better models make new replacements easier and therefore increase the need for this asset.

**Closest attempts, F/I.** [Vite ecosystem CI](https://github.com/vitejs/vite-ecosystem-ci) already runs downstream integration suites against selected versions. [Rolldown's tests](https://rolldown.rs/development-guide/testing) include output execution and Rollup-related tests. [Web Platform Tests](https://web-platform-tests.org/) already supplies cross-implementation tests and recurring browser results. None should be redescribed as missing infrastructure. The proposed extension is participant-owned behavioral obligations spanning toolchains, private downstream applications and replacement implementations. It might belong inside these projects. The incumbent is funded and moving: [Cloudflare's June 2026 announcement](https://blog.cloudflare.com/voidzero-joins-cloudflare/) commits engineering resources and a $1 million ecosystem fund; its [September update](https://blog.cloudflare.com/voidzero-update/) reports continuing releases and large-application feedback. They have not stalled. A separate runner would compete with their strongest position; a neutral evidence contribution might help them.

**Economics and physics, E.** A pilot matrix of 20 cases × 2 engines × 3 versions × 2 host environments × 3 repeats is 720 runs. At three CPU-minutes each, that is 36 core-hours before cold builds. At an assumed eight-core, 100 W machine, approximately 0.45 kWh of wall time-equivalent compute energy; cold builds and isolation overhead can multiply that. A €250 compute ceiling is ample for discovering whether the pilot is operationally reasonable, but is a budget, not a price quote. Expensive full-app suites should execute on participants' existing CI. Data availability and publication permission dominate early risk. Source, reduced reproductions and observations need separately understood sharing rights.

**Six-week start, I/E.** Spend roughly 12 hours obtaining commitments from two independent maintainers and one downstream owner; 20 hours adapting their existing tests to one replacement boundary; 12 hours observing/minimizing discrepancies; and 10–20 hours getting obligations adjudicated and publishing the permitted record. Choose one seam, such as module federation across Rollup/Rolldown. The demo target is one previously unrecorded real downstream dependency, a replacement that violates it, and an owner-approved repair or deliberately accepted incompatibility. Include the counterexample, exact versions and coverage gaps. Building a new bundler or new CI service is outside this pilot.

**Kill condition and builder fit, I.** Ask the existing ecosystem-CI maintainers whether the pilot's claimed missing behavior and private participation are already covered. If yes, contribute the case and stop the separate-project story. If owners will not accept or maintain an obligation, there is no federation yet. The builder's compiler knowledge, TypeScript ecosystem fluency, Rust, Wasm and low-operations Cloudflare deployment fit the entire path from semantic mismatch to runnable reduced case. The missing capability is community enrollment, not implementation.

### 2. B06 — A common memory-safety boundary across language ecosystems

**Seed, Q.** An audience member asks why Fil-C is not everything targeting LLVM IR; Pizlo answers “Send patches” ([Fil-C talk, 46:00](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=5F-2Y1LPRek&t=2760)). His tagged-pointer discussion shows why the task is not simply attaching a pass ([39:00–40:00](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=5F-2Y1LPRek&t=2340)). The opening is a concrete invitation around an existing working system.

**Full scale and new affordance, I.** Multiple source-language ecosystems and their dependencies can execute under one deliberately specified capability model, including supported cross-language calls. The expensive asset is an accepted, repeatedly exercised dependency closure and a precise account of unsupported constructs. Cheap agents could maintain frontends, library adaptations and regression work across far more of that closure than a small human team could previously support. This could change the cost of choosing a safe interoperability boundary. It does not make application logic correct, remove all source-language undefined behavior, or prove the trusted runtime.

**Closest attempts and limits, F.** [Fil-C](https://fil-c.org/) is active, with its site announcing release 0.687 on October 23, 2026; it already supports substantial C/C++ software and limited unsafe FFI. [GIMSO](https://fil-c.org/gimso) already defines capability semantics for incoming LLVM IR. Thus the catchy “all LLVM” observation is not an unclaimed pass idea. Its documentation also describes calling-convention restrictions, unsupported exception-handling instructions and pointer/integer semantics. [CHERI](https://www.cl.cam.ac.uk/research/security/ctsrd/cheri/) and [Morello](https://www.cl.cam.ac.uk/research/security/ctsrd/cheri/cheri-morello.html) already provide a much broader hardware/software capability program with substantial public and industrial support. These projects have not stalled; compatibility, deployment and ecosystem adoption remain central work. The viable contribution is a genuinely accepted second-ecosystem closure, not inventing memory-safe C or claiming every LLVM frontend already fits.

**One-shot value, I.** A stronger model might write the selected frontend port in one prompt. It cannot establish new correspondence between source semantics and the protected execution model merely by doing so. Independent maintainers must agree what the boundary promises; actual workloads must expose mismatches; the evidence must continue across compiler/runtime releases. If all that remains is a mechanical port against an already complete public specification and suite, treat it as an upstream contribution rather than a separate surviving project.

**Economics and physics, E/I.** A later small closure with 100 packages × 2 toolchains × 3 configurations × 10 CPU-minutes is about 100 core-hours, excluding compiler builds. On the same assumed eight-core/100 W machine that is 1.25 kWh before overhead. Development may require a 16–64 GB host; a €500 incremental compute ceiling is a reasonable pilot constraint, not an estimate of production cost. Runtime overhead, peak memory and application-specific latency need measurement; no universal multiplier is defensible here. The crucial physical limit is acceptable resource overhead on useful workloads. The crucial semantic limit is that a late pass cannot resurrect behavior already removed under earlier undefined-behavior assumptions. Toolchain and library redistribution rights must be checked for the selected closure; the project cannot assume arbitrary proprietary dependencies are available.

**Six-week start, I.** Work upstream with one explicitly supported raw-pointer subset of a small second frontend, a C host/dependency and no unexamined runtime escapes. A small Rust `no_std` component is one possible target if Fil-C maintainers consider it useful and not already supported. Use about a quarter of the 48–72 hours agreeing semantics and prior art, half on the executable boundary, and the remainder on independent review and observed performance. Seek an unreported cross-language mismatch or a useful mixed-language workload under a clearly stated common model. Publish the supported/unsupported boundary even if the proposed port fails. Do not pretend a demo certifies a language ecosystem.

**Kill condition and builder fit, I.** Kill the independent-project thesis if an existing branch already covers the target, maintainers reject the model, or the useful workload requires bypassing the common protection boundary. The builder's kernel, VMM and compiler experience is unusually relevant to ABI, ownership, instrumentation and trusted-runtime questions. LLVM-specific expertise still needs to be acquired or supplied by collaborators. This is an ecosystem transformation to help build, not a new kernel and not GrowOS.

### 3. B02 — A replicated, longitudinal laboratory for software methods

**Seeds, Q.** Jonathan Blow says objective comparison is hard because building twice leaves you “20 years older” ([1:00](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=e6crOMC9WCE&t=3600)). Evan You asks for “comprehensive evals that cover complete loops” ([ViteConf, 23:00](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=PfyIEgLzNYI&t=1380)). S07's attention evidence supplies an essential endpoint: accepted useful work per human decision/review effort, rather than generated code or agent utilization.

**Full scale and why now, I.** Build genuinely different, idiomatic implementations of the same useful workflows, then observe independent people using, changing, repairing and operating them. Repeat across architectures, agents and model generations. The field gains transportable evidence about when methods help, where they fail, and which human costs move. Automated implementation reduces the former engineer-years barrier to generating comparable alternatives. External requirements, delayed operational consequences and independent analysis remain costly; those are the research asset. E12's organizational-emergence experiment, the causal observatory and production architecture trials are subprograms of this one direction, not four additional businesses.

**Closest attempts, F/I.** METR already conducts real developer experiments. Its [February 2026 update](https://metr.org/blog/2026-02-24-uplift-update/) explicitly identifies selection bias, task substitution and unreliable task-time accounting under concurrent agents, and describes changing its design. This is a current competitor/collaborator, not proof that models still slow developers in October 2026. Existing empirical software engineering, the source reports' benchmark efforts and platform eval roadmaps also occupy the field. The proposed distinction is replicated implementation alternatives with subsequent blinded handoff/maintenance outcomes. An independent researcher should review that distinction and the study design before code is generated. No claim of an empty market survives this comparison.

**One-shot value, I.** The generator, randomizer and results page are cheap code. Novel, properly sampled outcomes, domain-owner acceptance, later defect discovery and a cumulative causal record cannot be recovered from a product prompt. A model may help run the experiments. It must not define the treatment, produce the competing solutions and then serve as the sole judge of the hypothesis it is rewarded for confirming.

**Economics and physics, E.** A modest generation campaign of 24 tasks × 3 variants is 72 runs. At an assumed 0.5 million input and 0.1 million output tokens per run, it uses 36 million input and 7.2 million output tokens. Budget €200–800 only after checking the selected models' actual rates; these are scenario allowances, not current price assertions. Eight independent maintainers × 90 minutes plus six hours of follow-up is 18 human hours; at an assumed €50–100/hour, €900–1,800. Human evaluation already exceeds a modest compute bill. Token counts do not identify inference energy without model/hardware/utilization data; record them and measured local energy rather than invent kWh. Private tasks need explicit publication limits. A six-week pilot can reveal a measurement failure or feasibility result, not a universal architecture winner.

**Six-week start, I.** Take one real workflow, two contrasting implementations and one concrete hypothesis about maintenance under a hidden change. Agree identical external requirements and obtain idiomaticity review before trials. Randomize participant order, separate active human effort from wall time and include a short delayed repair task. Use existing telemetry and publish the protocol, disagreements and null results. If participant compensation or recruitment is unavailable, shrink to four paired trials; do not substitute model judges and keep the same claim. The demo target is a previously unmeasured maintenance/understanding difference that a build-speed leaderboard would miss.

**Kill condition and builder fit, I.** Stop the causal claim if implementation equivalence, task selection or participant effects cannot be controlled well enough to distinguish explanations. Join an existing study if it already asks the same question. A systems builder who can implement both sides and understand runtime/compiler costs is a good technical investigator. Experimental design, recruitment and statistical interpretation are missing specialties; being a platform engineer does not uniquely qualify someone to infer causal laws. This ranks behind B20/B06 because its expensive work is precisely the work the builder has not yet demonstrated.

### 4. B03 — A live, empirically governed computational commons — the crazy pick

**Seed, Q.** Omar imagines a “whole system” that is “a live program being edited by thousands of humans and AIs” ([47:00–48:00](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=fB_lklm7xgU&t=2820)), then discusses keeping an up-to-date planetary view and preserving old decision versions ([1:03–1:05](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=fB_lklm7xgU&t=3780)).

**Full scale and why now, I.** Scientific models, observations, explicit assumptions and counterfactuals form a shared executing knowledge environment. Incomplete work stays local; accepted scientific snapshots stay reproducible; new observations update only causally affected results. The ambition is to change how a field accumulates scientific understanding, not replace Jupyter with another editor. Cheap agents lower the cost of porting libraries, maintaining connectors and exploring model couplings. They do not supply valid couplings, fresh measurements or authority to publish scientific conclusions.

**Closest attempts, F/I.** [Hazel](https://hazel.org/) already implements incomplete-program foundations, documents its live computational-commons vision, and lists 2026 work on typed interactive tables. [Pangeo](https://pangeo.io/) already supplies a scientific community, software and scalable computing infrastructure. [ESMF](https://earthsystemmodeling.org/) already supports coupled Earth-system components and grid remapping. None has failed merely because nobody wrote the integration UI. The residual to investigate is a domain-accepted live scientific dependency with uncertainty, partial work and frozen decision versions. This should be a collaboration around those efforts, not a claim to have invented the vision.

**One-shot value, I.** A model can create a beautiful live notebook. New environmental observations, accepted uncertainty models, scientist-owned coupling judgments and continuing cross-institution stewardship cannot be created by that notebook. If there is no scientist and no real arriving observation, the demo fails the premise.

**Economics and physics, E.** Start with a bounded coastal computation, not a global climate model. A scenario with 100 parameter combinations × 30 CPU-minutes is 50 core-hours; on the illustrative eight-core/100 W host, about 0.625 kWh before overhead. Cap the data slice at 10 GB and computation at €250 until the scientific question justifies expansion. Remote large-array execution should remain in existing scientific infrastructure. A full coupled planetary model can require orders of magnitude more compute; nothing about liveness removes numerical stability, resolution or conservation constraints. Use public data whose license permits the proposed derived publication, and preserve uncertainty instead of giving a live number unearned authority.

**Six-week start, kill condition and fit, I.** Obtain one scientist responsible for an ocean computation and one responsible for a small ecological consequence. Couple their existing models using existing scientific tools, ingest a genuinely new observation, and retain both the changed prediction and the old decision snapshot. Deliberately leave an unrelated branch incomplete. The desired observation is a scientifically accepted change in a concrete result, including what remains unknown. Stop if the coupling has no independently defensible meaning or duplicates an existing workflow without adding accepted capability. The builder can supply execution boundaries, Wasm components, versioned artifacts and partial-failure isolation; domain scientists supply the oracle. This is the most exciting destination, and a poor solo identity to adopt before obtaining that collaboration.

## Small ideas with a 100× destination

These are inversions, not evidence that the small original already passed. They change what must be accumulated and who must participate.

1. **A witness → an executable evidence commons, B20.** Stop asking how to sell a better diff. Ask which independently owned behaviors make a foundational implementation replaceable. The durable unit is an owner-accepted obligation linked to an observed counterexample and a continuing participant. Browser handoff, LiveStore upgrades, skill consumers, service fakes and module-federation failures are possible entries, not a demand for one universal runner. This is the best inversion.

2. **A workspace reproducer or Converter Witness → real failure worlds, B19.** A generated repro is cheap; a permitted failure that faithfully represents a customer's device/network/cloud combination is not. Link the reduced case back to an actual repair outcome. The scale change is sharing empirical coverage between vendors before the next customer rediscovers the same failure. Do not confuse an anonymized bundle that no longer fails with an asset.

3. **RigorBench, shim retirement, architecture follow-through → experimental software science, B02.** Better models can implement both competing approaches. The valuable output becomes a prospectively designed, externally adjudicated history of later human and operational consequences. A score increase without an independent endpoint is not that history.

4. **A codegen-elimination witness or GPU fixture → cost and numerical compatibility, B07/B10.** Accumulate accepted end-to-end envelopes across actual devices and releases. An application can then know whether replacing a runtime/device preserves its resource and numerical obligations. A disassembly screenshot or one more synthetic benchmark is only a seed.

5. **An analytical-integrity check or Sampling Witness → institutional meaning, B17.** Move from detecting one unit mismatch to preserving an adjudicated definition through several independently owned artifacts and revisions. Keep contested cases and missing observations explicit. Metadata retention is not meaning retention, and a model cannot decide a company's definition of a customer on its own authority.

6. **A discard drill or fork-upkeep casebook → capability continuity, B08.** Preserve the human task and the lawful inputs needed to perform it, not a particular application's continued existence. The large promise is that replacing or retiring software does not repeatedly erase useful cultural/scientific capability. Offline rebuilds and user-approved restorations are two branches of the same preservation mission.

7. **A GPUI assertion → shared accessible interaction semantics, B09.** The bridge code becomes inexpensive; users' chosen task semantics, observed assistive behavior and maintainers honoring the conventions remain hard. Build with existing accessibility communities. A model-approved accessibility tree is not a human outcome.

8. **A language fuzzer or RLBox adapter → accepted semantic qualification and safe ecosystem closure, B11/B06.** The shift is from generating attacks or ports to maintaining the independent contract, evidence and dependencies that other ecosystems can rely on. This must not become GrowOS in another vocabulary.

The attractive but unsupported inversions are also revealing: **a notification filter → an “attention operating system,” an invoice workflow → an “institutional trust network,” and a registry → a “commons.”** Each needs a concrete new capability and willing independent participants. The word at the right-hand side does not supply either.

## What this builder is unusually able to do

**I.** Nothing in the provided biography establishes unique access to users, hardware fleets, scientists, banks, apprenticeships or physical fabrication. Do not turn technical competence into imaginary distribution. The unusual advantage is a combination of implementation perspectives:

- **Compiler plus kernel/VMM experience:** inspect whether a claimed contract is actually enforced, where semantics were lost, and what the trusted boundary contains. This strongly favors B06 and helps B11/B12.
- **TypeScript plus Rust plus Wasm:** follow a compatibility failure across source tools, native engines, browser execution and a portable reduced case. This favors B20 more than another Cloudflare-only feature.
- **Cloudflare operations knowledge:** run the small coordination layer cheaply while participants retain their source and expensive execution. This is implementation convenience; the participant network remains the value.
- **Open-source, part-time constraints:** contribute one accepted cross-owner case or one missing semantic boundary before claiming a platform. A neutral accumulating artifact can compound while the builder is offline. An always-on support business or a new physical institution cannot be assumed to fit that schedule.

**I — Fit ordering beyond the top three.** B19's failure-world contribution, B07's runtime cost contracts and B11's language qualification are credible adjacent paths. B05's infrastructure capture, B16's physical fulfillment and B03's scientific commons need partners with observation rights and domain authority. Apprenticeship networks, maintainer staffing and bank acceptance processes are primarily organizational work. Their immunity to one-shot coding is not a reason for this particular builder to choose them.

That supports independent boundaries and observations; it does not support every proposed receipt product.

**Q/I.** Ben's “And I'm good” after tailoring a family display ([80, 1:33](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=HDdbU10r7Js&t=5580)) is a direct challenge to an endless-malleability business thesis. A perfectly useful finished app can have no continuing editing market. The right durable asset might be the observed household need, not the editor, fork manager or upkeep subscription.

**I.** The largest correction is equating **a problem that remains** with **a project whose value remains**. Effective settings, orphan cleanup, executable identity, exact metrics and deployment truth remain necessary. Their proposed implementations can still become defaults, documentation examples or ten-line patches. Their necessity does not protect the excitement of building another wrapper.

**I.** The second correction is treating oracle scarcity as automatic opportunity. If only the incumbent has the data, authority or expert team, naming that scarce thing identifies the incumbent's advantage. B20 has a possible outsider route because observations and obligations are distributed across owners. B06 has an upstream route. B02 has an independence/collaboration route. None has an automatic standalone-business route.

**I/E.** Most at risk of becoming 2–5× less exciting: **WhichBinary, restart receipts, nested-artifact adapters, hibernation hooks, shell diagnostics, one-app migration rehearsals, fixture generators, permission-case wrappers, UI jitter assertions and schema transformations.** Use them when useful. Do not confuse their present implementation difficulty with the next decade's project. Data-producing programs can also fail: synthetic “worlds,” vendor-judged “evidence,” unused contractual grades and empty “commons” have no external asset to protect.

**I/E — Why the tower is parked rather than declared physically impossible.** The two tests require an ambitious new affordance, not just a huge non-code budget. [ASU's structural account](https://hieroglyph.asu.edu/2014/08/structural-design-of-the-tall-tower/) already explored a 20 km tower and explains why a vertical-load estimate omits wind and buckling. As a scale check, raising a kilogram by 20 km adds approximately 0.196 MJ of potential energy, versus approximately 30 MJ/kg of kinetic energy at 7.8 km/s. Any large launch benefit must come from the detailed vehicle/atmosphere/staging system, not height alone. Even the article's illustrative five-million-tonne gravity-load steel quantity would imply €5 billion in raw material at an assumed €1,000/tonne, before erection, land, wind stability or operations; this is not a total design cost. No physics law here proves impossibility. No supplied evidence demonstrates that cheaper software changes the binding feasibility either. Keep it as a specialist engineering question, outside the 20 survivors and outside this builder's recommended program.

## Decision

**ONE pick: B20, the federated executable specification of the web ecosystem.** Begin by obtaining one accepted downstream compatibility obligation that existing upstream coverage misses. The six-week result should be a new jointly accepted observation, not a runner launch.

**Three best survivors:** B20's web evidence federation, B06's shared memory-safety ecosystem, and B02's replicated software-methods laboratory. **Crazy-but-not-physically-impossible pick:** B03's live scientific computational commons, pursued with its existing researchers and domain owners.

**Five-line TL;DR**

1. Twenty distinct ambitious directions pass; twenty-five passing extraction slots contain five duplicates.
2. The earlier small tools do not pass both tests at their original scope, even when their underlying problems persist.
3. Better models devalue implementation novelty and increase the supply of replacements that need independent acceptance.
4. The strongest seed is a witness inverted into an accumulating, participant-owned executable account of compatibility.
5. Choose B20; investigate B06/B02 next; require actual observations and participants before using the word “commons.”
