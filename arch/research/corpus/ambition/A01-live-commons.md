# A01 — Live commons: shared knowledge that keeps running

Research completed 2026-10-29, against the 2026-10-28 ambition brief. **Q** = supplied practitioner quotation; **F** = externally sourced fact; **I** = inference or proposal; **E** = estimate or proposed experimental threshold. Source captions are minute buckets, contain transcription errors, and establish what someone said rather than independently establishing their claims. All project descriptions, rankings, budgets, and acceptance thresholds below are I/E unless marked otherwise.

## Decision

**I — ONE pick: a Living Model Commons, beginning with a small, measured thermal-model commons.** Build a place where separately maintained models become reusable live public knowledge: people can inspect assumptions, change them directly, connect models, compare predictions with observations, and retain an accepted version while a replacement is incomplete. Its enduring asset is the participating community and its growing body of calibrated models, measurements, disagreements, and accepted meanings. The interpreter and hosting are replaceable.

**F/I — The closest existing attempt is Fairground, by Cyrus Omar, Michael Coblenz, and Anil Madhavapeddy.** Their 2025 paper explicitly proposes linked executable scientific notebooks, a distributed scheduler, and a pure Python subset. This is direct conceptual competition and a plausible upstream home. A generic computational-commons proposal would repeat their work. The useful opening is demonstrating an externally maintained, empirically grounded commons with real downstream reuse; the inspected sources do not establish that deployment at scale. [Fairground paper](https://hazel.org/papers/fairground-propl25.pdf).

**I — Do not pitch “Wikipedia, but functions” as the discovery.** Wikifunctions exists; Observable already combines reactive notebooks, imports, collaboration, and AI; Val Town hosts social executable code. The ambitious proposition is a durable institution for maintaining shared executable understanding across owners, including people who never edit source code.

### Wikifunctions / Abstract Wikipedia: the 2026 status changes the conclusion

**F — This is live work with downstream integration.** The October 17 newsletter reports evaluator-representation changes to address excessive memory usage, fixes for concurrent editing/rollback problems, and integration enabled for **Igbo and Dagbani Wikipedias following Malayalam**. It says local communities choose whether and which articles to integrate; nothing is inserted automatically. Thus the March statement that integration was missing must not be presented as the October state. [October 17 update](https://www.wikifunctions.org/wiki/Special:MyLanguage/Wikifunctions:Status_updates/2026-10-17).

**F — There is substantive opposition, not a verified closure.** A July 2026 RfC proposes pausing rollout, publishing costs and criteria, and conditional closure of Abstract Wikipedia. It explicitly excludes Wikifunctions from the closure question. Critics cite broken outputs, poor articles, and contributor burden; the project team's response argues for iterative improvement and reports progress. The RfC is evidence of contested utility and governance, not an independent evaluation or an adopted shutdown decision. [RfC, including the team's response](https://meta.wikimedia.org/wiki/Requests_for_comment/The_future_of_Abstract_Wikipedia).

**I — Lesson:** executable composition, editor convergence, and type representations are necessary engineering, but adoption is won through a worthwhile contribution and a useful output. A million agent-generated functions can increase stewardship debt. Copying Wikimedia's institutional scale while ignoring the local community's right to decline would repeat the hardest problem, not solve it.

## What agents change, and the barrier they do not cross

**I — The strongest historical barrier is the simultaneous cost of ecosystem construction.** A live language needs libraries, data adapters, domain views, examples, migration support, and maintenance before ordinary users can do useful work. Agents can now build many of those pieces concurrently, translate existing calculations, explain boundary mismatches, and propose repairs as upstream inputs change. Omar's own porting experience supports a reduction in this cost, rather than just a speculative claim that agents will like a new language.

**E — A transparent scale threshold:** suppose a million useful module-pages require 30 minutes each for initial integration: 500,000 human hours, roughly 250 engineer-years at 2,000 hours/year. If 1% need a 15-minute adjustment each week, upkeep is another 2,500 human hours/week. These assumptions are illustrative, not a measured market. Moving routine integration to agents could make a small editorial institution plausible; moving all 2,500 hours into a human review queue would merely relocate the failure.

**I — Three burdens increase with agents:** more proposed changes competing for attention; more inexpensive sabotage/spam; and more plausible but semantically wrong output. Typed holes protect exploratory continuity. Immutable releases protect dependency stability. Neither proves a sensor is calibrated, a model applies to this building, a learner understands a concept, or a policy owner endorses a decision.

**I — The one-shot distinction:** a stronger model can build an impressive live-commons demo, and probably most of the prototype described below. It cannot recreate observations that have not been collected, endorsements that owners have not given, adoption that participants have not chosen, or longitudinal evidence that does not exist. If the proposal's value is still only the demo, it fails the ambition brief.

## Could the starting version run on Workers and Durable Objects?

**I — Yes, for coordination, modest pure computations, interactive views, and a bounded shared model graph.** Use DOs for authoritative module state and collaboration; evaluate expressions locally or in isolated execution workers; retain large data separately. A planet-scale scientific solver is a different workload and should be invoked through a versioned external-compute boundary.

### One DO per module: reasonable identity, poor universal execution boundary

**F — Current limits matter:** SQLite-backed DOs have 10 GB per-object storage on the paid plan, inherently single-threaded execution, a soft limit of 1,000 requests/second per object, and configurable CPU budgets up to five minutes. Workers have 128 MB per isolate, including Wasm allocations. Multiple objects can share an isolate. A DO identity is therefore not a promise of a separate memory/security process. [DO limits](https://developers.cloudflare.com/durable-objects/platform/limits/), [Workers limits](https://developers.cloudflare.com/workers/platform/limits/).

**I — Suggested placement:**

| Component | Responsibility | Placement |
|---|---|---|
| Module record | Owners, accepted immutable revisions, draft operations, import subscriptions and publication policy | One `ModuleRoom` DO per independently maintained module or small module group; one deployed class serving many instances |
| Editing session | Presence and ordered edit operations, including expected base revision | Hibernating WebSockets attached to the room; browser keeps local draft state |
| Evaluation | Pure bounded expression graph with explicit holes, deterministic inputs and metered steps | Browser Web Worker for local preview; authoritative shared interpreter on the server |
| Untrusted foreign code | Restricted adapter or imported implementation | Dynamic Worker with narrow bindings and blocked/default-denied egress; optional facet only when isolated persistent state is actually required |
| Results and artifacts | Immutable values, source/manifests, input snapshots and large tables | Small metadata in SQLite; larger immutable objects in R2 or an external dataset store |
| Recompute propagation | Durable pending work, deduplication, retry, coalescing and bounded fanout | Transactional outbox in each room; alarm/queue delivery; stateless evaluation workers |
| Published view | A compatible result manifest plus status, age and dependencies | Cached immutable output; a small per-view authoritative pointer |
| Heavy scientific jobs | Batch simulations on appropriate CPU/GPU/HPC hardware | External executor with explicit input/output identities and resource budget |

**I — Do not deploy a DO class per module.** Class limits would be irrelevant overhead. Instances share a deployed supervisor/interpreter. For popular read-mostly modules, serve immutable results from caches and shard presence/subscription delivery so all readers do not hammer one coordinator. “Thousands of editors across the commons” is achievable with partitioned rooms; thousands continuously editing one room is an entirely different contention experiment.

### The minimum semantic protocol

1. **I — Immutable executable revisions.** A revision identifies source/AST, export types, interpreter version and resolved import identities. A published release references an explicit data-input vector separately. Hashes include the relevant semantics/configuration, not just source text. Reject module dependency cycles initially; hashing and scheduling recursive groups need a separate design.
2. **I — Live drafts and accepted code are visible separate states.** A malformed edit becomes a typed hole in the draft where supported. Independent expressions continue to evaluate. Consumers using an accepted revision keep using it until an owner or an explicit update policy adopts another. The UI can compare draft results with accepted results without quietly substituting one for the other.
3. **I — Status is data.** A result can be complete, partial, timed out, stale, or rejected. `Hole<Celsius>` is not zero degrees. An old value always retains its revision and observation age. A broken dependent computation must not masquerade as a new successful prediction.
4. **I — Direct manipulation writes typed operations.** Moving a parameter slider edits an expression/literal in that participant's scenario; sorting a table edits a transform. A shared accepted model changes only through its publication policy. The semantic operation log, not a generated commentary paragraph, explains what changed.
5. **I — Effects are explicit.** Pure functions receive immutable input values; time, randomness and sensor readings arrive as named/versioned inputs. A generated plan to fetch data or send a message is not an executed effect. A trusted connector owns the actual capability and its revocation. No arbitrary JS, raw HTML, network access, or secret-bearing bindings in the small core.

**F — This is not a missing Cloudflare sandbox primitive.** Dynamic Workers already support runtime-loaded code, controlled bindings and egress. Facets already give dynamically loaded DO classes separate SQLite storage under a supervisor. Custom limits constrain CPU time and subrequests. Use those facilities for foreign code; do not run arbitrary uploaded JS inside the coordinator's authority. [Dynamic Workers](https://developers.cloudflare.com/dynamic-workers/), [facets](https://developers.cloudflare.com/dynamic-workers/usage/durable-object-facets/), [custom limits](https://developers.cloudflare.com/dynamic-workers/usage/limits/), [egress control](https://developers.cloudflare.com/dynamic-workers/usage/egress-control/).

**I — Scope restraint:** a small typed expression graph with explicit holes can demonstrate a bounded slice of Omar's semantics. It is not Hazel's complete language or a proof of its metatheory. Use structured operations and an ordered DO log during the experiment; implement text parsing or Grove-level distributed editing only if observed collaboration needs them. Bound AST size/depth, type-checking work, result size, import fanout and queued proposals as well as evaluation steps: a pure expression can still exhaust resources before execution. A general-purpose language and a globally mergeable editor would consume the entire schedule.

### Incremental recomputation across DOs

**I — Feasible protocol, not automatic DO behavior:**

- A module commits its changed revision or input version and an invalidation/outbox entry in its local transaction. A durable alarm/queue mechanism drains that outbox. Messages carry an idempotency key, producer revision, and input identity; delivery can repeat.
- Dependents coalesce rapid updates and resolve a specific input/import vector. The evaluation key includes the expression, resolved imports, runtime semantics and input identities. Reuse complete cached results only for that key. Rendering-width edits should not rerun the model or refetch observations.
- Evaluate a module's expression graph in one execution placement where possible. Crossing a network boundary for every expression destroys the advantage of expression-level incrementality. Reuse inside a module; batch traffic at module boundaries.
- Persist results under immutable keys, then conditionally publish them only if their target vector is still relevant. A late computation for revision N cannot overwrite N+1. An interrupted computation can be retried from its recorded inputs.
- A displayed page switches its result-manifest pointer after all required results for that view are available. Otherwise it explicitly displays a partial/stale view. This prevents a complete-looking display from mixing incompatible results. It does not promise a globally atomic view of every page in the commons.
- A sensor network has no magical common instant. An explicit vector of observation versions gives reproducibility; freshness tolerances and missing observations remain visible. Concurrent upstream publishers can create new candidate vectors while an older vector is still computing.
- Cycles, adaptive solvers, mutable foreign state, and nondeterministic floating-point backends require explicit semantics. In the prototype, reject cycles, meter work, and label external outputs rather than pretending ordinary DAG invalidation solves coupled climate simulation.

**E — Latency envelope:** ten serial cross-object transitions at an assumed 10–50 ms each consume 100–500 ms before meaningful work; remote placement can be worse. These are budgeting assumptions, not measured Cloudflare guarantees. Local browser preview should target under 100 ms for a small scenario; authoritative publication can lag and show progress. An apparent 10× evaluator optimization is irrelevant if the UI waits on thirty remote calls.

### Economics, compute and energy

**F — Rates checked in current documentation:** paid DO compute includes one million monthly requests and 400,000 GB-s, then $0.15/million requests and $12.50/million GB-s. Duration is charged at a 128 MB allocation while active or unable to hibernate; sleeping eligible objects do not incur duration. SQLite adds usage-based storage and row charges. [DO pricing](https://developers.cloudflare.com/durable-objects/platform/pricing/).

**F — A decisive 2026 wrinkle:** Dynamic Workers charge $0.002 for each unique Worker ID/code combination created on a day beyond the included allocation; changing code can create another billed identity. Request and CPU rates are $0.30/million and $0.02/million CPU-ms beyond their included allocations. They also meter startup CPU. [Dynamic Workers pricing](https://developers.cloudflare.com/dynamic-workers/pricing/).

All numbers below are **E**, gross marginal arithmetic before allowances, billing rounding, tax, support, queues, R2 operations, inference and large-data transfer. Monthly examples use 30 days.

| Workload or design | Rough arithmetic | Implication |
|---|---|---|
| 10,000 edits/day; 20 affected evaluations/edit; 20 ms CPU/evaluation | 6 million evaluations/month; 120 million CPU-ms, about 33 CPU-hours; about $2.40 CPU and $1.80 requests at the quoted Worker rates | Small pure evaluation is cheap. Counting only this line gives a misleading total. |
| 10,000 distinct Dynamic Workers used each day | 10,000 × 30 × $0.002 = $600/month gross creation charge | Prefer a shared trusted interpreter over inert typed AST data; reserve per-code sandboxes for foreign code. Do not share an unsafe JS execution context to save money. |
| One million distinct Dynamic Workers used daily | About $60,000/month gross creation charge, before additional edited versions | Per-module dynamic code has a real floor even when individual calculations are tiny. |
| 1,000 DOs continuously active, including idle connections that prevent sleep | 1,000 × 0.128 GB × 2,592,000 s × $12.50/million GB-s ≈ $4,147/month | Hibernation and fanout architecture matter far more than code size. A million continuously active objects would be roughly $4.15M/month under the same assumptions. |
| 6 million evaluation results, four indexed/logical row writes each | 24 million writes/month; within a fresh account's documented 50-million monthly included SQLite row writes, before other traffic | Persist module-level results and batched edits. Per-expression durable traces can multiply this unexpectedly. Index updates count too. |
| 1,000 agent repairs/day at assumed $0.10–$1 each | $3,000–$30,000/month | Unbounded auto-repair can dwarf pure execution. Human/agent proposals need budgets, not a promise of free upkeep. These are scenario prices, not a provider quote. |
| One million modules eagerly reevaluated every minute at 20 ms each | 1.44 billion evaluations/day; 8,000 CPU-hours/day; about $576/day gross CPU | A live commons must compute on demand, coalesce changes and reuse results. “Live” cannot mean reheating all computation continuously. |

**E — Energy bound:** one million 20-ms evaluations consume about 5.6 CPU-hours. At an illustrative attributed 20–100 W per busy core equivalent, that is 0.11–0.56 kWh of CPU work, excluding memory, networking, idle allocation and facility overhead. The eager million-module/minute scenario becomes about 160–800 kWh/day on the same assumptions. These are order-of-magnitude workload estimates, not measured Cloudflare energy or a carbon accounting claim. Agent inference and physical simulations can dominate both.

**E — Prototype budget:** aim for $5–$50/month infrastructure, $50–$300 of deliberately capped model usage over six weeks, and $0–$200 for optional measurement hardware. Manual CSV import from an existing logger is sufficient. An open-source institution at scale needs grants, memberships or paid private hosting/support; public educational use should not depend on unlimited subsidized inference. No token economy is necessary.

**I — Availability claim:** a bounded bad edit can be confined to its draft, evaluation budget, and declared dependency cone. It is impossible to honestly promise that no edit can ever affect the whole service: runtime bugs, account exhaustion, widely shared bad releases, control-plane failures and authorization errors remain. Publication policy, reserved read capacity, quotas, fallback to explicitly labeled accepted outputs, and moderation make the limited promise credible. They do not justify an absolute security or uptime claim.

## Four ambitious ideas in this family

### 1. Living Model Commons — shared scientific knowledge that updates with the world

**1 — Seed, Q.** Omar's fish/ocean/ice-sheet coupling at [49:00](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=fB_lklm7xgU&t=2940s), his live-program Wikipedia at [48:00](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=fB_lklm7xgU&t=2880s), and Campbell's public scientific result collection at [48:00](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=uWnUnMphmPM&t=2880s). Independent reinforcement: de Moura's account of cooperation over checked shared definitions at [25:00](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=KzdYKeAqWhY&t=1500s).

**2 — Full scale, I.** A million maintained model-pages connect measurements, calculations, hypotheses and explanations across scientific specialties. A model is a living public reference that others actually import. An ecologist changes an assumption in a local scenario, follows its effect through linked models, and sees disagreement and empirical error alongside the result. Accepted research snapshots remain citeable. This changes how a field accumulates executable knowledge rather than merely publishing notebooks more conveniently.

**3 — Previously infeasible / now plausible, I/E.** Small model repositories were always possible. A continuously interoperating, maintained cross-domain collection was economically infeasible at the staffing threshold estimated above: every adapter, view and repair consumed expert engineering. Agents reduce that construction/translation burden and can continuously propose upkeep. They do not make climate prediction or consensus easy. The actual hypothesis is that the remaining domain review burden falls enough for useful cross-owner reuse to compound.

**4 — One-shot test, I.** The asset is a permissioned network of maintainers, empirical input/validation histories, stable shared concepts, and accepted downstream dependencies. A stronger model can clone the UI and regenerate adapters. It cannot manufacture tomorrow's room-temperature measurements, a lab's endorsement, or the observed reasons participants keep using a model. Public access and portability make this a public-good asset rather than a proprietary code moat.

**5 — Physics/economics/data/law, I/E.** Start with eight to twenty small thermal/energy/uncertainty modules and two independently maintained room models. One hundred loggers sampled every five minutes produce 28,800 readings/day; at an assumed 100 bytes/reading, raw observations are under 3 MB/day. Infrastructure is modest under the preceding budget. Calibration labor and legitimate access dominate. Publish only consenting, non-personal observations with explicit dataset/model licenses; occupancy and household routines need privacy treatment and need not be collected. Types for Celsius versus temperature differences and watts versus watt-hours prevent some mistakes, not invalid physical assumptions. Full atmospheric/ocean simulation belongs on appropriate external compute; IPCC describes the real resolution/compute tradeoff. [IPCC regional-model discussion](https://www.ipcc.ch/report/ar6/wg1/chapter/chapter-10/).

**6 — Closest attempts, F/I.** Fairground is closest to the full architecture; Hazel supplies the semantic foundations. Observable and marimo are practical authoring alternatives. Wolfram already hosts a broad computational library. Destination Earth supplies serious operational climate twins, so do not relabel a climate dashboard as an open field. The proposed difference must be demonstrated participation and reusable measured models across owners, not asserted absence. [Fairground](https://hazel.org/papers/fairground-propl25.pdf), [Climate DT](https://destine.ecmwf.int/climate-digital-twin/).

**7 — Six weeks for one person, E.** Build the narrow shared-runtime experiment specified below, and recruit two model owners who each consume the other's maintained computation. End with live pages, real held-out observations, direct controls, an intentionally broken candidate release, an unaffected accepted consumer, and a visible empirical model failure that types could not catch. The unusual artifact is this complete joint behavior plus genuine reuse, not another slider demo.

**8 — Strongest objection and cheap falsification, I/E.** Scientists may prefer notebooks plus exchanged files; maintaining shared semantics may cost more attention than reuse saves. In two days, manually connect two consenting owners' existing calculations using an Observable/marimo baseline and ten held-out observations. If no owner wants an upstream dependency, or independent local copies remain clearly easier, stop building a general commons. If owners want it but disagree on meaning, record that disagreement before designing an ontology. At six weeks, require both owners to perform an update/reuse without the builder acting as translator.

**9 — Builder fit, I.** Excellent fit for systems, typed execution, Wasm, small storage protocols and Cloudflare. Weak fit for independently inventing thermal science; a domain collaborator is required. Keep the runtime small and seek an upstream relationship with Fairground/Hazel if practical. The serious future work is model stewardship and community, which the builder must actually want to do.

### 2. A commons of executable public rules — institutions publish computations they stand behind

**1 — Seed, Q/I.** Omar's [54:00–55:00](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=fB_lklm7xgU&t=3240s) vision of properties used for abstract review, de Moura's [05:00](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=KzdYKeAqWhY&t=300s) proposal for synthesized code accompanied by a proof against a specification, and the corpus's incompatible-approved-design incident motivate it. Applying those ideas to institutional rules is an inference; no cited practitioner explicitly requests this product.

**2 — Full scale, I.** Municipalities, cooperatives, charities and standards bodies publish rule-pages with responsible owners, effective dates, executable examples, exceptions and appeal paths. People can test a proposed amendment by manipulating examples before an institution adopts it. Agents propose translations and impact analyses; authorized humans settle meaning and publication. Thousands of institutions can reuse rules while retaining explicit local authority. This could change public rule drafting and interoperability, not merely automate forms.

**3 — Previously infeasible / now plausible, I/E.** Existing rules-as-code projects prove the mechanism was possible. Broad coverage plus usable views for every local exception required too much specialist work. Agents reduce translation and scenario construction cost; typed partial results expose unknown facts rather than inventing a definitive answer. The remaining claim is scale across local rule owners, which can fail if legal/editorial review still consumes the entire former translation budget.

**4 — One-shot test, I.** The non-code asset is a network of institutions actually endorsing specific versions and continuing to publish amendments, plus adjudicated edge cases and the authority to correct them. A model cannot grant its output the force of an adopted rule. If the proposal becomes only an AI policy-to-code converter, discard it.

**5 — Physics/economics/data/law, I/E.** Computation is cheap: a million bounded 10-ms scenario evaluations are about 2.8 CPU-hours. Ten thousand rule changes/year needing even one expert hour each imply 10,000 hours of editorial work; at an assumed €100/hour that is €1M/year before operations. This needs funded institutions. Source availability does not imply an unambiguous rule or permission to distribute private case records. Begin with a consenting association's non-statutory room/equipment allocation policy, synthetic cases and human decisions; do not publish tax/benefit entitlement advice from a solo prototype. Catala explicitly addresses the legal/computational interpretation boundary and limits its intended scope. [Catala introduction](https://book.catala-lang.org/).

**6 — Closest attempts, F/I.** OpenFisca already models tax/benefit rules and reform effects; PolicyEngine already offers open microsimulation, policy exploration and validation; Catala already links executable code to legal text with lawyer-facing review. None should be described as a failed precursor. The residual is an adopted federation of authoritative owner-maintained rule-pages, not a superior rules engine. [OpenFisca](https://openfisca.org/doc/), [PolicyEngine](https://www.policyengine.org/us).

**7 — Six weeks, E.** Weeks 1–2: one consenting association supplies its current written allocation policy and twenty independently decided cases. Weeks 3–4: publish a live rule-page, direct scenario controls and explicit unresolved conditions using the existing core. Weeks 5–6: its actual policy owner adopts a revision; two separate clubs consume the rule under their own parameter sets. Demonstrate a conflict that stays unresolved until the owner decides, and show why an old decision still cites its old rule version. No autonomous booking or funds movement is required.

**8 — Kill, I/E.** The hardest problem may be political discretion that participants deliberately do not want made executable. Before coding, ask an owner to independently decide ten edge cases and state whether they will endorse their publication. If endorsement is unavailable, or the rule cannot be expressed without erasing necessary discretion, stop. Returning “needs a human decision” is legitimate but cannot be the only outcome of the system.

**9 — Fit, I.** Strong implementation fit, weak authority/distribution fit for a solo platform engineer. Rank below the scientific/learning commons unless a real institution volunteers ongoing ownership. This is not a governance platform to build speculatively.

### 3. Open Computational School — a maintained graph of explanations people can change

**1 — Seed, Q.** Tao's [20:00 proof indigestion](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=svl_1upFpQo&t=1200s), de Moura's [35:00–36:00](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=KzdYKeAqWhY&t=2100s) emphasis on proofs as communication, and Omar's [26:00–28:00](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=fB_lklm7xgU&t=1560s) compositional graphical notation. The independent signals support a need for usable understanding; extending them to a school is I.

**2 — Full scale, I.** A public computational curriculum in which lessons, simulations, worked examples and formal definitions import each other. A learner changes a model, follows a counterexample into a prerequisite page, and sees which assumptions were necessary. Teachers own adapted paths; explanatory alternatives coexist instead of one agent-generated textbook becoming canonical. A large teacher community maintains accessible interfaces and learning evidence across languages. This changes a field's ability to teach and reuse explanations.

**3 — Previously infeasible / now plausible, I/E.** Interactive lessons have existed for decades. Making an entire linked curriculum interactive, translated, accessible and maintained required prohibitive authoring/integration labor. Agents can draft models, localizations and views and repair compatibility. The ambitious new scale is not mass content output; it is a maintained network whose transfer-of-learning evidence keeps pace with it.

**4 — One-shot test, I.** The asset is a trusted teacher/contributor network and consented evidence of what real learners understand, forget and transfer to unfamiliar problems. A stronger model may generate better lessons than today's authors. It still cannot infer the actual outcome for learners who have not used them. If an excellent one-shot lesson equals the observed benefit, publish it and stop claiming a special platform advantage.

**5 — Physics/economics/data/law, I/E.** Ten thousand simultaneous learners doing a 5-ms local calculation twice/second imply 100 aggregate CPU-seconds/second, distributed across their devices; synchronizing every gesture through servers would unnecessarily multiply cost. The server stores curriculum revisions and opt-in outcomes. At one expert hour per hundred changed pages, a million-page collection still requires 10,000 expert hours per full review cycle. Use adult volunteers in the pilot, explicit consent, minimal records and checked content licenses. PhET's current usage terms distinguish permitted reuse; open access does not mean all material can be relicensed. [PhET licensing](https://phet.colorado.edu/en/licensing).

**6 — Closest attempts, F/I.** PhET already supplies researched interactive simulations and classroom/learner evaluation; Observable supplies remixable reactive explanations; Lean/mathlib and Blueprint supply shared formal concepts/exposition. Victor's explorable explanations establish the older idea of readers challenging an argument by modifying its model. The proposed distinctive hypothesis is cross-owner executable composition plus maintained evidence of transfer, not the invention of interactive pedagogy. [PhET](https://phet.colorado.edu/en/about), [explorable explanations](https://worrydream.com/ExplorableExplanations/), [Lean Blueprint](https://github.com/PatrickMassot/leanblueprint).

**7 — Six weeks, E.** Pick one adult-learning topic that already has an external task, such as thermal energy versus power and uncertainty. Build six linked module-pages and two teacher-authored routes. Recruit twelve to twenty consenting adult learners; compare a fixed explanation with direct manipulation of the same model, then a small unfamiliar transfer task several days later. Show a teacher updating a shared concept while another teacher's accepted route stays reproducible. The sample is a usability pilot, not a causal efficacy claim.

**8 — Kill, I/E.** Interactive manipulation can create engagement without understanding, and maintaining dependencies can burden teachers. A two-day baseline compares an existing PhET/Observable lesson with a generated custom lesson on a held-out task. If neither the learning task nor a teacher's adaptation needs cross-page computation, keep the educational artifact and drop the platform. Do not count clicks or time on page as educational success.

**9 — Fit, I.** Strong prototyping fit and a comparatively accessible public audience. Pedagogy, accessibility and recruitment are central missing skills; partner with one educator rather than declare a new school. It offers a plausible alternative if scientific model owners do not materialize.

### 4. Counterfactual Towns — communities inhabit and amend a model of their shared world

**1 — Seed, Q/I.** Omar explicitly proposes an up-to-date planetary view and hypothetical policy interventions at [64:00](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=fB_lklm7xgU&t=3840s). Lütke's physical making example and “fulfill our wishes” at [12:00](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=p7AhQO3Y-GY&t=720s) reinforce outcomes beyond software. A federation of resident-owned local worlds is the adjacent inference.

**2 — Full scale, I.** Residents, researchers and public authorities build a live computational model of their neighborhood. They move a proposed shade canopy, change opening hours or adjust a bus assumption, and trace consequences through independently maintained energy, access, noise and budget models. Competing models and objectives remain visible. Accepted plans retain the participants' actual permissions and cannot silently become construction orders. Federated towns gradually compose into regional models with real observation histories. This is a public medium for negotiation over counterfactuals, not a game skin for a dashboard.

**3 — Previously infeasible / now plausible, I/E.** Professional digital twins and participatory planning exist. A locally adaptable, inspectable computational world for every small community demanded a custom integration team. Agents can build interfaces, adapters and reduced models at many sites in parallel. Cheap construction may make a broad federation possible; it cannot supply causal validity or political legitimacy. The strongest version fails if those remaining costs do not scale.

**4 — One-shot test, I.** The asset is the federation's current local observations, calibration, resident participation, permission commitments and resolved disagreements. A model can create a plausible neighborhood simulator in one prompt. It cannot recreate a legitimate local planning process or data that property owners have not chosen to disclose.

**5 — Physics/economics/data/law, I/E.** A town model with 10,000 entities × 100 numeric state fields × 8 bytes is 8 MB for raw fields; history, indexes and runtime overhead make it much larger. Partition by place/model and use selective views. A rough 20-second compute job for 1,000 scenarios is 5.6 CPU-hours, but a physically credible fluid/traffic simulation can be orders of magnitude more expensive. Fast controls must operate on explicitly validated reduced models or cached ensembles. Infrastructure is plausible; a solo builder cannot deliver a live exact Earth simulator. Destination Earth's funded 2026–2028 phase demonstrates both the ambition and the institutional/compute scale. [ECMWF phase-three announcement](https://destine.ecmwf.int/news/phase-three-of-destination-earth-confirmed/). OSM requires attribution and ODbL compliance; public map data does not grant permission to enter buildings, observe residents, or enact a plan. [OSM license](https://www.openstreetmap.org/copyright).

**6 — Closest attempts, F/I.** Destination Earth is closest to the scientific twin; Dynamicland to communal manipulation; Croquet and Roblox to live shared worlds. None is stopped. The residual hypothesis is an open, locally governed federation whose editable model claims remain empirically accountable. It competes with existing planning practice and professional GIS as much as with software products. [Destination Earth](https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/policies/destination-earth), [Dynamicland communal science lab](https://dynamicland.org/2024/The_communal_science_lab.pdf).

**7 — Six weeks, E.** One shared workshop or community garden, represented by a two-dimensional scene rather than a 3D engine. Three custodians own separate shade, energy and access models; one small physical observation series grounds a single outcome. Residents compare two changes, directly alter assumptions, and record an actual disagreement. A broken agent edit leaves accepted models usable. The artifact is a negotiated scenario tied to observations and permission, with exportable computation underneath. No actuators or automated public decisions.

**8 — Kill, I/E.** A beautiful world can make a bad model more persuasive. In the cheap test, show the same measured scenarios as a spreadsheet and as a shared scene to five participants. Ask them to identify missing evidence and explain which conclusion would change their choice. If the scene increases confidence without improving those answers, reject this interface direction. If no local participant owns the underlying decision, stop regardless of technical success.

**9 — Fit, I.** Excellent systems fascination, poor default fit for a part-time individual because geography, domain science and legitimate community process dominate. It is the most crazy-but-not-impossible direction, and a possible destination for a successful Living Model Commons rather than the initial scope.

**E — Intended end-to-end demonstration:** a room model and a second independently owned room model share an outdoor-temperature conversion and energy calculation. A non-programmer changes an insulation assumption in a local scenario. An agent proposes a faulty revision of the shared computation. The draft shows a hole/type conflict while accepted consumers keep running. A type-correct but invalid physical assumption then fails against held-out observations. Its owner either rejects it or publishes a corrected version; the other owner chooses whether to adopt it. The viewer can inspect the exact calculation, observations, revision, and uncertainty, and export the scenario.

**I/E — What makes that worth doing:** cross-owner reuse, partial execution, deliberate publication, direct manipulation and empirical accountability occur in one actual workflow. This is a distinctive experimental target, not a universal novelty claim. If existing Observable/Unison/Fairground tooling supplies it with a small extension, use that tooling and spend the saved time on participants and observations.

**E — Continue only if:** both model owners voluntarily maintain/reuse a dependency; at least one intended user completes a real scenario task without reading source; incomplete work remains local and clearly labeled; and coordination effort is no worse than the baseline with a credible route to improvement. Cost and latency targets should be reported as measurements, not inferred from pricing tables. Six weeks cannot establish a network effect, scientific generality, or long-term retention.

**I — Cheapest decisive failure:** no two owners want a shared live dependency. This is more damaging than a slow interpreter, because faster code cannot create a reason to coordinate. Preserve the useful thermal lesson or upstream patch and abandon the general commons claim.

## Five-line TL;DR

1. **I:** Pick a Living Model Commons: a community-maintained network of measured, reusable live models, starting with two thermal-model owners.
2. **F:** Hazel/Fairground is the closest full-vision attempt; Observable, Unison, Val Town and Wikifunctions already occupy much of the mechanism.
3. **I:** Agents reduce the cost of libraries, adapters and views; observations, accepted meaning, authority and sustained participation remain the value.
4. **I/E:** Workers/DOs can support the bounded demo; hibernation, explicit revision/input identities, batched recomputation and Dynamic Worker creation charges determine feasibility.
5. **I:** Counterfactual Towns is the wildest plausible destination; kill the initial commons if real owners prefer independent notebooks and files.
