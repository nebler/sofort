## 1. A network that fulfils physical wishes — start with objects that can be repaired

### 1. Seed

**Q — Tobi Lütke:** “it's just computers to fulfill our wishes” ([p3-65, 12:00](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=p7AhQO3Y-GY&t=720s)). His examples span photographing a house, collecting everything for a Halloween installation, and his child's designed/printed goods entering a real store. **Q — Guillermo Rauch:** “just-in-time hardware, just-in-time shopping” ([10:00](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=p7AhQO3Y-GY&t=600s)). These are two participants in one conversation, not two independent customer cohorts.

**Q — Independent supporting signal:** Scott Leaman describes equipment that cannot be installed because actual placement differs from the plans ([p3-26, 07:00–08:00](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=4FlLqv21SxE&t=420s)). **I:** a delivered item is useful only when it fits its actual receiving environment.

### 2. Vision at full scale

**I — Field-changing destination:** commissioning a physical outcome becomes as accessible as asking for software. “Repair this latch,” “make this cabinet fit this alcove,” and eventually “build this accessible workspace” cause a network to measure the receiving world, design or select components, reserve production, arrange assembly and delivery, and confirm the resulting function. Existing merchants and fabricators do the manufacturing; local people can supply the unavoidable physical work.

**I — The cumulative public asset:** each rights-cleared object family acquires actual revision identities, measured mating surfaces, suitable processes/materials, unsuccessful substitutions, installation instructions and use histories. A later project can reuse that knowledge across suppliers. Million-scale coverage would change which low-volume repairs and adaptations are economically possible. A directory of printable files alone does not achieve that.

### 3. Why previously infeasible; why now

**I:** bespoke fabrication was already possible for wealthy customers and skilled makers. That is not the claimed breakthrough. What was economically infeasible was general, affordable fulfilment across a long tail where every variant demanded an engineer's measurement interpretation, CAD work, procurement and troubleshooting.

**E:** 100,000 part families × 2 engineering hours × €60/hour = €12 million of geometry preparation alone, before physical qualification. Reducing that preparation to ten minutes would make it €1 million; the assumed improvement is a hypothesis, not a measured model result. AI CAD tools and programmable procurement now make that component plausible to test. The engineering saving does not erase measurement, handling or liability costs. If those remaining costs dominate every viable use case, the universal consumer version fails.

### 4. One-shot test

**I:** a better model can reproduce all the planner, CAD and supplier-adapter code. It cannot infer a hidden mating surface, recover an unrecorded product revision, supply a missing material, collect a failed part from a household, or cause an independent workshop to honour a delivery commitment. The non-code value is **new physical measurements, reusable successful/failed installations, participant trust and committed fulfilment capacity**. Better models improve the network's economics; a vendor combining better models with its own comparable network is serious competition.

### 5. Physics and economics

**E — A modest polymer part, not an imaginary free object:**

| Cost term | Assumption | Amount |
|---|---|---:|
| Material | 40 g × €25/kg | €1.00 |
| Electricity | 0.3 kWh × €0.35/kWh | €0.11 |
| Machine depreciation | 3 hours × (€500 / 2,000 productive hours) | €0.75 |
| Handling/inspection | 15 minutes × €30/hour | €7.50 |
| Production subtotal | Before rejects | €9.36 |
| Accepted production | 80% yield; repeated production costs divided by 0.8 | €11.69 |
| Packaging and one shipment | €2 + €6 | €8.00 |
| Delivered repeat part | Excludes design, tax, insurance and platform overhead | **€19.69** |
| One-off reverse engineering | One additional hour × €30/hour | **€49.69 total** |

**I:** available €3 replacement clips should usually remain €3 purchased clips. The promising cases prevent disposal of a substantially more valuable object, need an unavailable variant, or permit local collection/batching. A network that subsidises hours of unpaid operator labour is not economically validated.

**E — Fit budget:** ±0.1 mm uncertainty on a pin and ±0.1 mm on its receiving hole makes nominal 0.3 mm diametral clearance range from 0.1 to 0.5 mm. A required 0.2–0.4 mm fit therefore cannot be guaranteed by those measurements. More reasoning cannot reduce that physical uncertainty; request a better measurement, print a fit ladder, or choose a more tolerant joint. Surface photographs also do not reveal polymer grade, fatigue history or internal damage.

**F/I — Capture and fabrication are separate budgets:** [OpenScan Mini](https://openscan.eu/pages/openscan-mini) advertises kits from €232 and approximately 9 cm scan volume. Its accuracy claims depend on preparation and operator skill; validate against known dimensions. **E:** borrow a printer or budget €250–€600 for a basic FDM setup, €30–€80 for dimensional/force tools, and €100–€250 for consumables, failed runs and local shipments. Allow another €50–€150 for paid CAD/model use and processing. These are planning allowances, not live quotes. CAD/mesh observations are small enough for ordinary storage; GPU conversion can be outsourced. No model training is required.

**F/I — Law:** the EU repair directive protects compatible/3D-printed spare parts in covered circumstances while retaining safety and IP conditions; it is not a blanket right to copy every object. Member-state implementation and the product category matter. The newer Product Liability Directive explicitly includes digital manufacturing files; its regime applies to products placed on the market or put into service after December 9, 2026. Its non-commercial open-source software exclusion should not be casually extended to all CAD files or supplied physical parts. Use rights-cleared designs and restrict the pilot to non-critical household mechanisms. [Repair rules](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/PDF/?uri=CELEX:02024L1799-20260731), [liability text, Articles 2/4 and recital 16](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/?uri=CELEX:32024L2853).

### 6. Closest attempts and where they stop

**F — Very close:** [Replique](https://replique.io/en/spare-parts-and-urgent-demand-on-demand/) already offers reverse engineering, qualified local production, digital inventory and logistics. Its MAN example reports a replacement bronze cooling-water manifold delivered in six weeks. Its [supplier network](https://replique.io/en/our-supplier-network/) uses onboarding, test production and recurring quality checks. This is evidence that fulfilment can work, and that the qualification work is part of the service—not a missing API someone overlooked.

**F:** [Spare Parts 3D/DigiPART/Theia](https://spare-parts-3d.com/faq/) covers identification, digital passports, additive-manufacturing suitability and drawing-to-CAD with engineering validation. Its FAQ estimates 6–10 weeks for an inventory pilot. [3YOURMIND's August 2026 release](https://www.3yourmind.com/news/use-ai-to-support-part-assessment) adds AI-assisted part assessment, production-queue and material-compatibility features. Zoo and Backflip occupy the generation/reconstruction stage above; Backflip was backed by a [$30 million round](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20241218887310/en/Backflip-Releases-AI-Model-That-Turns-Text-Into-Physical-Reality-Backed-By-30M-from-NEA-and-Andreessen-Horowitz) and is actively shipping in 2026.

**I — Residual, not established market whitespace:** a rights-cleared, public body of consumer-scale installed outcomes that transfers between independent workshops and product revisions. Existing industrial providers can extend downward and model companies can integrate them. The research must show that accumulated field observations materially improve subsequent fulfilment; “we also have an agent” contributes nothing.

### 7. Six weeks for one person

**E — Scope:** 72–90 builder hours, two cooperating printer operators, six consenting owners, eight real specimens across two or three families: storage-box latches, unpowered drawer knobs or furniture trim clips. Keep heat, mains electricity, protective equipment, vehicles and critical load paths outside the chosen family.

- **Weeks 1–2:** collect broken specimens and their mating objects; establish ownership/licensing and actual replacement alternatives. Record measured interfaces, acceptable function and owner effort. Complete two repairs manually with existing CAD/fabrication tools. That becomes the cost and success baseline.
- **Weeks 3–4:** implement the smallest open workflow connecting measurements, parametric source, selected material/process, operator commitment and observed installation. Use existing CAD and slicers. Manufacture on two different printers; retain failures and corrections. A human performs assembly, with their minutes recorded.
- **Weeks 5–6:** hold out two specimens or genuine size/revision variants. Give a second operator the reusable family record, not the original builder's private explanation. Reproduce a repair, fulfil one changed-size adaptation, and collect a week of ordinary use. Compare with the same model/tools given only generic instructions and initial photos.

**I — Demoable new result sought:** one previously undocumented physical wish reaches confirmed use, its discovered fit constraint repairs a different owner's related object on another printer, and the whole reproducible record is public. Show the object working beside the failed candidate and measured correction. This points toward physical invention as a shared capability; a generated order dashboard does not.

### 8. What would kill it; cheapest experiment

**I — Strongest objection:** sparse consumer demand plus handling dominates the saving; the “commons” is an unpaid reverse-engineering consultancy. **E — 48-hour test:** take two real broken objects to an existing repair/print operator, use current CAD agents, and measure total attention, fit iterations and delivered price. Then try one related held-out variant with the accumulated measurements. Stop the standalone platform if ordinary existing services achieve the same result cheaply, no owner values completion, or no reusable relationship between cases emerges.

**E — Continuation threshold, not claimed outcome:** target at least six of eight completed useful repairs, repeat-family operator time below 30 minutes, and a credible path below €25 per locally fulfilled repeat part. Eight specimens cannot establish reliability or demand at population scale; they can reject this particular starting family.

### 9. Builder fit

**I:** strong for physical identity/versioning, failure recovery, geometry pipelines, local/cloud coordination and publishing reusable data; weak for materials qualification and running a workshop. Recruit mechanical help around a bounded part family. Reuse CAD engines rather than writing one. The builder's durable contribution is connecting physical discovery to repeatable fulfilment, with the ambition residing in the expanding network.

## 2. An editable, continuously observed city

### 1. Seed

**Q — Scott Leaman:** “keeping a digital twin updated” and “how it looked last week” ([p3-26, 25:00](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=4FlLqv21SxE&t=1500s)). His SNCF account reports scanning every 6–8 weeks and about 10 GB/km ([08:00](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=4FlLqv21SxE&t=480s)). Those numbers remain attributed operational reports.

**Q — Independent identity/authority problem:** Christian Johansen describes premises that “don't even have a street address” and distinguishes whether to “trust an inspector” ([p3-28, 35:00–37:00](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=OkKmP9oATjk&t=2100s)). **I:** continuity must distinguish a physical site, its occupant and an authorized assertion; a location string or new scan cannot stand in for all three.

### 2. Vision at full scale

**I — Field infrastructure with a field-changing endpoint:** every participating building/street has an accumulating, inspectable physical history. People and agents can propose an equipment placement, alter a room or street design, and request the additional observations needed to decide whether it works. After authorized work occurs, new capture resolves what actually changed. Proposed edits never silently become observations.

**I:** use Gaussian splats for appearance, measured geometry for dimensional questions, and persistent real-place/asset identities for history. The value of “editing a city” is making plans against current reality and then checking the actual outcome. At scale the network connects property owners, utilities, accessibility groups and maintenance crews across ownership boundaries. It needs governed participation, not merely attractive 3D rendering.

### 3. Why previously infeasible; why now

**I:** individual surveyed twins have existed for years. The newly plausible step is inexpensive repeated interpretation and reconciliation of heterogeneous capture across millions of changing entities. Manual survey/BIM authoring could not economically sustain that granularity and cadence for ordinary places. Commodity cameras, splat pipelines and stronger visual agents reduce authoring labour; they do not supply access, stable registration or actual observations of hidden surfaces.

**F:** [3D Gaussian Splatting](https://repo-sam.inria.fr/fungraph/3d-gaussian-splatting/) demonstrated real-time novel-view rendering in 2023. That is a rendering result, not a guarantee of engineering-grade geometry. **I:** reject this idea if its only “newly possible” component is opening a splat in a browser.

### 4. One-shot test

**I:** a stronger model can build the viewer, segmenter and import tools. It cannot photograph an unvisited room, obtain a utility's data permission, reconstruct last month's unrecorded obstruction, or decide which owner has authority to accept a site change. **Non-code value: recurring physical access, contributed observations, adjudicated identity/continuity, and accumulated correspondence between plans and actual work.** Better models do not reveal an occluded pipe's condition.

### 5. Physics and economics

**E — Storage is manageable before capture is:** using the transcript's 10 GB/km as a scenario, 1,000 km is 10 TB per pass. Every eight weeks gives approximately 6.5 passes/year, or 65 TB/year. Two complete retained copies of that year's captures occupy 130 TB. At [R2 Standard's $0.015/GB-month](https://developers.cloudflare.com/r2/pricing/), the year-end storage run rate is roughly **$1,950/month**, before operations, derived assets and further years. Compression/deltas may reduce it, but that saving is unmeasured here.

**E:** if urban capture/cleanup requires 0.5 paid hours/km at €30/hour, 6,500 km/year costs **€97,500/year** in direct field labour. Existing vehicle routes can lower marginal collection cost; permission and useful coverage still need solving. A sensor network that asks volunteers to walk every street for free has not demonstrated its economics.

**F/E — Metric accuracy:** [Matterport Pro3](https://go.matterport.com/pro3) specifies ±20 mm at 10 m. Two captures plus registration can exhaust a centimetre-scale change budget even with professional hardware. **E:** assume ±20 mm error in each capture and ±20 mm registration error: a conservative difference budget is ±60 mm, not ±20 mm. A visually convincing splat is insufficient for a 20 mm clearance decision. Start with changes larger than 150 mm and obtain separate direct measurements for tighter fits.

**E — Compute:** 3 million Gaussians × 59 float32 values ≈ **708 MB** for a common uncompressed representation; training needs additional gradients/optimizer state and images. Tile the scene; do not promise a whole city in one phone's memory. A prototype with twelve 400-photo epochs at 4 MB/photo has **19.2 GB** of source images. Thirty GPU-hours at [Runpod's listed A40 rate of $0.59/hour](https://www.runpod.io/pricing) cost **$17.70** for the GPU rental alone. Thirty hours at an assumed 300 W is **9 kWh GPU energy**, excluding host, preprocessing and facility overhead. Runtime/memory adequacy must be measured; the hourly price is not evidence a scene fits.

**F/I — Data and law:** Google Maps 3D data is available for visualization, but its [Map Tiles policies](https://developers.google.com/maps/documentation/tile/policies) prohibit using that API for machine interpretation, object identification or extracting a reusable geodata corpus, and restrict storage. Build from legitimately reusable observations and licensed sources. The [EDPB's April 2026 video guidance summary](https://www.edpb.europa.eu/system/files/2026-04/summary_edpb_guidelines_201903_video_devices_en.pdf) identifies lawful basis, necessity, transparency and disclosure obligations. Owner permission to enter a courtyard does not by itself authorize publishing identifiable neighbours. Start with controlled ground capture, minimize incidental people/interiors, and make contribution/deletion rules workable.

### 6. Closest attempts and where they stop

**F — Extremely occupied:** [Niantic Capture](https://www.nianticspatial.com/products/capture) includes Scaniverse, bring-your-own-data integration, collaborative capture and trained field acquisition; it advertises centimetre-level enterprise capture. [Matterport](https://buy.matterport.com/www-cameras?lang=en) sells capture, measurements, tags, exports and AI defurnishing. [Google 3D Maps](https://mapsplatform.google.com/maps-products/3d-maps/) advertises coverage of 2,500 cities in 50 countries. [Bentley Reality Management](https://developer.bentley.com/apis/reality-management/) explicitly covers repeated surveys; [Reality Analysis](https://developer.bentley.com/apis/reality-analysis/rda-jobtypes/) already detects changes between point clouds or meshes. Change detection is therefore not the gap.

**F — Funded 2026 competition:** World Labs launched its [World API on January 21](https://www.worldlabs.ai/blog/announcing-the-world-api), raised [$1 billion in February](https://www.worldlabs.ai/blog/funding-2026), and on September 28 AMD announced an [$8.2 billion acquisition agreement](https://ir.amd.com/news-events/press-releases/detail/1299/amd-to-acquire-world-labs-to-advance-the-future-of-ai-compute), expected to close by year-end. Do not call the acquisition completed. Generated world models are a different source of information from capture, but compete directly for scene editing, representation and developer attention.

**F/I — Closest empirical attempt:** the [SNCF/Sopra Steria GTC session](https://www.nvidia.com/en-us/on-demand/session/gtc25-S71822/) describes shared point clouds/BIM/business data for planning and as-planned/as-built reconciliation. It already accomplishes much of this inside an institution. [Overture GERS](https://docs.overturemaps.org/gers/stability/) supplies persistent IDs but currently does not publish old-to-new successor links for split/merge changes. The possible opening is a recurring cross-owner observation and accepted-change network with defensible temporal identity—not a new map, renderer or claim that enterprise twins have failed.

### 7. Six weeks for one person

**E:** obtain one accessible courtyard/workshop complex, one responsible site operator and a second independent observer. Plan 60–90 builder hours, existing phone capture, €100–€300 for control targets/direct measurements or borrowed equipment, and €30–€100 processing/storage allowance.

- **Weeks 1–2:** directly measure 20 stable reference distances and capture an ordinary unchanged site under different light. Establish registration error. Separate renderable appearance from trustworthy measurements.
- **Weeks 3–4:** collect twelve small capture epochs, including naturally occurring or operator-approved moves/obstructions. Reuse an existing renderer and reconstruction pipeline. Preserve object/site identities, capture times, uncertainty and missing coverage.
- **Weeks 5–6:** let the second observer contribute a conflicting/staler view. Ask whether a real piece of equipment can be installed. Produce a proposed placement, request a targeted recapture, let the site operator decide, and record the observed physical installation/removal outcome.

**I — New result sought:** a publicly reproducible history in which two contributors' observations change a real placement decision; the later capture confirms what was done while stale/hidden regions remain explicitly unknown. The distinctive contribution is a shared, usable world record across contributors, tested against an actual decision. If Bentley or the site's existing twin already does all of it, publish the capture/identity contribution there.

### 8. What would kill it; cheapest experiment

**I:** maintaining freshness/metric trust may cost more than sending someone to the site; owners may have no reason to share. **E — 48-hour test:** two observers, four same-site captures, 20 reference distances, and one change exceeding 150 mm. Compare against a simple photo log and an existing point-cloud/change tool. Stop the infrastructure pitch if registration produces more false changes than useful findings, the operator needs no repeat decisions, or the ordinary log answers the same question more cheaply. Lack of a second real contributor kills the cross-owner thesis even if rendering works.

### 9. Builder fit

**I:** excellent for temporal databases, spatial streaming, content-addressed data, synchronization and browser tools. Reuse reconstruction research. The hard missing collaborators are a site owner and surveying expertise. A software-only fake city would hide the constraint that makes this idea valuable.

## 3. Make every participating device an agent-programmable physical capability

### 1. Seed

**Q — Chris Lattner:** “how do we set up the world to to be capable of programming all these devices” ([p3-38, 1:05:00](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ah9Tfebk7JA&t=3900s)). His context is fragmented CPU/GPU/neural/embedded hardware programming; extending it to sensing and actuation is **I**, not a quotation about home automation.

**Q — Brendan O'Brien:** “connect any two devices on the planet” ([p3-57, 00:00–01:00](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=QLr-0RgMNzo&t=0s)); his 16:00–18:00 account describes the empirical work needed for dependable connectivity. **Q — Travis Perkins:** Tailscale began with access to “my dog camera” ([p3-59, 01:00](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Cb5Sna3Y_sw&t=60s)). **I:** networking is a supplied primitive; the new question is what physical result an endpoint can reliably perform.

### 2. Vision at full scale

**I — Field infrastructure:** an owner can compose the devices around them into a new physical instrument or useful behaviour without becoming each manufacturer's integrator. A camera, fan, motorized stage and sensor can form an inspection rig; another owner can adapt that rig to different hardware. Devices expose measured capabilities—range, latency, uncertainty, calibration conditions, local operating limits—and can execute bounded programs locally.

**I:** the full destination is a programmable physical environment with reusable knowledge from deployments. The unit of value is successful capability composition across previously unintegrated hardware. An agent calling a light-switch API is already ordinary automation.

### 3. Why previously infeasible; why now

**I:** custom integration has always been possible, and off-the-shelf home automation already handles much of it. The ambitious remaining case is commissioning long-tail devices and physical compositions cheaply enough that small, personal instruments become worth building. Agents can translate documentation, generate adapters and propose calibration experiments; low-cost MCUs can execute the resulting local program.

**I — Important qualification:** this is the least secure “why now” of the shortlist. If adapter generation is the entire improvement, it becomes a feature of ESPHome, Home Assistant or Arduino and fails the ambition test. It survives only as an adopted network of newly measured device capabilities and useful deployed compositions.

### 4. One-shot test

**I:** protocols, firmware generators and policy engines are reproducible code. Non-code value is **device access, per-instance calibration, actual failure/energy observations, owner permission, and cross-vendor participation**. A stronger model still does not know whether a particular motor is stalled or a reused sensor is biased. If standard device descriptions already predict all relevant behaviour, there is no residual to build.

### 5. Physics and economics

**F/E:** a [Seeed XIAO ESP32-C3 board](https://www.seeedstudio.com/Seeed-XIAO-ESP32C3-p-5431.html) is listed at **$4.99**. A complete usable node needs sensor/driver, power, wiring and enclosure: **E €20–€50/node**, with better air-quality/force/optical sensors potentially costing more. Eight nodes plus a reused/€50–€100 gateway and references are roughly **E €300–€600**, not eight times the bare-MCU price.

**E — Power:** a 2 Ah, 3.7 V cell with 80% usable energy supplies 5.92 Wh; a complete continuously active 150 mW node lasts **about 39.5 hours**. [Espressif](https://documentation.espressif.com/esp32-c3_datasheet_en.html) specifies approximately 5 µA for one chip deep-sleep mode, but that excludes whole-board sensors, regulators, radio wake cycles and actuators. Deep-sleep marketing is not a battery-life estimate for an always-reachable device.

**E — Scale and control:** 1,000 devices × one 128-byte observation/second = **11.1 GB/day**, before protocol overhead. A 20 Hz local loop has a 50 ms tick budget; an internet round trip cannot be assumed to meet it. Put control and watchdogs beside the actuator. Cloud models can commission/revise the program, not close every physical feedback loop. Gateway inference on meaningful events also avoids turning every heartbeat into a paid model call.

**F/I — Networking/data:** [Iroh](https://docs.iroh.computer/) already provides encrypted direct QUIC connections with relay fallback and documents some microcontroller work. Do not assume it fits a selected ESP32 without measuring. [Tailscale subnet routers](https://tailscale.com/docs/features/subnet-routers) can reach local devices that do not run Tailscale themselves. Use a Linux gateway where appropriate. Reachability neither gives hard real-time guarantees nor establishes permission to control the actuator.

**F/I — Law:** commercial connected products can incur radio/product requirements and firmware lifecycle responsibilities. The [CRA reporting regime](https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/policies/cra-reporting) began for manufacturers on September 11, 2026; most CRA requirements apply from December 11, 2027, and the Commission distinguishes open-source stewards' timing. Product role and commercial activity matter. Keep the initial physical task bounded and reversible; a homebuilt controller's software proof cannot certify unknown wiring or a consumer appliance's modified safety case.

### 6. Closest attempts and where they stop

**F:** [Home Assistant's MCP server](https://www.home-assistant.io/integrations/mcp_server/) already connects AI agents to device state/control. [ESPHome](https://esphome.io/components/api/) supplies native APIs and locally programmed automations. [Arduino App Lab's March 2026 Edge Impulse integration](https://blog.arduino.cc/2026/03/04/train-and-deploy-your-own-ai-models-in-arduino-app-lab-now-fully-integrated-with-edge-impulse/) already supports training/deploying custom device models; [Arduino is part of Qualcomm](https://www.arduino.cc/qualcomm/). [Google's May 2026 Home offering](https://developers.googleblog.com/empowering-service-providers-and-hardware-partners-with-gemini-for-home/) explicitly offers proactive AI services over existing devices. These are direct competitors, not just libraries.

**F:** [Matter 1.6, June 2026](https://csa-iot.org/newsroom/matter-1-6-enables-more-intuitive-setup-multi-ecosystem-experiences-and-context-driven-control/), improves setup, shared controller management and context-driven control; the [specification catalogue](https://csa-iot.org/developer-resource/specifications-download-request/) also lists 1.6.1. Standard publication does not establish installed-product support. [W3C Thing Description](https://www.w3.org/TR/wot-thing-description11/) already has actions, properties, events, units, security and versions. [LabThings-FastAPI](https://labthings-fastapi.readthedocs.io/en/latest/) exposes laboratory hardware with typed actions, cancellation, concurrency and Thing Descriptions. [Code as Policies](https://code-as-policies.github.io/) demonstrated generated code for embodied control in 2022.

**I:** the generic device language/API/agent-control proposition is taken. The possible contribution is a field-tested physical capability network that calibrates real instances, shows what composes, and learns from failed deployment. Check whether LabThings/ESPHome communities want that evidence before building another standard.

### 7. Six weeks for one person

**E:** choose a low-voltage tabletop inspection task: photograph small objects from repeatable viewpoints using an MCU-driven stage, an off-the-shelf camera and a measured position reference. Use two versions of stage/sensor hardware, including one owned by a participant.

- **Weeks 1–2:** integrate using existing Arduino/ESPHome/LabThings components; measure positioning error, sensor bias, power and command latency. Establish the manually written automation baseline.
- **Weeks 3–4:** let an agent commission a second device variant through explicit calibration routines and generate a bounded local controller. Reuse W3C descriptions. Store measured uncertainty, conditions and firmware identity alongside the capability.
- **Weeks 5–6:** move the requested inspection behaviour to the other hardware. Disconnect the network, introduce a reversible stall, swap a sensor and repeat the task. The controller should retain declared limits or require recalibration, with behaviour checked by an external position/reference measurement.

**I — New result sought:** the same useful physical inspection task is commissioned on two heterogeneous rigs using accumulated measured capabilities, and its offline behaviour remains observable and bounded. Publish new hardware observations and useful deployment packages. A generic firmware/MCP demo would not satisfy the brief.

### 8. What would kill it; cheapest experiment

**I:** ordinary ESPHome/HA/Thing Descriptions may already make the task trivial, while calibration does not transfer. **E — 48-hour test:** move one existing automation between two real hardware variants using current tools. Add calibration only where a measured physical discrepancy matters. Kill the separate platform if no composition fails, the relevant correction is a one-line existing setting, or every new instance requires a specialist for hours. A meaningful contribution could then be the measured cases upstream.

### 9. Builder fit

**I:** strong systems fit for local execution, interrupted actions, capability boundaries, networking and heterogeneous hosts. Avoid a new MCU OS, language or cloud agent host. Mechanical/electrical validation and participant recruitment are the limits; generated adapters do not make them disappear.

## 4. A fabrication network that expands its own manufacturing capabilities

### 1. Seed

**Q — Lütke:** “making hardware essentially software” ([p3-65, 11:00](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=p7AhQO3Y-GY&t=660s)). **Q — Lattner's device-programmability question** ([p3-38, 1:05:00](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ah9Tfebk7JA&t=3900s)) supplies an independent systems ambition. **I:** combine the two: a network should be able to acquire a missing physical capability by manufacturing its tooling, instrumenting it, and teaching another participant to reproduce the process.

### 2. Vision at full scale

**I — Most crazy-but-not-impossible pick:** a requested product causes the network to assemble a manufacturing route, discover a missing fixture/process, make and qualify the tooling, and add that capability to other workshops. It begins with printers making jigs, scanners and assembly aids; it could grow into distributed fabrication cells that manufacture some components of additional cells.

**I — Field-changing endpoint:** each completed project expands the set of physical things the network can make. Designs travel globally; capacity and materials remain local where sensible. A production package contains geometry, tools, BOM, process conditions, calibration, inspection and assembly knowledge with measured applicability. The endpoint is a growing industrial capability commons, rather than a marketplace of nominally idle printers.

### 3. Why previously infeasible; why now

**I:** self-copying machines and open fabrication plans are old ideas. The potentially new capability is inexpensive adaptation of a multi-step production route across heterogeneous workshops, driven by observations and automatically maintained process/tooling dependencies. Human authors could not economically prepare and support every combination of machine, material, fixture and local substitute.

**I:** stronger agents may now absorb much of that integration/variant-engineering labour. The claim is conditional: if the remaining work is primarily manual assembly and material qualification, code becoming cheap will not unlock it. Demonstrate a previously impractical transfer of capability before treating this as a new industrial substrate.

### 4. One-shot test

**I:** a model can generate a production graph or print-farm scheduler. It cannot create **productive machines, available stock, actual calibration, a validated process, operator skills or a workshop willing to execute**. The accumulated asset is a participant network that has actually manufactured and transferred capabilities. Full recursive material self-sufficiency is not assumed.

### 5. Physics and economics

**E — Example expansion module:** a printer makes 400 g of scanner/inspection-rig structure, while purchased camera/electronics/motors/bearings/power cost €120. Plastic at €25/kg costs €10; six print hours depreciated at €0.25/hour cost €1.50; 0.6 kWh at €0.35 costs €0.21; two assembly hours at €30 cost €60. Total **€191.71**, before shipping, rejects, calibration references and design. Purchased parts represent **92% of the €130 component spend**. Counting printed components is a misleading measure of replication; report the externally sourced cost and function explicitly.

**E:** basic FDM capacity can be borrowed or acquired for hundreds of euros; a small equipped fabrication bench with drilling, soldering, extraction and additional processes plausibly costs **€2,000–€10,000**, with large uncertainty. Precision CNC, metal additive manufacturing and regulated production can exceed that range greatly. Materials, motors, bearings, power supplies and camera chips remain dependencies. A six-week two-workshop transfer pilot should use borrowed base machines and budget **€400–€1,000** for two expansion modules, references, failures and transport, plus paid model/processing costs.

**F — Semiconductor boundary:** TSMC's Arizona programme reached a planned **over $65 billion for three advanced fabs**, and its [March 2025 expansion announcement](https://prpoc.cld.tsmc.com/english/news/3210) describes **$165 billion total intended investment** including more fabs, packaging and R&D. Those totals are programme investments, not a per-fab quotation. **I:** a workbench network does not acquire semiconductor independence because it can print enclosures. Preserve explicit external dependencies rather than hiding them behind “self-replication.”

**E/I — Time, compute, data and law:** printer build time is hours; purchased components may add days or weeks, and calibration/assembly occupies people. A graph search runs on an ordinary host; paid CAD/vision/model calls can have a €50–€150 pilot budget, without training a model. Store manufacturing histories and failures; a few modules will not generate substantial storage cost. The hard data is process validity, not file size. Transfer only designs whose licence permits the intended fabrication. Commercially supplied machinery and digital manufacturing files bring product/liability obligations; the legal constraints in ideas 1 and 3 apply. Local workshops remain responsible for physical setup and appropriate machine operation.

### 6. Closest attempts and where they stop

**F:** [RepRap Ltd](https://reprapltd.com/about/) describes printers making the plastic parts for another printer and researches robotics/control. [Open Source Ecology's GVCS](https://wiki.opensourceecology.org/wiki/GVCS) explicitly pursues 50 open machines and economically feasible production; its [development account](https://staging.opensourceecology.org/development/) asks how modular documentation enables time-bounded collaborative builds. [Precious Plastic](https://www.preciousplastic.com/our-solutions/shipping-container) reports over 1,000 registered workspaces and distributed machine/workspace replication. [OpenScan](https://openscan.eu/pages/openscan-mini) and [OpenFlexure](https://openflexure.org/projects/microscope/) already demonstrate printable instruments with purchased electronics/optics. These projects establish that partial capability reproduction works; do not claim to invent it.

**F — Direct 2026 commercial competition:** [Vention's January 2026 $110 million financing](https://vention.com/blogs/industry/vention-funding-announcement-2026-1048) funds physical AI and a unified automation/hardware stack. Its [September MachineAgent announcement](https://vention.com/blogs/industrial-automation-journey/introducing-machineagent-1126) describes agentic design/program/deploy workflows with CLI/MCP, requiring its account, supported controller/components and cloud connection. It is actively attacking the integration work. Replique and 3YOURMIND cover distributed qualified production; Formlabs and Bambu cover printer operation.

**F/I — Historical caution:** [Protolabs' 2021 acquisition announcement](https://www.protolabs.com/en-gb/about-us/press/protolabs-reaches-agreement-to-acquire-3d-hubs-creates-the-world-s-broadest-digital-manufacturing-offer-for-custom-parts/) describes a network of about 240 premium manufacturing partners. A [2020 research paper](https://publications.aston.ac.uk/id/eprint/39726/1/Beltagui_et_al_forthcoming_The_role_of_3DP_and_open_design_on_socially_sustainable_SCI.pdf) documents 3D Hubs' move from a peer marketplace to commercial providers. That does not prove why every consumer fabrication network fails, but it defeats the assumption that connecting hobby printers automatically creates viable production.

**I — Residual:** a public, empirically exercised graph of capability expansion and transfer across independently owned, heterogeneous workshops. Existing projects already have plans and communities; the missing-property claim is low-confidence until operators demonstrate a genuine transfer problem. Contributing the adaptation/calibration layer to one of those communities is a likely better starting point than founding a rival network.

### 7. Six weeks for one person

**E:** two existing workshops, different printers/build envelopes, a purchasable common camera/motor kit, 75–90 builder hours. Choose an existing open scanning or inspection rig rather than inventing new optics.

- **Weeks 1–2:** have workshop B state a capability it lacks: repeatable small-object capture for dimensional inspection. Record B's available tools/materials and an externally measurable acceptance target.
- **Weeks 3–4:** workshop A makes the missing mounts/assembly jig; the agent adapts the open rig's parts and process to B's actual equipment. B assembles and calibrates it against a reference artifact. Record all specialist intervention and purchased inputs.
- **Weeks 5–6:** use B's new inspection capability to diagnose a real dimensional failure in an A-made part. Feed the measurement back into an adapted manufacturing recipe and reproduce the corrected part. Have a person who did not author the recipe execute the transfer from the published package.

**I — New result sought:** a documented physical feedback cycle: one workshop manufactures another's missing capability; that acquired capability discovers a manufacturing error and improves a later product at the originating workshop. Publish the measured capability gain, applicability bounds and external dependencies. The scanner itself is prior art. The original contribution must be a new verified cross-workshop transfer and adaptation, not a dependency-graph animation.

### 8. What would kill it; cheapest experiment

**I:** hardware variance and operator skill may make the “package” only a long instruction manual requiring bespoke consulting. **E — 48-hour test:** ask two operators to execute one existing open rig's build/calibration on different equipment. Measure every undocumented choice. Stop the platform if current documentation already transfers it easily, or if new measurements do not reduce the next transfer's work. Escalate beyond a single process only after an actual capability addition saves more engineering effort than its calibration/assembly consumes.

### 9. Builder fit

**I:** dependency solving, heterogeneous backends, build graphs, failure recovery and synchronization suit this builder unusually well. This also creates a trap: beautiful package machinery can conceal absent manufacturing knowledge. Mechanical collaboration and two real workshops are prerequisites for the experiment. The builder should own the transfer software and open observations, not a factory or a new robot foundation model.

## Scale boundary: Stephenson's tower remains a real ambition, but is a poor starting project

**Q:** Stephenson says “somebody ought to build” the tall tower ([p3-01, 1:15:00–1:17:00](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Ii-aeW5pHPQ&t=4500s)); the claim about doubled payload is attributed to earlier Landis calculations, not verified launch performance.

**F:** Keith Hjelmstad's [2014 structural account](https://hieroglyph.asu.edu/2014/08/structural-design-of-the-tall-tower/) discusses a 20 km tower, an approximately 25 km² base, jet-stream winds around 90 m/s, and a refined conceptual design around **250 million tonnes of steel**. It explicitly leaves construction, joints, wind loading and other problems unresolved. The optimistic strength-only calculation was not the completed design.

**E:** at an assumed €1,000/tonne, steel alone would cost **€250 billion**; even a factor-of-two error does not make it a software-side project. Erection, foundations, land, inspection, access and insurance are extra. This is not a claim of physical impossibility, nor a current steel quotation.

**I:** agent-written simulation can help explore that structure, but a tabletop lattice cannot validate high-altitude buckling, fatigue or construction. No evidence here establishes that cheap software removes its dominant constraints. Keep the tower as an ambition calibration and potential long-run use of a manufacturing/observation network; reject it as this builder's six-week starting mission.

## Ranking and decision

**E — Relative judgments, 1–5:** higher is better. “Residual confidence” measures confidence that the tested contribution adds something beyond named prior art, not confidence of commercial success. No participant recruitment, physical pilot or willingness-to-pay experiment has occurred.

| Rank | Idea | Ambition | Six-week physical result | Builder fit | Residual confidence | Main limiting resource |
|---:|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---|
| 1 | Physical fulfilment through reusable repair/adaptation knowledge | 5 | 5 | 4 | 2 | Measured fit/material suitability plus reliable human fulfilment |
| 2 | Continuously observed, editable city | 5 | 4 | 5 | 2 | Recurring capture access, registration and cross-owner participation |
| 3 | Agent-programmable physical capability commons | 4 | 4 | 5 | 1 | Calibration that adds value beyond mature automation ecosystems |
| 4 | Fabrication network that expands its own capabilities | 5 | 3 | 4 | 2 | Transferable process knowledge, assembly and purchased inputs |

**I — ONE pick:** **idea 1**, framed as a public repair/adaptation capability that can grow into general physical fulfilment. It offers the cheapest direct contact with the thesis: does new physical knowledge let a different person accomplish something useful? Its future depends on that answer and repeated participation, not on preserving a proprietary planner. Compare hard with Replique, Backflip plus local operators, and existing repair communities.

**I — Six-week commitment:** eight physical specimens; two independent fabrication paths; at least one held-out variant; delivered and confirmed use; public measurements, failures and reusable manufacturing source; all human intervention and external purchases counted. The owner experiences a completed repair. Code generation is an implementation detail.

**I — Hardest physical constraint:** recovering and reproducing the actual mating geometry and suitable mechanical behaviour across different material/process instances. A render cannot establish fit, a short load test cannot establish years of fatigue life, and neither can manufacture unavailable material. This constraint limits which families the network may responsibly claim to fulfil.

**I — Most crazy-but-not-impossible:** **idea 4**, because each new physical project could increase the network's productive repertoire. Its falsifier is equally physical: if capability transfer does not reduce the next workshop's effort, the supposed compounding is only documentation accumulation.

## Five-line TL;DR

1. **I:** Pick physical fulfilment, starting with actual repairs and adaptations whose discovered constraints can be reused by another workshop.
2. **F/I:** CAD agents, fabrication APIs, digital inventories, twins and device control already ship; a wrapper around them has little durable value.
3. **E:** A 40 g part can cost about €20 delivered or €50 as a one-off despite roughly €1 of plastic; handling, fit and qualification dominate.
4. **I:** In six weeks, produce original physical outcomes and a cross-printer held-out transfer, with time, failed fits and ordinary use recorded.
5. **I:** The larger bet is an expanding public ability to make and alter the world; better models help, but cannot supply missing observations, atoms or committed participants.
