Lens: what 1,000,000 tireless senior engineers would attempt. **Q** = supplied practitioner quotation or attributed claim; **F** = checked primary source; **I** = inference, proposal, or judgment; **E** = planning estimate or proposed acceptance threshold. **I — ONE pick: Fil-everything, beginning with a closed Flang/C native application.** Extend Fil-C's capability regime across language frontends, then accumulate a maintained distribution of applications whose entire user-level dependency closure obeys it. ## Economics: what the $420k actually licenses us to believe

**Q — Correct interpretation of video 88:** the opening headline combines roughly $400,000 attributed to unsuccessful earlier model runs with roughly $20,000 attributed to the successful later phase. At 15:00 Theo refines the latter to about **$24,000 at full API prices**, while estimating roughly **$500 of subscription usage** under the available allowances. The successful route used Microsoft's Go port as its starting point, with approximately two weeks of iteration. These are his estimates, not audited invoices or a controlled comparison. [88, 00:00–01:00 and 13:00–17:00](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ND_kRgU_WlY&t=780s).

**I:** use $24k as an existence signal for one favorable rewrite with a substantial existing oracle and intermediate port; use $420k as a warning about failed search. Neither is a price for proving TypeScript correct, rewriting an operating system, or creating the missing specifications. His elapsed time includes parallel workers and compute intervention. Subscription subsidies are unsuitable as a long-run infrastructure budget.

**E — Reproducible planning arithmetic:** assume a hypothetical workload with four input tokens per output token, input at $5/million and output at $20/million. That is **$8 per million total billed tokens**. This is a sensitivity assumption, not a quoted vendor tariff; caching, hidden reasoning, retries, and different models can change it substantially.

- 100 million total tokens → $800; 1 billion → $8,000; 1 trillion → $8 million.
- At that blend, $24k corresponds to 3 billion total tokens and $420k to 52.5 billion. These are illustrative budget equivalents; the transcript does not disclose enough billing detail to reconstruct actual token counts.
- A token floor from source length is misleading. Repeated context, failed proof searches, build diagnosis, abandoned translations, and repair dominate. Measure accepted modules and preserved obligations per dollar.
- For compilation and checking, use **$0.03–$0.10 per vCPU-hour** as an estimate, plus storage and large-memory machine charges. Record solver timeouts and compiler rebuilds separately. CPU and inference estimates below should be repriced before spending.

**E — Energy sanity check:** use a deliberately wide **0.1–10 joules per generated token** sensitivity range, not an asserted universal inference measurement. At the 4:1 token mix, a billion total tokens contains 200 million generated tokens: about **6–560 kWh of inference**, excluding other serving costs. A trillion total tokens implies approximately **6–560 MWh**. A million CPU-hours at an assumed 15 W per busy core adds **15 MWh** before memory and facility overhead. Actual provider energy cannot be inferred from the API bill; meter local checking and publish that separately.

**E — The million-engineer thought experiment has a real capacity limit:** one million simultaneously active agents generating ten output tokens/second each would produce 864 billion output tokens/day. At the hypothetical output price alone that is **$17.28 million/day**. The energy sensitivity above gives **1–100 MW** of inference power before other infrastructure. It is economically extravagant, not physically impossible. Most useful work should therefore be queued, bounded, cached, and stopped on an invariant failure, rather than keeping a million conversations active.

**I — Universal claims to discard:** exhaustive behavior preservation for arbitrary C, an automatic proof for every program, and exact agreement with every conflicting browser behavior are not goals that more engineers can guarantee. Specify supported inputs, environmental assumptions, allowed nondeterminism, and refusal conditions. An unsupported feature must remain visible. Better models reduce search cost; they do not repeal undecidability or turn an incorrect specification into the intended one.

## 1. Fil-everything: a maintained native capability ecosystem across LLVM languages

**Seed — Q:** Pizlo's audience asks for Fil-everything at 46:00. His earlier explanation identifies the leverage point: Clang's early LLVM IR retains pointer information that later optimization can obscure. [p3-23, 12:00–19:00](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=5F-2Y1LPRek&t=720s). **I:** the ambition is a shared safety regime for programs composed from several language ecosystems, including unsafe Rust and scientific Fortran, with no uninstrumented application library silently reopening arbitrary memory access.

**Full-scale vision — I; ambition rung: infrastructure for a field.** Build native distributions in which supported C, C++, Rust, Fortran, Zig, and eventually additional LLVM frontends share capability-aware calling conventions, allocation tracking, callbacks, library packaging, and an explicit trusted runtime. Maintain thousands of real applications and their dependencies through compiler and upstream releases. An operator selects a supported safety closure rather than commissioning a decade-long rewrite of each dependency. Language-specific ownership checks still matter; capability enforcement supplies a common deployment boundary beneath them.

**Why it died of engineer-years — I:** every frontend lowers allocations, pointer casts, descriptors, exceptions, atomics, and intrinsics differently. Every runtime and package system has assembly, allocator, linking, and platform assumptions. Coordinated compatibility work across thousands of packages is much larger than writing one compiler pass. Agents can perform the repetitive investigation, patches, rebuilds, and regression triage. The safety semantics and acceptance decisions still need specialist ownership.

**The oracle — I:** distinguish two independent obligations.

1. **Capability confinement:** for the supported IR/ABI, each memory operation must possess valid authority; unsupported operations reject compilation or trap. State the exact allocation-granularity and lifetime policy. Prove a small abstract propagation model, then validate the implementation against it with adversarial IR, frontend-generated cases, allocation reuse, callbacks, and optimizer variants. A model proof alone does not verify the full compiler/runtime.
2. **Useful behavior:** defined, accepted workloads must retain their outputs, exit behavior, and externally visible effects against the uninstrumented reference. Differential tests establish sampled compatibility, not absence of safety defects. A dependency manifest plus linker checks must expose all code outside the regime.

**F/I — Essential limits:** Fil-C uses a distinct ABI, and its runtime includes traditionally compiled trusted components beneath instrumented libc and user code. Its documentation explicitly requires the application dependency stack to be compiled for the regime. [Fil-C runtime](https://fil-c.org/runtime). Consequently, “no unsafe application escape” must not be presented as a proof about the kernel, trusted runtime, or hardware. At the talk's end, Pizlo demonstrates allocation bounds rounded to 16 bytes; a nearby wrong index may remain inside that allocation. [p3-23, 48:00–51:00](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=5F-2Y1LPRek&t=2880s). This is not Rust-style subobject correctness, freedom from data races, or application correctness.

**Closest attempts — F:**

- **Fil-C is already the substantive implementation.** Its current compiler documentation describes frontend adjustments and changes to optimization passes as well as the instrumentation pass. Merely running arbitrary optimized LLVM IR through it does not establish faithful source semantics. [Compiler design](https://fil-c.com/compiler). The public site lists 0.687, released October 23, 2026, and the existing C/C++ application ecosystem. [Fil-C](https://fil-c.org/).
- **SoftBound+CETS already implemented LLVM-level spatial/temporal pointer metadata and supplied mechanized safety models.** Its public distribution is tied to historical LLVM versions. That is evidence of both conceptual prior art and the compiler-maintenance burden, not evidence that nobody tried this. [Author's implementation and formalism](https://people.cs.rutgers.edu/~santosh.nagarakatte/softbound/).
- **Sulong/GraalVM already offers managed LLVM execution.** Oracle documents a mode that prohibits uncontrolled native memory/code access, requires bitcode throughout, and routes system interactions through its managed environment. A generic memory-safe polyglot LLVM runtime is therefore occupied. The proposed distinction is an open native Fil ABI distribution and its maintained package closure; benchmark the actual tradeoff against managed execution. [GraalVM execution modes](https://www.graalvm.org/dev/reference-manual/llvm/NativeExecution/).
- **rust-filc already connects Rust and Fil-C through a helper process.** Version 0.1.0 copies values and uses opaque handles; its documented limitations include shared memory, actual pointers, callbacks, and exceptions. This is close integration prior art, but not a shared native capability ABI. [Author's crate documentation](https://docs.rs/crate/filc-build/latest).
- **Zig has a Fil-inspired ABI proposal**, issue 36237. Its primary Codeberg page was inaccessible during this pass, so implementation status is **unverified**; treat it as a competing direction and inspect it before building. [Primary issue](https://codeberg.org/ziglang/zig/issues/36237). Do not claim the language is already supported.

**One-shot test — I:** an adapter is one-shottable. An independently used package closure, longitudinal compatibility evidence, accepted capability semantics, and cooperation from frontend/runtime maintainers are not. If a better model generates the adapter tomorrow, it becomes a contributor to the distribution. If there are no external users and no recurring maintenance obligation, this has collapsed into a useful upstream patch rather than a field-scale project.

**Physics, economics, data, law — E/I:** the full supported ecosystem might consume **10 billion–1 trillion total tokens ($80k–$8m)** and **100,000–10 million CPU-hours ($3k–$1m)** over staged releases, before specialist review and infrastructure operation. These are engineering envelopes, not measured translation rates. Runtime overhead may dominate generation cost: Pizlo reports roughly 4× median slowdown in the talk, with substantial workload variation. [p3-23, 11:00–12:00](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=5F-2Y1LPRek&t=660s). A 2× slowdown on 10,000 continuously busy cores adds 87.6 million core-hours/year; at the assumed CPU rate that is $2.6m–$8.8m/year. Public source is abundant; source-compatible runtime ports and legal redistribution rights must be tracked per dependency. Preserve the actual compiler/runtime/library licenses rather than assuming that instrumentation changes them. Reject deployments whose memory, latency, or energy budgets cannot tolerate the measured overhead.

**Six-week opening — I/E:** use 10–15 builder hours/week and one Linux x86-64 target. Pin matching Fil-C/Flang LLVM revisions; avoid an accidental compiler-version migration.

- **Week 1:** inventory the ABI and runtime closure; establish uninstrumented reference outputs and a small pointer/descriptor test set. Attempt one C caller and one Flang-produced function through the capability pass. This includes the two-day stop test below.
- **Week 2:** preserve pointer authority through the supported Fortran/C calling convention. Exercise arrays, descriptors, allocation/deallocation, and callbacks. Reject unsupported features explicitly.
- **Week 3:** compile the required Fortran runtime portions and application libraries into the regime. A raw native runtime dependency disqualifies a closed-application claim. Flang's descriptor design makes the data pointer, bounds, strides, and additional type information concrete obligations. [Flang runtime descriptors](https://flang.llvm.org/docs/RuntimeDescriptor.html).
- **Week 4:** run a small real package, provisionally MINPACK plus a C command-line caller, against its native reference. Keep the supported feature set modest; the descriptor stress programs exercise boundaries the application may not use.
- **Week 5:** test a stale pointer crossing the language boundary, a write outside the actual allocation, a forged pointer value, and an attempt to link an uninstrumented object. Report traps/refusals and untouched sentinel allocations. Run optimized and less-optimized variants. Cases need observable effects so dead-code elimination cannot make them vacuous.
- **Week 6:** publish source, pinned toolchain, package manifest, replayable hostile cases, performance and memory results, and explicit trusted components. Seek an independent rebuild and one real user's acceptance before expanding languages. Record failure if participation or closure does not materialize.

**New observable result sought — I:** one native application in which a Flang-created allocation crosses to C, comes back through a callback, and retains the same capability/lifetime restrictions through the whole journey, alongside an ordinary numerical workload that still works. A deliberately unsafe dependency fails the build. The research did not locate this exact maintained Flang/Fil-C result; Sulong and process-isolated Rust/Fil-C integrations mean broad polyglot-safety novelty claims would be false.

**Kill and cheapest experiment — I/E:** the strongest objection is that the supposedly shared LLVM layer has already lost essential provenance or that the Fortran runtime cannot join the ABI without a much larger frontend fork. Spend **two days and ≤$300** compiling the tiny cross-language case and inspecting pre-instrumentation IR. Kill this six-week scope if it requires accepting uninstrumented runtime calls, inventing authority from arbitrary integers, or maintaining incompatible LLVM generations. Do not rescue the headline with a helper process; that capability already exists. A successful toy is permission to investigate, not proof of the full architecture.

**Builder fit — I:** excellent systems/compiler fit; the largest unfamiliar area is Fortran runtime semantics. Reusing Fil-C and a pinned frontend is plausible part-time. Supporting all of Rust's runtime, arbitrary assembly, JITs, or a desktop browser within six weeks is not.

## 2. Proof-backed replacements for the foundations: coreutils, OpenSSL, SQLite

**Seed — Q:** de Moura's code-plus-proof vision, [p3-45, 05:00–12:00](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=KzdYKeAqWhY&t=300s), and Dillon's conformance-enabled rewrite, [Connect 13, 13:00](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=D_tnYlZe5t8&t=780s). **I:** spend the engineer-years recovering the actual contracts of foundational software and preserving them through successive implementations.

**Full-scale vision — I; ambition rung: changes what systems engineering can do.** A maintained public foundation of specifications and proof-backed implementations for utilities, cryptography, compression, storage engines, and their boundaries. An implementation may be regenerated or aggressively optimized while the accepted contracts, compatibility decisions, and independent evidence persist. The end state includes replacements used by distributions and applications, not a collection of theorem screenshots.

**Why impossible before / possible now — I:** proof construction, semantics recovery, optimized low-level code, compatibility quirks, and proof repair each demanded scarce experts. Agents can parallelize much of that labor while small proof checkers reject invalid derivations. The bottleneck becomes finding adequate specifications, connecting proofs to delivered artifacts, and getting real consumers to adopt the result. That is substantial new capacity, but it is already an active industrial field.

**Oracle — I:** each foundation needs a different contract.

- **coreutils:** compare observable bytes, exit codes, filesystem effects, links, permissions, locale behavior, and errors against GNU implementations under an agreed environment. Prove selected algorithmic contracts, then test their actual CLI integration. Do not prove an idealized sort while ignoring locale and I/O failures.
- **OpenSSL:** differential protocol behavior and official vectors are necessary but insufficient. Specify algorithm correctness, parsing, protocol state, randomness assumptions, and constant-time behavior separately. A proof that computes AES correctly does not establish absence of timing leakage or correctness of the TLS implementation.
- **SQLite:** preserve documented SQL results and the supported C API; separately specify transactions, file-format compatibility, allowed crash-recovery states, and filesystem assumptions. Multiple correct implementations can create different byte layouts. Existing behavior is a compatibility oracle, not an instruction to preserve corruption bugs. Match or justify each difference.

**Closest attempts — F:**

- **Canonical/uutils:** Ubuntu 26.04 LTS already uses rust-coreutils and sudo-rs by default, with traditional implementations available. A Rust rewrite of these tools is shipped distribution work. Rust memory safety does not imply complete functional verification. [Canonical release account](https://canonical.com/blog/ubuntu-26-04-lts-security-updates).
- **Microsoft SymCrypt:** July 2026 documentation describes Rust, Aeneas, Lean, agent-produced proofs, and reviewed specifications for SHA-3/ML-KEM. Its September paper reports **16.7 KLOC of Rust supported by 237 KLOC of Lean development**. These are production-oriented verification efforts, not a vacant AI-plus-proof category. [Project account](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/blog/verifying-rust-cryptography-in-symcrypt-from-standards-to-code/), [author paper](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/publication/scaling-verification-of-cryptographic-software-with-aeneas-rust-and-lean/?lang=zh-cn).
- **AWS:** aws-lc-verification documents proofs for portions of libcrypto and newer work using s2n-bignum/HOL Light for assembly and CBMC for C components. It also records caveats. This is assurance for stated portions and configurations, not a proof of all OpenSSL-compatible behavior. [AWS implementation and proof artifacts](https://github.com/awslabs/aws-lc-verification).
- **Turso/Antithesis/Aretta AI:** the SQLite rewrite already has differential testing, fuzzing, simulation, and formal-methods partnerships. Its July 7, 2026 post specifically discusses Aretta and an fsync defect. A new SQLite rewrite plus a fault harness duplicates funded work. [Turso testing account](https://turso.tech/blog/how-we-used-quint-to-find-over-10-bugs-in-sqlite), [formal-methods partnership](https://turso.tech/blog/the-final-boss-of-reliability).
- **Kim Morrison's lean-zip:** the public project already supplies a verified pure-Lean compression/decompression round trip. It carefully distinguishes that codec from high-level bindings that call system zlib. Generic “AI rewrites zlib with a proof” is taken. [Implementation and theorem](https://github.com/kim-em/lean-zip).

**I — Why attempts have not finished the whole foundation:** the cited projects have made real progress; describing them as stalled would be wrong. Their scope boundaries expose the remaining work: compatibility outside the kernel algorithm, trusted extraction and compilation, changing APIs, deployment configuration, and proof maintenance. SQLite's own documentation describes extensive failure testing and the proprietary TH3 suite. Source access does not grant access to every incumbent oracle. [How SQLite is tested](https://www.sqlite.org/testing.html).

**One-shot test — I:** a generated replacement or theorem script fails. The residual asset is a public, independently accepted specification tied to actual downstream obligations, together with adoption and a history of adversarial validation. Two better model generations can write more proofs; they cannot retroactively manufacture years of customer workloads or settle incompatible stakeholders' contracts. This mission needs maintainers and downstream users, not another model orchestration product.

**Physics, economics, data, law — E/I:** full-scale envelopes of **1–100 trillion total tokens ($8m–$800m)** and **1–100 million CPU-hours ($30k–$10m)** are conceivable for many components and iterations; specialist assurance and ongoing maintenance are additional. The SymCrypt code/proof ratio warns against pricing verification by implementation LOC. Nothing prevents this physically, but proof search can explode and some contracts need new mathematics or stronger decomposition. Use public test material according to its license; budget separately for proprietary suites and independent reviews. A functional proof is not a regulatory certification or a blanket right to redistribute a translated implementation.

**Six-week opening — I/E:** join one existing foundation rather than start an alternative SQLite. Scope one **C-locale decimal comparison path for a coreutils sort implementation**: week 1 agree its precise accepted grammar and exceptional cases with a maintainer; weeks 2–3 write a simple mathematical specification and prove the actual safe-Rust comparison function through a supported extraction path; week 4 connect it to a real command path and compare GNU behavior; week 5 seek withheld downstream inputs and adversarial review of preconditions; week 6 publish a runnable integrated artifact and the maintainer's disposition. Bound the theorem to the accepted function and input domain; locale, shell behavior, and the rest of sort remain separately tested. **E:** 100–400 million total tokens, $800–$3,200 inference, $100–$500 checking; 60–90 builder hours. Proof-tool familiarity is a serious schedule risk.

**New observable result sought — I:** an existing command's adopted comparison path with a checked theorem whose domain is visible, a real downstream workload, and adversarial cases withheld during implementation. This is a contribution to a large replacement mission, not a claim that verified sorting or upstream verification contributions are novel. Without a concrete unserved contract and downstream participation, the wedge fails this round's ambition requirement.

**Kill and cheapest experiment — I/E:** the strongest objection is that independent specification review costs more than proof writing and the project has no scarce contract to contribute. Spend **two days and ≤$200** checking the upstream issue/verification landscape and asking whether a named maintainer would use the scoped result. Do not commence a six-week proof exercise without an actual acceptance boundary. Strong systems skill does not automatically supply proof or cryptographic expertise.

**Builder fit — I:** technically compatible with Rust/Lean and open-source work, but less distinctive than the compiler mission. Commercial teams and verification specialists already have strong advantages. Rank it as an upstream contribution, not an independent foundation company.

## 3. Translate the C estate, including drivers, with observed hardware contracts

**Seed — Q:** Theo's unread rewrite and its high compatibility claim, [88, 00:00–01:00 and 16:00–19:00](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ND_kRgU_WlY&t=960s), plus de Moura's claim that AI changes proof-maintenance economics, [p3-45, 10:00–12:00](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=KzdYKeAqWhY&t=600s). **I:** the interesting extension is replacing real unsafe systems while preserving environmental behavior that their source code does not completely specify.

**Full-scale vision — I; ambition rung: infrastructure for a field.** An opt-in program that translates maintained C software and Linux driver families into safe Rust, with verified low-level abstractions where safe Rust alone cannot express the device interaction. Maintain upstream-compatible releases, supported hardware inventories, and evidence from real devices. The durable result is a shrinking unsafe deployment estate with accountable maintainers. “All C” means a declared, tracked estate; arbitrary source cannot be promised a universally equivalent safe translation.

**Why impossible before / possible now — I:** traditional translation often preserves raw pointers and unsafe operations, leaving humans to redesign ownership and callbacks. Drivers add physical device variants, DMA, volatile access, interrupts, teardown races, and kernel API changes. Agents can attempt many refactorings and repair build/configuration matrices. Hardware access, vendor knowledge, and actual observations remain scarce.

**Current TRACTOR status — F, checked rather than inferred from its name:** DARPA's program is active; MIT Lincoln Laboratory owns independent evaluation and public releases. [DARPA](https://www.darpa.mil/research/programs/translating-all-c-to-rust), [benchmark program](https://www.ll.mit.edu/r-d/projects/translating-all-c-rust-tractor-benchmarks). The current public report's cover is dated **October 13, 2026**; repository metadata is not its publication date. It covers six lead teams: **Aarno Labs, Galois, Intel, University of Washington, University of Wisconsin, and Yale**. Battery 01 has 150 constrained tests: Yale passes 148/150, and average performer failure is 17.8%. Its constrained pointer cases are far from an entire kernel. Intel's reported 1.56 unsafe operations/test is a syntactic metric, not a proof of safety. Milestone projects include Perlin noise and SPHINCS. These results do not establish complete C-to-safe-Rust translation. [Primary report repository](https://github.com/DARPA-TRACTOR-Program/Reports).

**F — Other direct competitors:** Microsoft's October 26, 2026 account says its FoSSE teams are already modernizing C/C++ into Rust with differential checks, held-out tests, and emerging Verus proofs. Its separate 400-million-line compiler-toolchain migration should not be relabeled a Rust rewrite. [Microsoft's dated account](https://commandline.microsoft.com/agentic-software-engineering-large-scale-code-migration-language-modernization-spec-driven-development/). Google's Android/Chromium work already introduces Rust into security-sensitive components and parsers; this is targeted replacement, not a claim to have translated the entire estate. [Google's engineering account](https://security.googleblog.com/2025/11/).

**F — Kernel and verification attempts:** Rust for Linux lists mainline users including Binder, PHY drivers, Nova, and Tyr, with other drivers outside mainline. Nova's GSP-based design is a substantive current driver effort. [Rust for Linux](https://rust-for-linux.com/), [Nova](https://rust-for-linux.com/nova-gpu-driver). **VELD** already applies Rust/Verus refinement to Linux drivers and reports experience with portions of an MSR driver. Its authors identify modeling the kernel/hardware interface and concurrency as central challenges. [VELD research](https://mars-research.github.io/projects/veld/). Rust standard-library verification is also organized and funded; the public challenge program includes Kani, Flux, VeriFast, and GOTO Transcoder. [Verification effort](https://github.com/runtimeverification/verify-rust-std/blob/main/README.md). None of these is a vanished attempt waiting for a generic translator.

**Oracle — I:** safe Rust's type system is an important check, not a functional specification. Compare protocol outputs and driver-visible behavior with the C implementation; use emulators to control schedules; prove ownership/state-machine obligations where feasible; and validate on physical devices. Specifically test cancellation, device removal, power cycling, malformed reports, and the absence of stale DMA authority after teardown. The original driver can contain a bug, and a simulator can share its mistaken device model. Resolve disagreements with datasheets, traces, and device owners. Any unsafe kernel/FFI shim belongs in the trusted surface.

**One-shot test — I:** generic translation fails decisively: DARPA and Microsoft already do it. A multi-vendor corpus of physical behavior, devices available for repeated qualification, vendor/maintainer agreements, and supported release history survives. A better model can write another driver; it cannot observe a device revision that nobody has supplied or authorize a kernel merge. The resulting value resembles a shared hardware qualification institution, not a translation website.

**Physics, economics, data, law — E/I:** for an illustrative **10,000-module estate** at **0.1–10 billion total tokens/module**, inference is **1–100 trillion tokens, $8m–$800m**. This is a portfolio scenario, not an estimate of Linux's driver count. Testing 1,000 device variants with $100–$1,000 acquisition/access cost adds $100k–$1m before lab automation and staff. Firmware versions and hardware revision scarcity can dominate. The Linux kernel's documented overall license is GPL-2.0 with a syscall exception, and individual-file licenses need checking; do not assume translation eliminates inherited obligations. [Kernel licensing rules](https://docs.kernel.org/process/license-rules.html). Firmware and vendor data require separate permission. Prototype with equipment the builder owns and public specifications.

**Six-week opening — I/E:** choose one cheap, documented I²C sensor and a small existing C driver, provisionally a TMP102-class temperature device, after checking current Rust/kernel work. Week 1 obtain the hardware and an upstream acceptance path; week 2 record C-driver behavior under read errors and power cycles; weeks 3–4 port a bounded state machine into safe Rust and prove a narrow ownership/transition property; week 5 run identical physical sequences against both implementations; week 6 publish a shared trace corpus, the supported device revision, and a tested integration. If kernel Rust bindings are missing, a C integration shim may be documented, but the result must be described as a partial port. **E:** 100–300 million tokens ($800–$2,400), $200–$600 devices/host, $100–$400 checking: **$1,100–$3,400**, 60–90 builder hours.

**New observable result sought — I:** a concrete, repeatable difference or demonstrated agreement between C and Rust across at least 100 physical reset/error sequences for an identified device revision, connected to an implementation the driver maintainer would accept. A renamed existing embedded Rust driver is insufficient. This research does not establish that the provisional sensor lacks a Rust implementation; that is an explicit opening selection gate.

**Kill and cheapest experiment — I/E:** hardware coverage and kernel integration may overwhelm one person's time. Spend **two days and ≤$300** verifying existing drivers, the necessary kernel abstractions, the device's reproducible behavior, and a maintainer's interest. Stop if a working port already covers the same hardware/obligations, or the next useful step is months of kernel infrastructure. **Builder fit — I:** excellent kernel/VMM skill match; weaker part-time logistics and hardware reach. Stronger if a maintainer or device owner provides sustained access.

## 4. An executable, formally specified web platform that independent engines can implement

**Seed — Q:** Gillilan wants to “use the web” rather than adopt another framework identity, [p3-21, 12:00–13:00](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=W7Ki3aXgmZU&t=720s). De Moura's synthesis vision supplies the verification direction. Omar imagines “the whole system is a live program being edited by thousands of humans and AIs,” [p3-19, 47:00–48:00](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=fB_lklm7xgU&t=2820s). **I:** the relevant extension of the live commons is a maintained executable semantic substrate for the web itself, with edits checked against agreed behavior and independently tested engines.

**Full-scale vision — I; ambition rung: changes what browser engineering can do.** Turn prose algorithms, security boundaries, scheduling, DOM behavior, layout rules, and compatibility exceptions into composable executable specifications. Independent engines, including newly generated implementations, can carry refinement evidence for covered subsystems and test the rest against a public oracle. A million engineers would attempt the browser engine too, but the durable institution is the semantic agreement and compatibility corpus from which multiple engines can be built.

**Why impossible before / possible now — I:** the burden is the size and entanglement of the platform, not ignorance of formal semantics. Specifications and implementations evolve together, and old websites depend on quirks. Agents make the translation of prose, obligation discovery, proof repair, and cross-engine experimentation more affordable. Browser-vendor agreement and independently observed compatibility remain essential.

**Oracle — I:** keep three layers separate: a reviewed formal interpretation of the relevant standard; browser traces and existing conformance suites; and permitted site-derived compatibility cases. A disagreement between engines is a case to adjudicate, not an automatic majority vote. Nondeterministic task scheduling and floating-point/rendering variation need sets of allowed outcomes. A theorem about an abstract DOM does not verify an engine's actual C++/Rust implementation, compositor, fonts, GPU, or network behavior. Require an explicit refinement boundary for any proof claim.

**Closest attempts — F:**

- **WebSpec** already provides a Coq browser-security model, invariant checking, counterexamples, executable tests, and proofs for covered changes. It directly occupies the proposed formal-model-to-browser-counterexample loop. [Author implementation](https://github.com/SecPriv/webspec), [paper](https://secpriv.github.io/webspec/report.pdf).
- **Core DOM** already formalizes DOM behavior in Isabelle/HOL. The idea of formally specifying the DOM is established research. [Archive of Formal Proofs entry](https://isa-afp.org/entries/Core_DOM.html).
- **WPT** is an existing shared compatibility institution; adding another generated test runner is not a new mission. Its repository and licenses must be used according to their actual terms. [WPT](https://github.com/web-platform-tests/wpt), [license](https://github.com/web-platform-tests/wpt/blob/master/LICENSE.md).
- **Ladybird** already uses AI-assisted subsystem translation and lockstep validation. Its February 2026 account reports 52,898 test262 cases and 12,461 regression cases without regressions for its translated pipeline, plus matching bytecode during real browsing. Those are sampled compatibility results, not complete engine verification. [Ladybird's account](https://ladybird.org/posts/adopting-rust/).
- **Servo** is an existing Rust engine with a long research/deployment history and current embeddability work. A new Rust browser is a direct competitor to both funded engine development and established vendors. [Servo scope and history](https://servo.org/about/).

**I — What remains:** extending partial formal models into an adopted, continuously maintained semantic body across subsystem interactions. Existing projects demonstrate progress, not general failure. A claim that their entire scope can be unified by one person in six weeks should be rejected.

**One-shot test — I:** a model can generate an interpreter or tests. It cannot decide what legacy compatibility the web will collectively retain, acquire permission to publish private site failures, or secure independent-engine adoption. The durable value is a standards/engine/site participant network with adjudicated cases and history. If it remains one person's alternative DOM model, it fails.

**Physics, economics, data, law — E/I:** an initial field-scale program at **1–10 trillion total tokens ($8m–$80m)** and **1–10 million browser CPU-hours ($30k–$1m)** is conceivable; the range excludes a fully proven optimizing engine and the full graphics stack. Exhaustive exploration of arbitrary browser state is infeasible; modular proofs and bounded counterexample search are necessary. Public specs and WPT provide a large starting corpus. Preserve license and attribution terms and obtain permission for site traces that include private state; do not present a small supported subset as official conformance certification.

**Six-week opening — I/E:** select one named interaction missing from existing models, provisionally custom-element reactions plus microtasks during node adoption, only after a WPT/Core DOM/WebSpec gap check. Week 1 agree scope with an existing model or engine maintainer; weeks 2–3 write an executable transition model and one precise invariant; week 4 derive traces and replay in three engines; week 5 reduce one concrete disagreement or expose a model error; week 6 contribute the accepted case and model amendment upstream. **E:** 150–500 million tokens ($1,200–$4,000) and $200–$600 browser checking: **$1,400–$4,600**, 60–90 builder hours.

**New observable result sought — I:** an accepted explanation and correction for a specific cross-subsystem disagreement, linked from the executable model to the real engines. Not an assumed undiscovered browser defect. A null result should report the scope already covered. **Kill — I/E:** spend **two days and ≤$200** checking whether existing WPT/model work covers the interaction and whether a vendor/model owner will engage. A lone conformance fixture with no semantic gap is the small-project trap from the preceding rounds. **Builder fit — I:** excellent TypeScript/Wasm/browser tooling fit, weaker formal web-semantics expertise and standards access. Standards coordination limits autonomy more than token budget does.

## 5. Port science's Fortran while preserving the scientific conclusions

**Seed — Q:** Kernighan recalls Fortran's adoption changing once its compiler “started finding optimizations that people didn't.” His point connects better results, access for scientists, and portability. [p3-61, 1:18:00–1:19:00](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=sMllOlxVgD0&t=4680s). Dillon's rewrite experience supplies the modern engineering lever; de Moura's observation that a simple reference can act as a specification supplies the checkable target. [Connect 13, 11:00–13:00](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=D_tnYlZe5t8&t=660s), [p3-45, 09:00–10:00](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=KzdYKeAqWhY&t=540s).

**Full-scale vision — I; ambition rung: changes what computational science can do.** Rescue and modernize scientific software together with its datasets, numerical contracts, calibration, and experiment histories. Produce maintained safe implementations that run on current machines, scale in parallel where useful, and reproduce the conclusions for which researchers rely on them. Scientists can retire obsolete runtime assumptions without gambling that a familiar graph still means the same thing. Source-language replacement is optional when modern Fortran already serves the science well.

**Why impossible before / possible now — I:** translation was only part of the labor. COMMON blocks, implicit typing, array layout, compiler flags, precision, global state, missing build environments, and undocumented experimental choices multiplied across decades of programs. Agents can recover structure and perform ports and repeated checks. The scarce inputs become the experiments and people who can judge whether a numerical difference matters.

**Oracle — I:** numerical equivalence has several distinct levels.

- For integer/control paths, prove or compare exact behavior under explicit overflow/indexing rules.
- For a fixed floating-point environment, differential testing can target bitwise equality, including initialization, rounding, precision, and compiler flags. Do not quietly let CPU/GPU reduction order change and then claim the same guarantee.
- For optimized parallel numerics, record scientifically justified error budgets, convergence properties, conserved quantities, and conditioning. A universal relative tolerance is inadequate near zeros, discontinuities, or chaotic trajectories.
- For the scientific result, reproduce the actual measurement or inference with its data provenance and uncertainty. Agreement with the old program can preserve an old scientific error; use independent formulations or high-precision references where available.

**Closest attempts — F:**

- **ORNL and collaborators** already studied LLM translation of parallel Fortran into C/C++ with GPU-capable programming models, evaluating more than 1,650 generated programs across 11 codes/proxies. Their reported success on this sample is not a complete production climate or nuclear code modernization. [Author publication record](https://impact.ornl.gov/en/publications/llm-driven-fortran-to-cc-portability-for-parallel-scientific-code/).
- **ForTress, October 15, 2026**, combines deterministic reusable translation rules with Lean-audited semantic obligations. Its authors report 17 benchmarks from 15 scientific programs, 193 rules, and 121 checked soundness proofs among 188 audit records; bitwise agreement is for evaluated tests under recorded configurations. A generic proof-assisted scientific translator is direct current competition. [Author preprint](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.22528).
- **LAFT, October 8, 2026**, translates Kessler warm-rain physics into JAX and validates numerical agreement. Its headline GPU result excludes compilation and transfers and is for a particular large-column workload. Do not reuse Kessler as an allegedly unattempted wedge or treat a kernel speedup as an end-to-end scientific improvement. [Author preprint](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.12662).
- **f2rust/rsspice** already mechanically ports nearly the whole SPICELIB API into safe Rust and runs translated regression tests. The author explicitly describes it as unofficial and less tested, despite substantial functionality. “Put NASA's Fortran in Rust/Wasm” is already occupied. [Translator](https://github.com/zaynar/f2rust), [rsspice documentation](https://docs.rs/rsspice/latest/rsspice/).

**I — What prevents full completion:** domain acceptance, hard-to-obtain experiments, verification of sensitive numerical behavior, hardware validation, and maintaining mixed-language deployments. The examples show significant progress. Calling Fortran itself obsolete, or all of science blocked on a new language, would miss the real problem.

**One-shot test — I:** the port fails; a participant-owned archive of reproduced experiments, expert-reviewed numerical contracts, negative results, and longitudinal hardware comparisons survives. Better models can translate the remaining code and strengthen proofs, but cannot conjure missing instrument data or decide a paper's acceptable scientific error without its domain assumptions. The project needs real scientific partners, not a gallery of generated kernels.

**Physics, economics, data, law — E/I:** a planning estate of **100 million legacy source lines**, at an assumed **1,000–100,000 total billed tokens/line over recovery, translation, and validation**, implies **100 billion–10 trillion tokens ($0.8m–$80m)**. This is an illustrative estate and amplification assumption, not a census of Fortran or a measured productivity ratio. At field scale, rerunning experiments can dominate: **10 million–1 billion CPU-hours** adds **$0.3m–$100m** at the common rate, with GPU and instrument costs separate. Start with public, redistributable source and data; public visibility alone does not establish permission to republish every dataset. Archived experiments without data or adequate reference environments may be unrecoverable and should be labeled so.

**Six-week opening — I/E:** select one modest Fortran numerical package and **two openly documented research examples that actually use it**, with a researcher willing to review their numerical acceptance conditions. MINPACK-style nonlinear fitting is a candidate family; choose the exact package after checking existing ports rather than asserting a gap. Week 1 secure datasets, licenses, original builds, and a scientific partner; week 2 reproduce the published/reference outcomes; weeks 3–4 adapt an existing safe-Rust port or build a bounded replacement, preserving arithmetic order where equality is required; week 5 compare x86-64, ARM64, and Wasm for the declared workload; week 6 publish code, data provenance, conditions, deviations, and the scientist's assessment. Prefer an existing port if the new asset is scientific validation. **E:** 100–400 million tokens ($800–$3,200), $200–$800 checking/data hosting, and $300–$1,000 domain-review allowance: **$1,300–$5,000**, plus 60–90 builder hours.

**New observable result sought — I:** two independently reviewed reproductions of actual scientific outcomes, now using a maintained safe implementation, with a published explanation of which numerical differences preserve or change the conclusions. A translated demo that resembles the old graph is insufficient. **Kill — I/E:** spend **two days and ≤$200** reproducing one reference result and confirming dataset access plus a scientist's willingness to define tolerances. Stop if source/data are missing, the old result cannot be reproduced, an existing project has already validated the same path, or the only claimed improvement is changing the language. **Builder fit — I:** strong portability/systems fit; scientific judgment is a partner dependency, so this ranks below the compiler path for a lone platform engineer.

## Ranking and decision

**I — Ranked for this builder, accounting for competition and six-week credibility:**

1. **Fil-everything through Flang/C.** Best direct practitioner seed, best systems/compiler fit, and a technically concrete open question. Conditional on closing the runtime and earning outside use. Native shared capability semantics, supported package closure, and maintenance distinguish it from Sulong and process-based integration. Do not pitch a generic LLVM safety pass as new.
2. **Scientific continuity through safe ports.** Strong non-code asset in domain-reviewed reproduced experiments. Translate less, validate more. Requires a researcher and existing data; ForTress, LAFT, national labs, and rsspice remove easy novelty claims.
3. **C/driver estate with physical contracts.** Excellent systems fit and physical observations that a prompt cannot create. Hardware breadth, Rust/kernel abstractions, and maintainer bandwidth are substantial constraints. TRACTOR and Microsoft make the generic translation layer crowded.
4. **Proof-backed foundations.** Enormous impact and now demonstrably feasible in parts, but Microsoft, AWS, Turso, uutils, and lean-zip already occupy the obvious entry points. Contribute a missing accepted contract; avoid competing on another rewrite.
5. **Executable web platform.** The most sweeping public-semantic mission, with severe specification, compatibility, and governance burdens. WebSpec/Core DOM/WPT/Servo/Ladybird substantially narrow the opening. A six-week result is likely a contribution, not evidence that this builder should found a new engine project.

**I — Most crazy-but-not-impossible:** the **executable, formally specified web platform**. A million agents maintaining a compositional semantic body and multiple engine implementations would change the economics of browser independence. Rendering, networking, nondeterminism, and compatibility mean it cannot be one monolithic equivalence proof. Physical feasibility survives; the proposed social and semantic coordination remains unvalidated.

**I — ONE pick and explicit six-week contract:** ship **a closed Flang/C application under Fil-C's capability regime**, with one real numerical workload, observable hostile cross-language cases, a link-time refusal of an unsafe dependency, and an independently reproduced release. The field-scale direction is an adopted polyglot native safety distribution; the opening artifact must show the new language boundary and actual closure. If the two-day experiment cannot preserve authority without a raw runtime escape, stop. **E:** **$2,300–$7,000**, 60–90 builder hours; **$10,000 ceiling**. Reserve later financing for ecosystem adoption, formalization, and performance, rather than extrapolating a whole civilization from one successful compile.

## Five-line TL;DR

1. **I:** Pick Fil-everything: extend a closed native capability ABI across frontends and maintain real application closures.
2. **F:** Generic rewrites are crowded: TRACTOR, Microsoft, AWS, Turso, uutils, Ladybird, and scientific-porting teams are already doing substantial work.
3. **I:** Trust needs the right oracle: confinement proofs, behavioral comparisons, physical traces, or scientific acceptance—and an honest trusted boundary.
4. **E:** The six-week Flang/C opening costs $2,300–$7,000 at explicit token assumptions; stop at $10,000 or a failed two-day feasibility test.
5. **I:** The durable asset is adopted semantics plus accumulated real-world evidence; the largest surviving moonshot is a formally specified executable web.
