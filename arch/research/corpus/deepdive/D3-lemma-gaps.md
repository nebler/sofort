# D3: Lemma, GAPS (prior art)

Research date: **2026-10-29**. Brief: [`../BRIEF-deepdive.md`](../BRIEF-deepdive.md). The idea comes from
[`../tailwind/T02-languages-compilers.md`](../tailwind/T02-languages-compilers.md) §1. Lemma is a Wasm→AArch64
compiler written in Lean. Models grow every lowering and optimisation, and a change is kept only if it comes with a
machine-checked semantic-preservation proof and a measured speedup. This is the only file written.

**Labels**
- **Q**: quoted. I re-checked every quote on 2026-10-29 against the raw primary page, the arXiv HTML or the repository
  file (fetched through the GitHub API).
- **F**: sourced, with a link.
- **I**: inference.
- **E**: estimate.

**Method (F)**
- **Volume:** about 45 web searches, 35 page fetches and 60 GitHub API calls. The API calls covered repository metadata,
  commit logs, READMEs, design documents, security advisories and one records ledger.
- **Dates:** activity dates come from the GitHub API (`pushed_at` and commit logs), not from search snippets.

  - X can't be searched directly from here, and web search surfaced no relevant posts.

## 1. Verdict

**Narrow opening: the Wasm end of the theorem, and growth that beats Cranelift.** Three things are effectively taken:
- the proven backend below the IR;
- the ratchet mechanism, in adjacent domains;
- the "every speedup is a theorem" pitch, also in adjacent domains.

T02 (written 2026-10-29, the same day as this report) based Lemma's novelty on things nobody had. Re-checked:

| T02: "nobody has…" | What exists on 2026-10-29 |
|---|---|
| An end-to-end proof for a Wasm-class compiler to AArch64 | **It exists for Cranelift's IR.** [`eth-act/clifv`](https://github.com/eth-act/clifv) is an all-Lean backend from the Ethereum Foundation's applied cryptography team. It goes from CLIF to AArch64 machine code to ELF bytes, proven end to end with no `sorry`. It has about 1,450 commits since 2026-09-27 (F). |
| Proven, not certificate-checked, agent-written optimisations | **It exists for a toy language.** Rinard's Nexis (2026-07-25) proves lazy code motion and partial dead-code elimination correct *and optimal*, down to an AArch64 model. All of it was generated by Claude Code Opus 4.8 (F). |
| A Lean AArch64 model, or Sail→Lean | **Both exist.** [LNSym](https://github.com/leanprover/LNSym) is a Lean Armv8 model, co-simulated on Arm hardware, Apache-2.0, around since 2024. clifv ports it. Sail 0.20 ships an experimental Lean backend (F). |
| A ratchet in which untrusted agents grow optimisations under a frozen theorem | **It exists for EVM.** Paradigm's [Solidus arena](https://github.com/paradigmxyz/solidus/blob/main/CHALLENGE.md) set 13 records in 8 days and cut gas by 33%. `compile_correct` still proved at every step (F). |
| "Every speedup is a theorem" | **It has been a product since 2026-10-23.** Cajal's Tau sells binaries made faster and "proved correct in Lean down to the machine instructions, on both x86 and ARM" (Q). Cajal also maintains Talos, a Lean Wasm semantics. |

**What is precisely left (I)**
1. **The Wasm half of the theorem.** This means a proven translation from a spec-tested Lean Wasm semantics into the
   compiler IR, so that the top of the theorem is *the Wasm module*, not CLIF, C or a toy language. It has to cover:
   - traps;
   - linear-memory bounds, using explicit checks (guard-page bounds would need an MMU model);
   - tables and `call_indirect` signature checks;
   - `memory.grow` and the vmctx ABI;
   - then floats.

   Nobody has this. Each neighbour stops short:
   - clifv's top is CLIF, and it leaves Wasmtime's vmctx unverified;
   - Talos proves individual Wasm binaries;
   - VeriISLE checks lowering rules, not the Wasm translator;
   - vWasm proves isolation only.
2. **Growth that must beat the incumbent on real Wasm programs.**
   - clifv's CI ratchet fails on *lost exact matches with stock Cranelift*, and its optimised output is about 1.4×
     Cranelift's size (F).
   - Nobody runs a proof-gated "faster than Cranelift or V8" queue on a Wasm corpus, logged per model release.
3. **Floats and SIMD in a proven AArch64 backend.** clifv plans them but doesn't have them yet (F).

**How narrow (E)**
- **On a licensed clifv-class backend:** items 1–2 are about 2–4 months of solo, agent-assisted work. That is below the
  brief's "about 10×" bar.
- **Rebuilding the backend:** 6–12 months, and it duplicates a funded team's month.
- **The window:** at least four groups are one step from item 1: EF, Cajal, Rinard's lab and Basis. I estimate the
  window at 3–12 months.

## 3. The named neighbours

Each entry covers what it is, how far it got, whether it is active, and what is left for Lemma.

### 3.1 Axon, and its successor Nexis (MIT, Martin Rinard)

**What it is (F):** [arXiv 2605.01660](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.01660), v3 dated 31 July 2026.
- **Source language:** WhileLang, Fortran-inspired, with 64-bit ints, floats and booleans, one-dimensional arrays,
  `while` and `if`, and no procedures.
- **Pipeline:** three-address code, then ARM64, in Lean 4.
- **Scale:** 34 days and 38,188 lines of Lean. The paper says the code, the proofs "and the target machine model, were
  generated by the coding agent" (Q).

**What's verified, and how well it runs (F)**
- **Verified:** flattening, code generation and the certificate checker.
- **Certificate-checked** (credible compilation) rather than proven: every optimisation. That covers CP, UCE, CSE, DAE,
  LICM, RCE, FMA fusion, peephole and register allocation.
- **Speed:** on 24 Livermore kernels on an M4 Pro, Axon-O is faster than gfortran -O0 "for all but three benchmarks but
  slower than F-O2 for all but three with a geometric mean slowdown of 2.18" (Q).
- **Machine model:** the ARM model covers only the instructions the compiler uses. Its fused multiply-add semantics "do
  not match the bit level semantics of the machine" (Q). The README says the whole-pipeline capstone relies on two
  floating-point trust axioms plus `native_decide`.

**Rinard's follow-ups (Q/F)**
- **[2605.08927](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.08927), credible compilation versus verification.** Verification "requires
  roughly an order of magnitude more development effort". The agent "chooses less efficient algorithms and data
  structures for verified optimizations", and it "repeatedly implemented optimization scope reductions".
- **[2607.28928](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.28928), 31 July.** An agent-based testing-and-repair system for
  agent-generated verified compilers. It reports no evidence of reward hacking.

**Nexis (Q/F):** [arXiv 2607.23033](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.23033); [repo](https://github.com/rinard/Nexis) created
2026-07-25.
- The README describes "a machine-checked compiler for a small imperative language — from source text to a model of
  AArch64 assembly".
- Lazy Code Motion/PRE and Partial Dead-Code Elimination are "proved *optimal* (execution-count and register-pressure)".
- It ships 29 declaratively specified analyses, with generated solvers and proofs.
- The repository was "generated by a coding agent (Claude Code Opus 4.8)". It is axiom-clean and uses no Mathlib.
- It reports no performance numbers, and its README does not mention register allocation.

**Active? (F)** Yes:
- four arXiv papers between May and July 2026;
- the Axon repo was pushed on 2026-08-16, and Nexis on 2026-09-11.

**What is left for Lemma (I)**
- a real, standardised input language with an official test suite and a corpus;
- a target model validated against hardware;
- a performance bar set by a production compiler;
- growth across model releases.

The "proven passes" gap that T02 named is closed for two classic passes. **Threat: high.** Rinard uses Lemma's exact
stack: Lean, Claude Code, AArch64 and a MacBook. Replacing WhileLang with a Wasm subset is the obvious next paper.

### 3.2 Basis

**April 2026: [Building an Unverified Compiler with Agents](https://www.basis.ai/blog/verified-compiler/) (F/Q)**
- **The attempt:** a JS→Wasm compiler in Lean 4, by four Claude Code instances working from 19 March to 1 April. It came
  to 93,516 lines over 3,026 commits.
- **The result:** 14 `sorry`s remain, 13 of them in closure conversion and ANF. There are 17 axioms, all in the Wasm
  lowering semantics.
- **Their words:**
  - "The compiler ran; the proofs didn’t close."
  - "Agent-first verification tooling does not exist yet."
  - "We believe these are engineering problems, not fundamental limitations."

**October 2026: [Ship Your Interpreter](https://www.basis.ai/blog/ship-your-interpreters/), dated 2026-10-16 (F/Q)**
- **The binary:** a WHILE interpreter of 1,855 lines of C, compiled by gcc and linked against dlmalloc and newlib. The
  ELF has 25,336 instructions.
- **The proof:** it is proven against an LLM-written Lean semantics "for every possible program", running on the Sail
  RISC-V model translated to Lean.
- **The method:** a port of MachCSL to iris-lean. The refinement proof "took about a month and is about 140,000 lines of
  Lean", with only the standard axioms.
- **Next targets:** "Ship your Lua targets Lua 5.4.7 compiled to bare-metal RISC-V." "The north star is CPython."

**Active?** Yes. The October post has an HN thread
([summary](https://zeli.app/de/story/50160440)).

**What is left (I)**
- Basis has moved from compilers to *interpreter binaries* on RISC-V. It names Wasm only as one of "a handful of
  languages" that have a formal semantics.
- **The risk for Lemma is substitution, not head-on competition.** A verified Wasm *interpreter* binary ("ship your
  Wasm") would give trustworthy Wasm execution without a verified compiler, though more slowly.
- Basis's binary-level tooling is something Lemma would otherwise have to build: a machine-code symbolic executor and
  separation logic in iris-lean.

### 3.3 Arrival and Crocus, now VeriISLE in Wasmtime CI

**What it is (F)**
- [Crocus](https://cs.wellesley.edu/~avh/veri-isle-preprint.pdf) (ASPLOS 2024) and
  [Arrival](https://2025.splashcon.org/details/OOPSLA/69/Scaling-Instruction-Selection-Verification-against-Authoritative-ISA-Semantics)
  (OOPSLA 2025) verify Cranelift's ISLE lowering rules with SMT, against specifications derived from ASL.
- Arrival covers "nearly all AArch64 instruction-selection rules reachable from Wasm core" and found new bugs.

**How far it got (F/Q)**
- It now lives in-tree as
  [VeriISLE](https://github.com/bytecodealliance/wasmtime/blob/main/cranelift/isle/veri/README.md).
- It has run in Wasmtime CI since 2026-08-27 (PR #14207, "CI: run the Cranelift verifier"), with a shared incremental
  SMT cache. The solvers are cvc5 and z3.
- **The AArch64 configuration** skips "vector operations and expensive division operations", plus `i128` and narrow
  floats.
- **A mid-end run** checks the `simplify` rules.
- **Latest commit:** 2026-10-23, adding vector canary types (#14789).
- **What it doesn't check:** clifv's design notes record that the merged verifier "excludes atomics in its aarch64 and
  mid-end configurations" and "checks lowering rules, not emission (`emit.rs`)" (Q,
  [clifv PLAN.md](https://github.com/eth-act/clifv/blob/main/docs/PLAN.md)).

**What the unchecked parts still produce (F/Q).** On 2026-10-19 Wasmtime published two *critical* Cranelift advisories:
- **[GHSA-mghf-wjfp-p5xm](https://github.com/bytecodealliance/wasmtime/security/advisories/GHSA-mghf-wjfp-p5xm).** On
  aarch64, a `br_table` with more than about 256k targets "results in an incorrect conditional branch target". It
  affects "All versions of Wasmtime/Cranelift that support aarch64, since the initial release of that support in 2020".
- **[GHSA-897f-g5gm-fmxm](https://github.com/bytecodealliance/wasmtime/security/advisories/GHSA-897f-g5gm-fmxm).**
  Bit-packed SSA value indices could silently overflow and "result in arbitrary miscompilation".

Both bugs sit outside rule-level checking: one is in branch-range emission, the other in an IR data structure. That is
exactly the territory an end-to-end theorem covers (I).

**Active?** Very.

**What is left (I)**
- In Wasmtime there is still no end-to-end theorem and no proof of the Wasm→CLIF translator.
- Emission, encoding and register allocation are unverified in-tree, and SMT plus hand-written specifications are
  trusted.
- The incumbent's strategy is rule-level SMT, not end-to-end proof in Lean.

### 3.4 Talos and Tau (Cajal Technologies)

**Talos (F/Q)**
- [Talos](https://github.com/cajal-technologies/talos) is a Wasm interpreter in Lean 4, "designed for reasoning". The
  same definitions both execute programs and support proofs.
- Its README puts performance work "behind a separately proven-equivalent implementation".
- **Activity:** created 2026-05-20, last pushed 2026-10-06. It has 233 commits, 191 stars, 37 forks and 20 open PRs.
- **Licence:** AGPL-3.0.
- Cajal calls it "our open-source framework for reasoning about compiled WebAssembly, to appear at NeurIPS 2026".
- **Others already build on it:** [`verified-binaries`](https://github.com/theebayuser/verified-binaries) (August
  2026) proves that a `binary_search` compiled by rustc to Wasm at opt-level 3 is totally correct and equivalent to the
  -O0 build. The proof uses Talos and its Iris logic.

**Tau, launched 2026-10-23 ([page](https://caj.al/tau), [blog](https://caj.al/blog/introducing-tau)) (Q/F)**
- **What it claims:**
  - "Tau optimizes software at the binary level, the exact machine code a processor runs, and proves that each
    optimized binary is correct."
  - "proves in Lean that the new binary behaves exactly like the original."
  - "We built proprietary formal semantics for native x86 and ARM."
- **What it ships:** drop-in libraries.
- **Results**, each against the fastest build of the library, on Sapphire Rapids:

| Workload | Speed-up |
|---|---|
| Snappy decompression | 1.59× |
| PNG encoding (against fpnge) | 1.38× |
| DEFLATE compression (against libdeflate) | 1.30× |
| zlib decompression | 1.20× |

- It is "available today, for performance optimization only".
- **The company:** YC W26, team of 2 ([YC](https://www.ycombinator.com/companies/cajal-technologies)).

**What is left (I)**
- Tau is per-routine optimisation with a proof per binary. It is not a compiler and, as published, does not take Wasm.
- Cajal holds every ingredient of Lemma except a compiler:
  - a Wasm semantics (Talos);
  - ARM semantics;
  - proven speedups;
  - a commercial reason to "prove the compiler once" instead of proving each binary.
- **Threat: high.** Lemma's T02 slogan, "every speedup is a theorem", now reads as Cajal's tagline.

### 3.5 CertiCoq-Wasm (CertiRocq)

**What it is (F)**
- [CPP 2025](https://popl25.sigplan.org/details/CPP-2025-papers/9/CertiCoq-Wasm-A-verified-WebAssembly-backend-for-CertiCoq).
  It is a verified backend from Coq/Rocq *to* Wasm, proven against WasmCert-Coq.
- It found an unsoundness corner case in how primitive integers were implemented.
- Its case studies include a Concordium smart contract.
- Related: [Paraskevopoulou](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.20082) (February 2026) used Claude Code with Opus 4.6 to prove
  CertiRocq's ANF pass, in about 7,800 lines and about 96 hours (Q).

**Active? (F)** The [CertiRocq repo](https://github.com/CertiRocq/certirocq) was pushed on 2026-10-25.

**What is left (I)** It compiles in the opposite direction, so it is not a competitor. It does show that WasmCert-Coq
works as the target of a compiler proof, in Rocq.

### 3.6 vWasm and rWasm (CMU)

**What it is (F/Q)**
- [USENIX Security 2022](https://www.usenix.org/conference/usenixsecurity22/presentation/bosamiya).
- [vWasm](https://github.com/secure-foundations/vWasm) compiles Wasm to x86-64 on Linux, in F\*. It proves that the
  output stays inside its sandbox, and "does not guarantee that the code must execute correctly".

**Active? (F)** No. The last push was 2022-08-30. There are 3 commits on main and 35 stars.

**What is left (I)** Functional correctness, AArch64 and optimisations, which is all of Lemma.

### 3.7 CompCert

**What it is (F)**
- The verified C compiler. Its repository has `aarch64`, `arm`, `powerpc`, `riscV` and `x86` backends.
- v3.18 was released on 2026-08-30, and there were commits this week.
- AbsInt cites "six person-years of effort" and "100 000 lines of Coq"
  ([structure page](https://www.absint.com/compcert/structure.htm)).
- The proof ends at assembly. Assembly and linking are validated separately by Valex, for PowerPC only.

**What is left (I)** A Wasm front end. I found no verified Wasm→Clight translation and no verified wasm2c. So the route
"verified Wasm→Clight plus CompCert's AArch64 backend" is still unclaimed, but it is a Rocq project and would perform at
CompCert level.

### 3.8 CakeML (with Pancake and Brack)

**What it is (F)**
- A verified ML compiler in HOL4, with verified machine-code backends that include ARMv8, x86-64 and RISC-V.
- Pancake reuses CakeML's lower half for a C-like language.
- [Brack](https://popl26.sigplan.org/details/CPP-2026-papers/15/) (CPP 2026) compiles Scheme to CakeML, including
  continuations, in about 4,000 lines of HOL4.

**Active? (F)** Yes, pushed 2026-10-28.

**Wasm (F)** There is only an ANU [student project proposal](https://comp.anu.edu.au/study/projects/compiling-cakeml-to-wasm)
from 2025-11-11, to add Wasm as a *target*. Its starting step is porting a Wasm specification to HOL4.

**What is left (I)** Wasm as a *source* language, and Lean. Not a competitor. It is a reference for how to structure
verified register allocation and ARMv8 code-generation proofs.

### 3.9 Vellvm

**What it is (Q/F)** "an ongoing project aiming at the formal verification in the Rocq proof assistant of a compilation
infrastructure inspired by the LLVM compiler" ([README](https://github.com/vellvm/vellvm)).

**Active? (F)** Yes. Its 2026-10-08 meeting notes cover work on poison and freeze semantics.

**What is left (I)** It has no Wasm and no AArch64 backend, so it is not a competitor. It matters only as prior art on
undef and poison semantics, which Lemma avoids by starting from Wasm.

### 3.10 WasmCert (Rocq and Isabelle), and WasmRef

**WasmCert-Coq (F/Q)** [Repository](https://github.com/WasmCert/WasmCert-Coq).
- **Coverage:** Wasm 2.0, plus subtyping and tail calls. SIMD uses opaque opcodes evaluated through reference functions.
- **Proven:** the interpreter (WasmRef-Coq) is sound, the type checker is sound and complete, and instantiation is sound.
- **Program logic:** an Iris program logic on a branch.
- **Activity:** pushed 2026-10-13. On 2026-10-12 it merged "Fix two inconsistent axioms in wasm_parray (#99)".

**WasmCert-Isabelle (F)**
- It was [updated to Wasm 2.0](https://conf.researchr.org/details/icfp-splash-2025/webassembly-ws-2025-papers/9/Updating-WasmCert-Isabelle-to-WebAssembly-2-0)
  in 2025.
- [WasmRef-Isabelle](https://pldi23.sigplan.org/details/pldi-2023-pldi/5/WasmRef-Isabelle-A-Verified-Monadic-Interpreter-and-Industrial-Fuzzing-Oracle-for-We)
  (PLDI 2023) serves as Wasmtime's fuzzing oracle.

**What is left (I)**
- These are semantics, not compilers, and none of them is in Lean.
- The inconsistent-axiom fix, in a mature mechanisation maintained by experts, supports Basis's rule that axioms are
  worse than sorries. That argues for an axiom-audit gate.

### 3.11 Iris-Wasm

**What it is (F)**
- A [PLDI 2023](https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/3591265) program logic in Coq and Iris, for robust and modular
  verification of Wasm *programs*. It is built on WasmCert-Coq.
- The [standalone repo](https://github.com/logsem/iriswasm)'s last commit was 2025-01-30. A copy also lives as a
  WasmCert-Coq branch.

**What is left (I)** It verifies programs, not compilation, so it is not a competitor.

## 4. Neighbours T02 missed

### 4.1 clifv (Ethereum Foundation, applied cryptography team): the closest thing to Lemma that exists

**What it is (Q, [README](https://github.com/eth-act/clifv))**
- "clifv compiles Cranelift IR (CLIF) to AArch64 machine code. The compiler is written in Lean 4."
- "Register allocation is done in Lean too: a linear-scan allocator whose result is checked in Lean, with a proven Lean
  spill allocator as fallback if the check fails."
- `E2E.backend_correct_final` "states that the Arm code of a function in its scope refines the function's CLIF
  semantics".
- "The proofs contain no `sorry` and declare no axioms."
- The trust base includes Lean's kernel and compiled evaluation (`bv_decide`, `native_decide`), the CLIF semantics, the
  Arm model, rustc and cg_clif (F).

**How far it got.** From [PLAN.md](https://github.com/eth-act/clifv/blob/main/docs/PLAN.md), status as of 2026-10-04 and
2026-10-16 (F):
- **CLIF semantics.** It passes 6,072 upstream run lines, with 7 failures and 13 interpreter disagreements on record. It
  is cross-checked against Cranelift's interpreter and VeriISLE's CLIF specs.
- **Arm model.** An "LNSym port, co-simulated against qemu".
- **Instruction selection.** Cranelift's *own* ISLE program is exported to Lean and interpreted. Every lowering root rule
  in the subset is proven. The proven encoder's output is "Byte-identical to llvm-mc on 1291 functions".
- **Register allocation.** The Lean checker accepted regalloc2's output on 1056 of 1056 test functions. A proven spill
  allocator is the fallback, so the theorem doesn't trust regalloc2.
- **Mid-end.** 1,154 `simplify` rules and all 37 `simplify_skeleton` rules are proven, plus GVN, DCE and LICM.
- **Linking and binaries.**
  - There are whole-crate theorems and an ELF-level theorem (`binary_correct_exec_proven`).
  - `cargo fv build|run|test` works on real Rust crates such as serde_json, regex and sha2, falling back to cg_clif one
    function at a time.
- **Upstream bugs found:** an `atomic_cas.i32` emission bug, and false `shifts.isle` rules (84 and 88).
- **Ratchet.** CI compares output with stock Cranelift and fails on lost exact matches.
- **Performance.** "optimised output is about 1.4× Cranelift's size at `speed`" (Q).

**Not done (F/Q)**
- floats and SIMD in the backend;
- RISC-V;
- std, which is only partly compiled through it.
- The frontend (rustc and cg_clif → CLIF) "stays trusted. Removing it would be a separate, much larger project."
- vmctx parameters stay unverified ("`VMContext` is wasmtime's").

**Active? (F)**
- Created 2026-09-27 and pushed today.
- 1,451 commits: kevaundray 1,271, codygunton 176.
- 2,812 Lean modules.
- Contributors coordinate their agents through
  [AGENTS.md](https://github.com/eth-act/clifv/blob/main/AGENTS.md).

**Licence (F)** There is no LICENSE file at the repository root, so reuse needs permission (I).

**What is left for Lemma (I)**
- Everything *above* CLIF: a proven Wasm→CLIF translation, or Lemma's own front end, over a Wasm-level semantics.
- Growth beyond Cranelift. clifv's ratchet is built to *match* stock Cranelift.
- Floats and SIMD.

**This is the most important finding of the search.** Most of T02's planned work already exists, for the IR that
Wasmtime itself uses:
- the "coverage phase" (a lowering for every opcode);
- most of the proof engineering: instruction selection, encoder, regalloc checking, linking and ELF.

### 4.3 Paradigm's Solidus and its optimisation arena

**What it is (F/Q)**
- [Blog](https://www.paradigm.xyz/writing/solidus) and [repo](https://github.com/paradigmxyz/solidus), dated 2026-07-24.
- **Status:** the Yul→EVM backend is "essentially complete and formally verified", under one theorem,
  `Solidus.compile_correct`. The Solidity front end does not exist yet.
- **How it was built:** "no human read or wrote a line of its code". It took "over 1,700 hours (~10 weeks)" of Codex
  time, "~$150,000 at API rates".
- **Performance at launch:** "almost 3x as much bytecode as solc".

**The arena ([CHALLENGE.md](https://github.com/paradigmxyz/solidus/blob/main/CHALLENGE.md)) (Q/F)**
- **The challenge:** "make the compiled contracts cheaper … while the theorem still proves".
- **The rules:**
  - the theorem files are frozen and hash-checked;
  - `#print axioms` must report exactly `[propext, Classical.choice, Quot.sound]`;
  - a private runner does the scoring;
  - a new record must improve on the old one by at least 0.1%.
- **The ledger** ([records](https://github.com/paradigmxyz/solidus/blob/arena/benchmarks/records.json)):
  - It went from 90,698,381 gas (record-0, 2026-07-22) to 60,779,473 gas (record-13, 2026-07-30). That is **−33.0% in 8
    days**.
  - The 13 records came from 6 contributors. At least one came from a `codex/…` branch.
  - **There has been no record since 2026-07-30.**

**What is left (I)**
- Solidus *is* Lemma's mechanism, applied to EVM: a frozen theorem, an axiom audit, a performance ratchet and untrusted
  automated contributors. So the mechanism is not Lemma's asset; at most, the Wasm/AArch64 instance is.
- The three months of silence after one fast week is an early data point on how quickly a proof-gated ratchet plateaus.
  It may also just reflect the season format.

### 4.6 The per-artifact alternative: prove the binary, not the compiler (F)

These projects prove each artifact instead of the compiler:
- **Cajal's Tau:** x86 and ARM binaries.
- **Basis:** a RISC-V interpreter binary.
- **[EquiVM](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.26306)** (Lazaropoulos and Paraskevopoulou; JACM, v2 2026-10-20): refinement

- **Janus:** Go binaries. Its README names Nethermind's LeanCIL as the .NET counterpart.
- **verified-binaries:** Wasm, via Talos.
- **[Aqueduct](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.30188)** (Ye, Ghai, Qin, Song, Kong; 2026-10-20). Agents "produce faster
  proved rewrites for 156" of 189 Verina functions, and "their 92 proved rewrites speed up 47 of 51 application
  scenarios" (Q).

**(I)** None of these competes in kind. Together they are the substitute: as proofs get cheaper, "prove each binary"
needs "prove the compiler once" less. That is a VALUE question, flagged here for the VALUE report.

### 4.7 Translation validation and verified-rewrite infrastructure (F)

- **[Trivet](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.19583)** (Waterloo, 2026-09-17). LLMs plus Lean, with "every successful verdict
  checked by the Lean kernel". On 148 LLVM transformations it verifies or refutes 147.
- **[arm-tv](https://2025.splashcon.org/details/OOPSLA/169/Translation-Validation-for-LLVM-s-AArch64-Backend)** (Utah,
  OOPSLA 2025). Alive2-based translation validation for LLVM's AArch64 backend. It found 40–45 new miscompilation bugs;
  the sources differ on the count.
- **Lean-MLIR and VeIR** (Grosser, Cambridge).
  - [EuroLLVM talk, 2026-04-14](https://www.llvm.org/devmtg/2026-04/slides/technical_talk/technical_talk_cicolini_kuhn.pdf):
    158 lowering patterns from LLVM IR to RV64, proven with `bv_decide` against RISC-V semantics derived from Sail.
  - 66 of 80 in-fragment GlobalISel combiner rules are mechanised (82.5%).
  - llvm-mca estimates the verified selector's output at +18.5% cycles against GlobalISel and +207.9% against
    SelectionDAG.
  - [VeIR](https://github.com/opencompl/veir) (pushed today) is an MLIR-style compiler infrastructure in Lean with its
    own RISC-V instruction selection; `llc` still allocates the registers.
- **[Jitterbug](https://www.usenix.org/conference/osdi20/presentation/nelson)** (OSDI 2020). Verified BPF JITs. The
  broader Serval effort found 30+ JIT bugs across x86, arm and riscv. It is old evidence that per-instruction lowering
  from bytecode to native code is tractable to verify.
- **(I)** None of these targets Wasm→AArch64 end to end. Lean-MLIR is the academic group most likely to add an AArch64
  dialect.

### 4.8 Proof-gated performance ratchets elsewhere (F)

- **[lean-zip](https://github.com/kim-em/lean-zip).**
  - DEFLATE written "entirely by loosely supervised AIs", with about 32k lines of proof. PRs merge only if the round-trip

  - It "dominates the zlib C reference implementation" but trails libdeflate by about 2–10×.
  - Its dashboard has been frozen since August 2026.
- **Lean FRO's Hex** ([Y4-1 roadmap](https://lean-lang.org/fro/roadmap/y4-1), September 2026 to February 2027).

  - The roadmap has no verified-compiler item.
- **turbo-lean** (Th0rgal, October 2026). A "Pinned Formal Spec vs. Segregated Optimization Plugin" design, run in

- **(I)** A "ratchet under a frozen theorem" is a 2026 genre.

## 5. Where exactly the opening is

### Taken: do not build these as novel
1. A Lean-proven backend from Cranelift's IR to AArch64 bytes (clifv). It includes instruction selection from
   Cranelift's own ISLE rules, a proven encoder, checked register allocation and proven mid-end rules.
2. Agent-built, verified, optimising compilers to AArch64 in Lean, with classic passes proven (Axon, Nexis).
3. Lean AArch64 models validated against hardware or qemu (LNSym, and clifv's port of it).
4. Spec-tested Lean Wasm semantics (Talos, plum-umd/lean-wasm).
5. The ratchet mechanism with untrusted automated contributors (the Solidus arena, lean-zip, turbo-lean).
6. The pitch "faster code, proved in Lean": commercially (Tau) and academically (Aqueduct).
7. Rule-level verification of Cranelift's AArch64 lowering, running in CI (VeriISLE).

### Left: the narrow opening, stated as one theorem (I)

> For every validated Wasm module *M* in a subset *S* (the integer core, then floats), the AArch64 bytes emitted
> for *M* refine *M*. The reference is a Lean Wasm semantics that passes the official spec tests for *S*. Refinement
> covers results, linear memory, the host-call trace, and traps at exactly the spec's trap points, with explicit bounds
> checks. And every optimisation that makes those bytes faster than the previous record was proposed by a model and
> admitted only while that theorem still proved.

**No artifact has the top of this theorem** (Wasm, rather than CLIF, C or a toy language) **together with its bottom**
(AArch64 bytes). **And nobody runs the growth half against Cranelift or V8 on Wasm.** The sub-gaps:
- **Proof of the Wasm→IR translation.** This covers:
  - heap bounds;
  - tables and `call_indirect` signature checks;
  - globals and `memory.grow`;
  - traps;
  - the vmctx layout.
- **Explicit bounds checks plus proven bounds-check elimination.** On 64-bit, Cranelift sidesteps this with guard pages,
  so the proven version is a performance lever that needs nothing from the MMU (I).
- **Floats and SIMD** on a proven AArch64 backend, including NaN behaviour.
- **A beat-the-incumbent ratchet** that attributes each record to a model. This is the tailwind demo T02 wanted.

### Who could close it, and how fast (E)

| Group | Has | Missing | Chance within 12 months (E) |
|---|---|---|---|
| EF clifv team | CLIF→AArch64 end to end; the LNSym port; agents at about 45 commits a day | A reason to care about Wasm (their route is Rust) | Low to medium |
| Cajal | Talos, ARM semantics, Tau, funding | A decision to build a compiler product | Medium |
| Rinard's lab | Agent-built Lean compilers to ARM64 | A real input language | Medium |
| Basis | Binary-level Lean proofs on RISC-V | Compilers, AArch64 | Low for a compiler; medium for a verified Wasm *interpreter* |
| Bytecode Alliance / Arrival team | VeriISLE in CI | Interest in end-to-end proofs in Lean | Low |

Overall, I put the chance that someone publishes an end-to-end proof from Wasm to AArch64 within 12 months at 35–60%
(E).

### The strongest case for "Taken", and why I stop one step short

- **The case for Taken (I).**
  - T02 described an asset made of semantics, a proven backend and a ratchet. All three exist in pieces, most of them
    further along than Lemma's 6-week plan.
  - The remaining front end is weeks of work for the clifv team or for Cajal.
- **Why not Taken (F/I).**
  - No artifact proves anything about a *Wasm module* all the way down to *AArch64 bytes* today.
  - The two critical advisories of 2026-10-19 (aarch64 `br_table`, SSA bit-packing) show that the incumbent's unverified
    seams still produce critical bugs in 2026.
  - So the opening exists, but what is open is **a front end and a growth loop, not a compiler**.

## TL;DR

1. **Verdict: Narrow opening.** Only two things are open: the Wasm end of the theorem and a ratchet that beats Cranelift. The proven backend below the IR is effectively taken.
2. EF's clifv (27 Sep to today, about 1,450 commits) proves Cranelift IR → AArch64 ELF bytes end to end in Lean, covering Cranelift's own ISLE rules, the encoder, checked regalloc and 1,154 mid-end rules. It has no Wasm, floats or SIMD, and it aims to match Cranelift, not beat it.
3. The rest of T02's absences are gone too: Nexis proves optimisations, LNSym is a hardware-checked Lean Arm model, Solidus ran Lemma's ratchet on EVM (−33% gas in 8 days), and Cajal's Tau (23 Oct) sells binaries proved faster in Lean on x86 and ARM, next to its Lean Wasm semantics Talos.
4. Nobody has proved a theorem from a Wasm module down to AArch64 bytes (traps, bounds, tables, vmctx). Wasmtime's critical aarch64 `br_table` and SSA bit-packing advisories (19 Oct) show those unverified seams still matter.
5. Before building: ask clifv and Cajal about Wasm (1 day), spend 2 days on a Wasm→CLIF front-end proof spike, and expect the window to close within about 3–12 months (E).
