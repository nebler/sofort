## Round 2 — moonshots on the capsule idea — `moonshots/`
The question: which ideas built on the capsule seed (a whole running program as a small, forkable, movable QuickJS-in-Wasm file, plus jitk/GrowOS agent-grown syscalls) have 100–1000x ambition, stay in the Cloudflare + TypeScript universe, and get more valuable as models get stronger instead of being eaten by them? Seven lenses each generated 8–9 ideas and ranked them: archaeology, biology, physics, economics, planet-scale Cloudflare, self-growing software and next-model survival. Overall verdict: every lens chose one narrowed bet, rated go or go-with-changes. The bets were an upgrade clinic for live-occupied software, immune repair colonies, a failure-search gate, notarised computation receipts, a "One → Eight → One" swarm adapter, fault-grown Node, and Oracle Foundry. The reports converge on four points: (1) the heap image is transport, not the moat; (2) authority, money and effects must be conserved outside the snapshot by a trusted broker; (3) the durable value is independent verification against reality (oracles, receipts, invariants); (4) every capsule-specific claim must beat a plain source + inputs + journal replay baseline, or be dropped.

### 01-archaeology: Computing archaeology: the computer as a living, forkable artifact
Lens: great old computing ideas that were right too early (Smalltalk, Lisp machines, Multics, KeyKOS/EROS, System/38, PJama, Grasshopper, Telescript, Emerald, Sprite, MOSIX, Condor, Erlang, Plan 9, Tandem, Xanadu, NLS, HyperCard, CICS, Linda, JavaSpaces, CAP, E, MirageOS, exokernels, Croquet), each mapped onto a portable MB capsule driven by strong agents. Headline (I): the resurrection worth having is "a computer you can own, cite, visit, change, and disagree with". Strong models change the labor economics but do not repeal distributed systems, and the recurring failure mode is that ordinary representations (journal, source+inputs, JSON state, notebook) win. Nine bets, all sharing one design contract: images are dependency-bearing artifacts; pause only at supported await boundaries; fork state but explicitly allocate effects through a trusted broker; publish to R2 before advancing the DO pointer; keep bulk data and compute outside the heap; be honest about what the destination can do. Reused measurements: **M** 99,854-byte gzip minimal checkpoint resumed in Node/Chromium/workerd/Dynamic Worker; **M** 1.5–6.4 MB compressed for representative examples; **M** 5–11× V8 CPU on warm glue. Scale correction: **E** 1M × 3 MB = 3 TB, 100M = 300 TB. The ONE bet is #2, "software that can be rebuilt while people are using it" (an upgrade clinic with 30 held-out occupied states). Verdict: **go**, as a bounded two-week experiment with an explicit stop rule if the capsule does not beat source+input replay.

#### 1. Executable citations: a link into the computation behind a claim
- **What:** Attach a forkable continuation, source-version links and an input journal to an analytical claim, so anyone can resume the exact analysis with one assumption changed and a "disagree here" button. The 100× version (I/E) is 100× more individually inspectable claims per analyst, with agents hunting counterexamples. A changed source marks dependent claims stale rather than overwriting them.
- **How it would work:** A TS app analyzing a public incident dataset parks just before a disputed tool input, after building a cyclic index, closures and recorded tool results. The image and journal go to R2. A browser Worker restores the image and forks two alternative replies, and an edge executor restores the same parent. The demo shows the divergence point, source hashes, an agent-generated counterexample within a declared envelope, and zero live writes.
- **Evidence / numbers:** No new numbers. First proof takes 10 working days, plus a week-two test with five unfamiliar engineers measuring time-to-challenge against a notebook/source+inputs baseline. Sources: [Xanadu](https://www.xanadu.net/zigzag/fw99/XUmodel.html), [NLS 1968](https://www.dougengelbart.org/content/view/374/), [Binder](https://mybinder.readthedocs.io/en/latest/introduction.html).
- **Prior art / who has it:** Xanadu, Engelbart NLS and HyperCard as lineage. Binder (shareable executable environments) as the main substitute. Notebooks and reproducible research are "occupied ground".
- **Verdict:** KEPT (#2): "build the public artifact". It is the clearest share-and-fork experience and the best public companion to #2. Weakness: heavy notebook/replay prior art.
- **Kill test / first proof:** **E** In the five-person comparison, the capsule fails to cut median time to a correct, inspectable challenge by at least 2× versus the notebook/source+inputs baseline.

#### 2. Software that can be rebuilt while people are using it
- **What:** Make an upgrade a reviewable family of migrated running programs, tested against actual old states and pending work before one future gets production authority. The 100× version (I/E) is 100× more safely maintained application variants per engineer, with an upgrade shipped like a PR carrying counterexamples and evidence.
- **How it would work:** Seed 30 held-out small workflow worlds with different object-graph shapes and pending order tasks, with the engine fixed and a stable dispatcher/object-root contract. An agent proposes a migration and new handlers for an address/schema change. The tests cover aliases, cycles, missing fields, already-applied effects and delayed replies, judged against independently authored invariants. Old continuations keep old code until a declared boundary, and versioned reply adapters handle old pending calls. The recorded suffix is shadowed through old and new worlds, then one candidate is promoted under a fenced DO writer epoch. A deliberately invalid migration must be rejected.
- **Evidence / numbers:** First proof takes two weeks with 30 held-out variants. It is compared against source+input replay or an explicit-state baseline using the same agent and budget, measured on human fixes, lost progress and interruption. Crucial limit (I): no rewriting of arbitrary suspended stack frames and no new engine into incompatible heap bytes. If every useful upgrade needs semantic export or replay, the live image must be re-evaluated. Sources: [PJama](https://citeseerx.ist.psu.edu/document?doi=2f5cc550a2e616a7b1fa9865bc9a9013ef97bbb7&repid=rep1&type=pdf), [Erlang code loading](https://www.erlang.org/doc/system/code_loading.html), [Squeak](https://smalltalkzoo.computerhistory.org/papers/Back%20to%20the%20Future.PDF).
- **Prior art / who has it:** Squeak and Genera (live occupied environments). PJama (named class evolution as the critical barrier). Erlang (explicit old/new code rules). `pi-world` already does "an agent edits a heap", so the differentiation must be verified evolution across many living variants.
- **Verdict:** KEPT (#1): **the ONE bet**. "PJama's hardest objection is the actual experiment". It grows more consequential as models create more software. Stop before building a platform if retained state does not make upgrades materially easier or safer than source+input replay.
- **Kill test / first proof:** **E** The proposal-and-gate process cannot complete 90% of the 30 held-out cases without hand-written per-case fixes while preserving all invariants. That would mean migration labor still scales with the number of worlds.

#### 3. Visiting agents: send the unfinished question to the data
- **What:** A data owner hosts an incoming capsule under a locally chosen namespace and output policy, so the computation visits data that is never copied to a central service. The 100× version (I/E) is one problem branching into visiting programs at 100 independent owners, which replace data lakes and per-pair integrations.
- **How it would work:** Three separately configured TS hosts: a browser with a local inventory fixture and two Workers with different private fixtures and tool schemas. A paused supply-allocation analysis visits each one, with capabilities rebound locally. Owners permit bounded aggregates and reject row export, arbitrary egress and overspending. An agent adapts a tool schema and the host validates the adapter. Privacy rule (I): after reading confidential data, the continuation stays at that owner unless the owner authorizes exporting the whole image. Otherwise only approved outputs leave and a fresh public-state capsule continues, which gives up unrestricted mobility.
- **Evidence / numbers:** First proof takes 1–2 weeks and publishes network traces, output bytes, restore identity and a rejected malicious query. It is compared against a normal remote-query/connector build. A raw heap can leak private input, including freed bytes. No claim of confidentiality from the host or of cryptographic proof. Sources: [Telescript](https://bitsavers.org/pdf/generalMagic/Telescript_Language_Reference_Oct95.pdf), [Emerald](https://web.cecs.pdx.edu/~black/publications/Emerald%20Mobility%20TOCS.pdf), [Plan 9](https://www.usenix.org/publications/compsystems/1995/sum_pike.pdf).
- **Prior art / who has it:** General Magic Telescript (agents/places/permits), Emerald mobility, Plan 9 namespaces, E object capabilities.
- **Verdict:** KEPT (#3): "ambitious second stage; test host participation before infrastructure". The biggest institutional moonshot with the hardest deployment boundary. Without a useful answer it is "an impressive transport demo searching for a reason to exist".
- **Kill test / first proof:** **E** Two of three representative data owners cannot offer a useful result without granting outputs or heap exports they would refuse in their normal workflow.

#### 4. NonStop repair: a failure becomes a farm of proposed recoveries
- **What:** A supervisor turns a failed capsule into isolated candidate repairs and tests them against the failure and against unaffected histories. It promotes one, and speculative children never get production write access. The 100× version (I/E) explores 100 materially different recoveries instead of 100 identical retries, aiming for 100× more incidents resolved per operator. Explicitly not an "AI SRE" chat window.
- **How it would work:** Three tiny TS services with 10 predeclared connector-change failures and an independent unaffected trace corpus. Snapshot at the last good boundary and keep the failure journal separate. Generate up to five candidates per failure on a fixed model/spend budget, patching only through a declared replaceable handler boundary. Replay the failing input, then the unaffected suffixes, and check business invariants and operation-ledger identities. A DO supervisor bounds restart intensity, fences the old writer and dispatches one tested continuation. Crashes are injected before and after promotion.
- **Evidence / numbers:** First proof takes 10 days. The model stays outside the trusted acceptance decision. Sources: [Armstrong 2003](https://erlang.org/download/armstrong_thesis_2003.pdf), [OTP supervision](https://www.erlang.org/doc/system/sup_princ.html), [Gray 1985](https://pages.cs.wisc.edu/~remzi/Classes/739/Papers/gray-why-do-computers-stop-85.pdf).
- **Prior art / who has it:** Erlang/OTP supervisors, Tandem NonStop process pairs. A debugger plus a normal deployment pipeline is a strong substitute.
- **Verdict:** RUNNER-UP (#4): "promising narrow application, strong risk of substitutability". Demoted because it easily becomes ordinary automated incident repair.
- **Kill test / first proof:** **E** On the fixed 10-failure set, capsule-assisted repair reduces neither human intervention nor recovery time compared with restart-from-source plus the same agent, traces, tests and budget.

#### 5. Executable agreements: many proposals, one authorized commitment
- **What:** Represent a multi-party negotiation as forkable continuations whose proposed effects become real only through an explicitly participating transaction/commit broker. The 100× version (I/E) has agents exploring 100 contingent plans across suppliers and calendars before anything commits ("buy this combination only if these conditions still hold"). This is a computational protocol, not legal enforceability.
- **How it would work:** One DO-backed broker holds transactional fictional stock and budget records, and two offer-producing Workers send real messages. A guest explores 20 order combinations, pauses for a human choice, and commits one against versioned reservations. The broker atomically records the winner, the resource changes and one outbox intent for an idempotent mock downstream. Tests cover duplicate winners, expiry, stale reservations, restored old writers, and a crash after downstream apply but before ack. A non-idempotent mock surfaces "outcome unknown" instead of retrying.
- **Evidence / numbers:** First proof takes two weeks. Cloned authority cannot clone funds: the budget is conserved across forks. Proves local transaction semantics, not global SaaS atomicity. Sources: [CICS syncpoints](https://www.ibm.com/docs/en/cics-ts/6.x?topic=work-synchronization-points), [JavaSpaces](https://svn.apache.org/repos/asf/river/site/trunk/content/release-doc/2.2.2/specs/html/js-spec.html), [Miller 2006](https://www.erights.org/talks/thesis/index.html).
- **Prior art / who has it:** IBM CICS units of work, JavaSpaces transactions and leases, Telescript and E authority models.
- **Verdict:** RUNNER-UP (#5): "research, not universal external rollback". Powerful, but it depends on participating resource systems.
- **Kill test / first proof:** **E** One adversarial run in the crash/expiry/fork schedule produces two committed winners for one reservation or exceeds the parent's budget.

#### 6. A personal mainframe that outlives its providers
- **What:** Give a person or small organization an exportable federation of continuing services whose identity and progress survive changes of host, software vendor and model provider. The 100× version (I/E) is 100× more useful long-lived automations per nontechnical owner without 100× the administration. The estate is portable manifests and bounded images, with bulk data owned separately.
- **How it would work:** Three interdependent duties with real guest-local state: renew an item, reconcile a public ledger fixture, and wait for an approval. Export their images, engine artifacts, manifest, journal and blob refs. Shut down the original Node host so its endpoint errors, then import into a second host and a browser inspector. Renew authority explicitly and complete the tasks without the original service. A grant revoked before import must stay revoked. Show one engine/app upgrade via a specified migration or qualified replay.
- **Evidence / numbers:** First proof takes 1–2 weeks. Hard limit (I): "forever" is a maintenance commitment. No eternally runnable vulnerable engine; provenance does not make imported state safe or truthful. Willingness to pay for continuity is unproven. Sources: [Multics](https://www.mit.edu/~Saltzer/publications/f7y/f7y.html), [EROS](https://www.usenix.org/legacy/event/usenix02/full_papers/shapiro/shapiro.pdf), [System/38](https://gordonbell.azurewebsites.net/computer_structures_principles_and_examples/csp0549.htm).
- **Prior art / who has it:** Multics, IBM System/38 → IBM i, KeyKOS/EROS (CapROS), Grasshopper.
- **Verdict:** RUNNER-UP (#7): "ownership requirement, weak first standalone business". It has the strongest ownership philosophy but weak immediate proof of demand.
- **Kill test / first proof:** **E** Any required program, engine or data dependency in the three-duty export can only be fetched from the disabled original service. A separate demand test is needed later to show customers value occupied-state export over plain data portability.

#### 7. Shared computational rooms that people and agents can fork together
- **What:** Synchronize people and agents around a deterministic running model, with branches that keep the group's exact computational context so alternatives are directly comparable. The 100× version (I/E) replaces slide decks and chats with a room holding the executable system, where 100 alternatives can be explored from one shared state. The room sleeps in R2 and reopens anywhere.
- **How it would work:** A small TS incident-response simulation runs on three browser replicas with a fixed QuickJS/Wasm model, and a DO orders the events. Model and tool calls happen once at the broker and enter the ordered input journal (the LLM is never invoked per replica). Replicas report semantic state/output digests at declared boundaries; heap bytes need not match. The proof forks after one failure, lets an agent propose a response and reconnects a dropped participant from an R2 checkpoint. A selected action is promoted as a new event after precondition checks against authoritative state, without merging heaps.
- **Evidence / numbers:** First proof takes 10 days. Sources: [Croquet 2003](https://tinlizzie.org/VPRIPapers/tr2003001_croq_collab.pdf), [TeaTime 2005](https://oneofus.la/have-emacs-will-hack/files/2005_Teatime_Framework_Design.pdf), [Engelbart 1962](https://www.dougengelbart.org/content/view/138/).
- **Prior art / who has it:** Croquet/TeaTime, Engelbart NLS. Substitutes: CRDT documents, normal multiplayer apps, shared notebooks (whenever only document state matters).
- **Verdict:** RUNNER-UP (#6): "demo after a domain is chosen; avoid a generic metaverse". It risks becoming a multiplayer simulation demo.
- **Kill test / first proof:** **E** In a 10,000-event reconnect/reorder/duplicate-delivery trial, replicas disagree at a committed boundary without detecting it and stopping before the action is published.

#### 8. A planetary exchange for unfinished computation
- **What:** Make a leased, verifiable continuation the tradeable unit of a distributed search market, so workers trade real progress instead of rebuilding tasks from prompts. The 100× version (I/E) is a frontier of a million dormant continuations claimed by browsers and edge workers, aiming for 100× more useful search frontier retained per coordinator. "Renting idle browser CPU" is the low-value version.
- **How it would work:** One bounded repair-search workload whose partial state holds a cyclic constraint graph and a captured continuation, with a cheap independent checker. Infrastructure: a DO per work partition for lease epochs and claims, R2 for images, and Queues as at-least-once wakeups. 100 logical branches run across Node workers, controlled browser workers and one hosted Worker. 30% of assignments are evicted, and only current-epoch outputs are accepted. Results publish only after an independent checker or a trusted replay, because browsers can fabricate a "completed" capsule.
- **Evidence / numbers:** First proof takes two weeks, with a predeclared CPU+bytes cost model. If verification costs as much as recomputing, the exchange loses its central advantage. Sources: [Sprite 1991](https://web.stanford.edu/~ouster/cgi-bin/papers/migration-spe.pdf), [MOSIX 1999](https://www.cs.columbia.edu/~orenl/papers/mosix4linux.pdf), [HTCondor checkpointing](https://research.cs.wisc.edu/htcondor/wiki-archive/pages/HowToCheckpoint/).
- **Prior art / who has it:** Sprite migration, MOSIX home deputy, Condor checkpointing, Linda tuple spaces, JavaSpaces. Existing queues and batch runners win if the frontier is just prompts or compact data.
- **Verdict:** RUNNER-UP (#9, "deliberately ranked low"): "demote until one workload wins the cost comparison". It risks losing to queues and cheap recomputation.
- **Kill test / first proof:** **E** At equal model-call budget and the defined eviction rate, capsule transfer fails to improve verified useful progress per total CPU-and-transfer cost by 2× over source+input or semantic-checkpoint scheduling.

#### 9. A computer foundry that grows the smallest environment a program needs
- **What:** Agents synthesize a program family's minimal host environment and adapters from executable contracts. Protection, credentials and acceptance tests stay outside that generated environment. The 100× version (I/E) is 100× more specialized computational habitats (legacy-data instruments, transient protocol interpreters), maintained as compatibility "species" against independent oracles.
- **How it would work:** Keep the QuickJS/Wasm engine and trusted broker fixed. Take three small generated programs that need different clock, encoding, file-view or query-tool conventions. An agent synthesizes guest-side adapters from a visible behavioral contract. Run the family in Node, browser and workerd against held-out host fixtures and known-bad mutants. Add one new behavior and regenerate the adapters without changing the broker allowlist.
- **Evidence / numbers:** First proof takes 1–2 weeks. Explicitly not a new kernel, not a formal equivalence proof, not automatic npm compatibility, and not an x86 jitk port. Sources: [Mirage 2013](https://anil.recoil.org/papers/2013-asplos-mirage/), [Exokernel 1995](https://doi.org/10.1145/224056.224076), [CAP 1979](https://www.cl.cam.ac.uk/events/50%2B5/assets/pdf/cap.pdf).
- **Prior art / who has it:** MirageOS/unikernels, exokernels, the CAP capability machine, and local GrowOS (already grows a narrow syscall subset against Linux with held-out gates). Another agent-written syscall would only repeat GrowOS.
- **Verdict:** RUNNER-UP (#8): "keep as an enabling research path; do not duplicate GrowOS". It risks recreating an entire systems-research agenda.
- **Kill test / first proof:** **E** Adding the new behavior requires expanding the trusted protection/broker core rather than adding a bounded untrusted adapter. If so, defer the foundry instead of growing it into a general OS project.

#### Rejected in this report
- **Another snapshot library, durable workflow engine, or sandbox**: ruled out of scope up front, since heap snapshots, durable execution and forks already have substantial prior art (quickjs-wasi, quickjs-rs, XS, Golem, TimeTravelJS).
- **"Git for heaps" / merging heap bytes**: pointer layouts and external actions admit no general useful merge. Merge application operations, proposals or journal events after precondition checks instead.
- **"An agent edits a persistent heap" as the pitch**: already explored by `pi-world`, so it is inadequate differentiation for #2.
- **Rolling a heap back to roll the world back**: restoring a heap cannot undo calendar invites, messages or non-participating payment APIs.
- **Global atomic transactions across R2, DO and external APIs**: none is assumed. A crash may leave an orphan image to collect.
- **Arbitrary instruction-level preemption, or restoring native jitk RAM into a browser**: not promised. Pause only at supported await boundaries.
- **"Any Cloudflare city" as a placement guarantee**: an architectural reach goal only. Workers run near ingress, DO location hints are best effort, and R2 location is not hot replication.
- **Signed checkpoints as proof of faithful execution, or Wasm as confidentiality from the host**: provenance is not proof of honest execution, and software isolation is not confidentiality.
- **Generic CPU sharing / renting idle browser CPU**: the weak version of #8.
- **An "AI SRE" chat window**: explicitly not the ambition of #4.
- **Ordinary AI-generated forms/cards**: do not by themselves justify capsules (HyperCard row).
- **A generic metaverse / 3D client**: 3D is not essential to Croquet's valuable idea (#7).
- **Another agent-written syscall**: repeats existing GrowOS work (#9).
- **Nine simultaneous products**: the bets are alternative experiments. Demoted bets stay only as sources of requirements.

### 02-biology: Biology, evolution and artificial life: software populations with inherited lives
Lens: biology, evolution and artificial life (Core War, Tierra, Avida, Koza GP, immune systems, quality-diversity, POET, endosymbiosis, morphogenesis, dormancy, metabolism), mapped onto capsules treated as "domesticated" program populations. A trusted host controls resources, division and effects. Headline (I): the opportunity is "software that inherits experience and earns the right to continue". A capsule carries an algorithm plus its cache, partial solution, failure history and pending continuation, and stronger models supply better mutations while independent execution decides what deserves resources. Cheap replication alone has no useful objective, and selecting for copy count "would optimize a fork bomb". There are no new M results. Reused numbers: **M-prior** 99,854 gzip bytes for a restored parked QuickJS promise; **M-prior** 1.5–6.4 MB compressed and 5–11× interpreter overhead. The ONE bet is **immune repair colonies for stateful API ingestion workflows**, limited at first to policy changes after explicit checkpoints. Verdict: go-with-changes (the report gives no explicit go label). It bets on one incident-to-survivor demo plus an honest capsule-vs-replay comparison, and drops the organism substrate if replay/JSON/source baselines match it.

#### Domesticated-population substrate (framing): genome, phenotype, environment, life cycle
- **What:** The shared design that makes the eight ideas meaningful. Each program keeps three separately versioned records. The **genome** is source, policy/module hashes and the state schema. The **phenotype** is the interpreter image plus learned state and pending continuation. The **environment record** holds the journal cursor, evaluator version, workload, grants, budgets and owner lease. A heap hash alone cannot reconstruct or authorize a living program.
- **How it would work:**
  - **Life cycle.** Division references an immutable parent image, gives the child a fresh identity and splits a parent-funded allowance; each child still needs its own memory, since there is no resident copy-on-write in browser/Node Wasm. Migration moves ownership by lease epoch and rebinds tools. Sporulation stores the image and its dependencies, then frees memory. Death revokes the lease. Apoptosis is a cooperative early exit, with host termination as the backstop.
  - **Mutation.** Mutation installs a candidate policy function through the guest engine, at quiescence, into a fixed event loop's policy registry. It never edits heap bytes at random. Recombination swaps typed modules or exported state, never concatenated heaps. Lineage is recorded as a DAG.
  - **"Epigenetics".** Means inherited non-code adaptation: same source, different caches or thresholds.
  - **Platform stack.** TS controller, Queues, R2 for images and journals, sharded DOs for population metadata and leases, QuickJS/Wasm in Workers or Dynamic Workers for short slices, and a trusted host LLM adapter.
  - **Common evaluation rule.** Candidates cannot write the grader, hidden cases, receipts, ledger or production authorization. Tools are read-only or simulated during selection. Only a trusted promoter grants a new effect lease.
  - **Ranking-changing evaluation.** Compare source plus fresh execution, source plus explicit state/journal replay, and full capsule state, with model, candidate count and spend held constant. A strong one-shot model plus a good checker is a required baseline. A finite archive is not open-endedness.
- **Evidence / numbers:**
  - Ancestors (F): Core War (1984), Tierra (Ray 1991/92; Ray himself separates his organisms from useful application programs), Avida (Nature 2003; stepping stones), Koza GP (1992). Kundu and Peterson 2010 is the yeast GAL-memory finding behind the epigenetics analogy (F for the finding, I for the software analogy).
  - Cloudflare limits (F, checked 2026-10-27): 128 MB per isolate including Wasm; Dynamic Workers allow 4 in-flight children per Worker request and 10 per DO I/O context.
  - Reused **M-prior**: 99,854 gzip bytes; 1.5–6.4 MB; 5–11×; freed payloads remain in image bytes.
  - Sources: [Tierra 1991](https://tomray.me/pubs/alife2/Ray1991AnApproachToTheSynthesisOfLife.pdf), [Avida 2003](https://www.nature.com/articles/nature01568), [Dynamic Workers limits](https://developers.cloudflare.com/dynamic-workers/platform/limits/).
- **Prior art / who has it:** Core War, Tierra, Avida, Koza genetic programming. GrowOS's proposal/hidden-probe/acceptance-gate discipline is borrowed, with an explicit "no second kernel-growth project under a biological name".
- **Verdict:** Framing, not a bet. "Organism" is an engineering model. Replication must follow externally verified utility.
- **Kill test / first proof:** n/a (each idea's proof is estimated at 1–2 weeks on this common substrate).

#### 1. Immune repair colonies: recover from the exact failing life
- **What:** Capture a failing service's last valid state, clone it into competing repair attempts, and keep the independently verified survivors as "recovery memory". The 100× version (I): every long-lived integration carries a repertoire of validated recovery phenotypes, so an outage anywhere produces reusable repair experience for compatible services elsewhere, including unusual states that source-only tests never reach.
- **How it would work:**
  - Days 1–3 (E): a resumable pagination/normalization workflow with a Map, duplicate-detection history and a pending provider reply, plus six documented fault classes (schema drift, missing fields, changed pagination). A held-out fixture set is frozen first. Only the normalizer/retry policy may change.
  - Days 4–7 (E): fork up to 16 children per incident, install model-proposed policies and evaluate them against independent expected records. A deletion/empty-output mutant must fail. Successes are kept as bounded "memory cells".
  - Days 8–10 (E): revive a successful phenotype on a second host and run related unseen faults.
  - Baselines: source-only repair, journal replay, JSON checkpoint recovery, and fixed retries plus rollback.
- **Evidence / numbers:**
  - F: Forrest et al. 1994 (negative selection, practical application unresolved); CLONALG 2002; GenProg 2012 patched 55 of 105 defects; Qi et al. 2015 found many such patches wrong or functionality-removing.
  - **E** success target: at least 2× lower median end-to-end recovery time than the best baseline on held-out stateful incidents, with no accepted semantic failure.
  - Sources: [Forrest 1994](https://conferences.computer.org/sp/pdfs/sp/1994/00044264.pdf), [GenProg 2012](https://www.cs.cmu.edu/~clegoues/docs/legoues-icse2012-genprog-preprint.pdf), [Qi et al. 2015](https://dspace.mit.edu/entities/publication/f994ba82-810b-4ea9-93b6-1573434eae3e).
- **Prior art / who has it:** Artificial immune systems (Forrest; de Castro and von Zuben's CLONALG) and automated program repair (GenProg). I: repair never lacked a metaphor; it lacked reliable correctness evidence, and capsules do not fix a bad oracle. Buyers: integration vendors, workflow platforms, SRE teams.
- **Verdict:** KEPT (#1): **the ONE bet**, scoped to stateful API ingestion workflows with policy changes after explicit checkpoints. It has the clearest buyer and the strongest reason to preserve an exact life, since a rare failing state is expensive to rebuild. It also makes the strongest video. Later additions: ecotype diversity and spore retention, then POET to grow the incident curriculum.
- **Kill test / first proof:** **E/I** Kill the capsule product if the best replay/JSON/source-repair baseline reaches the same held-out recovery quality within 20% of total recovery time and cost. Any accepted "repair" that suppresses required output invalidates the run.

#### 2. Software ecotypes: a service that fits its habitat
- **What:** Maintain a diverse population of stateful service specialists and route each workload to the lineage that fits it. The 100× version (I): millions of per-tenant, per-workload ecotypes adapt cache, retry, batching and parsing behavior under the same external contract. Compatible specialists migrate and colonize new niches without relearning.
- **How it would work:**
  - Setup (E): a TS API aggregator over a controlled provider with three habitats: hot repeated keys, broad cold scans, and throttled bursts.
  - A MAP-Elites archive of 12–24 niches, described by cache footprint and upstream request rate. Source mutations change policy; heap inheritance keeps the learned key distribution and warm indexes.
  - Week 2: abrupt workload shifts, a held-out fourth trace, and migration between two hosts.
  - Baselines: best static policy, a tuner plus cache warm-up, contextual policy selection, and source-only evolution with state export. Inherited memory is ablated with code held identical.
- **Evidence / numbers:**
  - F: Hutchinson 1957 (niches); MAP-Elites (Mouret and Clune 2015); Cully et al. 2015 (behavior repertoire for damaged robots).
  - **E** success target: at least 20% fewer upstream requests than the best static/tuned baseline at the same success/latency limits after a shift, with a separately measurable inherited-phenotype advantage.
  - Sources: [MAP-Elites](https://arxiv.org/abs/1504.04909), [Cully et al. 2015](https://www.nature.com/articles/nature14422), [Hutchinson 1957](https://www.wsl.ch/lud/biodiversity_events/papers/Hutchinson-1957bc.pdf).
- **Prior art / who has it:** Quality-diversity research (MAP-Elites, Cully). Conventional policy selection, caches and parameter tuning are the commercial objection. Buyers: API aggregators, ingestion platforms, expensive agent-tool services. Explicitly not a replacement for a mature CDN's global routing.
- **Verdict:** KEPT (#2): "the strongest alternative if repair correctness proves too difficult to establish". It must beat tuning plus caches on held-out useful work per total dollar.
- **Kill test / first proof:** **E/I** Kill the organism version if a conventional tuner/selector with explicit cache state comes within 5% of it on held-out useful work per total dollar, including generation, switching and snapshot costs.

#### 3. Stateful discovery archipelagos: preserve the search, not only its source
- **What:** Evolve algorithms by branching experienced search processes that keep their partial constructions and archives, instead of restarting each candidate from an empty heap. The selectable object becomes algorithm plus accumulated search state (partial packings, tabu memory, incremental evaluator, warm constraint graph). The 100× version (I): a persistent discovery engine runs millions of funded search lineages on verifiable problems, freezing them between grants.
- **How it would work:**
  - Task (E): 1-D bin packing with an independent feasibility checker and sealed instance families.
  - A JS search capsule holds the population of partial solutions and checkpoints at a periodic host call. Identical mature searches are branched, and only the subsequent neighborhood-selection/scoring functions are mutated. Recombination swaps validated partial packings or modules, not heaps.
  - Runs 8–16 islands, with dormant leaders in R2 and Worker slices scheduled through Queues.
  - Baselines: fresh-source LLM evolution, serial continuation, explicit checkpoints, best-fit decreasing, conventional local search.
- **Evidence / numbers:**
  - F: AlphaEvolve (announced 2025-05-14, paper 2025-06-16); Google's 2026-05-07 update reports infrastructure use (cache policies, DB heuristics); OpenEvolve already implements LLM evolution with QD and islands.
  - **E** gate: better held-out quality at equal total compute/model budget, and at least 2× faster time-to-target than fresh-source evolution, with the remaining gap to conventional search reported.
  - Sources: [AlphaEvolve paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.13131), [AlphaEvolve impact](https://deepmind.google/blog/alphaevolve-impact/), [OpenEvolve](https://github.com/algorithmicsuperintelligence/openevolve).
- **Prior art / who has it:** AlphaEvolve (Google DeepMind) and OpenEvolve; I: "AlphaEvolve on Workers" is occupied and weak positioning. Koza GP and Tierra as lineage. A mature solver with a good checkpoint format may already be better. Buyers: scheduling, logistics and infrastructure teams.
- **Verdict:** KEPT (#3): a trusted checker makes value legible. The differentiator is whether preserving expensive partial search state changes discovery economics. If not, keep the algorithm and drop the platform story.
- **Kill test / first proof:** **E/I** Kill the capsule thesis if explicit application checkpoints keep the same useful state for under 10% extra engineering/runtime cost with equal time-to-quality, or if inherited search shows no advantage over fresh candidates.

#### 4. POET for incidents: evolve environments that teach useful resilience
- **What:** Co-evolve failure environments and recovery programs, and transfer successful recovery phenotypes between incident families. The 100× version (I): an expanding executable curriculum of real operational failure modes keeps discovering resilience capabilities before production meets them. The product is discovery of failure classes, not live remediation.
- **How it would work:**
  - Start (E) from 10 documented scenarios in a toy webhook-to-index workflow. An environment genome is a bounded TS event schedule of schema changes, duplicates, reordering and failures; a solver genome is a recovery policy. A joint checkpoint captures both states and the journal cut.
  - An environment is admitted only if protocol constraints hold and some reference strategy can solve it. Novelty is judged on fixed behavioral outcomes (data loss, duplicate intent, progress, recovery path). Data-integrity invariants are non-negotiable.
  - Baselines: random protocol-valid faults, fixed chaos scenarios, coverage-guided fuzzing, and an LLM generating tests without co-evolution.
- **Evidence / numbers:**
  - F: Lehman and Stanley 2011 (novelty search beat objective search in maze/walking); POET 2019; Enhanced POET 2020 names a limited problem space and no universal progress measure as impediments.
  - **E** gate: at least three minimized, independently confirmed failure mechanisms absent from the seeds, plus improved recovery on a reserved, independently authored corpus. Ten similar variants count as one mechanism.
  - Sources: [POET](https://arxiv.org/abs/1901.01753), [Enhanced POET](https://arxiv.org/abs/2003.08536), [Lehman and Stanley 2011](https://gwern.net/doc/reinforcement-learning/exploration/2011-lehman.pdf).
- **Prior art / who has it:** POET / Enhanced POET (Wang et al.), novelty search. Chaos testing and coverage-guided fuzzing are the baselines. Buyers: workflow engines, agent platforms, integration vendors.
- **Verdict:** RUNNER-UP (#4, "POET incident foundry"): used later to expand the repair colony's incident curriculum. I: an ever-growing archive is not proof of open-ended progress.
- **Kill test / first proof:** **E/I** Kill the co-evolution claim if the final solver population fails to beat fixed/random/coverage-guided curricula on the independent corpus at equal budget.

#### 5. Endosymbiotic service assembly: domesticate specialists into larger organisms
- **What:** Let services acquire validated specialist modules or subordinate capsules, and select whole consortia by the useful work their cooperation enables. The 100× version (I): new services assembled from mature parsers, reconcilers, planners and indexes under explicit compatibility contracts, inheriting expertise instead of rebuilding it.
- **How it would work:**
  - Mapping: horizontal gene transfer installs typed modules or learned artifacts. Endosymbiosis keeps a separately isolated specialist capsule behind a narrow interface. Raw memories are never merged.
  - Pilot (E): three capsule types (format detector/parser, incremental deduplicator, schema reconciler) with typed JSON contracts and state-export schemas, assembled for unseen combinations of three formats and two schemas.
  - A trusted scheduler controls all grants; a specialist cannot inherit its caller's network authority. LLMs propose adapters and one module transfer.
  - A leave-one-component-out assay attributes contribution.
- **Evidence / numbers:**
  - F: Sagan/Margulis 1967 (symbiotic origin of eukaryotes, cited as historical theory); Woese 1998 (genetic annealing, lateral gene transfer).
  - **E** gate: solve at least two held-out combinations with less total generation and integration work than both a hand-composed pipeline and a fresh single-model implementation, passing round-trip/data-conservation checks and counting messaging and memory overhead.
  - Sources: [Margulis 1967](https://courses.seas.harvard.edu/climate/eli/Courses/EPS281r/Sources/Origin-of-life/Lynn-Margulis-Sagan-1967.pdf), [Woese 1998](https://academic.uprm.edu/lrios/6006/woese-universal%20ancestor.pdf).
- **Prior art / who has it:** Dependencies, plugins and service composition already provide capability acquisition; I: "not a new package manager with organism terminology". Buyers: vertical integration and data-migration vendors. Cross-customer raw heaps are a poor donor format.
- **Verdict:** RUNNER-UP (#6). The likely survivor is a component-contract technique folded into the other ideas.
- **Kill test / first proof:** **E/I** Kill it as a separate product if typed packages plus ordinary RPC deliver the same held-out compositions with no more integration work and lower operating cost.

#### 6. Morphogenetic edge tissue: repair the topology of a service
- **What:** Synthesize local rules that make a distributed service rebuild a useful topology after nodes, queues or workload regions disappear. The 100× version (I): a service grows bounded processing "organs" near demand, differentiates replicas into ingestion/indexing/repair roles, and restores function after damage without a handwritten plan for each topology.
- **How it would work:**
  - Pilot (E): 32–64 logical cells over a local workerd/TS harness, each choosing a role from bounded neighbor messages and queue pressure.
  - A separate DO caps total replicas and grants unique ownership epochs; "growth" is a resource request, not self-authorized replication.
  - An LLM proposes rules and a deterministic simulator evaluates many failure schedules. This is NCA-inspired rule synthesis, not NCA training.
  - Test: delete a third of the cells and delay/reorder messages while building an independently checked index. Baselines: a desired-state reconciler, a conventional autoscaler, fixed overprovisioning.
- **Evidence / numbers:**
  - F: Lenia (Chan 2019); Growing Neural Cellular Automata (Mordvintsev et al. 2020) trained growth/regeneration rules. Neither establishes correct distributed recovery.
  - **E** gate: at least 20% lower recovery cost than the best conventional baseline at the same integrity and backlog limits across held-out failures.
  - Sources: [Lenia](https://www.complex-systems.com/abstracts/v28_i03_a01/), [Growing NCA](https://distill.pub/2020/growing-ca/).
- **Prior art / who has it:** Lenia and Growing NCA as lineage. Established queueing/control and reconciliation algorithms (desired-state reconcilers, autoscalers) may stay simpler and better. Possible users: intermittently connected ingestion or distributed indexing systems.
- **Verdict:** RUNNER-UP (#7, ranked low). It has "the largest gap between inspiring science and operational proof" and is "the weakest direct extrapolation from the cited science". Partitions and duplicate events break naive grid analogies.
- **Kill test / first proof:** **E/I** Kill the direction if local rules miss the predeclared cost advantage over reconciliation/autoscaling on held-out asynchronous failures. A regrowth animation does not rescue it.

#### 7. Recovery spore banks: preserve adaptations that current selection would erase
- **What:** Keep a diversity-preserving archive of executable service phenotypes and resurrect the right one when an old failure regime returns. The 100× version (I): a compact "biodiversity reserve" of rare but validated recovery states across versions and tenants, so operators need not rediscover extinct operational knowledge.
- **How it would work:**
  - Pilot (E): run a stateful ingestion workflow through three regimes and keep 20–50 distinctive checkpoints with their exact engines and synthetic journal inputs.
  - Compare retention policies (newest-only, highest-score-only, random, quality-diversity) under the same byte limit.
  - Return to an earlier regime after a schema/host-adapter version change. A resuscitation assay restores each candidate, revalidates grants and checks correctness; an intentionally incompatible image must be rejected.
  - Baselines: deployment rollback, JSON state, journal replay.
- **Evidence / numbers:**
  - F: Ulrich et al. 2018 (Bacillus spore dormancy; the first two years of a planned 500-year study, not 500 years of survival); Ray 1995 network Tierra already proposed a biodiversity reserve.
  - **E** gate: recover useful work at least 2× faster than the best baseline while preserving data invariants, and pick a rare useful phenotype more reliably than newest-only retention at the same capacity.
  - Sources: [Ulrich et al. 2018](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371/journal.pone.0208425), [Ray 1995 network Tierra](https://results.atr.jp/35th/techreport/TR-H-0145/index_e.html).
- **Prior art / who has it:** Ray's network-Tierra reserve, plus ordinary backups, rollback images and checkpoint recovery. Buyers: long-tail integration vendors and seasonal/event-driven services. Partial multiplier (I): if regenerating becomes cheaper than preserving, spores lose value, so only expensive accumulated experience is worth keeping.
- **Verdict:** NARROWED (#5): "more convincing as a feature of the leaders". Keep diversity retention inside repair colonies or discovery archives.
- **Kill test / first proof:** **E/I** Kill the standalone offering if source rollback plus replay/application state matches useful recovery within 20% after compatibility and archive-maintenance costs.

#### 8. Metabolic discovery commons: a food web paid for useful work
- **What:** A bounded ecosystem in which discovery programs earn future compute from independently validated outputs and from useful contributions to other programs. The 100× version (I): billions of mostly dormant specialists form a research economy of constructors, checkers, translators and paying consumers, while unproductive lineages exhaust their allowance and sleep or die.
- **How it would work:**
  - Pilot (E): 100–1,000 logical organisms with a small bounded active pool, on a packing/counterexample task family with constructor/checker/translator roles. Genomes are mostly generated once; there is no LLM call per organism per tick.
  - Accounting: the controller debits CPU slices, model/tool use and checkpoint bytes. Forking splits credit, restore never resets debt, and a rolling tenant cap acts as a homeostatic guard.
  - Pay only on externally accepted checked work; cap dependency depth and ban circular payments. A zero-utility prolific replicator and a mutually endorsing pair must both fail to earn.
  - Baselines: centralized priority scheduling and conventional island search.
- **Evidence / numbers:**
  - F: Tierra 1992 (scarce CPU/memory; parasites, cooperation, cheating); Cannon 1929 (homeostasis); Kerr, Wyllie and Currie 1972 (apoptosis).
  - **E** gate: at least 20% more independently accepted task improvements per total dollar across sealed seeds, with no quota overspend. I: this checks a mechanism; it cannot prove an open economy ungameable.
  - Sources: [Tierra 1992](https://tomray.me/pubs/tierra/), [Kerr et al. 1972](https://www.nature.com/articles/bjc197233), [Cannon 1929](https://journals.physiology.org/doi/10.1152/physrev.1929.9.3.399).
- **Prior art / who has it:** Tierra (Ray). The baseline is centralized scheduling. Possible buyer: an organization with a continuous backlog of objectively checkable optimization tasks. "Do not pitch a token economy"; an open marketplace is premature.
- **Verdict:** NARROWED (#8, last): "ambitious but weak as a first product". Stronger models also exploit reward proxies better, and collusion, fake demand and self-payment are predictable failures. Keep resource accounting and mortality as infrastructure.
- **Kill test / first proof:** **E/I** Kill the commons product if accepted improvements per dollar fail to beat centralized scheduling by the predeclared 20%, including credit-assignment overhead.

#### What "billions across the edge" would actually require (scale arithmetic)
- **What:** A reality check on the "billions of organisms" framing. Population size is a storage/discovery claim before it is a concurrency claim. Most individuals must be dormant, only externally triggered or funded ones wake, and a model should not mutate every organism.
- **How it would work:** A billion lineage records can point to fewer distinct immutable images. Mutation runs on a tiny frontier, and each validated mutation is reused across many compatible individuals. The "ecology worth building is task-driven":
  - niches = real workload/failure regimes
  - carrying capacity = host admission
  - food = externally funded useful work
  - extinction = resource reclamation
- **Evidence / numbers:**
  - **F** R2 Standard (2026-10-26): $0.015/GB-month, $4.50 per million Class A ops, $0.36 per million Class B ops, free egress.
  - **E** storage for one billion distinct images, one version: 0.1 MB → 100 TB / ~$1,500/month; 1.5 MB → 1.5 PB / $22,500; 3 MB → 3 PB / $45,000; 6.4 MB → 6.4 PB / $96,000.
  - **E** operations: one billion writes ≈ $4,500 and one billion reads ≈ $360. A daily full-population checkpoint for 30 days ≈ $135,000 in writes plus $10,800 in reads.
  - **E** compute and memory: 1% waking daily at 100 CPU-ms ≈ 278 CPU-hours/day. 0.1% resident at 3 MB ≈ 3 TB of state.
  - Source: [R2 pricing](https://developers.cloudflare.com/r2/pricing/).
- **Prior art / who has it:** n/a.
- **Verdict:** Framing constraint. "Sleeping has no resident guest RAM" does not mean free preservation, and model inference can dominate every cost term. None of this establishes Cloudflare capacity.
- **Kill test / first proof:** n/a.

### 03-physics: Physics, time and many worlds: the edge as a multiverse engine
Looks at capsules through physics, time, causality and branching execution: Everett relative states, Feynman sum-over-histories, Jefferson's Time Warp, Lamport light cones, Landauer/Bennett reversibility, Wilson coarse-graining. Its main claim (I) is that the 100x opportunity is to make irreversible actions scarce and simulated experience plentiful: "conditional rehearsal" of the real decision program before a trusted broker releases one bounded real effect. All nine ideas share three rules. Recorded past, hypothetical future (from a versioned simulator) and committed reality stay separate. Each fork gets its own environment overlay. A lucky simulated future is never promoted into the production heap (the "lucky-future trap", E: action A has EV −0.8 but some +10 branch shows up among 10,000 samples almost certainly). They also share one Cloudflare substrate (Workers/Dynamic Workers, DO coordinator, R2 anchors, Queues/Workflows, browser viewer) and a commit protocol built on effect intents, preconditions and idempotent receipts. The ONE bet is idea 3, the failure-search gate, with the counterfactual debugger as its inspection interface and MCTS search underneath. Verdict: go-with-changes, narrowed to one configuration-update connector and three independent invariants, and it must beat equal-budget ordinary property testing. The report has no new runtime measurements.

#### 1. The action compiler: MCTS over live continuations
- **What:** Compile a proposed agent action into an executable search tree whose nodes are real suspended program states, then pick the action with the strongest validated outcomes. The 100x version: every consequential tool call becomes a request to an edge planning engine that searches thousands of continuation/environment pairs and returns a contingent policy, its assumptions and its first permitted effect.
- **How it would work:** Decision nodes alternate with environment nodes. Generate a small, diverse action set, fork the paused heap plus environment overlay, run cheap continuations, back up utility and invariant failures, and spend more rollouts near choices that could change. Candidates share scenario seeds for paired comparisons, a held-out scenario set stays out of search, and the objective is bounded reward plus hard admissibility constraints. It is an execution substrate for planning, not a new MCTS algorithm.
- **Evidence / numbers:** E: the first proof takes 10–14 days with 8–16 candidate configurations and 100 held-out simulator seeds. E (illustrative cost): 10,000 branches × 50 CPU-ms = 500 CPU-s (8.3 core-minutes), 7.8 s lower bound on 64 lanes, 30 GB moved if each child fetches its own 3 MB image. F: Workers allow 128 MB per isolate and Dynamic Workers 4 in-flight children per request (10 per DO I/O context). Sources: [UCT, Kocsis & Szepesvári 2006](https://aima.cs.berkeley.edu/~russell/classes/cs294/s11/readings/Kocsis%2BSzepesvari:2006.pdf), [LATS](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.04406), [Dynamic Workers limits](https://developers.cloudflare.com/dynamic-workers/platform/limits/).
- **Prior art / who has it:** UCT (2006); LATS (MCTS with language-model actions, values and reflection, 2023/2024).
- **Verdict:** KEPT (#3). It has the largest planning upside but should start only where environments are owned and reward is checkable. A poor simulator can confidently pick the wrong action faster, and if a stronger single-pass model matches search at lower cost, this becomes an optional expensive mode.
- **Kill test / first proof:** Kill if, on held-out tasks, executable search does not improve realized utility over equal-total-budget candidate generation plus ordinary tests. First proof: a TS deployment planner with a QuickJS-Wasm checkpoint, simulated capacity/failure/traffic and one version-checked config write through the owned connector.

#### 2. The counterfactual production debugger
- **What:** Turn a captured production failure into an executable experiment: rewind to a tool boundary, change one input or supported policy decision, and compare the resulting causal histories. The 100x version: an agent investigates incidents by running hundreds of interventions against a captured program and a declared environment model, returning the smallest reproducible failure and a repair hypothesis with explicit limits.
- **How it would work:** Replay of the unchanged prefix checks every tool-request digest and returns the recorded answer. At the intervention point the image forks. A later recorded reply is reused only if the request, input provenance and declared dependencies still match; otherwise replay stops ("unknown external response") or asks a branch-local simulator. It starts with response substitutions and policy-config inputs, not arbitrary code hot-swapping. Source changes replay from a compatible source boundary.
- **Evidence / numbers:** E: the first proof takes 7–10 days, using a TS order coordinator with duplicate-completion, timeout and inventory-race bugs on synthetic tenant data, with a closure, Map and pending promise in the checkpoint, loaded in browser and Node, and zero production dispatches. Sources: [rr report 2017-05-16](https://arxiv.org/abs/1705.05937), [Pernosco](https://pernos.co/), [Fowler, Event Sourcing 2005-12-12](https://www.martinfowler.com/eaaDev/EventSourcing.html).
- **Prior art / who has it:** rr (record/replay, reverse debugging), Pernosco (history and data-flow inspection), event sourcing, TimeTravelJS (overlaps on browser history branching).
- **Verdict:** KEPT (#2). It is the best entry interface with immediate developer value and the smallest trust leap, because exact replay plus shared witnesses keep their value even if counterfactual prediction fails. Model fidelity limits the counterfactual claims, and the risk is an attractive viewer that adds less than a good conventional reproducer.
- **Kill test / first proof:** Kill if, in blinded seeded-incident trials, engineers with the executable incident do not reach correct diagnoses faster than engineers with structured logs plus a conventional reproducer, once capture and integration cost are included.

#### 3. The failure-search gate: rehearse disasters before releasing a write
- **What:** An agent's proposed write must survive adversarial execution under tool failures, stale observations and malicious data before the broker will consider committing it. The 100x version: consequential agents continually face an automated adversary that searches thousands of worlds for a counterexample, and every allowed effect carries a replayable test history plus independently enforced invariants.
- **How it would work:** The adversary mutates only contract-permitted inputs: retryable failures, duplicate deliveries, reordered independent completions, expiry, approval revocation, stale reads and prompt-injection content in data fields. Illegal simulator responses form a separate robustness suite. An independent checker evaluates invariants (target identity, grant freshness, spending bound, "no second logical release"), and search keeps minimized witnesses. "Passing" means no failure was found under these contracts and budgets. A broker-enforced allowlist gives a stronger bounded guarantee.
- **Evidence / numbers:** E: the first proof takes 10–14 days, with one config tool, three independently authored safety predicates, 10,000 cumulative suffix rollouts, and ten seeded defects plus a held-out set, compared against random fuzzing and source reruns at equal CPU/model budgets with the oracle frozen before search. Sources: [FoundationDB SIGMOD 2021](https://www.foundationdb.org/files/fdb-paper.pdf), [Antithesis deterministic hypervisor 2024-03-20](https://antithesis.com/blog/deterministic_hypervisor/), [GrowOS controller](../../jitk/grow/README.md).
- **Prior art / who has it:** FoundationDB deterministic simulation and fault injection, Antithesis deterministic hypervisor. The GrowOS lesson about independent oracles, held-out cases and deliberate wrong implementations also applies.
- **Verdict:** KEPT (#1), and the ONE bet. It has a clear effect boundary, its stakes rise as agents improve, and it produces executable failure evidence. The risk is that simulator fidelity and ordinary testing do most of the work, which would leave forkable heaps as optional plumbing. Simulator contracts and acceptance checks must stay outside the proposing model's control.
- **Kill test / first proof:** Kill if, at equal total compute with the same frozen oracle, the guided multiverse gate finds no additional held-out material defects over ordinary property-based fault testing. The public proof: an agent proposes an action, a legal failure timeline breaks it, the witness replays, a revised policy survives a disclosed held-out suite, and one version-checked real write is recorded.

#### 4. Time Warp society: agents that run ahead of one another
- **What:** Cooperating agent programs consume tentative messages and advance without waiting, then roll back dependent work when a tentative assumption is revoked. The 100x version: a distributed agent organization prepares plans, artifacts and negotiations across the edge while causally stable decisions cross a separate real-effect frontier.
- **How it would work:** Messages carry causal IDs, virtual timestamps and tentative/confirmed status. A retracted assumption sends invalidations to every descendant that consumed it. Those descendants restore their last unaffected checkpoint and reuse content-addressed pure artifacts whose inputs still match. Late real-world facts arrive at the current boundary rather than being assigned to a fossil-collected past. One authoritative scheduler checkpoints actor states and pending deliveries together, because independent per-actor copies can create impossible worlds.
- **Evidence / numbers:** E: the first proof takes 10–14 days, with three TS actors (compatibility checker, release-note writer, rollout planner), conservative vs. optimistic delivery, and tracking of the invalidation DAG, wasted CPU/tokens and completion latency, including highly coupled and adversarially late cases. Sources: [Jefferson, Virtual Time 1985](https://worrydream.com/refs/Jefferson_1985_-_Virtual_Time.pdf), [Chandy & Lamport 1985](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/wp-content/uploads/2016/12/Determining-Global-States-of-a-Distributed-System.pdf).
- **Prior art / who has it:** Jefferson's Time Warp (antimessages, GVT) and Chandy–Lamport distributed snapshots. Time Warp is still an active simulation technique.
- **Verdict:** RUNNER-UP (#5). It is an ambitious concurrency product, but it must beat conservative scheduling after wasted work is counted. Saved-state cost, too much optimism and rollback propagation can eat the gained parallelism, and it cannot unsend real effects.
- **Kill test / first proof:** Kill if, on the target latency-dominated workload, optimistic completion time is no better than conservative scheduling once rollback, duplicate inference and coordination are counted. Demote before attempting global distribution.

#### 5. Speculative interfaces: prepare the next interaction, then select it
- **What:** Fork an app's suspended controller into several likely next interactions so the path the user picks has already done its expensive pure work. The 100x version: an interface keeps an executable cloud of possible next states across browser and edge, offering instant previews while committing only the interaction actually chosen.
- **How it would work:** The fork captures controller state, not DOM or socket state. Branches return immutable view models or render commands, and only the chosen branch renders, with the others labelled as previews. A predicted input must match user intent and current data versions before cached work is reused. Branches dispatch no reservations, network requests or analytics events.
- **Evidence / numbers:** E: the first proof takes 7–10 days, using a browser Web Worker with a QuickJS-Wasm editor controller paused on `nextInput()` and five forked edits, compared against optimized memoization/prefetch on a held-out click trace (p50/p95 latency, hit rate, bytes, CPU, wasted work), with no battery claim unless measured. Source: [Zilles 2004, "Increasing Interactivity by Predicting User Actions"](https://www.cs.utexas.edu/~skeckler/wild04/Paper4.pdf).
- **Prior art / who has it:** Zilles 2004 (predicted-input execution with checkpointing and commit), ordinary prefetching and optimistic UIs.
- **Verdict:** RUNNER-UP (#8). It makes a strong visual demo but has a weaker durable advantage over prefetch as models get faster. It deserves a demo, not priority over safety and debugging.
- **Kill test / first proof:** Kill if held-out interaction traces show no worthwhile latency improvement over optimized prefetch/memoization within the same CPU and transfer budget.

#### 6. The causal microscope: replay only the light cone
- **What:** Capture the causal dependency graph of tool interactions and fork only the part of execution an intervention affects. The 100x version: the engine explores equivalence classes of causal histories ("which ordering or observation could have caused this?") instead of millions of redundant total-order schedules.
- **How it would work:** The system logs parent event IDs, send/receive edges, resource/version reads and declared writes, applies conservative independence rules, and enumerates allowed completion orders for conflicting pending operations from a broker/event-loop checkpoint. It starts with whole-capsule suffix replay, because inside an opaque heap any input can affect everything afterwards, and optimizes at explicit actor/module boundaries later. Authoritative independence comes from audited tool contracts, not model suggestions or timestamp proximity.
- **Evidence / numbers:** E: the first proof takes 7–12 days, with four tool/actor operations with explicit read/write sets (independent catalog reads plus two conflicting inventory updates), comparing bounded exhaustive enumeration with dependency-guided exploration, plus a deliberately wrong independence annotation. Sources: [Lamport 1978](https://lamport.azurewebsites.net/pubs/time-clocks.pdf), [COPS SOSP 2011](https://www.cs.cmu.edu/~dga/papers/cops-sosp2011-abstract.html), [Flanagan & Godefroid DPOR, POPL 2005](https://patricegodefroid.github.io/public_psfiles/popl2005.pdf).
- **Prior art / who has it:** Lamport happened-before, COPS causal consistency, dynamic partial-order reduction (Flanagan & Godefroid).
- **Verdict:** RUNNER-UP (#4). It is a valuable correctness and performance layer for ideas 1–3 (and for 2–4), probably not a standalone company.
- **Kill test / first proof:** Kill if conservative dependency tracking keeps nearly all schedule permutations on representative agent workflows, so its total cost is no lower than naive bounded exploration. In that case keep the graph for inspection and drop the optimization claim.

#### 7. The history telescope: coarse-grain worlds without losing witnesses
- **What:** Compile huge execution trees into several property-specific resolutions, with exact anchors and witnesses underneath a navigable abstract history. The 100x version: months of agent experience become a compact, searchable causal atlas that zooms from a semantic outcome class back into an executable incident and refines itself when a detail turns out to matter.
- **How it would work:** Three layers: exact content-addressed snapshots and journal anchors, a causal event index, and a lossy semantic navigation layer. For a given invariant, states are grouped by validated predicates (grant version, pending logical effect, resource ownership), not by similar LLM summaries. Each class holds exact representative pointers and scenario weight. A class is split whenever probes show its members behave differently. Learned classes only guide search unless a formal abstraction proves more.
- **Evidence / numbers:** E: the first proof takes 10–14 days, generating 10,000 bounded histories of one approval/retry controller with a hidden version-dependent bug, using hand-authored predicate classes plus model-proposed refinements, reporting index bytes, retained exact bytes and query time separately, and demonstrating one false grouping being split. Sources: [Wilson 1971 renormalization](https://journals.aps.org/prb/abstract/10.1103/PhysRevB.4.3174), [Clarke et al. CEGAR, CAV 2000 / JACM 2003](https://www.cs.cmu.edu/~emc/papers/Papers%20In%20Refereed%20Journals/Counterexample-guided%20abstraction%20refinement.pdf).
- **Prior art / who has it:** Wilson's renormalization group as inspiration, and counterexample-guided abstraction refinement (Clarke et al.) as the software mechanism.
- **Verdict:** RUNNER-UP (#7). It is necessary at scale, but property-preserving abstraction is hard and it is not the first product. Selling "compressed understanding" before witness preservation is proven would be premature.
- **Kill test / first proof:** Kill if a grouping used to prune search loses a known material failure on the withheld bounded corpus. Then stop pruning with it; it can stay as a labelled navigation index.

#### 8. The entropy budget: spend compute on worlds that change the decision
- **What:** Schedule forks by expected decision value per CPU/token/byte, keep surprising or dangerous witnesses, and reconstruct cheap redundant history. The 100x version: a multiverse engine that metareasons about its own search: when to branch, where to checkpoint, when to ask for a real observation, and when to stop.
- **How it would work:** The scheduler keeps a candidate ranking with uncertainty, a hard risk-exploration allocation and observed branch costs. It spends the next batch on scenarios that could change the selected action or expose a forbidden effect, and stops on a deadline, on dominated alternatives or when simulator fidelity is too low. Sometimes the best next step is a real read. It keeps rare catastrophic witnesses whatever they do to the average reward, and picks checkpoint spacing from measured restore/recompute costs.
- **Evidence / numbers:** E: the first proof takes 7–10 days, comparing random, fixed-stratified, UCT-style and decision-change scheduling under equal CPU/model-call budgets with a hidden rare-failure stratum and varied checkpoint spacing. I: freed high-water bytes retained in snapshots (from prior measurements) make naive compression-based "state entropy" misleading, and Shannon entropy, image size, heap allocation and joules are distinct quantities. Source: [Shannon 1948](https://people.math.harvard.edu/~ctm/home/text/others/shannon/entropy/entropy.pdf), plus Landauer and Bennett as cited in the lens table.
- **Prior art / who has it:** Shannon, Landauer and Bennett (lineage). Fixed UCT, diversity sampling and hand-built risk strata are strong cheap substitutes.
- **Verdict:** NARROWED (#9). It is demoted as an independent moonshot. Keep the resource-allocation research as the optimizer inside the search engine (ideas 1 and 3) and drop the standalone thermodynamics framing. A standalone "entropy dashboard" has weak buyer value.
- **Kill test / first proof:** Kill if the learned allocation policy shows no held-out improvement over fixed risk-stratified sampling and ordinary UCT after its own inference cost.

#### 9. The observer laboratory: test what an agent could know
- **What:** Fork the same decision process under different lawful information views to expose actions that depend on unavailable, stale or unauthorized knowledge. The 100x version: every multi-party automated decision is rehearsed from each principal's actual information frame, and the system identifies the smallest observation or authorization needed to act responsibly.
- **How it would work:** Take a public checkpoint before any private observation, then feed branch-specific views through tools. Never fork a privileged heap and mask afterwards. For each information set, test candidate policies across multiple hidden states consistent with that view, and require the policy to choose from its own observations rather than peeking per hidden state. Conflicting objectives need an explicit aggregation/authority rule, since majority vote across copies of one model is not consensus.
- **Evidence / numbers:** E: the first proof takes 7–12 days, using a TS support controller with tools that differ for customer, support worker and admin, crossed with hidden account-owner and approval states. It includes a privileged-data leak, an approval revocation and a case that needs a fresh authorized read, is checked against an independent role policy, and is compared with ordinary permission/unit tests on held-out templates. Sources: [Halpern & Moses JACM 1990](https://groups.csail.mit.edu/tds/papers/Halpern/JACM90.pdf), [Everett 1957](https://journals.aps.org/rmp/abstract/10.1103/RevModPhys.29.454).
- **Prior art / who has it:** Halpern & Moses knowledge in distributed systems; Everett's relative states as motivation.
- **Verdict:** RUNNER-UP (#6). It is a credible specialist failure suite inside the failure-search gate, with permissions and information provenance as the concrete wedge. A broad "observer worlds" marketplace is not yet a focused business.
- **Kill test / first proof:** Kill if, on held-out permission/staleness cases, view-based execution finds no material mistakes beyond conventional authorization and data-flow tests at comparable integration effort. Then keep it as a small test mode.

### 04-economics: Economics, law and societies of agent-computers
Lens: economic institutions (accounting, contracts, escrow, markets, insurance, agency law, commons) for portable, stateful computers. Headline finding: copyable state and scarce rights need different machinery. A snapshot can carry evidence, but spendable balance and authority must be conserved outside it by a broker or ledger. A local ledger probe confirmed this (M). The ONE bet is **notarised computation**: a replayable invoice-reconciliation receipt with one real continuation exported and resumed after independent checking, built in two weeks. Verdict: **go-with-changes**. Build the receipt/verifier, but drop the capsule-specific pitch (keep the economic product, drop the heap-image requirement) if a source-plus-signed-journal baseline serves buyers equally well.

#### Economic constitution: portable evidence, externally conserved authority
- **What:** a cross-cutting rule for every idea in the report. Snapshots may hold budget displays, receipts, signed mandates and grant references. Spendable balance and current authorization are enforced by a trusted broker or settlement ledger outside the snapshot.
- **How it would work:** five invariants. (1) Conservation: `unallocated + allocated-unspent + reserved + spent = funding`, in integer units; children receive transfers and never mint money. (2) Effect identity: a stable operation ID plus request digest is persisted before dispatch, and a fork never re-dispatches a pending payment. (3) Authority fencing: a broker-side epoch increments on migration or transfer, so old copies can compute but can't cause brokered effects. (4) Evidence scope: signatures, timestamps and replay prove only what they prove. (5) Liability and exit: a real principal and counterparty, with no ownerless obligations. Bookkeeping follows TigerBeetle-style immutable two-phase transfers in a SQLite-backed Durable Object, with an outbox, stable IDs and reconciliation for the payment provider.
- **Evidence / numbers:** local `ledger-probe.ts`, Node v24.22.1 (M). 100 copied records, each with budget 10 and spending 6, admitted **600 units**. A shared ledger admitted **one** 6-unit post, rejected **99** and left 4 units. 100 retries of one operation charged once, and a changed amount under the same ID was rejected. An old epoch was rejected. An unresolved hold blocked a second request. **1,000 seeded traces × 100 ops** preserved conservation. Scope (M): a single-process integer state machine, not a distributed, crash or security test. Seed priors (M, not rerun): **1.5–6.4 MB** compressed snapshots and **5–11×** overhead vs V8 on glue. Sources: [TigerBeetle two-phase transfers](https://docs.tigerbeetle.com/coding/two-phase-transfers/), [CF DO SQLite storage (2026-09-21)](https://developers.cloudflare.com/durable-objects/api/sqlite-storage-api/), [Agoric Open Systems (1988)](https://papers.agoric.com/papers/markets-and-computation-agoric-open-systems/full-text/).
- **Prior art / who has it:** Miller & Drexler's agoric systems (1988), which named weak demand and accounting/negotiation overhead at small scale as the obstacles. Agoric `xsnap` (resumable XS workers) and Zoe offer safety (custody kept separate from contract logic). TigerBeetle. Existing rails: Cloudflare Pay Per Crawl (2025-07-01), x402 v2 (already has authorization, upfront and escrow flows, and excludes client budget management), Stripe/Tempo MPP (2026-03-18), Cloudflare Agents payments (x402 + MPP), AWS Bedrock AgentCore payment sessions with budgets, and Google AP2 signed Checkout/Payment Mandates.
- **Verdict:** KEPT as the foundation for all eight ideas, not a product. "Do not rewind the money with the computer": the broker journal stays monotonic, and ledger PITR must reconcile external settlement before spending reopens. The report's proposed additions are task-level acceptance plus reproducible evidence linked to the purchase, and the question of how authority changes when a continuation forks or transfers.
- **Kill test / first proof:** the ledger probe above, which decided the architecture question but does not qualify an implementation.

#### 1. Notarised computation: an invoice you can run
- **What:** sell work together with a portable evidence package that a verifier chosen by the buyer can replay. 100× version: every consequential machine-produced deliverable comes with an executable account of how it was produced, a common evidence language across vendors, auditors, procurement and dispute services.
- **How it would work:** the evidence contract covers source/bootstrap/module hashes, the initial deterministic environment, ordered tool requests and replies, an output digest, mandate and broker signatures, payment receipt references, checkpoint roots and verifier instructions. Replay from the initial state verifies the whole trace. Starting from a signed intermediate checkpoint verifies only the suffix and trusts the issuer for the prefix. Model inference stays recorded external input. Product boundary: publish a permissively usable receipt/envelope format and a small verifier, then build one seller/buyer integration on it.
- **Evidence / numbers:** first proof estimated at **8–10 working days** (E). No measurements yet. Trust ladder (I): artifact integrity, then signer identity, replay consistency, authenticated external inputs, contemporaneous authorization, task-specific correctness, and legal enforceability, each needing its own evidence ("a perfectly replayed lie is still a lie"). Sources: [RFC 3161 (2001)](https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc3161), [W3C PROV (2013)](https://www.w3.org/TR/prov-overview/), [EU Reg. 2024/1183 Arts 45k–45l](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2024/1183/oj/eng). A DO hash chain is not a qualified ledger.
- **Prior art / who has it:** RFC 3161 timestamps, W3C PROV, the durable-journal and snapshot prior art in the v8 landscape report, and EU eIDAS qualified electronic ledgers / trust-service providers (a later integration).
- **Verdict:** KEPT (#1), and the ONE bet. It is the best independent artifact with the clearest evidence value and a credible route into buyer/seller integrations. Main risk: a signed journal may be enough. "Notarised" means a technical witness service here, not a legal notarial act.
- **Kill test / first proof:** Proof: a bounded invoice-reconciliation agent on fixture invoices and a labelled sandbox paid-rate endpoint, using QuickJS/Wasm with one genuinely parked continuation holding a closure. It produces a signed manifest, with the verifier key operated separately. A Node CLI and a browser verifier replay it with no outbound calls. Corrupting an invoice byte, substituting a tool response or splicing in a checkpoint must each fail at the matching boundary, and artifact bytes and full verification time are recorded. Kill: three external integration engineers find the capsule saves no meaningful verification or continuation work versus source plus a signed journal, and prefer the smaller baseline.

#### 2. Fork firms: an agent hires copies of its current self
- **What:** a working agent funds narrowly mandated copies of its current continuation, buys useful subresults and closes each child's books. 100× version: short-lived firms form around problems, keep work in progress, choose between internal production and outside suppliers, and return leftover funds and evidence to their owners.
- **How it would work:** at a quiescent boundary the agent forks the real heap and gives each child its own task input and a fresh broker grant. Child budgets transfer out of the parent's allocation, and children can't fund themselves. Receipts separate purchased results from internal transfers. A broker enforces depth, fan-out and total-spend caps. With a single owner, "hiring itself" is cost allocation, not revenue. Clones share failure modes and are not independent reviewers.
- **Evidence / numbers:** first proof estimated at **1–2 weeks** (E). The proof starts with 100 test credits, gives 20 to each of 3 children and keeps 40 at the parent. Sources: [Coase, The Nature of the Firm (1937)](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1468-0335.1937.tb00002.x), [Spawn (1992) §I.C](https://www.waldspurger.org/carl/papers/spawn-ieee-tse-feb92.pdf).
- **Prior art / who has it:** agoric service-trading objects. Spawn (1992) already had a sponsorship hierarchy and funding conservation across forks, so neither recursive funding nor budget conservation is new.
- **Verdict:** KEPT (#2). It is the strongest visual economic action, makes unusually direct use of continuation state, and is the best second demo once receipt and grant semantics work. Main risk: fresh parallel tasks are simpler and just as effective.
- **Kill test / first proof:** Proof: a coordinator works through a fixture supplier catalogue, hits a real tool await and forks three children. One child exceeds its grant and is refused. Another retries a paid call after a lost acknowledgment, and the merchant records one charge. The ledger stays conserved. Results are compared against cold-start tasks with shared source/history. Kill: on a preregistered task set, true forks show no preparation-cost or completion-time advantage over fresh tasks with shared context at equal quality.

#### 3. Executable work orders: escrow pays for an accepted result
- **What:** a buyer escrows payment against a signed work order whose deliverable, acceptance rule and dispute evidence are executable. 100× version: agents buy whole chains of subcontracted work from unknown vendors, with payment released by agreed predicates and portable evidence instead of bilateral SaaS integrations.
- **How it would work:** one signed bundle holds the prose terms, typed parameters, acceptance-code hash, verifier version, deadline, evidence-access rights, dispute procedure, and a precedence rule for when prose and code disagree. The first scope is objective transformations of committed inputs: a balanced reconciliation, a schema migration against pinned fixtures, or extraction with source offsets. Funds release against a verifier's receipt. Subjective quality goes to a named adjudicator or human. It sits above x402/MPP and reuses their funds flow and escrow.
- **Evidence / numbers:** first proof estimated at **7–10 working days** (E). Sources: [Szabo (1997)](https://firstmonday.org/ojs/index.php/fm/article/view/548), [Grigg, Ricardian Contract (2004)](https://iang.org/papers/ricardian_contract.html), [Agoric Zoe offer enforcement](https://docs.agoric.com/guides/zoe/offer-enforcement).
- **Prior art / who has it:** Szabo's smart contracts, Grigg's Ricardian contracts, Agoric Zoe, and x402/MPP escrow mechanisms. What stalled the old work: oracles and incomplete specifications, since safe asset exchange can't tell whether the work is good.
- **Verdict:** KEPT (#3). It is the strongest near-term purchase/settlement case and turns receipts into consequential behavior, but it should follow the receipt experiment. Main risk: acceptance predicates can't capture buyer value, and stronger models game tests more.
- **Kill test / first proof:** Proof: buyer and seller Workers plus a DO for test-credit escrow, with a Ricardian-style order for reconciling an invoice batch and the seller supplying idea 1's replay package. Scenarios: valid, malformed and late delivery, plus a deadline/receipt disagreement that freezes the order, and a crash between the release request and its acknowledgment, reconciled by operation ID. Kill: in a blind adversarial set of 20 outputs, the preregistered rule releases funds for even one materially invalid deliverable. Automatic settlement is then dropped for that class, though evidence-assisted adjudication may survive.

#### 4. A spot exchange for work already in progress
- **What:** a capsule buys its next bounded execution slice from eligible hosts, and moves when a better offer outweighs the migration cost. 100× version: ongoing agent work becomes portable demand that routes among capacity suppliers, with an execution history independent of any supplier's account.
- **How it would work:** hosts bid on a fixed, metered unit of execution defined by runtime hash, isolation, locality/data constraints, memory/fuel ceiling and deadline. A buyer-announced maximum plus independent host asks is enough for a prototype. Reverse second-price auctions only hold under narrow assumptions. Move only if expected savings exceed transfer, checkpoint and verification cost plus interruption loss and a margin. The old owner is fenced and ineligible destinations are excluded.
- **Evidence / numbers:** at **3 MB and 10 Mbit/s**, payload transfer alone takes **2.4 s** (I/E, illustrative inputs, not measured). First proof estimated at **1–2 weeks** (E). EC2 Spot gives a best-effort two-minute warning (F). Sources: [Spawn (1992)](https://www.waldspurger.org/carl/papers/spawn-ieee-tse-feb92.pdf), [Vickrey (1961)](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1540-6261.1961.tb02789.x), [Myerson–Satterthwaite (1983)](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/0022053183900480).
- **Prior art / who has it:** Spawn's workstation computational economy, Vickrey auctions, EC2 Spot interruption notices. Historical limit: allocating remote tasks is easier than safely moving arbitrary work in progress.
- **Verdict:** RUNNER-UP (#7). Technically attractive but economically weak for tool-dominated glue: if remote inference dominates the bill, shaving interpreter CPU barely matters. Constrained markets (scarce tool access, data-local execution) may work before an open compute market. Browser suppliers can't be trusted with confidential plaintext.
- **Kill test / first proof:** Proof: a registry Worker, a clearing DO and three real local hosts (Node, browser, workerd) with synthetic prices. Rebind a pinned capsule at a tool boundary, force an interruption, fence the old owner, and compare against finishing locally and replay recovery with a multi-megabyte image. Kill: after full migration and risk costs, no representative workload beats the best fixed eligible host by **≥20%** at equal reliability (E). The exchange is then demoted, and migration stays a resilience feature.

#### 5. Licensed knowledge supply chains, with receipts attached to the answer
- **What:** agents buy data under explicit terms and carry access receipts and provenance into every derived work order. 100× version: an answer funds a network of specialist data suppliers, discloses which paid sources supported it, and can be audited or recomputed without confusing access, redistribution and training rights.
- **How it would work:** provider, content digest, terms digest, receipt, permitted use/retention and audit-access conditions are bound to each tool result. Large licensed bodies sit in access-controlled blobs, and the guest holds only windows or handles. A fork may need a new licence grant. Contradictory or unrecognized terms escalate to a human or another supplier. Enforcement happens only at trusted boundaries; it is not universal DRM.
- **Evidence / numbers:** first proof estimated at **1–2 weeks** (E). Sources: [Cloudflare Pay Per Crawl crawler flow (2026-04-23)](https://developers.cloudflare.com/ai-crawl-control/features/pay-per-crawl/use-pay-per-crawl-as-ai-owner/crawl-pages/), Grigg's Ricardian identifiers, W3C PROV.
- **Prior art / who has it:** Cloudflare Pay Per Crawl (`402`, `crawler-price` and `crawler-charged` headers), x402/MPP, Ricardian contracts, W3C PROV. A charge proves neither quality nor comprehensive rights, and paid crawling is not permission to redistribute or train.
- **Verdict:** RUNNER-UP (#4). Data-vendor demand is plausible and payment rails exist, but the capsule's advantage depends on ongoing derived work. The market disappears if free substitutes are just as good.
- **Kill test / first proof:** Proof: three fixture publishers behind a paid Worker endpoint, using a real x402/MPP sandbox or a labelled mock. A research capsule picks two sources, parks, moves and answers. The verifier checks digests, allowed derivations and receipts. A redistribution fork is refused by the export broker, with no claim to stop an operator reading memory. Kill: in a blinded paid-vs-free comparison, purchased sources add no buyer-valued improvement that covers acquisition and governance cost. The marketplace is then dropped and provenance kept as a feature.

#### 6. Underwrite the action, then use replay to settle the claim
- **What:** quote a bounded warranty for a specified agent action, attach its scope to the capsule, and judge claims from committed inputs and signed receipts. 100× version: a broad market prices narrow risks of delegated actions, making responsibility a purchasable part of a work order.
- **How it would work:** the first cover is limited to an observable loss, such as a duplicate charge by the defined broker or a transformation failing a committed postcondition. It is bound to program/build, tool versions, action class, maximum exposure and period. Pricing is by model/tool/policy cohort, because forks are not independent draws, and a model upgrade or shared tool failure can hit the whole book at once.
- **Evidence / numbers:** illustrative arithmetic (E): probability **0.001** × capped loss **$100** gives an expected indemnity of **$0.10** before expenses, capital and margin. **10,000** correlated copies give up to **$1M** of nominal exposure. First proof estimated at **1–2 weeks** (E). Sources: [Munich Re aiSure](https://www.munichre.com/en/solutions/for-industry-clients/insure-ai.html), [Mosaic + aiSure (2026-02-26)](https://www.mosaicinsurance.com/resources/press-releases/~/mosaic-partners-with-munich-res-aisure-to-provide-pioneering-coverage-for-ai-vendors/).
- **Prior art / who has it:** Munich Re aiSure and Mosaic Insurance (AI performance cover). Incumbents are partners or competitors, not proof of an empty market.
- **Verdict:** RUNNER-UP (#6). Potentially very valuable, but credible pricing, licensed risk carriers, loss data and distribution lie far beyond a two-week software demo. Hashing code does not make an arbitrary agent actuarially priceable.
- **Kill test / first proof:** Proof: a **simulated warranty reserve** (not an insurance sale) with fixture work orders, declared scenario frequencies, injected broker failures, an independent verifier approving or rejecting payouts, and a common-mode outage, tracking peak reserve shortfall and false claims. Kill: in a blinded claims set, capsule evidence can't separate covered from excluded events better or more cheaply than ordinary structured broker logs.

#### 7. Transferable mandates: sell, hand over, or inherit work in progress
- **What:** transfer stewardship of a running procedure through a signed handover that carries its state and evidence while reissuing its real authority. 100× version: agent operations outlive a device, vendor, employee or owner, and are assigned or succeeded under clear rules instead of dying with an API account.
- **How it would work:** the old principal signs an assignment proposal, and the new principal and counterparty accept. The registry records the transition, holds unsettled obligations and increments the epoch. Eligible grants are reissued and non-transferable ones suspended. The retained copy can compute but can't spend. Possession of the file does not convey title, licences, obligations or power of attorney, and a timeout is not death or succession.
- **Evidence / numbers:** first proof estimated at **1–2 weeks** (E). Sources: [15 USC §7001(h) E-SIGN (2000)](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/15/7001), [UNCITRAL Model Law on Automated Contracting (2024-07-11)](https://uncitral.un.org/en/mlac), [BGB §§164–168](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/englisch_bgb/englisch_bgb.html) / §1922.
- **Prior art / who has it:** US E-SIGN electronic agents, the UNCITRAL MLAC (a model law, not enacted everywhere), German BGB agency and succession rules. These argue for representing a real principal, not for software legal personhood.
- **Verdict:** RUNNER-UP (#5). An important continuity problem with necessary authority semantics, but many cases are just ordinary export plus credential rotation. The first useful version is a company handing a long-running task to another authorized operator. "Runs for decades" can't be inferred from today's Wasm.
- **Kill test / first proof:** Proof: two authenticated principals exchange a fixture procurement task paused before its final action. Restoring the original copy and attempting a purchase gets refused, and a succession simulation needs a labelled executor-authorization fixture, never a dead-man timer. Kill: in three real enterprise handover scenarios, exporting app state plus ordinary credential rotation is no harder than transferring the capsule. Mandates are then demoted to an export feature, and estate/personhood is avoided.

#### 8. Agent guilds and governed commons that clones cannot capture
- **What:** organizations pool agent budgets, tools and reusable work under a charter whose resource rules, sanctions and appeals are enforced by explicit institutions. 100× version: durable machine-assisted cooperatives maintain shared knowledge and infrastructure, admit accountable participants, buy work and adapt their rules.
- **How it would work:** a guild of real principals owns a paid data/compute pool and a versioned charter. Agents are delegates. Forks inherit lineage and share their principal's quotas and votes. Reputation is scoped evidence (task class, policy/model version, independent customer, denominators, disputes, expiry), not a scalar each copy inherits. The guild aggregates related-party transactions and charges real membership/bond costs. "Three verifiers" means three independent principals. Knowledge is non-rival, while inference, licences and attention are scarce and are governed separately.
- **Evidence / numbers:** first proof estimated at **1–2 weeks** (E). Sources: [Ostrom Nobel lecture slides (2009-12-08)](https://www.nobelprize.org/uploads/2018/06/ostrom-lecture-slides.pdf), [Douceur, The Sybil Attack (2002)](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/wp-content/uploads/2002/01/IPTPS2002.pdf).
- **Prior art / who has it:** Ostrom's commons design principles and Douceur's Sybil analysis. Agents cut monitoring cost but make identity multiplication nearly free, which worsens the admission problem.
- **Verdict:** RUNNER-UP (#8). It has the largest societal ambition, the weakest immediate buyer and the hardest nontechnical validation. It is worth a bounded mechanism experiment, not the flagship. A central firm may coordinate better, and that alternative should be tested.
- **Kill test / first proof:** Proof: three fixture members, and a charter stored by hash with quotas, a quorum, staged suspension and appeal. 100 real continuation branches of one member get neither 100 votes nor 100 allocations. A purchased result enters the library subject to its licence. A wrong sanction is appealed and reversed in an append-only record. Kill: the charter costs more human governance time per accepted deliverable than one accountable maintainer with quotas, without better outcomes or member control. Pooled billing is then kept and the autonomous-society claim dropped.

#### Rejected in this report
- **"An agent bought an API call" as a demo**: already a weak standalone demo, because x402, MPP, Cloudflare Agents payments and AWS AgentCore are existing rails to use, not to rebuild.
- **Pitching escrow or replay protection as missing from x402**: x402 v2 already defines authorization, upfront and escrow flows.
- **Re-pitching signed purchase authorization / mandates**: AP2 already defines signed Checkout and Payment Mandates. Integrate existing formats, and note a protocol mandate is not automatically a legal power of attorney.
- **Token balances or spendable funds inside the snapshot**: falsified by the ledger probe (100 copies admitted 600 units against one authoritative ledger), since a copied key stays the same key and a signed balance goes stale.
- **"Agoric, invented again"**: Agoric's xsnap and Zoe already combine JS execution, snapshots, accounting and contracts, so Sofort's contribution must be the independently usable browser/server/edge work artifact and its economic semantics.
- **Heap image where it adds nothing**: if a capsule adds bytes without materially reducing recovery, handoff or integration work versus source plus journal or a durable workflow, keep the economic product and drop the heap-image requirement.
- **Building a currency, global court, risk carrier, cloud scheduler or new legal person first**: existing DOs, R2, Queues, workflow recovery and x402/MPP supply the infrastructure, and the eight ideas are competing paths, not launch dependencies.
- **Software process as a legal person / mapping human-representative rules directly onto software**: the agency and e-signature laws argue for a real principal with explicit authority instead.
- **Universal DRM via capsules**: once plaintext enters a buyer's memory, a copyable capsule can't prevent copying, so enforcement only happens at trusted boundaries.
- **Calling a homemade DO hash chain a qualified ledger or "notarised"**: eIDAS presumptions need qualified trust-service providers and don't establish the truth of recorded work.
- **Treating forks or clones as independent (reviewers, insurance pool, voters)**: they share failure modes, and correlated copies are one risk cohort and one principal's quota.
- **Billion-agent scale as "all active, independent payers"**: 3 MB × 1B is **3 PB**, and ten checkpoints is **30 PB** before dedup (E). The direction instead is shared immutable images, small lineage/authority records, selective wake and aggregate settlement.
- **Honest-host replay as confidential computing / ZK / attestation**: it provides none of these, browser heaps are hostile, and a browser signer can't attest its own honest execution.

#### Physics and bill every idea must survive

  - Repair allocator and stack limits, and meter work between awaits.

#### B. The computer follows its data
- **What:** Move an agent's continuation to the next data domain instead of dragging data back to the agent's home region. The 100× version is a planetary query/planning program that visits thousands of independently run data owners, reasons locally through narrow capabilities, and brings back only authorized summaries.
- **How it would work:** A two-domain incident investigator analyzes synthetic EU logs and then US deployment metadata, keeping its live continuation between domains. Three paths with identical results are compared: a fixed home executor plus Hyperdrive, a stationary executor with Smart Placement, and one explicit mid-task handoff. Placement uses fetch handlers, because RPC placement isn't supported. Every exported field is audited, and restricted raw records never enter the mobile heap.
- **Evidence / numbers:** **E**: if a 3 MB move costs 0.5 s and avoids 20 sequential round trips each cut by 100 ms, the net gain is about 1.5 s. If it avoids only two, stay put. A minimum predicted benefit and a dwell time prevent ping-pong. Lineage (F): Obliq moved closures and kept state objects local; MapReduce scheduled work near its input. Sources: [Obliq 1995](https://www.usenix.org/publications/compsystems/1995/win_cardelli.pdf), [MapReduce 2004](https://research.google/pubs/mapreduce-simplified-data-processing-on-large-clusters/).
- **Prior art / who has it:** Obliq and MapReduce as lineage. Cloudflare Smart Placement and Hyperdrive are the falsification baseline.
- **Verdict:** KEPT (#2). It has the strongest infrastructure-buyer argument and an unusually good baseline to falsify against. A win justifies a location-aware continuation scheduler; a loss saves months. The hard boundary is per-destination capability/data policy. Privacy is host policy, not a property of Wasm. It is the next experiment if an adopter has a real multi-domain workload.
- **Kill test / first proof:** **E** Demote migration if it fails to improve p95 completion time by 30% on a real, chatty two-domain workload against the optimized stationary baseline, at equal result quality and access policy.

#### C. A billion sleeping agents as an object population
- **What:** Persist each waiting program as immutable state plus a wake address, with no executor allocated while it sleeps. The 100× version gives every order, repair, instrument, contract and long-lived question an executable future that can wait years, wake on mail/webhook/sensor input, and move to a suitable host.
- **How it would work:** Store 10,000 genuinely distinct suspended programs with varied retained data and pending calls, and no heartbeat. Destroy all execution owners. Wake a random sample via HTTPS and inbound email. Report object count, stored bytes, operation count and the three wake classes. Immutable bases are packed and deduplicated but keep random access, integrity, deletion and bounded restore chains. Long-lived timers live outside the queue, since queue retention is finite.
- **Evidence / numbers:** **E**: a billion full images cost $1,500–96,000/month before replicas and history. The $15/month unique-delta population is a research target, not today's snapshot size. Target p95 <250 ms only for the prepared 100 KB class, with 3 MB cold misses tested separately. - **Kill test / first proof:** **E** If realistic capsule customers get no necessary capability beyond native Workflows/DO hibernation at a competitive total cost, drop the standalone sleeper product. Keep persistence as infrastructure for the other ideas.

#### D. One program, live cells across cities and devices
- **What:** A program is a graph of resumable cells, each placed with its data or its human, talking through declared messages. The 100× version is a company or simulation as one inspectable executable graph spanning hundreds of cities and millions of devices. Cells move, fork and retire without rebuilding the whole application.
- **How it would work:** Four cells: EU telemetry, a US planner, a phone-owned annotation cell and a browser-owned private document index. Each cell has a DO mailbox and a Wasm continuation, and cells exchange typed events. An existing CRDT is used for annotations only. The phone is disconnected for 60 s while the others continue, then reconnects and converges. A root manifest records checkpoint hashes and mailbox offsets. Agents may generate and repair the graph, but a deterministic checker enforces schemas, permissions and merge algebra.
- **Evidence / numbers:** **E/I**: each DO is single-threaded, so there is no planetary shared RAM. At most 4 active guests run per request. At an assumed 100 ms RTT, one serial remote dependency per step caps a chain at about 10 steps/s no matter how much CPU is available. Irreversible shared decisions serialize through an owner. Sources: [Obliq 1995](https://www.usenix.org/publications/compsystems/1995/win_cardelli.pdf), [Shapiro et al. CRDTs 2011](https://perso.lip6.fr/Marc.Shapiro/papers/2011/Comprehensive-CRDTs-RR7506-2011-01.pdf).
- **Prior art / who has it:** Obliq (distributed lexical scope) and CRDTs. Neither merges arbitrary execution heaps.
- **Verdict:** RUNNER-UP (rank 4 of 8). It suits collaborative incident rooms, multiplayer simulations and distributed research organizations. Stronger models cannot wish away partitions or invent a correct arbitrary-heap merge.
- **Kill test / first proof:** **E** If one independently written cell migration/partition scenario requires coordinated recovery changes across every other cell, the abstraction has failed. Return to ordinary services with explicit checkpoints.

#### E. Every URL is a resumable computer
- **What:** A URL identifies a computation with a mailbox, durable history and a downloadable continuation, not just a document or endpoint. The 100× version makes the internet an operating environment where every long-lived problem has an address, receives events, grants limited capabilities, forks futures and resumes on its owner's device.
- **How it would work:**

  - Authenticated `POST /events` wakes the computation.
  - `POST /fork` makes a proposal branch.
  - `GET /checkpoint` exports an authorized snapshot.

  - Storage: a DO holds only the live address/authority record, and R2 holds immutable content.

Limits (I): a public URL is a name, not a permission. Previews, crawlers and GET prefetch must never approve, send or buy, and email replies are input, not authorization. Lineage (F): [Plan 9, 1995](https://9p.io/sys/doc/9.html).
- **Prior art / who has it:** Plan 9 (per-process namespaces). Cloudflare Computer preview is adjacent, so the URL must buy exportability and user ownership, not just a nice actor address. Ordinary CRUD is the baseline.

#### F. Become a swarm for thirty seconds, then one file again
- **What:** An agent forks its actual parked continuation into bounded speculative branches, joins typed results, and returns to one portable continuation. The 100× version lets every hard decision briefly rent hundreds of specialists across data owners and devices, then collapse back into one compact, accountable future without keeping a permanent swarm.
- **How it would work:** The investigator's closure and index are preserved, and the program forks into 8 branches. Each branch gets a distinct hypothesis as its pending tool reply, plus read-only synthetic incident tools. Bounded structured findings come back and resume the parent's pending `swarm()` call with the reduced result. The parent is then checkpointed. The eight heaps are never merged. 32 branches are added only after 8 pass.
- **Evidence / numbers:**
  - Fan-out (**E/I**): two coordinator DOs with 4 active Dynamic Workers each fit the published limits.
  - Budget (**E/I**): 30 s is wall time, not CPU, so guest CPU is capped at 100 ms per invocation.
  - Transfer (**E/I**): eight 3 MB transfers total 24 MB. Plain Wasm instances are not portable copy-on-write forks.
  - Cost (**E/I**): 32 × 30 CPU-s is about $0.0192 of Workers CPU alone. Inference, identities, coordination and snapshots are extra.
  - Rate limits (**E/I**): Paid frontier rate limits may not admit 32 immediate calls.
  - Sources: [Cilk 1996](https://dspace.mit.edu/entities/publication/39a8b7ab-7690-4016-94c0-5a0bfb328fa8), [MapReduce 2004](https://research.google/pubs/mapreduce-simplified-data-processing-on-large-clusters/).
- **Prior art / who has it:** Cilk (fork/join, work/span) and MapReduce. Neither makes arbitrary parallel state mergeable.
- **Verdict:** KEPT (#1), and **the ONE bet** (with A's phone return). It is the clearest step beyond "a snapshot exists", because topology becomes an attribute of the program: one, many, one again. Its business case must beat a single strong agent. Its systems case survives even if swarming loses, because the same machinery gives speculative execution, branch inspection and recoverable continuations.
- **Kill test / first proof:** **E** On a held-out incident/code-repair set, if 8 branches cannot improve success by 10 percentage points at equal budget, or halve completion time at equal success, against one strong agent, stop selling swarming. Keep fork as a debugging/speculation primitive.

#### G. The last mile is a computer: a personal device mesh
- **What:** Use a person's consenting devices as intermittent capsule executors, with edge recovery when they disappear. The 100× version makes billions of foreground browsers a cooperative compute fabric. Small programs visit local capabilities and spare compute, while Cloudflare handles addresses, rendezvous and durable fallbacks.
- **How it would work:** A laptop and two real phones voluntarily process ten non-sensitive research batches in foreground tabs, with pause/withdraw controls. State moves via WebRTC DataChannel with TURN credentials and an HTTPS fallback. When a phone closes without warning, its work reruns at the edge from the last acknowledged checkpoint. Bytes, wall time, duplicate work and coarse battery/thermal effects are measured.
- **Evidence / numbers:** **E/I**: images of 100 KB–3 MB, 8 MiB raw cap, no background-uptime promise. Relaying 1 TB through Realtime costs about $50 after the allowance, while one million 20 ms Worker slices cost about $0.40 of CPU. Chasing CPU savings can therefore lose to network/coordination cost. A result hash proves bytes, not honest execution, and re-execution can erase the savings. The executing device sees the plaintext. Sources: [BOINC 2004](https://boinc.berkeley.edu/grid_paper_04.pdf), [Sprite 1991](https://web.stanford.edu/~ouster/cgi-bin/papers/migration-spe.pdf).
- **Prior art / who has it:** BOINC (public-resource computing with validation) and Sprite (reclaiming idle workstations). Both succeeded for suitable work.
- **Verdict:** RUNNER-UP (rank 8 of 8, last). The strongest buyer wants local private-data access or offline continuity, not cheaper cloud CPU. Same-owner devices come first; cross-owner jobs need cheap independent checking and explicit data grants.
- **Kill test / first proof:** **E** If a useful workload has neither necessary local-data access nor a measured total-cost/latency benefit over Workers after validation and transfer, drop the general compute mesh. Keep personal-device continuity from A.

#### H. A continuation CDN: replicate possible futures
- **What:** Place immutable parked continuations near likely next interactions, so users get a fast local speculative resume while one authority decides the committed future. The 100× version caches executable futures like static assets: a program looks locally ready on several continents and becomes authoritative only when an event is accepted.
- **How it would work:** One 100 KB checkpoint, three geographically distinct real clients, cached immutable images, and simultaneous replies. All three compute previews. A DO accepts one logical event/version, and the other two become explicit proposal branches. Warm and cold reads are compared against one-region execution. The mutable head pointer never goes in KV or an unvalidated CDN response.
- **Evidence / numbers:** **E/I**: three replicas triple storage. A 3 MB image at 348 locations would be 1.044 GB per checkpoint before versions. No Cloudflare API pins an image in every city's cache. R2 allows one write/s to the same key, so keys must be immutable and versioned. A remote commit fence keeps its WAN RTT. Sources: [R2 limits](https://developers.cloudflare.com/r2/platform/limits/), [Emerald 1988](https://people.eecs.berkeley.edu/~prabal/resources/osprelim/JLH%2B88.pdf), [CRDT report 2011](https://perso.lip6.fr/Marc.Shapiro/papers/2011/Comprehensive-CRDTs-RR7506-2011-01.pdf).

#### The common contract: what actually moves
- **What:** The minimal capsule format, capture boundary and authority-handoff protocol shared by all eight ideas.
- **How it would work:**
  - Capsule fields: `{format, engineHash, abiHash, computerId, branchId, checkpointHash, parentHash, rawLength, memoryAndGlobals, pendingCallDigests, journalCursor, requestedCapabilities}`, plus an optional blob manifest. Credentials, grants and the effect ledger stay outside portable memory.
  - Capture: pause at a guest tool promise with an empty Wasm stack, drain runnable jobs within a budget, and restore only on the exact compatible interpreter/ABI.
  - Handoff step 1, prepare: epoch `e` stops new effects and uploads immutable `h` to R2.
  - Step 2, commit: the authority DO records `head=h`, the journal position and epoch `e+1` in one transaction.
  - Step 3, activate: the destination verifies hash/build/size and gets an epoch-scoped grant. The broker rejects stale-epoch effects.
  - Step 4, dispatch: write an operation record before the effect, use provider idempotency keys, and block and reconcile uncertain non-idempotent effects.
  - Step 5, fork/join: branches get read-only or simulated tools. Join means resuming the parent with a typed result or promoting one child. Heap merging is not part of the contract.
- **Evidence / numbers:** No new numbers. Scope is supported by the earlier [quiescent-boundary evidence](../portability/02-wasm.md#31-preferred-path-quickjs-heap-continuation-empty-wasm-stack). Excluded from the capsule: the native V8 heap, TCP connections, DOM, Browser Run processes, model weights and container processes.
- **Prior art / who has it:** n/a.
- **Verdict:** Shared design contract, not ranked. It makes two explicit compromises. The authority DO stays stationary at first, which costs a geographic hop per effect, because moving it needs a forwarding protocol and changing `locationHint` doesn't do that. A file can run offline with effects disabled but cannot recreate cloud-held grants or commit globally. Exportability is about execution state, not authority.
- **Kill test / first proof:** n/a.

#### "One → Eight → One": the smallest retweetable demo

Each slot creates a fresh Wasm instance, and the checkpoint is passed as request data. Ambient outbound access is denied and tools are brokered.

  - Budget (**E**): 100 runs means 800 branch restores on 8 stable identities, and at most 2.4 GB of branch image reads from a 3 MB image.
- **Prior art / who has it:** The expert-reply defense names Vercel `quickjs-wasi`, LangChain `quickjs-rs`, XS snapshots, Golem and Cloudflare Computer/Agents/Workflows. The claimed contribution is the explicit cross-host, fan-out, join and authority contract.

Billion-scale storage claims come only after the proof. If phone support fails, publish the failure matrix and drop the phone claim. #### Rejected in this report
The report has no rejected-ideas list. These are the positions it explicitly dismisses.
- **A giant deployment map / animated planet** — a weak contribution on its own. Don't draw eight fake cities; several branches may share a colo.
- **Another general agent platform ("give every agent a computer", "cheapest runtime")** — Cloudflare Computer already occupies this. Build an adapter instead.
- **"Wakes under X ms anywhere"** — no defensible finite X for universal global wake. Publish per-class, per-location measurements instead.
- **Moving a DO by changing `locationHint`** — existing DOs do not relocate, so a hint change is not migration.
- **One global DO registry/scheduler, or one queue for a billion wakes** — single-threaded and rate-limited (55.6 h per billion through one queue). Shard instead.
- **Ordinary KV or CDN cache as an ownership fence / current-head truth** — eventually consistent (60 s or more).
- **One Dynamic Worker identity per capsule wake** — about $2M/day at a billion identities. Use stable-ID adapters with snapshots as data.
- **Container filesystem snapshots as continuations** — they exclude memory and processes, so they cannot resume a JS stack.
- **Merging arbitrary child heaps** — outside the contract. Join through a typed result or promote one child.
- **"Sleep cheaply" as the pitch, or a billion-agent identity count** — Workflows and DO hibernation already do this. Without useful state it is only a storage demo.
- **Cheaper CPU from volunteer browsers** — loses to transfer and validation cost. Result hashes don't prove honest execution.
- **Every-URL internet-OS language as a first milestone** — an extrapolation for after the proof.

### 06-selfgrowing: Software that grows its own substrate
Lens: self-growing software, with agents as the substrate. The report treats a missing function, module, syscall or workflow as a recoverable "semantic fault". The program parks, behavior is grown against an authoritative reference, qualified outside the proposer, cached, and the program retries. Headline finding: GrowOS shows huge leverage when a small semantic gap blocks many programs (4% to 81% of the corpus, M). But human helpers and environmental overfitting qualify that result, so the unit of trust must be a versioned contract plus an environment profile, not "passed held-out". The ONE bet is **fault-grown Node, with the oracle factory as its core and the capsule as its output**. Verdict: **go-with-changes**. Build it as a qualified Node API subset, not "a Node.js that grows itself", and kill it if it cannot unlock unseen programs beyond ordinary shims at a useful cost.

#### Thesis: missing behavior becomes a recoverable fault
- **What:** make implementation availability a runtime concern. A capsule becomes a running program plus the substrate grown for it plus the evidence that justifies that substrate. The ambitious end state is an operating environment whose application-specific implementation starts empty.
- **How it would work:** park the program on a miss. Acquire examples from an authoritative reference, grow the missing behavior, qualify it outside the proposer, and retry. After acceptance, ordinary code handles later calls with no inference. The enduring asset is the independent behavioral contract, the execution history and the accepted component library. The testable claim is "zero prewritten implementations for the chosen application/OS API profile" on a disclosed, counted seed (interpreter, isolation boundary, resource controller, effect broker, loader, acceptance machinery).
- **Evidence / numbers (all M, from GrowOS phase 2):**
  - **3,198/3,947** comparable compiled programs ran byte-identical to Linux, versus **159** before (**81.0% vs 4.0%**). Held-out: **644/792 (81.3%)**.
  - **8** accepted handlers hold **218** grown lines. The **74-line** mmap handler alone accounts for **2,924 of 3,039** newly passing programs. It relied on **55** manually supplied kernel-helper lines, and a human loader-placement change unlocked **50** programs.
  - The lower bound is **77.5%** over all **4,127** compiled programs (**180** path-embedding rebuilds excluded). Stock-layout ELFs reach **73.2%**. N=1 transaction per target, on macOS/HVF.
  - **4** handlers passed the gates but were excluded for environmental overfitting (they mirrored oracle-container paths; sysinfo reported **8 GB** in a **32 MiB** guest).
  - The existing controller separates proposal from acceptance and keeps hidden artifacts outside the proposal repo (F). Its mutant gate grades the tests, not the candidate.
  - Sources: [Phase-2 experiment](../../jitk/grow/EXPERIMENTS-PHASE2-2026-10-27.md), [Corpus results](../../jitk/scriptc-corpus/RESULTS-PHASE2.md), [Grow controller](../../jitk/grow/README.md).
- **Prior art / who has it:**
  - Lineage (F): tracing JITs, from TraceMonkey (Gal et al., PLDI 2009), which supply guard/exit discipline. Denning's working set (1968) and Launchbury's lazy semantics (1993) supply the economics. FlashFill's precursor (Gulwani, POPL 2011) and Sketch (Solar-Lezama) supply synthesis from examples and oracles.
  - Closest new precedent: `pi-world`, an agent-grown QuickJS/Wasm heap with checked revisions and rollback.
  - Node portability is occupied: Cloudflare Workers Node APIs, `unenv`, WebContainers.
- **Verdict:** KEPT as the framing for all eight ideas. A literal "OS with zero lines written in advance" is a research direction, not an honest description. A model cannot synthesize a capability the seed does not expose, and accepting more model-written code is not itself success.

#### Common implementation contract (shared by all eight proposals)
- **What:** one mechanism under every idea. It needs neither a V8 replacement nor a new workflow platform.
- **How it would work:** six steps.
  1. Detect a miss at an explicit surface (module resolver, API registry, interpreter host-call boundary), not from arbitrary exceptions.
  2. Park on async demands. Synchronous misses discard the instance and replay from a prior boundary, and no unjournaled write may escape.
  3. Pin the oracle and environment (versions, fixtures, locale, scheduling, memory, observable outputs) before sampling.
  4. Grow in a disposable branch. The model cannot edit the supervisor, effect authority, grader or sealed audit. Requests for new primitives are counted as seed changes.
  5. Qualify and publish immutable artifacts. Keep frozen validation and the fresh final audit separate, and cache by code, oracle/profile, seed ABI and evidence hashes.
  6. Resume under a pinned revision. Code and guest state roll back together, and delivered effects need broker reconciliation.

  Deployment (I): a TypeScript coordinator in a Durable Object, R2 for code, capsules and receipts, and Queues/Workflows for growth jobs. Node/Linux oracles run in a local process or Container. Start with a fixed Wasm interpreter plus generated guest JS modules.
- **Evidence / numbers:**
  - F: Dynamic Workers load generated JS/Wasm with chosen bindings. Ordinary Workers prohibit `eval`, `new Function` and byte-buffer Wasm compilation.
  - M (not rerun): quiescent QuickJS/Wasm transfer was shown locally across Node, Chromium, workerd and Dynamic Workers. Snapshots are **1.5–6.4 MB** compressed, glue runs **5–11x** slower than V8, and the QuickJS allocation accounting was defective.
  - Sources: [Dynamic Workers](https://developers.cloudflare.com/dynamic-workers/getting-started/), [Workers standards](https://developers.cloudflare.com/workers/runtime-apis/web-standards/), [roadblocks](../v8/03-roadblocks.md).
- **Prior art / who has it:** Cloudflare Dynamic Workers. The existing GrowOS grow controller.
- **Verdict:** KEPT as shared infrastructure. Minimal runtime code does not imply a minimal heap, complete replay, or a safe public tenancy service.

#### 1. Fault-grown Node: a runtime that learns the API a program just requested
- **What:** run a Node-shaped program in a constrained JS capsule, where each missing API becomes a growth job qualified against a pinned real Node oracle. 100x version: submit an npm program to Workers and the Node environment it needs grows until it runs, with explicit refusals for unsupported native/process semantics.
- **How it would work:**
  - Trace-JIT guard discipline plus oracle-guided synthesis, with the authoritative reference kept outside the model.
  - Bundle without changing application source. Real misses trigger growth, and Node supplies results for public and fresh sealed operation sequences.
  - Accepted components are reused by later packages with no model call. If native Workers or an existing shim already runs a package, use that.
- **Evidence / numbers:**
  - First proof is **10 working days** (E): freeze **10** dependency-bearing programs plus **10** unseen ones from different packages.
  - Candidate API families, chosen after a dependency census: `path.posix`, `StringDecoder`, `EventEmitter`, read-only files.
  - A StringDecoder target must survive arbitrary chunk boundaries and invalid encodings, and restore while holding an incomplete character.
  - Report every miss, refusal, helper addition, attempt, token receipt, cold delay and unseen-program pass.
  - Demo is 75 s (I).
  - Sources: [Jha et al., ICSE 2010](https://people.eecs.berkeley.edu/~sseshia/pubs/b2hd-jha-icse10.html), [Node StringDecoder](https://nodejs.org/api/string_decoder.html), [Cloudflare Node APIs](https://developers.cloudflare.com/workers/runtime-apis/nodejs/).
- **Prior art / who has it:** Cloudflare Workers Node compatibility, `unenv` polyfills, WebContainers. Jha et al.'s oracle-guided component synthesis.
- **Verdict:** KEPT (#1 of 8, shortlist #1), and the ONE bet. It is the strongest first product and public experiment, has an existing oracle, and makes growth visible within a minute. The full Node promise is far away: native addons, process control, threads, sockets, scheduler behavior and filesystem durability are separate obligations. The decisive comparison is native Workers support and conventional shims.
- **Kill test / first proof:** Kill: after two weeks and a fixed proposal budget, the grower unlocks no unseen program beyond the conventional polyfill baseline (E).

#### 2. Every capsule carries the runtime grown for exactly it
- **What:** package a program with only the API implementations its declared behavioral envelope needs, grown and qualified together. 100x version: a bespoke executable environment per program, portable between browser and edge, replaces generic container/runtime images, and its manifest says what happens outside the qualified envelope.
- **How it would work:** exokernel/library-OS specialization, with agents manufacturing the candidates. A content-addressed registry shares accepted fragments. It differs from tree shaking only if it synthesizes a smaller implementation of the behavior rather than deleting unreachable exports. Model upgrades can rebuild old capsules against unchanged evidence, while existing capsules keep running on pinned artifacts.
- **Evidence / numbers:**
  - First proof is **1–2 weeks** (E): **6** programs using distinct subsets of one library surface.
  - It compares tree shaking, a shared full polyfill and grown per-program modules, measuring engine + modules + state bytes, cold load, retained heap and restore.
  - Existing **1.5–6.4 MB** state (M) can swamp kilobyte runtime savings.
  - Demo is 60 s (I).
  - Source: [Engler, Kaashoek, O'Toole, SOSP 1995](https://pdos.csail.mit.edu/6.828/2008/readings/engler95exokernel.pdf).
- **Prior art / who has it:** exokernels and library operating systems. Ordinary bundling and tree shaking.
- **Verdict:** KEPT (#3 of 8, shortlist #3). It turns acceptance into a portable artifact. A shared runtime may beat bespoke copies, so deduplicate honestly. If the compression benefits fail, qualified runtime ownership may survive at feature-level scope.
- **Kill test / first proof:** Kill: counting the shared engine, capsule state and component/evidence storage, the grown form achieves less than a **2x** reduction in total transferred bytes versus ordinary bundling, with no separate operational benefit (E). It is then demoted to a packaging feature.

#### 3. The blank operating system: applications grow the services beneath them
- **What:** an empty implementation registry above a fixed capability monitor, where programs grow a virtual filesystem, descriptors, pipes, timers and process-like actors on demand. 100x version: an entire application OS emerges from attempted use, with no prewritten implementations for its advertised profile, and Linux or a specified model as oracle.
- **How it would work:** a fixed QuickJS/Wasm machine exposes bounded memory, clock input and a tool channel, with the engine and monitor counted separately. An in-memory object-store primitive is supplied, but no directory, descriptor or pipe policy. Agents grow cooperating multi-module services, going beyond GrowOS's one-file handlers, compared against a Node process with a temp filesystem (or Linux where syscalls are advertised). Scheduling is restricted to an explicit cooperative actor model, not Linux process equivalence.
- **Evidence / numbers:**
  - First proof is **10 days** (E): three unchanged TS programs request file reads, directory creation and producer/consumer byte transport.
  - Hidden tests vary path names, structure, operation order, capacity and errors. The proof persists at a pipe wait and restores to finish. No VM change or KVM is needed.
  - Demo is 90 s (I) and publishes prewritten versus grown line counts and every seed change.
  - Sources: [Kay, Early History of Smalltalk, HOPL 1993](https://worrydream.com/EarlyHistoryOfSmalltalk/), exokernel (SOSP 1995).
- **Prior art / who has it:** Smalltalk's message-passing cells, exokernels, GrowOS (whose mmap helpers show that seed primitives matter). "Software should be grown" is a paraphrase, not a verified Kay quote.
- **Verdict:** RUNNER-UP (#5 of 8). It is the biggest systems hook with lower near-term confidence, because shared state and seed expansion make autonomy easy to overclaim. A tiny library OS is a stepping stone, not "Linux grown from nothing".
- **Kill test / first proof:** Kill: the first cross-service integration needs per-program handwritten exceptions in the monitor to pass the unseen sequence suite (E).

#### 4. Software that rewrites itself toward a held-out oracle forever
- **What:** run a service as an immutable behavioral contract plus a continuously searched population of implementations. Accepted revisions improve cost, latency, size or compatibility while preserving the contract. 100x version: an old application becomes a standing optimization problem that every model release can rewrite.
- **How it would work:** a pinned original serves as oracle. The grower replaces modules and data representations within a fixed budget and keeps competing revisions rather than hill-climbing. It requires exact output/error/effect-trace agreement, optimizes one declared metric and caps the others. Parked capsules are replayed into the new revision (old heap images stay tied to old builds and need replay or a checked state converter). A new model may suggest better test generators but cannot weaken the incumbent contract. "Forever" means a persistent search facility with stopping rules, not perpetual progress.
- **Evidence / numbers:**
  - First proof is **two weeks** (E): a deterministic TS document-transform or routing service, one public workload plus **two** withheld input families.
  - The winner is confirmed on an untouched workload, and rollback is shown after a fresh audit finds a regression.
  - Demo is 75 s (I).
  - Sources: [STOKE, ASPLOS 2013](https://theory.stanford.edu/~aiken/publications/papers/asplos13.pdf), [Kephart & Chess, autonomic computing 2003](https://www.cs.tufts.edu/comp/250SA/papers/kephart2003.pdf).
- **Prior art / who has it:** STOKE superoptimization, autonomic computing / MAPE-K, CI refactoring bots (as a weaker baseline).
- **Verdict:** KEPT (#2 of 8, shortlist #2). It is the best long-term model-release thesis and most directly monetizes each better model. Start with pure transformations, where the oracle is cheap, prove net savings, and only then earn stateful migrations. Whole distributed services bring consistency, storage-migration and effect problems first.
- **Kill test / first proof:** Kill: the winner produces no net operating savings after amortizing growth and qualification over the customer's actual execution volume (E).

#### 5. Grow the oracle: a behavioral map of the software ecosystem
- **What:** agents discover behavioral distinctions, propose discriminating probes, and turn reference executions into versioned compatibility contracts that drive growth. 100x version: a living executable map of Node, browsers, runtimes, SDKs and OS personalities, where each upstream release reveals what must grow or migrate.
- **How it would work:** trusted adapters run model-suggested probes against pinned references, splitting input variation from environment variation (renamed and restructured files, locale, resource limits, longer sequences). Failed implementations contribute reusable distinctions, and new evidence can invalidate and repair old components. The agent's generated expected answer never becomes the oracle. Use Node for Node APIs, browser/spec/WPT relations for Web APIs, and Linux only for Linux contracts.
- **Evidence / numbers:**
  - First proof is **1–2 weeks** (E): **3** bounded API families (one stateful), withheld environment variants and an independent mutant bank.
  - A known-correct control must not be rejected.
  - F: independently written versions showed dependent failures in Knight & Leveson's 1986 experiment. Test262 acknowledges omissions and errors.
  - Demo is 60 s (I).
  - Sources: [Daikon, Ernst et al. 2001](https://plse.cs.washington.edu/daikon/pubs/invariants-tse2001-abstract.html), [McKeeman, Differential Testing 1998](https://www.cs.tufts.edu/comp/150FP/archive/bill-mckeeman/DifferentailTesting.pdf), [Csmith, PLDI 2011](https://www.flux.utah.edu/paper/yang-pldi11).
- **Prior art / who has it:** Daikon, McKeeman differential testing, Csmith, Test262, Web Platform Tests.
- **Verdict:** RUNNER-UP (#4 of 8), but folded into the ONE bet as its core. It is mandatory capability for the other bets and less dramatic as a standalone launch. Standalone demand and distribution need validation. A browser majority flags a discrepancy, while a spec or chosen compatibility target settles it. Three models agreeing is weaker still.
- **Kill test / first proof:** Kill: on the blinded mutant/control bank, generated evidence gives no improvement in bug detection over a fixed generator at the same correct-control acceptance rate and oracle budget (E).

#### 6. API resurrection: grow a replacement from a surviving behavioral fossil
- **What:** grow an API or application backend from a reference executable, recorded sessions or an executable spec, keeping the quirks its clients depend on. 100x version: a deployed system sheds its original stack and vendor while preserving user workflows, growing only when an unseen client behavior demands it.
- **How it would work:** specification mining plus oracle-guided distinguishing queries. Models propose adapters and schema mappings, and capsules replay the same history into old and new implementations. A "fossil" (a lawful recording with precise state/effect boundaries) survives model churn and can be recompiled. Once the old oracle is gone, the contract stops at the evidence collected.
- **Evidence / numbers:**
  - First proof is **10 days** (E): an owned TS CRUD service with pagination and conditional updates. Sessions and initial DB states are split as wholes (random adjacent splits leak state), with budgeted resettable oracle queries.
  - The output is a Durable Object-backed replacement, repeated with trace-only evidence. Unsupported behavior must fail explicitly, not be served from a lookup table.
  - Demo is 75 s (I).
  - Source: [Gulwani, Flash Fill story, 2021-09-14](https://blog.sigplan.org/2021/09/14/the-story-of-the-flash-fill-feature-in-excel/).
- **Prior art / who has it:** specification mining, oracle-guided synthesis, FlashFill in Excel, ordinary migration consulting.
- **Verdict:** RUNNER-UP (#6 of 8). It is a plausible paid migration wedge with lower novelty and a high risk of becoming migration consulting. There are hard limits once the reference disappears. Start with customer-owned systems, not cloning SaaS from screenshots.
- **Kill test / first proof:** Kill: the trace-only system fails more than **10%** of preregistered unseen sessions after the fixed growth budget (E). That kills trace-only resurrection for this class, while the reference-query version may survive.

#### 7. A malleable application that grows at the point of use
- **What:** a user corrects a running app's behavior. The system forks its capsule, grows the behavior and needed substrate, checks preservation obligations, and lets the user adopt or undo the branch. 100x version: software evolves with every person and community, and tools share persistent data and exchange qualified extensions.
- **How it would work:** grow the requested action and its validation helper in a fork of the user's state. Unchanged behavior outside the changed surface is preserved, and the changed surface is tested against user-approved examples plus independent invariants. There is no equivalence demand for the intentional change. Migrations are transactional and versioned. Adopt, extend, undo without data loss, and reject conflicting extensions visibly.
- **Evidence / numbers:**
  - First proof is **two weeks** (E): a task/roster app with the user request "split this shift across two people".
  - Demo is 90 s (I).
  - Sources: [Victor, Learnable Programming 2012](https://worrydream.com/LearnableProgramming/), [Ink & Switch, Malleable Software 2025](https://www.inkandswitch.com/essay/malleable-software/), [pi-world](https://github.com/igouss/pi-world).
- **Prior art / who has it:** Bret Victor, Ink & Switch (which says AI codegen alone does not solve malleability), `pi-world` as an immediate implementation precedent.
- **Verdict:** RUNNER-UP (#8 of 8, last). It has real user value and strong lineage, but close existing prototypes and the least objective oracle for intended changes. Treat it as a later application of a robust substrate, not a reason to build a new app builder.
- **Kill test / first proof:** Kill: in a five-user task study, most users cannot explain what changed and reverse it without developer help (E).

#### 8. The grower grows itself: agents manufacture their own computing substrate
- **What:** an agent evolves its module resolver, editor, planner, component library and public test generator by running descendants in forked capsules against a frozen external audit. 100x version: a tiny stable seed plus a goal, and the agent grows its own tools, runtime services and apps, including better tools for growing the next generation.
- **How it would work:** descendants may change only editing/planning tools, module packaging and public probe generation, never acceptance code, capability policy, model budget or audit access. A branching archive is kept. A descendant must use its changed tooling to grow one further runtime component (limited self-hosting). Winners are judged once on untouched tasks. The model weights stay external and unchanged.
- **Evidence / numbers:**
  - First proof is **1–2 weeks** (E): **20** bounded descendants versus **20** trials of the unchanged harness, counting all search, inference and oracle cost.
  - F: DGM reports benchmark gains and cross-benchmark transfer with frozen foundation models. Its SWE-bench diagnosis saw private test patches (Appendix C.3).
  - Demo is 90 s (I).
  - Sources: [DGM, Zhang et al., v3 2026-03-12](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.22954v3), [Thompson, Trusting Trust 1984](https://www.cs.cmu.edu/~rdriley/487/papers/Thompson_1984_ReflectionsonTrustingTrust.pdf), [Varela/Maturana/Uribe, Autopoiesis 1974](https://repositorio.uchile.cl/bitstream/2250/160309/1/item_34548860810.pdf).
- **Prior art / who has it:** Darwin Gödel Machine (direct prior art), self-hosting compilers, autopoiesis (as a design analogy only).
- **Verdict:** RUNNER-UP (#7 of 8). It has the highest model complementarity and lowest near-term confidence. Defer it until ordinary substrate growth and independent auditing work, or it mainly evolves ways to exploit a weak scoreboard. It is not a claim of runaway self-improvement.
- **Kill test / first proof:** Kill: the evolved harness shows no gain on untouched tasks at equal total search-plus-execution budget versus the unchanged harness (E).

#### Model-release thesis: a factory with an oracle (cost model and test)
- **What:** the durable abstraction is a factory with an oracle, not a repository of generated implementations. Stronger models widen what can be grown, from pure functions to stateful APIs, multi-module OS services and application protocols.
- **How it would work:**
  - Cost per newly supported program (E) = `(proposal + oracle + independent audit + seed-engineering cost) / newly passing unseen programs`.
  - With acceptance probability `p` and `g` unseen programs unlocked per reusable component, the proposal term is approximately `C / (p × g)`. Retries are correlated, so report actual receipts.
  - Test the thesis (E): freeze the split, seed APIs, evidence, audit generator and limits. Compare at least two model generations at equal proposal count and equal total budget, tracking incorrect accepts on a fresh audit.
- **Evidence / numbers:** no measured claim that a later model wins. GrowOS is N=1 (M). Verification cost may grow as fast as implementation scope (E).
- **Prior art / who has it:** none named.
- **Verdict:** KEPT as framing. The assets a model release can't replace are captured contracts, histories, controlled reference environments, independent audits, versioned artifacts, effect ownership and deployment adapters. If a standard coding agent plus CI gives the same qualified result at equal effort, the product boundary has disappeared.
- **Kill test / first proof:** a higher acceptance rate does not count as progress if incorrect acceptance rises.

#### Rejected in this report
- **A wrapper that asks a model to write a polyfill** — better coding agents would absorb it; the value is in contract and qualification.
- **Literal "operating system with zero lines written in advance"** — dishonest as stated. The seed must be counted, and the claim narrowed to zero prewritten implementations for a chosen API profile.
- **An agent changing a persistent heap, as the differentiator** — `pi-world` already does it. Differentiation must come from independent oracle qualification, demand-triggered growth and cross-program evidence.
- **Inventing Node in a browser / reimplementing familiar APIs** — occupied by Workers, `unenv` and WebContainers. Reuse them when they win.
- **Treating "passed held-out" as the unit of trust** — four GrowOS handlers passed but overfit the oracle environment. Trust needs a versioned contract plus environment profile.
- **Synthesis by tree shaking only (idea 2)** — merely deleting unreachable exports is not the idea. Also no claim of a global minimum, and no claim that a finite trace proves future paths.
- **"Linux grown from nothing" (idea 3)** — overclaims what a tiny library OS demonstrates.
- **"Rewrite forever" as perpetual progress (idea 4)** — narrowed to a persistent search facility with stopping rules.
- **Voting implementations or models into truth (idea 5)** — dependent failures (Knight & Leveson). A spec or chosen target must adjudicate, and three models agreeing is weaker still.
- **Cloning arbitrary commercial SaaS from screenshots (idea 6)** — replaced by customer-owned systems with recorded evidence.
- **A chat box that repeatedly regenerates the whole app (idea 7)** — called a weak bet and demoted.
- **Recursive intelligence improvement / "alive" autopoietic software (idea 8)** — overstated. DGM shows bounded empirical self-improvement only, and the model weights stay external.

### 07-nextmodel: What survives the next model
Lens: strategy, contrarian and adjacent concepts. The report asks which capsule products get more valuable, not less, when the next model ships, and it takes the builder's 2–5× excitement haircut as a design stress test. It surveys what model releases absorbed in 2024–2026 (wrappers, prompt tooling, RAG plumbing, generic agent loops, autocomplete-only coding) and what became more valuable (execution substrates, verification/oracles, fresh evaluation, trust/authority, distribution/data rights, physical-world hooks). Headline finding (I): persistence itself and "an OS for agents" fail the test. The defensible asset is an independent relationship to reality, and capsules are the transport, not the moat. There are no new measurements. The ONE bet is **Oracle Foundry**, with Failure Seed Bank as its accumulation loop and one Future Browser view as its demo. Verdict: **go-with-changes**, with two separate day-14 go/no-go gates: one for the product, and one for the capsule-specific investment, which must beat source + inputs + event replay.

#### Shared frame: accepted work, not tokens, and forks without authority
- **What:** a cross-cutting economics and architecture rule for all nine ideas. The economic unit is accepted work, and speculative computation never carries write authority.
- **How it would work:** `cost per accepted outcome = (generation + execution + verification + human adjudication + failure/recovery cost) / accepted outcomes`. Spend part of the abundance on fresh tests and adversarial experiments, not only on more candidates against a stale score. Every proposal must answer three questions. Does another model release increase the useful work passing through it? What accumulates that a competitor can't regenerate from public information? Why does it need a capsule rather than a transcript, DB row or ordinary durable workflow? Common architecture: bounded QuickJS/Wasm orchestration, host-side model calls and secrets, a fixed interpreter artifact, R2 for content-addressed checkpoints, Durable Objects (DOs) for a per-branch effect-authority ledger, Queues, Workflows where those are enough, and separate Node/Container backends for native or bulk work. A selected branch presents intended effects and expected remote versions to a broker and commits only after revalidation. Discarding a fork can't undo a sent message, charge or motor movement.
- **Evidence / numbers:** M-prior (not rerun): realistic snapshots **1.5–6.4 MB** compressed; guest glue **5–11×** slower than direct V8. The local spike shows a quiescent pending-tool continuation across Node, Chromium and local workerd, not production edge service or native-jitk-to-Wasm conversion. F: with a fixed positive false-accept probability, more attempts mean more chances to pass incorrectly (I). Jevons, *The Coal Question* (1865) is the lineage, applied conditionally (I). Sources: [Jevons](https://www.econlib.org/library/YPDBooks/Jevons/jvnCQ.html?chapter_num=9), [SWE-bench Verified reassessment (2026-02-23)](https://openai.com/index/why-we-no-longer-evaluate-swe-bench-verified/) (59.4% flawed tasks on the audited 138-task subset, F), [AlphaEvolve (2025-05-14)](https://deepmind.google/blog/alphaevolve-a-gemini-powered-coding-agent-for-designing-advanced-algorithms/) (0.7% of worldwide compute recovered, F).
- **Prior art / who has it:** absorbed or bundled capabilities (F): ChatGPT search (2024-10-31), OpenAI Responses API with web/file search, computer use and Agents SDK (2025-03-11), Structured Outputs (2024-08-06), the Anthropic prompt improver (2024-11-14), Gemini 1.5 1M context (2024-02), Claude 1M context GA (2026-03-13), Claude Managed Agents (beta 2026-04-08; permission policies 2026-09-10), Claude Code (2025-02-24). Counterexamples that grew (F): LangChain/LangGraph 1.0 (2025-10-22), Cursor (>$100M recurring revenue 2025-01-16, ARR >$500M 2025-06-06, vendor-reported), Codex (>5M weekly users 2026-06-02). Complements (F): E2B Series A, Cloudflare Dynamic Workers beta (2026-03-24), AlphaProof, W3C VC 2.0, Stripe/OpenAI Agentic Commerce Protocol, Cloudflare Pay Per Crawl, Gemini Robotics.
- **Verdict:** KEPT as the foundation and proof discipline, not a product. Every first proof compares capsule restore against event-log replay plus ordinary checkpoints at equal budgets.
- **Kill test / first proof:** two-week discipline (E): reuse existing snapshot machinery. Measure cold/warm latency, CPU, model spend, artifact bytes, false accepts/rejects and recovery. Seal held-out tests outside the candidate's authority and rotate them after exposure.

#### 1. Oracle Foundry: acceptance infrastructure for machine work
- **What:** package a task, an independently owned reference and its acceptance procedure as an executable experiment that any model can compete to satisfy. 100× version: an acceptance network where autonomous workers deliver checkable outcomes and acceptance owners are accountable for their specifications and evidence.
- **How it would work:** a narrow tool ABI and an independent checker stop the proposer from redefining success. Stronger models generate more candidates and harder counterexamples and may help improve evaluators, but promotion needs independently checked evidence. The start is one small reference-backed contract (a bounded SQLite query/API subset or a pure data-format function), reusing GrowOS's external Linux oracle, held-out probes and mutant grading. It builds a customer-owned archive of semantic edge cases.
- **Evidence / numbers:** no measurements. First proof (E): **20** bounded attempts over **3** small contracts. Scores (E): stronger 5, solo 4, signal 4, proof 5 = **18/20**. Sources: [Necula, proof-carrying code (Jan 1997)](https://doi.org/10.1145/263699.263712), [AlphaProof (2024)](https://deepmind.google/blog/ai-solves-imo-problems-at-silver-medal-level/), [AlphaEvolve (2025)](https://deepmind.google/blog/alphaevolve-a-gemini-powered-coding-agent-for-designing-advanced-algorithms/).
- **Prior art / who has it:** Necula's PCC (untrusted producer, host checks a proof against a policy), AlphaProof (Lean verification), AlphaEvolve (automated evaluators). What stalled it: cheap checking needs an adequate spec and a tractable proof/reference, which are expensive to create, and most software lacks them. Tests are evidence, not proofs.
- **Verdict:** KEPT (#1), the ONE bet. It has the best fit with GrowOS knowledge and an independently checkable wedge. The asset is a trusted reference plus fresh counterexample history. An LLM judging another LLM collapses the premise. Main risk: building and maintaining a trustworthy oracle may cost more than the work it accepts. Demo: a candidate passes its visible tests, the oracle finds a counterexample, the user forks at that input, and the verdict replays from a downloaded artifact.
- **Kill test / first proof:** Proof: run against reference outputs, a sealed test set and independently designed wrong implementations, publishing full denominators. It passes if it rejects all seeded semantic mutants, accepts the known-good reference, and finds at least one wrong candidate that passes the visible tests. Repeat grading from an exported capsule on a second host and compare with a source-plus-event-log package. Kill: at equal evaluation and human-adjudication budget, it fails to reduce wrong acceptances on untouched candidates versus the project's existing independent CI, with false rejections no higher.

#### 2. Future Browser: choose among executable futures before acting
- **What:** present proposed actions as branches of a running workflow, each with its assumptions and observable consequences. 100× version: speculative, forked reality as a primary UI, where users compare executable futures and authorize a revalidated plan instead of reading a confident paragraph.
- **How it would work:** pause a real orchestration program before a write, fork it into policy alternatives against isolated state copies, show ledger differences, then revalidate the chosen branch against the authoritative system and commit through the broker. Branches should show variation in assumptions, not just answers. Ten copies of one wrong premise aren't independent evidence.
- **Evidence / numbers:** scores (E): stronger 5, solo 3, signal 5, proof 4 = **17/20**. Sources: [Jefferson, Virtual Time (Jul 1985)](https://doi.org/10.1145/3916.3988), local [v8 landscape](../v8/02-landscape.md).
- **Prior art / who has it:** Jefferson's Virtual Time (optimistic execution, rollback, antimessages), plus existing interpreter branching and time-travel systems. What stalled it: speculation works only where state and communication are modeled, rollback can't retract real effects, and world models are expensive with limited coverage.
- **Verdict:** KEPT (#2). It is the clearest answer to why capsules should exist and has the strongest short video. Its weak point is prediction: the runtime can't infer unobservable external state. The asset is the tested adapter and effect contract. A generic branching chat UI is easy to copy and platform vendors can add it. In the ONE bet it survives as one demonstration view.
- **Kill test / first proof:** Proof: a TS/DO synthetic subscription account (credits, invoices, cancellation rules) forked into cancel/downgrade/credit futures. A concurrent account change makes the chosen stale plan get rejected, and fresh revalidation then executes one change. One continuation exports to browser/Node and is compared with replay from the original request. Kill: two branches meeting the modeled conditions produce materially different real outcomes because essential API state can't be observed or version-checked. The workflow is then demoted to advisory simulation.

#### 3. Capsule Lab: executable notebooks for AI scientists
- **What:** make an AI experiment a resumable, inspectable capsule holding its procedure, pinned inputs, observations and acceptance contract. 100× version: a scientific literature of living experiments that others can resume, challenge or fork without rebuilding a hidden notebook session.
- **How it would work:** the capsule holds orchestration state, code/input hashes, model request metadata, a tool journal and metrics, with large artifacts in R2 by hash. Deterministic replay uses recorded model/tool responses. New inference is a labelled new branch, not a reproduction. A model upgrade can continue a parked experiment with a new proposer while keeping earlier observations.
- **Evidence / numbers:** scores (E): stronger 5, solo 3, signal 5, proof 4 = **17/20**. F: Pimentel et al. (MSR 2019) studied **1.4 million** Jupyter notebooks for reproducibility. Sources: [Pimentel et al. MSR 2019](https://2019.msrconf.org/details/msr-2019-papers/36/A-Large-scale-Study-about-Quality-and-Reproducibility-of-Jupyter-Notebooks), [AlphaEvolve results notebooks](https://github.com/google-deepmind/alphaevolve_results).
- **Prior art / who has it:** Jupyter and the notebook/workflow ecosystems (serious substitutes), and AlphaEvolve's iterative proposal plus automated evaluation. Lesson: a readable notebook doesn't guarantee execution-order, dependency or data reproducibility. Capturing RAM alone keeps undocumented state and ties the experiment to an obsolete engine.
- **Verdict:** KEPT (#3). It is the best expansion into reproducible AI research and starts with bounded computational experiments, not clinical claims or GPU state. Adoption and long-term compatibility, more than heap serialization, are the hard parts. A proprietary snapshot encoding would undermine adoption.
- **Kill test / first proof:** Proof: a TS agent optimizes a scheduling heuristic against a pinned simulator. It pauses before evaluation, the owner is destroyed, it resumes on another host and reproduces the deterministic metrics, then forks with a different proposal/model on a sealed workload. Baselines: rerun-from-source and a notebook-style manifest. No claim of reproducible floating-point/GPU execution. Kill: capsule recipients can't reproduce and fork results more reliably or with less reconstruction effort than recipients of pinned source, data and an event log, once engine/dependency packaging is counted.

#### 4. Failure Seed Bank: preserve mistakes until better models can repair them
- **What:** freeze minimized agent failures as executable challenges and revive them whenever a model, tool or policy changes. 100× version: a distributed memory of operational mistakes, where new capabilities qualify against old failures and fresh adversarial variants before they get authority.
- **How it would work:** a TS reducer minimizes inputs while keeping an independent invariant violation. Capsules sit in R2 with indexes in a DO, and a failure restores directly at the problematic decision instead of replaying the full prefix. Each upgrade is both a possible repairer and a stronger challenger. Confidential capsules stay tenant-scoped, and shareable abstractions are derived only where rights permit.
- **Evidence / numbers:** first proof (E): **12** synthetic failures (stale reads, duplicate tool replies, timeout uncertainty, bad transformations). Scores (E): stronger 5, solo 4, signal 4, proof 4 = **17/20**. Sources: [FoundationDB paper (2021)](https://www.foundationdb.org/files/fdb-paper.pdf), [Lennon & Jones, microbial dormancy (2011)](https://www.nature.com/articles/nrmicro2504), [MAP-Elites (2015)](https://arxiv.org/abs/1504.04909).
- **Prior art / who has it:** FoundationDB's deterministic fault simulation, regression test suites, MAP-Elites (keeping diverse solutions), and seed banks (as design inspiration only). Lesson: production states are expensive to reconstruct, and optimizing an aggregate score loses rare behaviors.
- **Verdict:** RUNNER-UP (#4), folded into the ONE bet as Oracle Foundry's accumulation loop rather than a separate first product. A public frozen collection eventually becomes training material. The valuable dataset needs real users and data rights, and synthetic examples alone have little moat.
- **Kill test / first proof:** Proof: run two model or harness versions against all cases at equal budgets, score fresh held-out variants separately, and record irreducible failures instead of dropping them. Kill: improvement on the stored bank fails to predict improvement on untouched variants or newly collected failures. It then remains a regression archive, and the transferable-reliability claim dies.

#### 5. Internet Wind Tunnel: a branchable test world for agents
- **What:** a small executable web where agents meet realistic APIs, changing state, conflicting actors and failures, with checkable outcomes. 100× version: a federated, permissioned digital twin of consequential parts of the internet for rehearsing plans and testing robustness before deployment.
- **How it would work:** linked TS services with DO-backed authoritative state. Outcomes depend on explicit application state and independently maintained invariants, not on an LLM scoring a world it generated. The agent's capsule, plus separately versioned service states, event queue and pending-call ledger, form a whole-world checkpoint that can be forked with different event orderings. Fidelity comes from calibration against opt-in real API traces.
- **Evidence / numbers:** scores (E): stronger 5, solo 3, signal 4, proof 3 = **15/20**. Sources: [WebArena (2023-07-25)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2307.13854), [TheAgentCompany (2024-12-18)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.14161), [FoundationDB (2021)](https://www.foundationdb.org/files/fdb-paper.pdf).
- **Prior art / who has it:** WebArena, TheAgentCompany and FoundationDB's simulated faults are strong prior art. Benchmark teams and well-funded simulation vendors compete. Lesson: static tasks become familiar, environments drift, and cloning a website doesn't clone user incentives or external services.
- **Verdict:** RUNNER-UP (#5). Ambitious and relevant, but validating the world dominates building the UI, and whole-world consistency makes two weeks tight. Start with one ecosystem (subscription commerce). A generic internet simulator is rejected. Kept as a calibrated long-term expansion.
- **Kill test / first proof:** Proof: catalog, account and checkout services with a light browser UI. Includes out-of-stock races, expiring authorization and duplicate replies, one whole-world checkpoint forked with two event orderings, and reconciliation against a small reference trace set, kept private/local. Kill: an agent's success ranking in the twin reverses on the small real/reference workflow because essential behavior is missing. Don't scale before predictive validity is repaired.

#### 6. Cybersyn for one company: a calibrated organizational wind tunnel
- **What:** a live, uncertainty-aware model of one operational loop that rehearses interventions before recommending them. 100× version: a digital twin of a whole company (departments, queues, budgets, delegated agents) with explicit observation gaps and accountable human owners.
- **How it would work:** a TS discrete-event model fitted on a consented queue history (or a labelled synthetic fixture), with separate fitting and withheld periods. One checkpoint forks into staffing, triage and escalation policies with exposed uncertainty. Local agents handle routine exceptions and unresolved ones escalate. A DO holds live observations, capsules preserve controller decisions and R2 stores the fixture.
- **Evidence / numbers:** scores (E): stronger 4, solo 3, signal 5, proof 3 = **15/20**. Without a data partner, two weeks proves mechanics only (E). Sources: [Ashby, Introduction to Cybernetics (1956)](https://ashby.info/Ashby-Introduction-to-Cybernetics.pdf), [Beer, Fanfare for Effective Freedom (1973-02-14)](https://kybernetik.ch/dwn/Fanfare_for_Freedom.pdf), [NASA digital twin paper (2012-04-16)](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/citations/20120008178).
- **Prior art / who has it:** Ashby's requisite variety, Beer's Cybersyn (recursive autonomy, timed escalation; abandoned after the 1973 coup, which is not evidence it was technically falsified), NASA digital twins. Lesson: keep local autonomy and escalate exceptions instead of feeding a central brain. Agents don't remove hidden incentives, causal ambiguity or resistance to being modeled.
- **Verdict:** RUNNER-UP (#6). Worth pursuing with a data partner. Synthetic company theater is weak evidence, and a convincing narrative of a company isn't a calibrated twin. Start with one support/escalation queue, not "replace management". The asset is calibration data and integration relationships. Kept as a calibrated long-term expansion.
- **Kill test / first proof:** Proof: compare predictions with an ordinary queueing baseline. On real data, test no-intervention forecast accuracy first, since causal intervention claims need later prospective evidence. Kill: on withheld periods, the twin can't beat a simple queueing/seasonal baseline on the decision-relevant metric. Stop adding departments.

#### 7. Agent Residency: portable identity, obligations and accountable authority
- **What:** give a long-lived agent a portable record of its owner, execution lineage, obligations and revocable grants, while keeping effect authority outside its copied memory. 100× version: a residence system for agents moving among hosts and organizations that answers "who authorized this action and who owns the unresolved obligation?"
- **How it would work:** typed commitment transitions (`offered → accepted → working → delivered → verified`) with named principals, deadline and artifact hashes, instead of treating agent prose as binding ("I promise", "I request" and "I authorize" are different acts). A DO issues short-lived broker authority bound to job, branch, action and owner epoch, and the capsule holds only logical references. A new model inherits the task record but not the former model's trust.
- **Evidence / numbers:** scores (E): stronger 4, solo 2, signal 4, proof 5 = **15/20**. Sources: [Smith, Contract Net Protocol (Dec 1980)](https://www.reidgsmith.com/The_Contract_Net_Protocol_Dec-1980.pdf), [W3C VC Data Model 2.0 (2025)](https://www.w3.org/TR/2025/REC-vc-data-model-2.0-20250515/).
- **Prior art / who has it:** Contract Net (task announcements, bids, awards), W3C credentials (issuer/holder/verifier), managed-agent platforms (Claude Managed Agents already bundles much of the residence experience), IAM and workflow engines. Earlier multi-agent protocols didn't provide real-world counterparties, trust or legal enforceability.
- **Verdict:** NARROWED (#7). A useful protocol and public failure test, but cloud/IAM bundling makes the standalone company weak. Offer an adapter/reference protocol, with the opening in cross-provider, customer-controlled evidence and grants. Keep the authority protocol only where another proposal needs it.
- **Kill test / first proof:** Proof: move Node→browser→Node at a pending tool call, copy the old capsule, revoke the previous epoch and show its write rejected. Restore after a crash between dispatch and ack against a toy idempotent service, and compare with a plain durable-workflow version (identity correctness doesn't need a heap image). Kill: an ordinary workflow-plus-IAM baseline supports the same handoff, revocation and obligation tracking with less integration work and no loss of portable evidence.

#### 8. Speculation Budgeter: spend cheap labour where it buys checked progress
- **What:** split a fixed budget among candidate generation, execution, checking and new-test discovery, suspending branches whose next step isn't worth its cost. 100× version: an operating layer for vast populations of speculative workers, optimizing accepted progress under compute, oracle and human-attention limits.
- **How it would work:** a TS scheduler compares the single best model, fixed fan-out and adaptive allocation under the same total budget. It freezes unpromising capsule branches in R2, resumes a few, and reserves budget for fresh checks, with heavy optimization in a host tool. Priority scores are heuristics, not valid bounds. An LLM's confidence isn't a branch-and-bound bound. If one strong attempt dominates, fan-out should shrink.
- **Evidence / numbers:** first proof (E): **20** bounded optimization tasks with independently checked objectives. Scores (E): stronger 4, solo 2, signal 4, proof 4 = **14/20**. Sources: [Land & Doig (Jul 1960)](https://jmvidal.cse.sc.edu/library/land60a.pdf), [MAP-Elites (2015)](https://arxiv.org/abs/1504.04909), [Jevons (1865)](https://www.econlib.org/library/YPDBooks/Jevons/jvnCQ.html?chapter_num=9).
- **Prior art / who has it:** Land–Doig branch-and-bound, MAP-Elites, Jevons, and cloud schedulers (crowded implementation space). Lesson: branching only helps if selection signals separate useful paths and overhead is controlled. Correlated branches can waste all the abundance.
- **Verdict:** NARROWED (#8). Evaluate it as an optimization inside the winning product, not a generic agent cloud. The economics are sound, but the space is crowded and the capsules' extra value is uncertain. A raw spot-compute broker or snapshot wrapper has little solo advantage. The possible asset is scheduling policies learned from authorized workloads.
- **Kill test / first proof:** Proof: report outcome quality, accepted outcomes per unit cost and scheduler/checkpoint overhead, without counting sleeping branch records as resident computers. Kill: across the preregistered task set, adaptive scheduling loses to the single-best-model baseline once all execution, checking and persistence costs are included. Don't rescue it by publishing only the tasks where fan-out wins.

#### 9. Reality Slot: simulation-first experiments with a scarce physical oracle
- **What:** agents rehearse experiments in simulation, then spend a limited real-apparatus slot to tell competing hypotheses apart. 100× version: a network of reproducible, agent-accessible physical experiments with portable procedures, measured outcomes and explicit calibration histories.
- **How it would work:** TS/Wasm simulates candidate models, and the agent chooses the measurement with the most expected discriminatory value (a hypothesis, not a proven active-learning result). It parks while a human collects the observation and resumes from the returned artifact. The capsule stores orchestration, model/input versions and observations. Apparatus state and camera-clock calibration stay external, since a sleeping capsule can't pause the apparatus.
- **Evidence / numbers:** scores (E): stronger 5, solo 2, signal 5, proof 2 = **14/20**. Setup and calibration make the schedule uncertain (E). Sources: [NASA digital twin (2012)](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/citations/20120008178), [Gemini Robotics (2025-03-12)](https://deepmind.google/blog/gemini-robotics-brings-ai-into-the-physical-world/).
- **Prior art / who has it:** NASA twin (simulation joined to vehicle observations) and Gemini Robotics (physical actions as model output). Lesson: the sim-to-real gap from unknown parameters and unmodeled effects. Physical setup, calibration and turnaround, not reasoning, limit automation.
- **Verdict:** RUNNER-UP (#9), deferred as a later adjacency. Keep the physical-oracle direction, but defer a marketplace until one instrument has repeat demand. The scarce asset is apparatus access plus calibration/outcome data. Robotics fleets and pharma labs are unrealistic first markets for a solo builder.
- **Kill test / first proof:** Proof: a camera-observed pendulum with an existing phone/laptop, manual release and pinned geometry. Damping models are simulated, the agent picks between two proposed measurements, and predictions are tested on a separate observed run. No actuator or hardware purchase. Kill: the selected experiment gives no more discrimination on held-out physical observations than a fixed, simple measurement schedule after calibration/setup costs.

#### Rejected in this report
- **Persistence itself / "an OS for agents" / "a place for your agent to live"**: model vendors and clouds can bundle runtimes, memories, approvals and execution histories (Claude Managed Agents already does), so durability alone isn't a moat.
- **"A cheap durable home for every agent"**: already exposed to platform bundling, with no lasting differentiation without customer-owned authority, data or workflow.
- **"A universal oracle powered by a better judge model"**: fails independence. The next model can recreate the judge and may share its mistakes.
- **"A full company/internet twin generated from documents"**: impressive animation isn't calibrated causal prediction. Scope down to observable state transitions.
- **"Millions of agents for almost nothing"**: ignores inference, host tools, checking, persistence and correlated search. Measure accepted outcomes under a fixed budget.
- **"Every output carries a proof"**: valid only for a formalized claim under explicit assumptions. A signed journal or a passed test suite isn't a universal correctness proof.
- **"The heap is the whole experiment"**: false when tools, datasets, service state, credentials or apparatus live outside it. Export the dependency and authority contract too.
- **A generic "AI evaluator marketplace"**: no plausible solo advantage, unlike reference-backed scope plus counterexamples.
- **A generic branching chat UI**: easy to copy, and platform vendors can add it.
- **A generic internet simulator**: would compete with benchmark teams and well-funded simulation vendors.
- **A new identity provider, legal-personhood scheme or "agent bank"**: a solo builder should ship an adapter/reference protocol instead.
- **A raw spot-compute broker or snapshot wrapper**: little solo advantage over cloud schedulers.
