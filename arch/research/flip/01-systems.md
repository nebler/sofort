# Systems whose economics might flip

Research date: **2026-10-28**. Lens A: labour-intensive systems ideas; Lens B: isolation and execution at agent scale. Ten candidates, with explicit rejection decisions. Written against `BRIEF.md`; GrowOS itself is the supplied baseline, not a discovery of this report.

**I — Recommendation:** test an **agent-grown device-driver environment**: retain an existing Linux driver and grow the small target-OS adapter it needs, with independent behavioural tests. This has unusually direct historical evidence of labour and scarce expertise blocking adoption. Add proof obligations to small state machines after the behavioural loop works. A generic new OS, sandbox cloud, or optimisation agent does not survive this prior-art search.

## Evidence and interpretation

- **F** = sourced fact, including a paper's or vendor's reported result; it does not mean independently reproduced.
- **M** = measured in this research. **There are no M results:** this was desk research; no experiments, hardware access, Docker, VM changes, accounts, or deployments.
- **I** = inference, proposed architecture, judgment, or conditional prediction.
- **E** = estimate or proposed numerical success threshold. All customer counts, prices, effort savings, and pilot budgets below are hypotheses, not market research or quotations.

**I — Important correction to the premise:** code generation becoming cheaper does not automatically make checking cheap. Driver models, concurrency specifications, compiler semantics, and deployment integration can dominate. A compiler accepting a patch is a weak oracle. Tests are strong evidence on their tested domain, not proofs. A proof establishes a stated property under a model; it does not establish that the model describes Linux, the device, or the customer's requirement.

**F — The flip has concrete but uneven evidence.** AutoVerus reports proofs for over 90% of 150 specified Rust tasks, many in under 30 seconds or three model calls; these are predominantly small benchmark problems, not an OS verification result. [Yang et al., OOPSLA, October 2025](https://jaylorch.us.s3.amazonaws.com/publications/autoverus.pdf). KVerus subsequently reports upstream-accepted proofs for 23 Asterinas memory-management functions, while its repository-level benchmark success is 51%, illustrating both progress and remaining work. [Liu et al., v2, 2 August 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.03822v2).

**I — Evaluation rule:** rank opportunities by independent oracle quality, repeated expert labour removed, ability to demonstrate improvement in two weeks, and distance from existing solutions. Treat better code models as making the *generator* easier to copy: the lasting asset must be accepted specifications, independent tests, deployment integration, or a corpus of difficult failures.

## 1. Grow the driver environment, keeping the mature driver

**Lens A; strongest provisional bet.**

**1 — Idea and origin. F:** run an existing device driver outside its original kernel by implementing the kernel services it expects. Goel and Duchamp demonstrated Linux-driver emulation in Mach in January 1996. [Original paper, USENIX 1996](https://www.usenix.org/legacy/publications/library/proceedings/sd96/full_papers/goel.txt). Genode's DDE-Linux is a concrete descendant: the driver remains Linux code, while a tailored environment supplies allocation, interrupts, scheduling, and other dependencies. [Genode, historical driver-porting guide, undated and explicitly marked partially outdated](https://genode.org/documentation/developer-resources/porting_device_drivers).

**2 — Why niche. F:** Genode's 2021 retrospective explicitly identifies expensive individual ports, risky updates, and the rare intersection of Linux and Genode expertise; its i.MX8 graphics port took two months. It already introduced reusable code and tooling to generate dummy functions. This is unusually strong evidence of the exact labour bottleneck in the brief. [Genode 21.08 release notes, August 2021](https://genode.org/documentation/release-notes/21.08).

**3 — What flipped, and 6 — current competitors. F:** AUTODRIVER already implements a generation/validation loop for kernel-driver co-evolution: 235 validated corpus cases; 56.4% compilation success on 55 evaluated cases. Its boot checks are narrower than functional driver correctness. [Kharlamova et al., 24 November 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.18924). Genode is actively maintaining the revived approach: release 26.08 updates ARM driver environments to Linux 6.18.19. [Genode, August 2026](https://genode.org/documentation/release-notes/26.08). **I:** the open wedge is automated *cross-OS adapter semantics and maintenance*, beyond stub generation or same-OS compilation repair. It is unproven that agents beat Genode's existing methodology.

**F/I — Stronger alternative to beat:** LeVasseur and colleagues proposed retaining the driver's entire original OS in an isolated VM, avoiding reimplementation of its internal semantics. [OSDI, December 2004](https://www.usenix.org/events/osdi04/tech/full_papers/levasseur/levasseur.pdf). **I:** include this driver-VM approach as a baseline: if the customer can afford it, an adapter must win on measured footprint, latency, or assurance. Porting fewer lines is not automatically better than retaining the working environment.

**4 — Oracle. I: strong for a pinned virtual device and specified operations; weak for arbitrary hardware.** Use the unmodified driver on Linux as a reference, a separately maintained QEMU device model, and identical external I/O workloads against the target. Compare packets/blocks/serial data, error outcomes, reset and cancellation behaviour. Compare legal state transitions rather than demanding identical internal register traces. Instrument the adapter's allocation, lock, timer, and interrupt contracts. A boot banner is insufficient. A device model inferred from the same driver is not an independent oracle.

**5 — 100× model test. I:** more valuable: more devices and kernel versions become economical, while hardware behaviour remains a reason to validate. The implementation agent itself loses defensibility.

**7 — Value. I/E:** alternative-OS vendors, embedded platform teams, and appliance builders buy a supported driver port and future update assurance. A plausible wedge is 20 platform teams spending €25k–€100k/year, or €0.5m–€2m/year; that is a small specialist business until reusable adapter coverage compounds. The evidence supports avoided expert projects, not a giant TAM.

**8 — Two-week proof. I:** days 1–3 pin one small QEMU-backed Linux driver with a tractable dependency closure; a concrete starting candidate is the PL031 RTC driver, with read/set time, alarm, cancellation, and interrupt scenarios. Freeze the driver, clock model, and Linux version; abandon this selection if its dependency closure exceeds the pilot. Days 4–8 grow only missing environment functions in a small userspace target harness; fail unsupported calls rather than return fake success. Days 9–11 exercise interrupts, allocation failure, invalid requests, and reset against the reference. Days 12–14 upgrade to a nonidentical source revision or related device variant without teaching the agent its held-out tests. Rust can own the harness and adapter state; C FFI retains the original driver. This proves environment growth, not a complete GrowOS hardware integration. A Linux/QEMU workstation is a future experiment prerequisite; no special VM is assumed available here.

**9 — Demo, tweet, fatal reply, kill test. I:** show a frozen source-driver hash, the missing-service failures, accepted adapter patches, and a held-out reset/error workload. Tweet: “The Linux driver stayed unchanged. An agent grew its new OS environment, then survived the tests it never saw.” Fatal expert reply: “Genode already generates these stubs; your successful no-ops are hiding the semantics.” **E:** kill if more than two engineer-days go into device-specific hand fixes after the harness exists, if any held-out failure is concealed by a stub, or if existing DDE reuse solves the same port more cheaply. Success requires a second held-out variant, not a polished one-driver demo.

## 2. Proof-maintained compatibility handlers and systems components

**Lens A plus B; most natural assurance extension of GrowOS.**

**1 — Idea and origin. F:** prove implementation refinement of an explicit systems specification, then maintain the proofs as code changes. Klein and colleagues demonstrated seL4's machine-checked kernel correctness in 2009. [Original SOSP paper, October 2009](https://sel4.systems/Research/pdfs/sel4-formal-verification-os-kernel.pdf). Hyperkernel instead designed a finite, verification-friendly kernel interface, separated address spaces, and checked LLVM-level handlers using SMT. It verified 50 handlers; the paper reports about 15 minutes on eight cores and explicitly excludes multiprocessor verification and parts of initialization/glue. [Nelson et al., SOSP, 28 October 2017, §§1–2](https://unsat.cs.washington.edu/papers/nelson-hyperkernel.pdf).

**2 — Why niche. F:** seL4 proof-engineering analysis reports roughly 20 person-years and over 200,000 Isabelle lines for the initial development. [Klein et al., 2017, proof-estimation study](https://www.trustworthy.systems/publications/full_text/Klein_AKMMO_17.pdf). Effort totals depend on what is included: another team account separates 12 person-years for the functional proof from 12–13 for tools/libraries/frameworks. Do not treat the brief's approximate total as a precise universal number. [Klein, *Proof Engineering Considered Essential*, April 2014](https://trustworthy.systems/publications/nicta_full_text/7923.pdf). IronFleet likewise required about 3.7 person-years to develop its methodology and two verified distributed systems. [Hawblitzel et al., SOSP 2015, §7](https://pdos.csail.mit.edu/6.5840/papers/ironfleet.pdf).

**3 — Flip and 6 — prior art. F:** VeriStruct reports verification of 128/129 functions across eleven Rust data-structure modules, with ten modules succeeding completely. [Sun et al., revised 3 March 2026, TACAS 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.25015v4). AutoVerus and KVerus above already cover proof-generation and maintenance loops. Kani's 2026 paper reports over 16,000 harnesses checked per code change in the Rust-standard-library campaign; it supports bit-precise checking and contract-based reasoning. [Delmas et al., ASE 2026](https://www.amazon.science/publications/kani-a-model-checker-for-rust). **I:** “LLM writes Verus proofs” is occupied. A narrower deliverable is compatibility components whose behavioural regression evidence and immutable contracts survive each repair.

**4 — Oracle. I: strong relative to a frozen property; weaker relative to full Linux behaviour.** Freeze a small specification separately from the agent's writable implementation/proof files. Differential Linux tests establish correspondence on samples; Verus proves the stated transition and invariants; Kani checks a deliberately finite harness with successful unwinding checks or explicit bounds. Mutation tests detect vacuous contracts. Disallow added assumptions, admitted lemmas, weakened preconditions, and changes to trusted stubs without explicit review. Record compiler, verifier, solver, contracts, and FFI assumptions. Rust plus SMT verification alone is not end-to-end verified machine code.

**5 — 100× model. I:** more valuable as generated privileged code increases, but generic proof-writing services become commodities. Specification selection and maintenance evidence remain valuable.

**7 — Value. I/E:** VMM, storage, embedded, and OS teams pay for fewer high-consequence regressions in resource accounting, queue logic, buffer boundaries, or parsers. Ten customers at €50k–€150k/year implies €0.5m–€1.5m/year initially. Certification artifacts could raise value, but this prototype is not a certification claim.

**8 — Two-week proof. I:** choose three deterministic GrowOS leaf components: descriptor-table transitions, range/permission checks, and bounded message or ABI decoding. Extract each into pure Rust; keep the current runtime wrapper. Spend days 1–4 freezing contracts and Linux comparison cases, days 5–9 generating implementation/proof repairs, days 10–14 applying API changes and deliberately injected bugs. Include sequences, negative inputs, and state preservation on error. Exclude scheduling, real DMA, and a whole memory subsystem. **E:** require three maintained components, at least ten meaningful mutants rejected, no oracle edits, and a recorded reduction in human repair time against ordinary tests plus manual review.

**9 — Demo/tweet/kill. I:** demonstrate a Linux regression case that ordinary examples miss, followed by a verifier counterexample and repaired handler. Tweet: “Grow a syscall handler, then grow the proof that its resource invariants survive every call in the stated model.” Fatal reply: “Your agent proved a spec it invented, with a trusted wrapper doing the hard part.” **E:** kill if independent contract review costs more than the saved implementation work, if the proof omits the changed state, or if a meaningful seeded violation passes. This is an evidence experiment, not proof that the entire kernel is correct.

## 3. ISA ports and instruction selectors grown against executable semantics

**Lens A; best oracle structure after finite compatibility components.**

**1 — Idea and origin. F:** generate or verify compiler backends against formal machine semantics instead of separately hand-writing and trusting every target rule. CompCert's verified compilation work dates to the 2000s; CakeML's 2016 backend uses twelve intermediate languages and supports multiple ISAs. [Leroy, CACM, July 2009](https://www.cs.utexas.edu/~bornholt/courses/cs345h-22au/papers/compcert.pdf); [Tan et al., ICFP 2016](https://cakeml.org/icfp16.pdf). **I:** extend that idea with an untrusted agent proposing target rules and a trusted checker accepting each supported translation.

**2 — Why niche. F:** ARRIVAL's authors explain why mature industrial compilers cannot simply be rewritten to fit a clean-slate verified compiler: their size, optimisations, and existing engineering investment matter. [McLoughlin et al., OOPSLA, October 2025, §1](https://cfallin.org/pubs/oopsla2025_arrival.pdf). CompCert's own manual distinguishes its verified core from some unverified source transformations. [CompCert 3.18 manual, 30 August 2026, §3.2.9](https://compcert.org/man/manual003.html). **I:** porting labour includes ABI, relocation, exception, atomic, and memory-model details; instruction arithmetic is only one slice.

**3 — Flip and 6 — prior art. F:** Sail's RISC-V model is adopted by RISC-V International and supplies executable semantics and proof-tool outputs. [Official model repository, inspected 28 October 2026](https://github.com/riscv/sail-riscv). ARRIVAL verifies nearly all AArch64 selectors reachable from Wasm core and requires 2.6× fewer handwritten specifications than prior work. [OOPSLA 2025 paper](https://cfallin.org/pubs/oopsla2025_arrival.pdf). ARM-TV reports 46 previously unknown LLVM AArch64 backend bugs. [Berger et al., October 2025](https://web.tecnico.ulisboa.pt/nuno.lopes/pubs.php?id=armtv-oopsla25). VeriISLE already incorporates these ideas into Cranelift's repository. [Project README, inspected 28 October 2026](https://github.com/bytecodealliance/wasmtime/blob/main/cranelift/isle/veri/README.md). **I:** the candidate is a tool for a neglected target/extension's missing rules and port regressions, not another general AArch64 verifier.

**4 — Oracle. I: strong for bounded sequential instruction semantics; incomplete for an OS port.** Independently supplied Sail/ASL semantics plus IR semantics can check register/flag/memory effects of a selector. Spike/QEMU provide additional differential execution for RISC-V. Cross-ISA kernels require additional calling-convention, privilege, page-table, trap, atomic, and platform checks. Two emulators agreeing is not proof of hardware correctness. Keep unspecified behaviours explicit rather than treating one emulator's choice as mandatory.

**5 — 100× model. I:** more valuable as custom ISA extensions proliferate; the model supplies cheap target support, while target semantics and acceptance evidence remain necessary. A “port anything” consultancy without tooling loses value.

**7 — Value. I/E:** RISC-V IP vendors, custom accelerator teams, and language-runtime maintainers pay for a supportable backend extension with conformance artifacts. Five to twenty engagements at €25k–€100k implies €0.125m–€2m of project revenue, not established annual demand. Small customer count and upstream review are material constraints.

**8 — Two-week proof. I:** start with an existing tiny Rust IR and interpreter; ask an agent to add twenty missing RV64 integer/bit-manipulation lowerings. Check their semantics against a pinned Sail model, then run emitted ELF tests in Spike or QEMU. Hold out signedness, zero-extension, and boundary-shift cases. On days 10–14 change one semantics requirement or target extension and regenerate affected rules. Do not attempt a full LLVM backend. For GrowOS later, make a separate port acceptance suite for trap save/restore, page tables, atomics, and ABI layouts before asking an agent to port the kernel.

**9 — Demo/tweet/kill. I:** a newly enabled extension runs a test binary, while a deliberately bad lowering produces a minimized input and register-state disagreement. Tweet: “One ISA specification, twenty agent-written backend rules, independent counterexamples for the bad ones.” Fatal reply: “Existing TableGen/ISLE generators already do this; your rule subset is the easy part.” **E:** kill if ten held-out rules need over two engineer-days of manual specification glue, if correctness checking dominates the whole two weeks, or if ordinary intrinsics solve the prospective customer's problem. Count supported rules and excluded semantics explicitly.

## 4. Certified binary hot-region translation with a conservative fallback

**Lens A; technically attractive, commercially narrow.**

**1 — Idea and origin. F:** dynamically translate or optimise binaries while preserving their visible execution. Dynamo optimised native PA-RISC streams in place, rather than translating between different ISAs. [Bala, Duesterwald and Banerjia, PLDI, June 2000](https://www.cse.iitm.ac.in/~krishna/courses/2022/odd-cs6013/BalaDusterwaldBanerjiaPLDI2000.pdf). Transmeta's Code Morphing translated x86 execution onto its VLIW implementation. [Klaiber, Transmeta technical white paper, 19 January 2000](https://www.cs.cmu.edu/afs/cs/academic/class/15745-s09/www/papers/paper_aklaiber_19jan00.pdf). **I:** revive expensive per-binary translation work with generated, checked hot regions and fallback on unsupported cases.

**2 — Why niche. F:** Dynamo explicitly had to recover its own runtime overhead. Binary transformation research documented the robustness-versus-overhead tradeoff in 1995. [Graham, Lucco and Wahbe, *Adaptable Binary Programs*, USENIX 1995](https://www.usenix.org/conference/usenix-1995-technical-conference/adaptable-binary-programs). **I:** this idea did not die: Rosetta, QEMU, and DBT tooling demonstrate lasting value. The remaining graveyard component is comprehensive, cheap retargeting and verification, not binary translation itself.

**3 — Flip and 6 — competitors. F:** Guaranteed Guess already generates x86-to-ARM/RISC-V assembly with LLMs and tests: reported correctness is about 99% on HumanEval but only 49% on BringUpBench; it reports 1.73× runtime performance relative to Rosetta 2 on its comparison workloads. These are test results, not universal translation guarantees. [Heakl et al., 17 June 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.14606). Box64 0.3.8 adds persistent translated-code caching and ongoing dynarec/wrapper fixes. [Project release post, 13 October 2025](https://box86.org/2025/10/new-box64-v0-3-8-released/). DynamoRIO already supplies broad runtime code manipulation on x86/ARM families. [Maintainer documentation, inspected 28 October 2026](https://dynamorio.org/).

**4 — Oracle. I: strong for a restricted pure region; weak for arbitrary whole binaries.** The source machine execution is a reference. For straight-line integer regions, compare complete architectural state and memory effects using symbolic equivalence plus randomized execution. Do not use LLVM Alive2 alone to claim equivalence of arbitrary x86 and ARM binaries. For signals, syscalls, self-modifying code, floating point, and concurrency, keep an established translator; reject replacement unless entry/exit and exception boundaries are checked. A fallback cannot undo side effects from an already incorrect hot region.

**5 — 100× model. I:** more possible ports, but models cannot remove ISA memory-model differences. A generic translator is easier to reproduce; a validated archive of opaque industrial binaries may retain value.

**7 — Value. I/E:** owners of expensive source-unavailable software moving to ARM or RISC-V; sell an accepted translation and support contract. Three to ten projects at €50k–€200k gives €0.15m–€2m project revenue. Licensing, source recompilation, and existing emulation can erase the opportunity.

**8 — Two-week proof. I:** translate ten pure x86 integer leaf routines to AArch64 with a Rust extraction/test harness. Freeze the accepted instruction subset and integer widths. Validate branchless regions symbolically and all functions differentially; leave any excluded routine on the existing interpreter. Compare against source recompilation when source is available and a mature DBT when it is not. Measure end-to-end time including dispatch, not only the accepted region's execution.

**9 — Demo/tweet/kill. I:** show a faster translated checksum plus a flag-handling counterexample rejected before installation. Tweet: “The agent guesses the assembly. Independent machine-state checks decide which translations can ship.” Fatal reply: “You benchmarked compiler-friendly toy functions and omitted signals, aliasing, and x86 memory ordering.” **E:** kill if no material customer workload remains after exclusions, if a baseline compiler matches performance, or if accepted regions improve total runtime by less than 10% at an amortizable translation cost.

## 5. Superoptimisation and synthesised compiler rewrites

**Lens A; real economic flip, weak new-business wedge.**

**1 — Idea and origin. F:** search for the smallest equivalent instruction sequence. Massalin's 1987 superoptimizer used exhaustive enumeration, fast execution tests, and pruning. The original paper explicitly notes exponential search growth and severe pointer-model difficulty. [Massalin, ASPLOS, October 1987](https://www.brinckerhoff.org/clements/2214-csc530/Files/massalin-1987.pdf).

**2 — Why niche. F:** Souper later used synthesis over LLVM-like IR and identified optimisations subsequently implemented in LLVM and MSVC; as an automated pass it produced a Clang binary 4.4% smaller in the reported experiment. [Sasnauskas et al., 2017/2018](https://research.google/pubs/souper-a-synthesizing-superoptimizer/). **I:** search and integration costs restricted how much could be pursued; hardware-dependent performance and semantic edge cases persist even when candidate generation is cheap. This was already an active field, not a fully abandoned idea.

**3 — Flip and 6 — prior art. F:** Iago is explicitly an LLM replacement for Souper's enumerative synthesis. On its SPEC-derived sample it found 7.4% fewer optimisations, but 35.7% of its found optimisations were not found by Souper. [Mukherjee, LLVM Developers' Meeting, October 2025](https://www.llvm.org/devmtg/2025-10/). AlphaEvolve already combines model proposals with evaluators; Google reports a 23% improvement to a Gemini-training kernel and a 1% reduction in total training time. [Google Cloud, 2025 announcement](https://cloud.google.com/blog/products/ai-machine-learning/alphaevolve-on-google-cloud/). **I:** this directly occupies the generic proposal/test/keep loop.

**4 — Oracle. F/I: strong within supported loop-free IR; weaker for general programs.** Alive2 validates LLVM refinement, but its loop checking is bounded and its tooling warns against unsupported interprocedural transformations. [Lopes et al., PLDI, June 2021](https://web.ist.utl.pt/nuno.lopes/pubs.php?id=alive2-pldi21); [Alive2 maintainer README, inspected 28 October 2026](https://github.com/AliveToolkit/alive2/blob/master/README.md?plain=1). **I:** pair correctness checking with actual target benchmarks, covering poison, undefined behaviour, aliasing and floating-point assumptions. Never accept instruction count as the sole performance oracle.

**5 — 100× model. I:** more valuable at fleet scale, but much less defensible as a standalone agent. The winner probably already owns the compiler, workload, or deployment fleet.

**7 — Value. I/E:** chip/toolchain vendors and compute-heavy service owners pay for validated gains. A customer spending €10m/year on the relevant compute saves €100k/year from a real 1% improvement; do not price against its entire cloud bill when the optimised path covers only a fraction.

**8 — Two-week proof. I:** an LLVM IR extraction queue, agent proposals, Alive2 acceptance, and hardware benchmarking for twenty real hot leaf functions. Use existing LLVM/Souper/Iago-style baselines and held-out inputs. **E:** require at least three useful accepted rewrites and positive one-year savings after search and integration costs.

**9 — Demo/tweet/kill. I:** show an accepted rewrite, its proof/check log, and a noisy benchmark confidence interval. Tweet: “A 1987 search problem, a 2026 proposal engine, and a checker that can say no.” Fatal reply: “Iago and AlphaEvolve already do this, and LLVM will absorb your rules.” Kill the generic product now; retain only a customer-specific target with genuinely missing semantics or deployment integration.

## 6. Partial evaluation and workload-specific whole-program specialisation

**Lens A plus B; credible, but use the existing compiler machinery.**

**1 — Idea and origin. F:** specialise a program with respect to its known inputs, leaving a residual program for the unknown inputs. Futamura's 1971 paper proposed obtaining compiled programs and compilers by specialising interpreters. [Original English paper, 1971](https://fi.ftmr.info/PE-Museum/PE-Original-English1971.pdf). Jones, Gomard and Sestoft systematised the techniques in 1993. [Authors' book page, June 1993](https://studwww.itu.dk/people/sestoft/pebook/).

**2 — Why niche. F:** the implementers' Mix retrospective describes the critical role of annotations and binding-time analysis. [Sestoft and Søndergaard, PEPM 2024](https://pure.itu.dk/ws/portalfiles/portal/102597804/mix-history-20231218T0709.pdf). **I:** deciding what is static, preventing code explosion, handling external state, and paying the compilation cost limited general application. PGO, JITs, templates, and database query compilation already captured much of the value; the graveyard is the universal specialiser, not specialisation itself.

**3 — Flip and 6 — prior art. F:** weval already derives whole-function compilation from mostly unmodified interpreters with lightweight annotations. Its PLDI 2025 evaluation reports 2.17× for SpiderMonkey and 1.84× for Lua relative to the evaluated interpreters, not relative to their best JITs. [Fallin and Bernstein, June 2025](https://c1f.net/pubs/pldi2025_weval.pdf). The Bytecode Alliance implementation combines Wizer snapshots and partial evaluation. [weval repository, inspected 28 October 2026](https://github.com/bytecodealliance/weval). **I:** agents can supply integration annotations, extract stable configuration, and repair residual-program mismatches. Agent-generated repeated jobs also offer more reuse. Neither improvement is evidence that every workload benefits.

**4 — Oracle. I: strong differential oracle if external effects are controlled.** Run the original interpreter/program and the residual program against the same inputs, clock, filesystem, network transcript, and configuration. Enforce exact guard conditions around specialised assumptions and a semantically complete fallback. A profile only tells which cases happened; it does not authorize removing the other cases. A whole-program optimisation claim also requires accounting for dynamically loaded modules and runtime dependencies.

**5 — 100× model. I:** more valuable when it helps produce cheap one-off compiled variants. Less valuable if the model simply writes a faster direct implementation. Retain it where interpreter semantics are complex and the canonical interpreter remains the trustworthy source.

**7 — Value. I/E:** organisations repeatedly running policy engines, embedded DSLs, or stable-config transformation jobs buy reduced per-run CPU and startup. A €2m/year eligible execution workload with a measured net 10% saving offers €200k/year of value; much smaller workloads will not pay for a specialist service. Sell into an actual repeated workload before building a universal tool.

**8 — Two-week proof. I:** use an existing Rust bytecode interpreter for a small policy DSL, or integrate weval into an already supported interpreter. Freeze one program/configuration, specialise it, and compare on randomized held-out inputs plus injected configuration changes. Include compilation, snapshot loading, guard checks, and fallback costs. Keep effectful operations in an explicit host interface. **E:** target a net 20% improvement beyond straightforward constant propagation/LTO/PGO on the chosen repeated workload; calculate the run count at which preparation breaks even.

**9 — Demo/tweet/kill. I:** show two configurations, one specialised and one routed through fallback, producing identical decisions. Tweet: “The old interpreter remains the specification; the agent turns stable workload facts into a checked fast path.” Fatal reply: “weval/Truffle/PGO already gets this speedup, and your configuration never stays stable.” Kill if a baseline equals it, stable assumptions cannot be guarded, or the real workload changes before amortisation. A revived technique can be useful infrastructure without being a company.

## 7. Capability microkernels with generated service wiring and compatibility adapters

**Lens A plus B; framework extension, not a replacement desktop OS.**

**1 — Idea and origin. F:** use small protected components with explicit authority and fast communication. Liedtke argued that microkernel inefficiency was not inherent, distinguishing poor implementation from the architecture. [*On Microkernel Construction*, December 1995](https://os.itec.kit.edu/deutsch/65_1029.php). EROS combined capability-based invocation with persistent objects, drawing on KeyKOS; it did not merely implement Unix with smaller modules. [Shapiro, Smith and Farber, SOSP, December 1999](https://www.princeton.edu/~rblee/ELE572Papers/Fall04Readings/Eros.pdf).

**2 — Why niche. F:** Liedtke's 1996 account explicitly discusses early microkernels' disappointing efficiency and flexibility. [*Toward Real Microkernels*, September 1996](https://os.itec.kit.edu/deutsch/65_1010.php). Genode's driver retrospective supplies direct evidence of the compatibility and expertise burden of a component OS (§1). **I:** separate address spaces and capabilities do not make existing libraries, protocols, and drivers fit automatically. Nor does more code generation remove migration and ecosystem costs. Mach, L4, and capability systems should not be lumped into one “failed microkernel” story.

**3 — Flip and 6 — prior art. F:** Microkit 2.1.0 added initial x86-64 support, capDL-based initialization, and multicore support across its platforms in November 2025. [Release notes, 26 November 2025](https://docs.sel4.systems/releases/microkit/2.1.0). LionsOS already provides composable task-specific components on seL4 and explicitly says driver/component verification is incomplete. [Project status, inspected 28 October 2026](https://lionsos.org/). **I:** agents could generate adapter glue and authority configurations for many small appliances. Growing untrusted generated code increases the value of explicit authority boundaries, but no recent sourced number here establishes demand for another microkernel vendor. The evidence of growth is sandbox demand in §8, an adjacent market rather than proof of this one.

**4 — Oracle. I: strong for a fixed authority graph; weak for intended policy.** capDL/system descriptions can expose exactly which capabilities are initially granted; static graph checks can reject unwanted paths. Integration tests can check permitted operations and denial. Proving a service has no initial direct path is not proving confidentiality: other services can delegate authority, proxies can become confused deputies, and shared memory/I/O matter. A Linux compatibility run checks function, not least privilege.

**5 — 100× model. I:** more useful because there are more independently generated components to isolate; the proof of architectural separation matters more than the ease of producing bindings. A generic “agent writes a microkernel” becomes obsolete as a differentiated product.

**7 — Value. I/E:** secure embedded/appliance builders pay for integration and a reviewable authority architecture. Five engagements at €50k–€200k would be €0.25m–€1m of project revenue. Sell a bounded deployment outcome; do not presume cloud providers will abandon Linux.

**8 — Two-week proof. I:** start from an existing Microkit/QEMU example. An agent produces Rust RPC adapters and a configuration for a parser, storage service, and untrusted client. Freeze an independent policy: parser can read input, storage can persist approved output, client cannot access raw backing storage. Exercise failed calls, restart of a service, and malformed messages. Use existing seL4 and configuration tools, not a new kernel. **E:** add two held-out service APIs with less than one engineer-day of hand glue each.

**9 — Demo/tweet/kill. I:** a malicious client fails to obtain storage authority while the useful pipeline works. Tweet: “Let the agent assemble the appliance; keep the authority graph small enough to inspect and check.” Fatal reply: “Microkit, Genode, and existing binding generators already do the hard part.” Kill the standalone product if the work is mainly templating or if most applications require passing through broad Linux authority. Retain as a possible frontend to existing frameworks.

## 8. Dense, forkable, stateful execution: VM fork and library-OS economics

**Lens B; strongest demonstrated demand flip, most occupied product space.**

**1 — Idea and origin. F:** clone initialized execution state cheaply instead of booting and configuring a machine for each task. SnowFlock offered distributed VM fork with lazy state transfer and subsecond cloning. [Lagar-Cavilla et al., EuroSys, April 2009](https://www.scs.stanford.edu/~rumble/papers/LagarCavillaEurosys09.pdf). Separately, Drawbridge placed an OS personality inside an application process and exported a narrow host ABI; its Windows library OS added about 16 MB of working set in the reported application configurations. [Porter et al., ASPLOS, March 2011](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/publication/rethinking-the-library-os-from-the-top-down/). Exokernel's earlier contribution was separating resource protection from application-level management, not just using a smaller guest. [Engler, Kaashoek and O'Toole, SOSP, December 1995](https://pdos.csail.mit.edu/6.828/2008/readings/engler95exokernel.pdf).

**2 — Why niche / premise correction. F:** Drawbridge is now archived as a completed research project. [Microsoft project page, inspected 28 October 2026](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/project/drawbridge/). **I:** neither virtualization nor cheap snapshots died. Snapshot consistency, cloned external connections, image distribution, secrets, memory accounting, and application compatibility make general adoption harder than a fork benchmark suggests. Labour is relevant to library OS personalities, while VM cloning is primarily a demand/integration flip.

**3 — What flipped. F:** DeepSeek's DSec production report describes approximately 160-node units serving 3 million sandboxes/day, over 380,000 concurrent sandboxes, and over 5,000 creations/second. It reports node capacities up to 800 microVMs or 3,200 containers. These are platform figures across heterogeneous backends, not 380,000 concurrent microVMs on 160 nodes. [Huang et al., report, 19 September 2026](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.22978v1). **I:** this is direct evidence for Lens B: idle waiting and bursty creation make per-task memory and prepared-state reuse economically meaningful.

**4 — Oracle. I: strong for functional resume tests, incomplete for isolation.** Run the same command sequence from a cold baseline and from cloned state; compare outputs and persistent effects under a controlled clock/entropy/network setup. Test branch divergence, parent termination, crash/restart, exhausted memory, and dirty-page isolation. Cross-tenant isolation still requires security review and host/runtime assurance. Never claim that an I/O comparison establishes a secure VM boundary.

**5 — 100× model. I:** more valuable if it causes more parallel attempts and generated jobs; fewer attempts per task could reduce demand, so the benefit depends on execution volume and state reuse. Faster models do not eliminate memory or image I/O.

**6 — Already doing it. F:** Firecracker is a mature minimalist VMM; its published 5 MiB-class figure is VMM overhead, not total Linux guest plus workload memory. [AWS launch, 26 November 2018](https://aws.amazon.com/blogs/aws/firecracker-lightweight-virtualization-for-serverless-computing/). E2B's current runtime already restores prebooted snapshots, demand-loads memory, and uses copy-on-write storage; forks use the same path. [E2B runtime, inspected 28 October 2026](https://github.com/e2b-dev/runtime). DSec implements resource reclamation and dense scheduling at production scale. **I:** a generic fast-sandbox API is disqualified; competitors already have the operator experience and implementation.

**F — Additional direct competition:** Microsandbox exposes local microVM fork/snapshot workflows; Modal's September 2026 SDK release supports both VM and gVisor sandbox runtimes; Unikraft markets prepared environments, live forks, and density directly at AI-agent and CI workloads. [Microsandbox repository, inspected 28 October 2026](https://github.com/superradcompany/microsandbox); [Modal release notes, 28 September 2026](https://modal.com/docs/sdk/go/releases); [Unikraft product page, inspected 28 October 2026](https://unikraft.com/). **I:** treat Unikraft's latency/density headlines as vendor claims until compared under matching working sets and concurrency. Even the kernel-trimming/snapshot subset has a direct commercial incumbent.

**7 — Value. I/E:** model labs and CI/evaluation operators buy lower cost per successful rollout at a latency SLO. At 100,000 simultaneous sandboxes, saving 50 MiB of *committed/touched* memory each is about 4.8 TiB. This arithmetic says nothing about hardware bill reduction until placement and billing granularity are included. A platform spending €5m/year on applicable execution would obtain €500k/year from a real 10% cost reduction; it may prefer an internal optimisation.

**8 — Two-week proof. I:** on an ordinary dedicated Linux/KVM test host, compare cold boot, existing Firecracker snapshot restore, and E2B's available runtime path for three realistic prepared images. Add a small Rust workload runner and report usable-command p50/p99, total proportional/touched memory, dirty-page growth, and errors at increasing concurrency. Prototype only one unsolved mechanism, such as workload-aware idle-page reclamation. The required host is a future prerequisite, not permission to use the repository's kvm-dev VM.

**9 — Demo/tweet/kill. I:** fork a warmed build environment into divergent branches and publish cost at equal success rate. Tweet: “VM fork is old. Millions of agent environments make its memory and state semantics matter again.” Fatal reply: “Your cold-start win disappears under real working sets; E2B already does the cloning.” **E:** kill the new product unless it beats the strongest available baseline by at least 25% on cost per accepted task at comparable isolation and latency, or solves an actual unmet compatibility constraint. A smaller kernel is useful only when total working-set and operational measurements bear it out.

## 9. Proof-carrying generated plugins and software isolation in one process

**Lens A plus B; restrict the policy before expanding the language.**

**1 — Idea and origin. F:** software fault isolation rewrites distrusted code to confine writes and control transfers inside a shared address space. [Wahbe, Lucco, Anderson and Graham, SOSP, December 1993](https://www.scs.stanford.edu/nyu/05sp/sched/readings/sfi.pdf). Proof-carrying code instead has the producer attach evidence satisfying the host's safety policy, checked before execution. [Necula, POPL, January 1997](https://homes.cs.washington.edu/~mernst/teaching/6.893/readings/necula-popl97.pdf). Singularity used closed software-isolated processes, typed channels, and ownership separation; it was not arbitrary native code sharing a heap without protection. [Hunt and Larus, Microsoft Research, October 2005](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/wp-content/uploads/2005/10/tr-2005-135.pdf).

**2 — Why niche. F:** Necula's dissertation explicitly identifies establishing policies, generating proofs, and proof size as practical difficulties. [*Compiling with Proofs*, 1998, ch. 8](https://www.cs.cmu.edu/~rwh/students/necula.pdf). Singularity's processes could not dynamically load or generate code. **I:** imposing a closed, safe software ecosystem made compatibility expensive; unconstrained native plugins cannot simply inherit the guarantees of a safe host language. SFI itself was not abandoned and Wasm already captures much of this benefit.

**3 — Flip and 6 — competition. F:** Microsoft's Wassette runs Wasm components as permission-controlled agent tools over MCP, using Wasmtime. [Microsoft, 6 August 2025](https://opensource.microsoft.com/blog/2025/08/06/introducing-wassette-webassembly-based-tools-for-ai-agents/). Akamai now offers a Wasm-based agent sandbox with an additional restricted runtime for generated JavaScript/Python. [Product documentation, inspected 28 October 2026](https://www.akamai.com/products/akamai-agent-sandbox). On 9 April 2026, Wasmtime published twelve advisories, eleven found using LLM tooling; two were Critical. [Maintainer report, 9 April 2026](https://bytecodealliance.org/articles/wasmtime-security-advisories). **I:** scale raises demand for isolation, and stronger models also raise pressure on the trusted runtime. A proof-producing code generator is plausible; “Wasm tools for agents” is already occupied.

**4 — Oracle. I: strong for a restricted host policy; weak for arbitrary intent.** A minimal checker can validate a bounded DSL's memory, capability-use, and resource-consumption properties. Runtime fuel and host-call permissions remain enforcement mechanisms. If proofs are checked only on source, explicitly trust the compiler and linking path; to claim binary PCC, validate a certificate over the actual executable or use a proven compilation chain. Do not equate a Wasm type check, a model's safety explanation, or an artifact signature with proof that a plugin will use permitted data appropriately.

**5 — 100× model. I:** more valuable: inexpensive proof production makes the original producer/consumer asymmetry attractive, and generated plugins multiply. The same stronger model increases pressure on checker implementation and specification completeness.

**7 — Value. I/E:** database/UDF platforms, agent-tool hosts, and low-latency extension systems buy a narrowly guaranteed policy beyond sandbox memory isolation. A 10 ms process boundary used 100m times/day consumes roughly 278 wall-clock-equivalent hours/day; actual CPU and latency savings require measurement. Five early customers paying €20k–€100k/year yields €0.1m–€0.5m/year. Existing Wasm is the price/performance baseline.

**8 — Two-week proof. I:** a Rust host accepts agent-generated programs in a loop-free plugin DSL for structured-data filtering, with explicit host capabilities and bounded output. Require a checkable certificate that every output field comes from permitted inputs and that accesses and sizes stay within the declared bounds. Interpret the checked DSL for the initial experiment, avoiding a false binary-proof claim. Compare against a Wasmtime implementation of the same policy; keep unsupported computations outside the plugin. **E:** synthesize twenty held-out tasks and reject ten policy-violating mutants.

**9 — Demo/tweet/kill. I:** a plugin can filter approved fields but a secret-field dependency fails checking even though it is memory safe. Tweet: “Generated tools can carry a checkable statement about what data they may use, not just a signature saying who sent them.” Fatal reply: “A small typed DSL already guarantees this; the model adds nothing, and Wasm is enough.” Kill if the incremental property has no buyer, if the DSL is too restrictive to be useful, or if a simple existing type checker delivers the same artifact at lower cost. Do not build a new general-purpose safe language in this experiment.

## 10. Firmware rehosting and peripheral models grown from failures

**Lens A; attractive loop, generic version already revived.**

**1 — Idea and origin. F:** execute embedded firmware in an emulator, supplying missing device behaviour or replacing identifiable hardware-abstraction-layer calls. HALucinator used reusable high-level handlers and peripheral models. [Clements et al., USENIX Security, August 2020](https://www.usenix.org/system/files/sec20summer_clements_prepub.pdf). P²IM inferred peripheral-interface behaviour to run firmware without the hardware, executing 79% of its 70 sample firmware without manual help. [Feng, Mera and Lu, USENIX Security, August 2020](https://www.usenix.org/conference/usenixsecurity20/presentation/feng). **I:** these are prior automated approaches, not a claim that every model used to be handcrafted.

**2 — Why niche. F:** HALucinator describes the per-device burden and heterogeneity; its abstraction deliberately bypasses low-level details. Fuzzware later reduced MMIO input complexity but still targets rehosting/fuzzing rather than establishing a complete physical-device twin. [Scharnowski et al., USENIX Security 2022](https://sites.cs.ucsb.edu/~chris/research/doc/usenix22_fuzzware.pdf). **I:** reaching a firmware service and accurately reproducing its hardware semantics are different objectives. Hidden timing, interrupts, DMA, calibration state, and analog effects are not eliminated by generating more code.

**3 — Flip and 6 — direct prior art. F:** DevGen already generates QEMU virtual devices from Linux drivers with a compilation/execution repair loop: usable models for 44 of 50 PCI/PCIe drivers. [Wang et al., ACL demo, July 2026](https://aclanthology.org/2026.acl-demo.24/). FirmCure reports 90.5% service interactivity across 21 images and five architectures. [Hong et al., 23 June 2026, preprint](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.24549). FirmPilot explicitly uses evidence-guided iterative environment recovery; it reports web reachability rising from 25.49% to 52.39% versus FirmAE on its corpus. [Shen, Zhang and Xu, 16 July 2026, preprint](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.14903). **I:** “agent repairs firmware emulation until it boots” is directly occupied. The remaining candidate is a *fidelity-tested* model for a specific device family, not a broader reachability demo.

**4 — Oracle. I: weak without hardware; potentially strong for recorded, controlled behaviours.** A physical board with documented stimuli and captured I/O/interrupt traces supplies independent evidence. A separately implemented vendor simulator or RTL model may substitute on its stated scope. Firmware execution progress, a listening port, and agreeing with a model derived from the same firmware do not establish fidelity. Use held-out fault/error stimuli, not just replay of the training trace. Recorded traces alone underdetermine the device: state-changing interactions between observations can still differ.

**5 — 100× model. I:** more valuable for model construction and lab automation, but physical access, independent stimuli, and real-time constraints remain limiting. Device-family expertise and customer regression suites are the asset.

**7 — Value. I/E:** industrial/embedded vendors buy CI without large hardware farms, reliable regression reproducers, or support for discontinued boards. Ten device-family contracts at €20k–€100k/year imply €0.2m–€1m/year. Customer permission to use firmware/specifications and real hardware availability are prerequisites for a later pilot, not assumed inputs.

**8 — Two-week proof. I:** use an open-source MCU firmware and an existing independently implemented virtual board/peripheral as the reference. Remove one peripheral from a second emulator. Let the agent infer a replacement from public docs and training workloads, then test independent held-out initialization, timeout, and interrupt scenarios against the untouched reference. Begin with UART/SPI, not a complex radio or GPU. This proves model-repair methodology in emulation; a physical-board fidelity claim requires a later hardware test. No hardware experiment was performed here.

**9 — Demo/tweet/kill. I:** show identical application-level behaviour under malformed input, timeout, and reset, plus a reachability-only model that fails. Tweet: “Booting is easy to fake. The missing peripheral has to survive the same independent fault scenarios as the reference.” Fatal reply: “Your model is another permissive stub; FirmPilot already gets farther.” **E:** kill if fewer than 90% of a predeclared held-out behavioural suite match, if one required device family cannot supply an independent reference, or if existing HAL-level emulation already satisfies the customer's tests.

## Where the GrowOS loop extends, and where it changes character

**I — Driver environment (§1):** missing kernel-service fault → agent supplies adapter implementation → run the same fixed driver and external workload on Linux and target → accept only with error-path tests. The kernel API is the missing surface; the device's driver logic can remain mature code. This is the most direct new application of the existing growth machinery.

**I — Verified handler (§2):** minimized behavioural mismatch → independent contract check → agent repairs code and proof → differential regression plus invariant checking → accept. The contract must be separately controlled. The supplied 81% GrowOS held-out-program pass figure is context from the brief, not this report's measurement; it says nothing by itself about security or all-input correctness.

**I — ISA port (§3):** unsupported lowering or trap-state mismatch → generate target-specific implementation → compare against authoritative sequential semantics and ABI tests → accept. The oracle must expand beyond Linux syscalls: Linux cannot tell whether a new trap trampoline preserves every architectural register under every interrupt condition.

**I — Firmware/peripheral (§10):** blocked MMIO/HAL call → agent proposes missing behaviour → independently stimulated reference comparison → accept. Without a reference this is often “make execution proceed,” a materially weaker loop than syscall differential testing.

**I — Shared design requirement:** version the oracle, its unsupported behaviours, the accepted implementation, and the exact evidence together. Keep generation and acceptance inputs separate. A hidden test is useful only if it was not generated to match the candidate. Do not allow the agent to repair the scoreboard. Mutation and second-target tests are inexpensive checks against a demo that learned the harness rather than the abstraction.

## Ranked top three

**1 — Driver-environment growth (§1). I:** strongest direct evidence of a manual-labour bottleneck, immediately adjacent to GrowOS, and testable without inventing a new kernel. The output is a maintained compatibility layer rather than replacement device code. **Principal objection:** Genode already automates much of the mechanical work, and the hard semantics may lack a strong oracle. Ranking is conditional on beating that baseline on a held-out driver/revision.

**2 — Proof-maintained finite compatibility components (§2). I:** strongest model-improvement tailwind and smallest security-relevant artifact a single engineer can plausibly close in two weeks. **Principal objection:** AutoVerus, VeriStruct, KVerus, Kani, and the original verification teams already occupy the technique. The remaining value must be independent behavioural contracts and maintained integration, not proof generation alone.

**3 — ISA-extension/port acceptance machinery (§3). I:** executable ISA semantics offer an unusually good acceptance oracle, and one accepted target rule can be reused broadly. **Principal objection:** existing compiler generators and verification already handle the mainstream cases; a buyer must have a real unsupported target or extension. Kernel ports remain a larger follow-on because platform and concurrency semantics exceed an instruction selector.

**I — Remaining order:** 4: workload specialisation (§6), conditional on a repeated customer workload; 5: fidelity-tested firmware models (§10), conditional on an independent reference; 6: restricted binary hot-region translation (§4); 7: capability-system assembly (§7); 8: narrowly policy-checked plugins (§9); 9: generic superoptimisation (§5, reject as a standalone product); 10: generic sandbox infrastructure (§8, reject as a new entry point). This is a ranking of opportunities for this builder, not technical importance or total market size.

**I — Why rank the demonstrated huge market lower?** Dense agent execution (§8) has the clearest new demand, but E2B, Firecracker, Unikraft, and production lab infrastructure already implement the obvious revival. Greater market size is not evidence that this builder has a differentiated entry point. Firmware rehosting (§10) has the same problem plus a weaker oracle. Specialisation (§6) is a reasonable fourth candidate only with a repeated real workload.

## The ONE bet

**I — Build a maintained driver-adapter grower, with a deliberately narrow promise: “An existing Linux driver, a new execution environment, and independently checked external behaviour across updates.”** Use GrowOS's failure capture and repair orchestration, but do not couple the initial proof to finishing a whole kernel integration. Do not synthesise a new GPU/network driver from prose.

**I — The decisive artifact:** one frozen driver carried across two source revisions or closely related virtual devices, with an adapter grown from missing-service failures; a reference harness the agent cannot modify; error/reset/interrupt scenarios; and a ledger of manual interventions. Small state-machine proofs from §2 are a differentiator to test after correct behaviour, not an excuse to call unsupported device semantics verified. Termite is the intellectual ancestor of spec-based synthesis, but mature-driver reuse reduces the need to reconstruct every device protocol.

**F — Why not revive Termite wholesale?** Termite combined OS/device specifications to generate driver C; Termite-2 reported that the older output's structure, maintainability, and synthesis scalability blocked adoption, rejected the purely push-button conception, and introduced user-guided synthesis. [Ryzhyk et al., SOSP, October 2009](https://ryzhyk.net/publications/Ryzhyk_CKSH_09.pdf); [Ryzhyk et al., OSDI, October 2014, §1](https://www.usenix.org/system/files/conference/osdi14/osdi14-paper-ryzhyk.pdf). Pancake already verifies a practical Ethernet driver and uses a verified CakeML-derived backend; its 2025 paper reports about three person-months for verification after manual transcription, rather than claiming generation was free. [Zhao et al., 14 January 2025](https://trustworthy.systems/publications/papers/Zhao_LTUTSTASSSMNH_25.pdf). **I:** these projects make specification and proof assistance attractive, but they also kill “nobody does verified driver construction.” Use their semantics/tooling where appropriate. The new question is whether the repetitive adapter/update work can be eliminated with independently checked evidence.

**E — Two-week go/no-go:** continue only if the held-out variant works without oracle edits, meaningful failure mutants are rejected, adapter-specific manual intervention stays below two engineer-days after harness setup, and a competent engineer using existing DDE/tooling would have spent materially more. If those fail, stop the driver bet and run §2's smaller proof-maintenance experiment. Even a successful technical pilot needs customer evidence: one team with an actual unsupported driver and a recurring update problem, not enthusiasm for the architecture.

## Five-line TL;DR

1. **I:** The best revival to test is growing the OS environment around an existing driver; Genode documents the manual cost directly.
2. **I:** Add independently frozen contracts and maintained proofs to small GrowOS handlers; general LLM proof generation is already crowded.
3. **I:** Executable ISA semantics make checked backend extensions plausible; they do not automatically validate an entire kernel port.
4. **F/I:** Dense sandbox demand is now production-scale, but generic microVM clouds, superoptimisers, and firmware-repair loops have substantial prior art.
5. **I:** The scarce asset is an independent, non-vacuous acceptance oracle and repeatable maintenance evidence; cheap code alone does not create the opportunity.

## Rejected ideas and versions — one line each

- **I — A new general-purpose unikernel:** GrowOS already occupies the user's calibration; evaluating another one adds little without a distinct driver, port, or verification wedge.
- **I — A wholesale exokernel replacement for Linux:** application-level resource policy was the actual proposal, but deployment/ecosystem migration remains; the library-OS/density subset is evaluated in §8.
- **I — “Microkernels were inherently slow, now AI fixes them”:** Liedtke rejected the inherent-slowdown premise in 1995; today's question is ecosystem integration and authority, not recovering a forgotten IPC trick.
- **I — Resurrect KeyKOS/EROS/Coyotos as a complete OS:** explicit authority is valuable, but no sourced 2024–2026 evidence here establishes that agents solve the complete compatibility, persistence, and deployment package; §7 retains only the bounded assembly problem.
- **F/I — Orthogonal persistence as ordinary agent checkpointing:** EROS's single-level store promised consistent persistent system state, not restoration of arbitrary external-service effects; VM snapshots already cover much of the local-state use case. [Shapiro and Adams, USENIX, June 2002](https://www.usenix.org/legacy/event/usenix02/full_papers/shapiro/shapiro.pdf).
- **F/I — Opal-style single address space as the new sandbox:** Opal separated addressability from access rights in protection domains; shared addresses alone are not the isolation mechanism, and no concrete labour-cost flip was established. [Chase et al., TOCS, November 1994](https://homes.cs.washington.edu/~levy/opal.pdf).
- **F/I — Singularity as a drop-in Linux alternative:** its closed-code/ownership assumptions are central; arbitrary native packages and dynamic loaders do not gain those properties from cheap porting. [Hunt and Larus, October 2005](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/wp-content/uploads/2005/10/tr-2005-135.pdf).
- **F/I — Plan 9 as an “agent OS”:** private mutable namespaces and remote 9P resources fit composable environments, but the authors already proposed customized views for computing agents; no specific new buyer or acceptance oracle separates a new OS from Linux namespace/tool adapters. [Pike et al., Summer 1995](https://9p.io/sys/doc/9.html).
- **I — Another CompCert/CakeML from scratch:** actively developed verified compilers and existing support businesses already exist; use them or extend target coverage rather than duplicate the foundation.
- **I — Generic superoptimisation agent:** Iago and AlphaEvolve directly occupy the revival; retain only a neglected target/workload with an actual deployment advantage (§5).
- **I — Universal partial evaluator:** weval and established JIT/PGO machinery cover important cases; a real stable workload and an amortisation calculation must precede generalisation (§6).
- **I — Universal binary-to-binary recompilation:** DBT is alive, and test-checked LLM transpilation already exists; keep only independently checkable hot regions or a specific expensive legacy target (§4).
- **I — Another fast agent-sandbox cloud:** mature snapshot-based runtimes and production-scale DSec make cold-start alone inadequate; only measured cost/compatibility gains remain (§8).
- **I — “Proof-carrying” plugins that merely pass tests or carry signatures:** neither is Necula's producer-supplied proof of a host-defined policy; existing Wasm runtimes are the baseline (§9).
- **I — Autonomous firmware boot repair:** FirmCure and FirmPilot directly occupy it; independent behavioural fidelity is the remaining, harder question (§10).
- **I — Arbitrary device-driver synthesis from datasheets:** Termite/Pancake already identify the reusable specification problem; undocumented hardware, timing, DMA, and errors prevent treating source generation as the whole cost.
