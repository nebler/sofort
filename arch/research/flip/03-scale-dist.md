# Old distributed-systems ideas whose demand flipped

Lens B — distributed systems, infrastructure, and data. Research cutoff: **2026-10-28**.

**[I] Main finding:** the strongest opportunity is making resource ownership survive an agent's death: leases, durable creation records, fencing, and garbage collection across sandbox and artifact providers. Admission control has the clearest evidence of immediate pain, but the generic product is crowded. Capability delegation and deterministic simulation are also being revived explicitly; their existence is a reason to narrow a bet, not rename it.

**Evidence labels:** **F** = sourced fact, including an explicitly attributed vendor report; **M** = measured in this research; **I** = inference or proposed design; **E** = estimate, illustrative workload, commercial hypothesis, or proposed acceptance threshold. **There are no M claims:** this was desk research, with no deployments or experiments. Proposed demos and tweets below describe what to build, not results already obtained. Prices and market wedges marked E are hypotheses, not market-size research.

**[I] Historical correction:** most of these ideas did not die. Fair scheduling, transactional logs, deduplication, and leases already underpin large systems. The flip is that small application teams now inherit workloads and failure modes that previously justified a specialist infrastructure team. Where evidence only establishes adoption friction, the report says so rather than inventing a historical failure.

## What scale is actually established in 2024–2026?

- **[F] Sandbox creation really did grow by orders of magnitude at one provider.** E2B's history reports 40,000 starts/month in March 2024 and more than 15 million/month during 2025; **[I] that is at least 375×**, with the same monthly-start denominator. It also reports two billion cumulative starts by October 2026. Cumulative starts are neither concurrent machines nor monthly usage. [E2B history, dated milestones; accessed 2026-10-28](https://e2b.dev/about).
- **[F] Parallel environments are a production and training workload.** Modal reported over one billion cumulative sandbox launches in May 2026, thousands of concurrent RL environments, more than $300 million annualized revenue, and sandboxes exceeding one-third of revenue. Those are company claims, not audited segment revenue. Its June engineering account describes RL environments ranging from thousands to millions of containers. [Modal, 2026-05-21](https://modal.com/blog/modal-series-c); [Modal engineering, 2026-06-01](https://modal.com/blog/reinforcement-learning-infrastructure-problem).
- **[F] One human request can consume much more work.** Anthropic reports roughly 4× chat token use for agents and 15× for multi-agent research in its own system. **[I] These are token multipliers, not evidence of 15× API requests or 15× workers.** [Anthropic engineering, 2025-06-13](https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/multi-agent-research-system).
- **[F] Capacity failure is already visible.** Datadog's 2026 report, based on customer LLM telemetry, reports nearly 8.4 million rate-limit errors in March 2026, approximately one-third of LLM-call errors. In February, rate limits caused 60% of errors. **[F] Counterweight:** 59% of agentic requests in its sample made only one service call; only 18% made three or more. This is a selected customer population, not a census of agents. [Datadog, 2026 report, February/March observations](https://www.datadoghq.com/state-of-ai-engineering/).
- **[F] Machine principals already outnumber people.** CyberArk's survey of 2,600 security decision-makers in 20 countries reports 82 machine identities per human in 2025. The 2026 successor survey of 2,900+ reports 109:1. These ratios include workloads and credentials, not just AI agents; they are survey findings, not a 109× growth rate. [CyberArk 2025 report, pp. 4–6, preserved PDF](https://insyte.amplifye.ai/amplifyai-website/assets/Cybersecurity-and-compliance/2025-Identity-Security-Landscape.pdf); [2026 survey](https://www.paloaltonetworks.com/idira/idira-identity-security-landscape).
- **[F] The pressure extends beyond agents.** Ericsson estimates 22.3 billion total IoT connections at end-2025, including 4.5 billion cellular connections. Its 47.1 billion total for 2031 is a forecast, not current demand and not 10× growth. Roblox reports over 18 trillion Kafka messages/day in September 2026, after more than 2.5× annual growth in each of the preceding two years; **[I] that implies more than 6.25× over two years, not 100×.** [Ericsson, June 2026 outlook](https://www.ericsson.com/en/reports-and-papers/mobility-report/dataforecasts/iot-connections-outlook); [Roblox engineering, 2026-09-04](https://about.roblox.com/newsroom/2026/09/how-roblox-scaled-kafka-platform-over-18-trillion-messages-a-day).
- **[F] Public web workloads changed measurably.** Cloudflare reports AI user-action crawling grew more than 15× during 2025 across its observed network. It separately reports overall Internet traffic growth of 19%; conflating those figures would exaggerate the general scale change. [Cloudflare Radar review, December 2025](https://blog.cloudflare.com/radar-2025-year-in-review/).
- **[F] Data governance has infrastructure-scale cardinality.** The Unity Catalog authors report more than 100 million governed tables, 400,000 models, 75,000 workspaces, and approximately 60,000 API requests/s across regions in their 2025 paper. This demonstrates the size of a deployed catalog, not the annual growth rate or a count of agent-generated assets. [Chandra et al., SIGMOD Companion, June 2025 paper](https://www.databricks.com/sites/default/files/2025-06/unity-catalog-open-universal-governance-lakehouse-beyond.pdf).

**[E] A useful stress workload, not an adoption forecast:** 100 people × 10 simultaneous jobs/person × 10 child jobs × 10 tool operations = 100,000 tool operations in a burst. Relative to one job and ten operations per person, that is 100× operations and 100× jobs. Models need not get confused for this to overload a shared quota. Separate tests should scale identities, retained snapshots, concurrent operations, and arrival rate independently; they do not have interchangeable costs.

## 1. Admission control and fair queueing: a request must obtain capacity before creating work

1. **Old idea [F].** Demers, Keshav, and Shenker's 1989 fair-queueing algorithm approximates bit-by-bit service so long packets do not capture bandwidth merely by being long; it protects well-behaved sources from aggressive ones. Welsh, Culler, and Brewer's **SEDA (2001)** connects stages with explicit queues and adjusts resources, batching, and load shedding to stay conditioned under overload. Neither says that a queue creates capacity. [Fair queueing, SIGCOMM 1989](https://people.eecs.berkeley.edu/~sylvia/papers/FQ1989.pdf); [SEDA, SOSP 2001](https://cs.uwaterloo.ca/~brecht/servers/readings-new/seda-sosp01.pdf).

2. **Why application adoption lagged [F/I].** This was never niche in networking. The integration boundary is the problem: Inngest describes rate checking and concurrency enforcement embedded in different subsystems, preventing synchronous endpoints from sharing flow control and coupling their scaling. **[I]** Per-process semaphores and independent provider limits leave no owner of a whole task's resource budget. [Inngest Constraint API, 2026-02-23](https://www.inngest.com/blog/announcing-the-constraint-api).

3. **What flipped [F/I].** Datadog's observed rate-limit failures establish actual pain; E2B's 375× monthly-start growth establishes plausible executor churn. **[I]** Retries and child spawning turn an inbound request limit into a weak proxy for downstream load. The admission unit should include tenant, root task, resource class, deadline, and reservation, with bounded backlog and backoff.

4. **Oracle [I]: strong for accounting, conditional for fairness.** An executable serial ledger can check that admitted reservations never exceed a configured capacity. A weighted-service oracle can check allocations only for declared work costs and continuously backlogged tenants. Unknown job duration and non-preemption weaken latency guarantees; measure realized service, not just starts.

5. **100× better model [I]: more valuable.** A smarter planner can generate more useful work and predict cost better. It cannot make a finite provider quota cease to exist. If fewer calls solve the same task, demand per task falls; total demand remains an empirical question.

6. **Already doing it [F].** Temporal made priority and fairness GA on **2026-05-05**. Inngest already offers concurrency, throttle, tenant-key scheduling, and atomic capacity leases. Restate **1.8, 2026-10-15**, enables virtual queues by default with organization/team/user concurrency limits; its announcement lists rate limits and priorities as future additions. LiteLLM and Bifrost already sell model/tool budgets and rate controls. [Temporal](https://temporal.io/changelog/priority-fairness-generally-available); [Inngest controls](https://www.inngest.com/docs/durable-execution/flow-control); [Restate 1.8](https://restate.dev/blog/announcing-restate-1-8); [LiteLLM](https://docs.litellm.ai/docs/simple_proxy); [Bifrost](https://www.getmaxim.ai/llm-gateway). **[I] Remaining hypothesis:** shared reservations across model tokens, tool calls, and sandbox lifetimes, surviving child delegation and uncertain outcomes. The sources do not prove no vendor supports this.

7. **Value [E].** Buyer: platform team with $100k–$1m/month combined execution and inference spend. A 5% reduction in waste or idle reservation is $5k–$50k/month; a $1k–$5k/month integration could fit. A new general-purpose queue is a poor wedge.

8. **Two-week proof [I/E].** TypeScript gateway plus PostgreSQL reservations and a discrete-event load generator; two model-shaped mock services and one sandbox-shaped service. Carry a root budget through 100 tenants and 10,000 child jobs. Crash workers between acquire, start, settle, and release. Compare existing gateway/workflow controls before adding anything. Target: no over-admission, no starvation of a continuously eligible tenant, and recovery of abandoned reservations within two lease periods.

9. **Demo/tweet/kill [I/E].** Demo: one runaway tenant stalls a FIFO baseline; a controlled scheduler bounds its impact. Tweet: “Ten thousand children, one shared budget: every start is accounted for before work runs.” Expert killer: “Temporal plus LiteLLM already does this; your cost estimator just hides overspend.” Kill if that stack meets the same invariants with ordinary configuration, or reservation pessimism costs more useful throughput than it saves.

## 2. Leases and distributed garbage collection: make abandoned work collectible

1. **Old idea [F].** Gray and Cheriton's **1989 leases** grant time-bounded rights for file-cache consistency, with expiration bounding recovery delay after failures. That paper is not a sandbox collector. Waldo's **Jini architecture (1999)** applies renewable leases to resources and service registrations; a grantor can reclaim after expiration without relying on a final goodbye. Java RMI explicitly uses expiring remote-reference leases for distributed GC. [Gray–Cheriton, SOSP 1989](https://pages.cs.wisc.edu/~remzi/Classes/739/Fall2018/Papers/leases89.pdf); [Waldo, CACM July 1999](https://cseweb.ucsd.edu/groups/csag/html/teaching/cse291s03/Readings/waldo99jini.pdf); [RMI DGC specification](https://docs.oracle.com/en/java/javase/18/docs/api/java.rmi/java/rmi/dgc/package-summary.html).

2. **Why the application gap persists [F/I].** Expiration is already common; ownership spanning APIs is harder. Inngest's **2026-10-08** sandbox beta explicitly leaves destroy-on-success and permanent-failure cleanup to application code. A **2026-06-27** maintainer issue records hash-named base images accumulating across Modal, Vercel, Daytona, and OpenComputer. It calls this storage hygiene, not a correctness incident. **[I]** This is direct evidence of missing lifecycle closure, but not evidence of a large unpaid market. [Inngest beta](https://www.inngest.com/blog/sandboxes-are-a-durability-problem); [background-agents issue #841, open when accessed](https://github.com/ColeMurray/background-agents/issues/841).

3. **What flipped [F/I/E].** Hundreds of times more sandbox starts mean rare leaks stop being rare operational events. **[E]** At 1 million creations/day, a 0.1% forgotten-resource rate leaves 1,000 resources/day. If each runs an extra hour at an illustrative $0.05/hour, that is $1,500/month; 24 hours becomes $36,000/month. These are sensitivity calculations, not measured leakage. Snapshot accumulation adds a different, slower-growing storage bill.

4. **Oracle [I]: strong inside a declared ownership boundary.** An inventory reconciliation checks every managed resource against a live run, explicit retention root, or unexpired lease. Safety means retained artifacts are never collected; liveness means unowned resources eventually disappear after provider recovery. A provider DELETE acknowledgement is not proof of deletion. An unreachable provider must produce an unresolved cleanup record, not a green result.

5. **100× better model [I]: more valuable.** Better agents make resource creation cheap and longer unattended jobs useful. The collector belongs outside their reasoning. A paused worker that wakes after lease expiry must be fenced at the tool boundary; expiry alone does not stop it issuing a new create.

6. **Already doing it [F].** Kubernetes owner references, cascading deletion, and finalizers solve graph cleanup within its object model; Cloud Custodian marks cloud resources for timed action. Daytona exposes auto-stop/archive/delete. Inngest ties sandbox operations to durable steps and supports pause/clone. AgentCube's design includes idle/maximum-lifetime collection and retrying failed cleanup. [Kubernetes GC](https://kubernetes.io/docs/concepts/architecture/garbage-collection/); [finalizers](https://kubernetes.io/docs/concepts/overview/working-with-objects/finalizers/); [Cloud Custodian](https://cloudcustodian.io/docs/gcp/resources/project.html); [Daytona lifecycle controls](https://www.daytona.io/docs/en/python-sdk/sync/sandbox/); [AgentCube design, 2026](https://github.com/volcano-sh/agentcube/blob/main/docs/design/agentcube-proposal.md). **[I] Gap hypothesis:** one run-scoped ownership graph spanning machines, snapshots, temporary credentials, and external jobs, with crash-safe collection and explicit retention. This is a composition opportunity, not an undiscovered GC algorithm.

7. **Value [E].** Buyer: coding-agent operator, RL/evaluation platform, or enterprise sandbox platform. Start with two providers and snapshots; $500–$3k/month for fewer leaks and less cleanup engineering, potentially larger enterprise contracts for lifecycle evidence. Modal's reported sandbox business shows budget exists upstream; it does not establish willingness to pay for a separate collector.

8. **Two-week proof [I/E].** A Rust or TypeScript controller with PostgreSQL intent records, run epochs, renewable leases, retention roots, and provider adapters against two local fake APIs. Persist creation identity before making an external call; reconcile an acknowledged-but-unrecorded create by that identity. Inject lost replies, late creates, duplicate deletes, provider outages, and old workers waking. Begin with private mock resources; live provider tests are a later phase, outside this research.

9. **Demo/tweet/kill [I/E].** Kill the parent at every create boundary; cancel the run; show every owned machine and unretained snapshot eventually collected while retained output remains. Tweet: “The agent died after creating its machine. Its cleanup obligation did not.” Expert killer: “This is a Kubernetes finalizer or an onFailure handler.” Kill if a standard controller plus existing provider TTLs handles uncertain create, retained snapshots, stale workers, and restart recovery with little custom logic, or buyers find leaks immaterial.

## 3. Capability delegation: delegate a smaller authority, not a shared administrator key

1. **Old idea [F].** Dennis and Van Horn's **1966** work organizes protected computations around capabilities to objects. A superior computation can grant restricted capabilities to an inferior sphere, but cannot grant rights not implied by its own capabilities. It also assigns resource expenditure and retained-object charges to principals. **SPKI (1999)** represents authorization and allows delegation of all or a subset of granted rights. **Macaroons (Birgisson et al., 2014)** use chained MACs and added caveats for decentralized attenuation, including contextual restrictions and third-party discharge. They do not inherently enforce a global spending counter. [Dennis–Van Horn, CACM March 1966, pp. 145, 149](https://www.princeton.edu/~rblee/ELE572Papers/Fall04Readings/ProgramSemantics_DennisvanHorn.pdf); [MIT's December 1965 technical-report precursor](https://dspace.mit.edu/entities/publication/4f4d9ee2-671a-4dfd-a679-7b24fae5812a); [SPKI RFC 2693, September 1999](https://www.rfc-editor.org/info/rfc2693/); [Macaroons, NDSS 2014](https://research.google/pubs/macaroons-cookies-with-contextual-caveats-for-decentralized-authorization-in-the-cloud/).

2. **Why niche [F].** The macaroon authors explicitly identify poor incremental deployability, complexity, and efficiency as barriers to earlier decentralized credential systems. **[I]** An API that only accepts a broad OAuth scope cannot magically enforce an arbitrary caveat; a trusted mediator must translate and enforce it. The old idea's elegance does not remove connector work.

3. **What flipped [F/I].** The 82:1 and 109:1 machine/human survey ratios make per-person permissions an inadequate inventory model. **[I]** A root agent spawning disposable workers needs rights scoped to a particular run, resource, action, and lifetime. Authentication answers who; attenuation answers what a child may receive. Spend must additionally be atomically reserved against the shared root ledger, rather than copied into each child's token.

4. **Oracle [I]: strong for declared policy.** Generate delegation trees and check authority(child) is a subset of authority(parent), expiry never expands, revocation takes effect at the next governed access, and concurrent children cannot double-spend. The oracle cannot prove that an authorized action matches an ambiguous natural-language intention.

5. **100× better model [I]: more valuable.** A competent agent can still receive hostile input, have a bug, or act after its task was revoked. Model judgment may reduce accidental misuse; deterministic limits remain useful for all workloads and principals.

6. **Already doing it [F].** AWS launched AgentCore Identity in August 2025; Auth0's November 2025 GA includes Token Vault, asynchronous authorization, and fine-grained retrieval access. Microsoft Entra has agent-specific service principals. Cloudflare's **Cap'n Web, September 2025**, is explicitly object-capability RPC. Most decisively, **SatGate already documents macaroons, child budgets, MCP enforcement, and cascade revocation**. [AWS, 2025-08-15](https://aws.amazon.com/blogs/machine-learning/introducing-amazon-bedrock-agentcore-identity-securing-agentic-ai-at-scale/); [Auth0, 2025-11-19](https://auth0.com/blog/auth0-for-ai-agents-generally-available/); [Entra agent identities](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/entra/agent-id/agent-identities); [Cap'n Web, 2025-09-22](https://blog.cloudflare.com/capnweb-javascript-rpc-library/); [SatGate specification](https://cloud.satgate.io/docs/reference/macaroons); [implementation](https://github.com/SatGate-io/satgate). **[I]** Generic “macaroons for agents” fails the prior-art test. An interoperability and conformance adapter may survive; absence of a larger competitor is unproven.

7. **Value [E].** Buyer: security/platform team already paying for IAM or gateways. $20k–$100k/year for a specific high-value connector and revocation evidence is conceivable; selling a new credential format alone is weak. Integration into an incumbent is more plausible than replacing enterprise identity.

8. **Two-week proof [I/E].** TypeScript mediator around three mock tools; use an existing macaroon implementation and a transactional budget ledger. Root grants “read project A, create at most two sandboxes, no deletes, expires in ten minutes.” Generate 10,000 delegation/replay cases and kill/restart the issuer. Compare SatGate directly at the contract level before implementing another token format.

9. **Demo/tweet/kill [I/E].** A worker follows malicious tool text but cannot acquire a broader tool, spend siblings' reservation, or act after revocation. Tweet: “The child receives a project and two machine starts, not the parent's cloud key.” Expert killer: “That is SatGate plus scoped OAuth; offline caveats cannot enforce consumption.” Kill if existing products satisfy the deployment boundary or the mediator can be bypassed with leaked provider credentials.

## 4. Content addressing and deduplication: shared immutable inputs, private mutable work

1. **Old idea [F].** Quinlan and Dorward's **Venti (2002)** identifies stored blocks by content hash, coalesces duplicates, and provides write-once archival storage on which backup and snapshot clients can be built. Reusing its principle for environments requires a manifest, immutable chunks, and a separate writable layer; a hash of a directory name is insufficient. [Venti, FAST 2002](https://www.usenix.org/conference/fast-02/venti-new-approach-archival-data-storage).

2. **Why it was not universal [F/I].** CAS already succeeded in archives and build systems. The **Foundation storage paper (2008)** reports Venti's multiple high-performance disks made its throughput economics unsuitable for their consumer setting. **[I]** Execution adds mutable files, runtime memory, credentials, network state, and cache invalidation; archival deduplication alone does not give a safe hot clone. [Rhea et al., USENIX 2008](https://www.usenix.org/legacy/events/usenix08/tech/full_papers/rhea/rhea_html/index.html).

3. **What flipped [F/I/E].** Repeated sandbox creation and parallel RL rollouts multiply copies of the same toolchain and model scaffolding. **[E]** A 2 GB common base cloned for 10,000 jobs would require 20 TB of base transfer without sharing, compared with 2 GB plus private changes in an ideal shared-store case; actual savings depend on worker locality and existing caches. This is a workload calculation, not a benchmark.

4. **Oracle [I]: strong for bytes; conditional for execution.** Reconstruct a manifest and verify chunk hashes against a full copy, then compare test results between cold and cloned environments. Environment variables, random seeds, open sockets, time, and secret state must have explicit snapshot semantics. Equal filesystem bytes do not prove equal external behavior.

5. **100× better model [I]: more valuable where useful search branches.** More candidate programs, tests, and evaluations increase reusable setup. Better models could reduce redundant attempts, but cannot make identical bytes require fresh transfer or installation for correctness.

6. **Already doing it [F].** Modal shipped sandbox disk snapshotting in **January 2025**, then directory snapshots in **February/March 2026**. Its July 2025 GPU-snapshot article describes shared file caching and CPU/GPU checkpointing. Inngest's October 2026 beta has snapshot-and-clone steps. E2B boots machines from snapshots. These are closely related implementations, not proof they all expose the same CAS abstraction. [Modal, 2025-01-21](https://modal.com/blog/modal-product-updates-jan-2025); [Modal, 2026-03-04](https://modal.com/blog/product-updates-directory-snapshots-glm-5-billing-updates-and-more); [Modal, 2025-07-30](https://modal.com/blog/gpu-mem-snapshots); [E2B architecture](https://e2b.dev/). **[I] Remaining hypothesis:** portable, auditable artifact manifests with retention and dependency-aware collection across providers. Section 2's orphan-image issue is evidence that immutability creates a collection obligation, not evidence a new storage engine is needed.

7. **Value [E].** Buyer: eval/RL operator or CI platform. If 100,000 jobs/day each avoid ten seconds of paid setup on one vCPU, they avoid about 278 vCPU-hours/day; monetary value depends heavily on provider billing, memory, and warm-pool costs. Sell measured wall-time and billed-resource savings, not theoretical dedup ratios.

8. **Two-week proof [I/E].** Rust chunker plus local filesystem CAS and copy-on-write-style manifests; keep artifacts below 200 MB for a local proof. Generate 1,000 logical branches of a small repository, mutate a few files, reconstruct, and compare hashes and test outputs with full copies. Test retention roots and failed uploads. No VM or Docker implementation is required to test storage semantics.

9. **Demo/tweet/kill [I/E].** Show shared chunks, independent branch writes, and safe collection of abandoned versions. Tweet: “A thousand experiments share one immutable setup and retain only their differences.” Expert killer: “OCI layers, BuildKit, and provider snapshots already do this.” Kill if provider-native caches erase the benefit, or secret isolation and portability require recreating the whole runtime. **Disposition: use the primitive; reject a generic clone platform.**

## 5. Incremental and differential dataflow: update context when inputs change

1. **Old idea [F].** McSherry, Murray, Isaacs, and Isard's **Differential Dataflow (2013)** extends incremental computation to nested iteration, tracking changes at partially ordered logical times and reusing earlier work. It is stronger than rerunning a batch every few seconds, and different from approximate “semantic caching.” [Authors' CIDR 2013 publication and paper](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/publication/differential-dataflow/).

2. **Why niche outside specialist systems [F/I].** The paper motivates the difficulty of maintaining iterative queries with earlier models. **[I]** Adoption also requires change feeds, a maintained dependency graph, bounded state, and defined deletion semantics. Pure recomputation is easy to operate when queries are infrequent; arbitrary network tools and LLM transformations do not automatically have cheap incremental operators.

3. **What flipped [F/I].** Unity Catalog's 100 million tables and Roblox's event volumes establish large data/metadata surfaces; they do not prove that every agent needs streaming SQL. Materialize's **2025-06-30** engineering/product argument explicitly targets agents needing to see the effects of their own actions without waiting for batch ETL. **[I]** At hundreds of independent readers of the same business state, maintaining shared context can be cheaper than repeating joins for every loop. [Materialize, 2025-06-30](https://materialize.com/blog/ai-agents-need-digital-twins/).

4. **Oracle [I]: strong for deterministic transformations.** At each logical watermark, compare maintained views with full batch SQL over the same input snapshot. Include inserts, deletes, late events, restarts, and schema changes. An LLM summary is not an exact differential oracle: version the model/prompt and recompute affected deterministic units instead of pretending generation is algebraically incremental.

5. **100× better model [I]: more valuable if it makes more decisions on live data.** Better query synthesis reduces developer labor, but running a costly join 1,000 times remains costly. If the application only reads once or a small OLTP index already suffices, the engine adds unnecessary state and lag.

6. **Already doing it [F].** Materialize explicitly markets agent-ready, incrementally maintained views and MCP access, claiming over one million updates/s and sub-second freshness on its solution page. Feldera's DBSP engine incrementally evaluates SQL and documents consistency with batch evaluation, spill to disk, and restart support with connector conditions. **[I]** Neither is untouched ground. [Materialize solution, accessed 2026-10-28](https://materialize.com/solutions/ai-transformation/); [Feldera technical documentation](https://docs.feldera.com/); [DBSP theory and implementation](https://docs.rs/dbsp/latest/dbsp/). **[I] Gap hypothesis:** a compiler that generates a versioned context DAG and proves deletion/permission invalidation against batch execution for a narrow source set. This is an integration layer atop these engines.

7. **Value [E].** Buyer: operational ML/agent platform with many consumers of shared derived state. A $200k/year recomputation bill reduced by 30% creates $60k/year of headroom; $10k–$30k/year for the narrow integration is plausible. The actual data-change/read ratio decides economics.

8. **Two-week proof [I/E].** TypeScript CDC/event fixture generator plus an existing incremental engine and SQLite/DuckDB batch oracle. Maintain account balances, inventory, and permission-filtered context over 100,000 rows. Replay a deterministic update log while 1,000 synthetic agents read. Target exact watermark equality and 10× less recomputation work on a workload with 0.1% changes per cycle; report memory and restart cost too.

9. **Demo/tweet/kill [I/E].** Change one account permission and remove its derived context before the next authorized read. Tweet: “One source update refreshes a thousand agents' shared context once.” Expert killer: “Materialize already does the hard part; your context joins are ordinary SQL.” Kill if indexes and batching are within 2× cost/latency, or invalidation cannot cover the outputs the buyer actually uses.

## 6. Durable journals, sagas, and idempotent effects: a retry must not become another purchase

1. **Old idea [F].** Birrell and Nelson's **RPC implementation (1984)** addresses duplicate calls and uncertain failure outcomes; Garcia-Molina and Salem's **Sagas (1987)** split long transactions into interleavable transactions with compensating actions. A saga is not a global atomic transaction, and compensation is not literal time reversal. **[I]** The revived pattern records nondeterministic decisions, uses stable effect identities, and reconciles uncertain external outcomes. [RPC, TOCS February 1984](https://birrell.org/andrew/papers/ImplementingRPC.pdf); [Sagas, 1987](https://doi.org/10.1145/38713.38742).

2. **Why the application gap persists [F/I].** Durable execution is already a substantial category: Temporal reported 130 billion monthly actions and 1,500+ paying customers in August 2024. DBOS explicitly documents at-least-once steps versus exactly-once persisted workflow outcomes. **[I]** Recording “the tool returned” cannot atomically encompass an unrelated provider's irreversible action. [Temporal, 2024 milestone listing](https://temporal.io/news); [DBOS execution guarantees](https://docs.dbos.dev/explanations/concurrent-executions).

3. **What flipped [F/I].** Multi-step agents now call production tools, branch, wait, resume, and replan. LangGraph Platform reported nearly 400 production customer deployments by May 2025. **[I]** More actions and retries multiply opportunities to duplicate an effect, even with a perfect model and otherwise correct SDK. [LangGraph, 2025-05-14](https://www.langchain.com/blog/langgraph-platform-ga).

4. **Oracle [I]: strong with a cooperative sink; weak without it.** A serial effect ledger checks one approved operation produces at most one effect and eventually resolves. With a provider honoring stable idempotency keys, retries can recover the original outcome. Without provider deduplication or a queryable business identifier, a crash after external commit creates irreducible ambiguity: expose an unknown/reconcile state rather than promise exactly-once behavior.

5. **100× better model [I]: more valuable.** Smarter reasoning cannot observe an acknowledgement that was lost. It may even re-express the same approved action with a new tool-call ID. Effect identity must come from the approval/business operation, not the model's wording or transport attempt number.

6. **Already doing it [F].** Temporal, Restate, DBOS, LangGraph, and Inngest all occupy durable orchestration. Restate explains recording model responses and tool results and pinning in-flight execution to immutable deployment revisions in its October 2025 agent article. An **August 2026 CapLease preprint** directly studies semantic reissuance across retries/delegation; its matched server ledger has the same safety under the same stateful assumptions, and effect protection requires an idempotent sink. This is research evidence, not a universal production guarantee. [Restate, 2025-10-09](https://restate.dev/blog/resilient-serverless-agents); [DBOS, 2025-02-24](https://www.dbos.dev/blog/durable-execution-crashproof-ai-agents); [Xu et al., 2026-08-03](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.01710v1). **[I] Gap hypothesis:** narrow provider-specific effect contracts and conformance fixtures, not another workflow engine.

7. **Value [E].** Buyer: operations platform executing refunds, orders, or account changes. Avoiding a few duplicated $500 operations and hours of reconciliation may justify a $1k/month connector. The buyer must value the provider-specific failure contract, not just “durability.”

8. **Two-week proof [I/E].** TypeScript journal on PostgreSQL with a fake refund provider in two modes: stable idempotency key and no deduplication. Crash after every durable transition and external write; deliberately replan with a fresh call ID. Keep a stable authenticated approval ID so two separately approved identical purchases remain distinguishable. Target zero duplicates only in the supported sink mode; demonstrate the other mode's explicit uncertainty.

9. **Demo/tweet/kill [I/E].** Lose the “refund succeeded” reply and restart every worker; show one refund or an unresolved state, according to the sink contract. Tweet: “The agent retried twenty times. The approved refund still happened once.” Expert killer: “Your workflow ID changed, and you do not control the payment provider.” Kill if reliable business-operation IDs cannot be established or existing workflow/provider idempotency handles the case without a sellable integration.

## 7. Provenance and lineage: an execution trace is not a dependency graph

1. **Old idea [F].** Green, Karvounarakis, and Tannen's **Provenance Semirings (2007)** annotate tuples and compose annotations through relational operations; symbolic polynomials capture how inputs contribute to outputs. W3C **PROV-DM (2013)** models entities, activities, derivations, responsibility, and delegation, including software agents. Neither establishes that a generated statement is true merely because its inputs are recorded. [PODS 2007 paper](https://www.cs.ucdavis.edu/~green/papers/pods07.pdf); [PROV-DM, 2013-04-30](https://www.w3.org/TR/prov-dm/).

2. **Why richer provenance remains incomplete [F/I].** OpenLineage's **August 2026** proposal identifies a structural limitation: job inputs × outputs can imply edges that are not real. It proposes explicit dataset/column/job relationships, still experimentally. Unity Catalog documents missing lineage for some operations, loss across renames, and lineage events surviving transaction rollback. **[I]** Rich dependency semantics and instrumentation coverage remain harder than collecting span trees. [OpenLineage, 2026-08-04](https://openlineage.io/blog/explicit-lineage/); [Unity Catalog limitations, accessed 2026-10-28](https://docs.databricks.com/aws/en/data-governance/unity-catalog/data-lineage).

3. **What flipped [F/I/E].** The 2025 Unity Catalog paper's 100 million tables and 400,000 models show the catalog cardinality already exists. Anthropic's November 2025 MCP engineering account describes agents with hundreds or thousands of tools across dozens of servers. **[E]** A thousand runs producing ten derived artifacts each create 10,000 new dependency-bearing objects per batch. **[I]** Human recollection is not an invalidation mechanism. [Anthropic, 2025-11-04](https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/code-execution-with-mcp).

4. **Oracle [I]: strong for captured mechanical dependencies; weak for semantic causality.** Instrumented reads/writes can verify which versioned bytes entered a transformation. A deterministic pipeline can compare selective invalidation to a full rebuild. This cannot establish that an LLM actually relied on each input or discover an uninstrumented dependency; conservative edges must be labelled as such.

5. **100× better model [I]: more valuable for mechanical provenance.** Better models create more plausible, reusable outputs; tracing their sources and revoking stale derivatives matters more. If the model stops needing a particular transformation, its lineage disappears naturally, but software artifacts still have versions and dependencies.

6. **Already doing it [F].** OpenLineage, Unity Catalog, and W3C PROV cover much of the data model. OpenTelemetry's 2024–2025 work standardizes model and agent telemetry; Restate plus Langfuse already integrates execution journals with tracing and evaluation. [OpenTelemetry, 2024-12-05](https://opentelemetry.io/blog/2024/otel-generative-ai/); [agent conventions, 2025](https://opentelemetry.io/blog/2025/ai-agent-observability/); [Restate/Langfuse, 2026-04-09](https://restate.dev/blog/langfuse-restate-announcement). **[I] Gap hypothesis:** artifact-level dependency and permission invalidation spanning source records, prompts, sandbox outputs, and downstream actions. No evidence here establishes an empty market, or that tracing vendors cannot add it.

7. **Value [E].** Buyer: data/ML platform or enterprise agent operator. Start with “which outputs must be rebuilt or hidden after this source version/permission changes?” rather than a universal trust dashboard. A $10k–$50k/year integration is plausible if it cuts a recurring manual incident/rebuild process; if the buyer only wants a trace waterfall, existing tools win.

8. **Two-week proof [I/E].** TypeScript artifact manifest emitter and SQLite dependency index, exporting PROV/OpenLineage-compatible relationships. Build a small synthetic source → extraction → report pipeline and a second independent branch. Delete/change a source and compare selective recomputation with a full rebuild. Inject a hidden read to prove the oracle detects incomplete capture. Keep source evidence, model output, and asserted dependency distinct.

9. **Demo/tweet/kill [I/E].** Revoke one source document and show affected derived artifacts blocked, unrelated work untouched, and unsupported external copies reported. Tweet: “One source was revoked. Here are the exact captured derivatives that must stop serving.” Expert killer: “You cannot retract a copied email or infer an LLM's causal reasoning from logs.” Kill if the valuable outputs lie outside controllable storage or conservative dependencies invalidate nearly everything.

## 8. Deterministic simulation testing: explore the failure schedules generated systems will meet

1. **Old idea [F].** FoundationDB built a deterministic simulator before its database, controlling simulated processes, disk, network, and failures in one process. The **2021 SIGMOD paper** describes this longstanding architecture; Will Wilson presented it publicly in **2014**. The contribution is repeatable execution under adverse schedules with asserted invariants, not merely replaying saved LLM text. [FoundationDB paper, §4 simulation testing](https://www.foundationdb.org/files/fdb-paper.pdf); [Antithesis account of the history and method](https://antithesis.com/docs/resources/deterministic_simulation_testing/).

2. **Why niche [F].** Antithesis's technical guide states that making all nondeterministic dependencies pluggable is generally impractical for existing production systems; its deterministic hypervisor is an alternative. External dependencies still need mocks or plugs, and state-space exploration remains hard. **[I]** A replayable failure is only useful when an assertion recognizes it.

3. **What flipped [F/I].** More sandbox jobs and generated integrations increase the number of implementations and schedules to validate. There is also evidence the repair loop works in practice: Antithesis's **September 2026** account reports an agent mutation campaign with 19 mutations over 46 runs, falsifying 11 of 13 proposed properties, and discovery of three upstream rqlite bugs. Two properties were not successfully falsified. **[I]** This simultaneously supports the demand and kills the generic “agent writes distributed tests until green” novelty claim. [Antithesis, 2026-09-25](https://antithesis.com/blog/2026/mutation-testing/).

4. **Oracle [I]: strong for explicit safety invariants; conditional for liveness and model behavior.** Check no over-admission, no lost acknowledged update, no stale epoch write, no unauthorized resource retention, and eventual cleanup after recovery. Record/replay model responses when testing infrastructure. Mutation testing must demonstrate that the workload exercises deliberately broken properties. Simulation proves nothing about omitted provider behavior.

5. **100× better model [I]: more valuable, with less labor per harness.** Stronger agents can build mocks and minimize traces, but cannot eliminate hardware/network failure schedules. If they generate their own invariants, independence from the implementation still matters; a better model may reduce coding errors without removing verification value.

6. **Already doing it [F].** FoundationDB and Antithesis cover the core technique; Antithesis now supplies agent skills and mutation testing. Aiven reports **2,200 logical hours** testing Diskless Kafka with Antithesis in March 2026. **[I]** This is an active commercial category, not a dormant method awaiting a wrapper. [Aiven, 2026-03-05](https://aiven.io/blog/deterministic-simulation-testing-in-diskless-apache-kafka). **[I] Gap hypothesis:** reusable fault-contract suites for a narrow agent integration boundary, such as provider creates plus workflow cancellation. Prefer feeding a mature simulator over writing a hypervisor.

7. **Value [E].** Buyer: team maintaining a costly stateful connector or scheduler. $20k–$100k/year for a domain-specific suite and maintained provider models could be plausible; a two-week prototype alone does not establish that business. The most immediate value may be internal verification for sections 1 and 2.

8. **Two-week proof [I/E].** Rust discrete-event simulator for a small lease/resource controller, with injected clock, transport, durable store, and provider models. Explore 10,000 seeded schedules; shrink failures; inject five hand-written mutants. Target reproducible failures and killing all five mutants. This is bounded component simulation, not full-system FoundationDB equivalence.

9. **Demo/tweet/kill [I/E].** A worker resumes after its lease expired and creates an orphan; replay the exact seed after the fix and inspect the invariant. Tweet: “Seed 4182 found the three failures in the one order our integration test never tried.” Expert killer: “Antithesis already does this, and your fake cloud is wrong.” Kill a standalone product if a normal Antithesis harness solves the integration or provider models cannot be validated against real contracts. **Disposition: build into the chosen bet; reject a generic testing platform.**

## 9. Tail-at-scale methods: optimize completion of the whole job, not median calls

1. **Old idea [F].** Dean and Barroso's **The Tail at Scale (2013)** explains how fan-out amplifies rare latency outliers. It describes service classes, shallow queues, head-of-line reduction, delayed hedges, tied requests, and cancellation. Hedging after a delay controls duplicate work; indiscriminately duplicating every request is not the proposal. [CACM February 2013 paper](https://users.cs.utah.edu/~stutsman/cs6450/public/papers/tail-at-scale.pdf).

2. **Why not everywhere [F/I].** The paper's within-request replication examples exploit replicated, largely read-only data. **[I]** Paid model generation, shared provider bottlenecks, streaming responses, and irreversible tool actions weaken those assumptions. Cancelling a client future may not stop the provider's compute or billing. Tail tolerance was successful at hyperscalers; portability to arbitrary tool APIs is conditional.

3. **What flipped [F/I/E].** Cloudflare's observed 15× user-action crawling and long agent workflows increase machine-origin requests. **[E]** With independent 1% slow-call probability, the chance of at least one slow call is 1−0.99^n: 9.6% at ten calls, 63.4% at 100, and 99.996% at 1,000. Independence is an assumption; shared outages make it inappropriate. **[I]** The relevant measure is accepted end-to-end job completion under a cost budget.

4. **Oracle [I]: strong for simulated schedules and deterministic reads; weak for answer equivalence.** Use the same recorded latency/failure trace for baseline and candidate; measure p99 completion, useful work, cancellation latency, and billed work. Two plausible generated answers are not necessarily equivalent. Never hedge a non-idempotent effect without the effect contract in section 6.

5. **100× better model [I]: mixed, usually more valuable infrastructure.** Fewer reasoning steps reduce one source of fan-out; more useful parallel research or training raises it. A model cannot prevent a slow storage device, overloaded queue, or provider outage.

6. **Already doing it [F].** LLM serving is being rebuilt around the same broad resource-interference concerns. **DistServe (2024)** separates prefill/decode and reports up to 7.4× request capacity or 12.6× tighter SLO in its evaluations. **Splitwise (ISCA 2024)** reports 2.35× throughput at fixed cost/power in its studied designs. **llm-d 1.0 (2026-10-20)** includes multi-tenant flow control, cache-aware routing, disaggregation, and upgrade controls. These are workload-specific results, not general 10× industry demand estimates, and phase splitting is not identical to hedging. [DistServe](https://arxiv.org/abs/2401.09670); [Splitwise](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/publication/splitwise-efficient-generative-llm-inference-using-phase-splitting/); [Google/llm-d 1.0](https://cloud.google.com/blog/topics/ai-infrastructure/llm-d-1-0-an-open-source-stack-for-distributed-inference). **[I]** A new distributed inference engine is heavily contested. Application-wide deadline/cancellation propagation remains an integration hypothesis.

7. **Value [E].** Buyer: latency-sensitive research/search or eval platform. A service missing a five-minute SLA because one out of 100 children stalls may pay for reduced failed-job/retry costs. Price against completed tasks and extra paid inference; a 20% p99 improvement is worthless if it doubles cost without user value.

8. **Two-week proof [I/E].** TypeScript fan-out harness with 100 mock read-only tools, correlated and independent delays, root deadlines, bounded delayed hedging, and acknowledged cancellation. Compare no hedge, naive hedge, and budget-aware hedge. Target ≥30% lower p99 with ≤10% extra simulated paid work only where spare independent capacity exists; include a saturated case where hedging must turn off.

9. **Demo/tweet/kill [I/E].** Let 99 children finish and one stall; cancel or hedge according to a declared result contract, and show every attempt's cost. Tweet: “Your hundred-call agent inherits the slowest call. Give the whole task a deadline.” Expert killer: “All your alternatives share the same congested provider, and cancellation is fictional.” Kill if savings vanish under correlated load, answer quality falls, or real provider billing ignores cancellation.

## 10. CRDTs and local-first state: many writers can merge state without a central lock

1. **Old idea [F].** Shapiro, Preguiça, Baquero, and Zawirski's **2011 CRDT report** gives sufficient convergence conditions: state-based semilattice merges or suitably delivered commutative operations. Kleppmann, Wiggins, van Hardenberg, and McGranaghan's **local-first essay (2019)** argues for local ownership, offline work, and collaboration with servers in supporting roles. This is not a theorem that arbitrary concurrent programs merge correctly. [CRDT technical report, January 2011](https://dsf.berkeley.edu/cs286/papers/crdt-tr2011.pdf); [local-first, April/Onward! 2019](https://www.inkandswitch.com/essay/local-first/).

2. **Why niche for broad application state [F/I].** The 2019 authors document history growth, performance problems, permissions questions, and user-interface challenges. Automerge's **July 2025 v3** reports over 10× lower memory use by using compressed representations at runtime; its Moby Dick example falls from 700 MB to 1.3 MB. These are project benchmarks, not a guarantee for arbitrary histories. **[I]** Convergence alone cannot enforce exclusive ownership or a shared spending ceiling. [Automerge 3](https://automerge.org/blog/automerge-3/); [release chronology](https://automerge.org/blog/).

3. **What flipped [F/I/E].** IoT's billions of connections give a genuine disconnected/remote replica driver. Short-lived sandbox workers create another potential writer population, but this research found no comparable primary measurement demonstrating a 100× surge in concurrent CRDT editors. **[E]** Simulate 1,000 disposable workers contributing observations to a shared run document. **[I]** Monotone observations and independently keyed results fit better than agents concurrently rewriting the same source function.

4. **Oracle [I]: strong for convergence; weak for meaning.** Reorder, duplicate, delay, and partition operation delivery, then verify equal resulting state and preserved datatype invariants. Separately test application constraints. A converged document that contains two contradictory code edits has passed convergence and failed the user's task.

5. **100× better model [I]: mixed.** Strong agents can resolve semantic conflicts after merge, but do not strengthen CRDT convergence into transaction isolation. Better agents plus worktree isolation may make shared character-level editing unnecessary. This candidate can become less valuable if ordinary branches and explicit integration are sufficient.

6. **Already doing it [F].** Automerge already supplies the general sync engine and history tooling. Zed exposes multiple parallel agent paths and threads; that demonstrates multi-agent editor demand, not that its agents solve coordination by CRDT. **[I]** Do not present a generic collaborative agent workspace as missing infrastructure. [Automerge 3](https://automerge.org/blog/automerge-3/); [Zed agents, accessed 2026-10-28](https://zed.dev/docs/ai/agents). **[I] Narrow possible gap:** ephemeral-replica retirement, bounded history, and reviewed merges for structured agent work products. Deleting a replica's history while it may return is a real protocol question, not routine TTL cleanup.

7. **Value [E].** Buyer: collaborative editor vendor or edge/IoT application team. Likely an SDK feature or services engagement initially; $5k–$25k/year for a narrow synchronization integration is conceivable. No evidence establishes a standalone agent-CRDT market. A single-author report should remain single-author if that solves the problem.

8. **Two-week proof [I/E].** TypeScript/Automerge structured investigation document with independently keyed findings, explicit review states, and epoch-based replica retirement. Replay 1,000 synthetic short-lived actors and one late returner. Compare with append-only events plus a central database and with ordinary branch merge. Target convergence, bounded retained metadata after agreed retirement, and no silent acceptance of retired-writer edits.

9. **Demo/tweet/kill [I/E].** Partition half the workers; let both sides add findings, reconnect, then review contradictory claims explicitly. Tweet: “A thousand disconnected workers can merge observations. Agreement on the answer is still a separate step.” Expert killer: “An append-only table solves this; CRDTs add history and stale-permission problems.” Kill if central append-only state or worktrees meet the actual offline/latency needs. **Disposition: reject as the lead bet; direct scale-breakage evidence is weakest here.**

## Where the rebuilding is real, and where a gap is only a hypothesis

- **[F] Active rebuilds:** fair schedulers in Temporal/Restate; capacity leases in Inngest; snapshots and runtime reuse in Modal/E2B; agent identity in AWS/Auth0/Entra; object capabilities in Cap'n Web; budgeted macaroons in SatGate; incremental context in Materialize/Feldera; durable effects in workflow engines; richer lineage in OpenLineage; agent-assisted simulation and mutation testing in Antithesis; distributed inference in llm-d/DistServe/Splitwise. These are the dated primary sources in the candidate sections, not claims of equivalent feature completeness.
- **[I] No well-established complete solution found in this search:** crash-safe ownership closure across a run's mixed external resources; a portable conformance suite for joint descendant authority/budget/resource consumption; exact captured dependency invalidation across generated artifacts and permission changes. These are bounded search findings, not statements that nobody is doing them. Source limitations and adjacent competitors are recorded above.
- **[F/I] Most defensible unfilled boundary:** the documented cross-provider snapshot-retention issue plus application-owned cleanup in a current durable-sandbox beta. **[I]** It establishes an engineering seam to investigate. It does not establish a defensible company, and existing provider-native orchestration may close it quickly.
- **[I] Important distinctions:** admission controls starts; capabilities control authority; an effect journal controls replay; leases bound continued ownership; GC closes outstanding obligations; lineage identifies dependencies. One does not substitute for all the others. Shared durable state or a coherent composition of existing systems is usually necessary.

## Ranked top three

**1. Run-scoped resource ownership and collection across providers — candidate 2. [I]** Best combination of real scale, a concrete open failure report, strong bounded oracle, and a two-week proof. Sell cancellation/retention closure for two providers before broadening to all cloud assets. Largest risk: a small, easily internalized integration with insufficient budget or leakage pain.

**2. Root-task admission and reservation across heterogeneous resources — candidate 1. [I]** Strongest immediate pain evidence, including observed rate-limit failures. The market is crowded; differentiation must survive direct comparison with Temporal/Restate/Inngest plus LiteLLM/Bifrost/SatGate. Largest risk: ordinary configuration already solves it, or worst-case reservations waste too much capacity.

**3. Versioned artifact lineage with permission/deletion invalidation — candidate 7. [I]** More differentiated than another trace UI, with an oracle for deterministic captured pipelines. OpenLineage's current explicit-edge proposal shows the data-model boundary is still moving. Largest risk: incomplete capture or uncontrolled downstream copies makes the promised outcome unverifiable. Use conservative semantics and reject claims about hidden model reasoning.

## The ONE bet

**[I] Build a run-resource collector with a fault-tested ownership contract.** A cancelled or abandoned run should leave no unretained managed sandbox or snapshot after providers recover, and a stale worker should not be able to recreate one. Start with **machines plus snapshots across two provider contracts**. Integrate a workflow engine rather than replace it. Do not begin with universal IAM, a new queue, or a new VM runtime.

**[I] Minimal contract:** durably record create intent and a stable provider identity; associate resources with a run epoch; renew ownership leases; require mediated admission for every new creation; preserve explicit retention roots; reconcile ambiguous results; retry idempotent cleanup; and report unresolved obligations until provider inventory confirms deletion. Where a provider cannot reconcile creation identity, state the weaker guarantee and decline the strong contract. Revoking a run does not revoke third-party credentials unless those credentials are explicitly managed through the same boundary.

**[E] Two-week decision:** days 1–3 model the protocol and two provider contracts; days 4–7 implement controller, retained outputs, and crash recovery; days 8–10 build deterministic failure schedules and mutants; days 11–14 compare with existing lifecycle/TTL/finalizer approaches and produce a reproducible demo. Require zero erroneous deletion of retained fixtures, eventual collection under eventual provider availability, and rejection of old-epoch creates in the test model. Production claims require subsequent provider validation; this report did not perform it.

**[E] Commercial kill gate:** before committing beyond that proof, obtain three prospective operators' concrete lifecycle failures and cost/engineering estimates. Stop if ordinary TTLs/finalizers or provider-integrated sandboxes solve them, if no one will mediate creation through the collector, or if the buyer's expected savings cannot support even a $500/month service. The feature may still be worth contributing upstream.

## Five-line TL;DR

1. **[F/I]** A 375× increase in one provider's monthly sandbox starts makes machine lifecycle a different workload from provisioning a developer's laptop.

2. **[F]** Rate-limit failures, orphaned snapshots, and incomplete lineage are documented current problems; ubiquitous thousand-agent deployments are not established.

3. **[F/I]** Queues, macaroons, snapshots, durable execution, incremental views, and simulation already have serious modern implementations.

4. **[I]** The remaining bets sit at provider and ownership boundaries, with explicit invariants and narrow oracles.

5. **[I]** Bet on crash-safe run-resource collection; use deterministic simulation to try to kill it before selling it.

## Rejected ideas and why

- **[I] Generic fair agent queue:** Temporal, Restate, and Inngest already occupy it; only heterogeneous root-task reservations remain worth testing.
- **[I] Macaroons/budget tokens for agents as a standalone invention:** SatGate directly covers the proposed revival, and durable consumption still needs a ledger.
- **[I] Generic agent checkpoint/workflow platform:** Temporal, Restate, DBOS, LangGraph, and Inngest already provide the central machinery.
- **[I] Universal exactly-once tool calls:** unsupported without a cooperative sink or a reconcilable business identity; journaling does not repair the external commit gap.
- **[I] New sandbox snapshot/dedup cloud:** Modal/E2B and related runtimes are already rebuilding it; portable retention is the narrower seam.
- **[I] New streaming SQL/context engine:** Materialize/Feldera already implement the hard primitive; use their engines and test a specific invalidation integration.
- **[I] Generic agent observability/lineage graph:** existing tracing and catalogs are substantial; only a testable invalidation outcome distinguishes the proposed wedge.
- **[I] Generic agent-driven deterministic testing platform:** Antithesis already has both the agent harness loop and mutation testing; use it or build a bounded domain suite.
- **[I] Another distributed inference engine:** llm-d and the serving research ecosystem are actively implementing the resource/tail techniques; a two-week general competitor is implausible.
- **[I] CRDTs as the default answer to parallel coding:** convergence does not preserve program meaning, and direct evidence of a new widespread scaling failure is weak.
- **[F/I] Consensus or sharding as a new standalone bet:** Inngest already documents separating constraint enforcement and moving its queue toward FoundationDB; the primitives are essential but do not identify a new buyer problem by themselves. [Inngest, 2026-02-23](https://www.inngest.com/blog/announcing-the-constraint-api).
- **[I] Global “IoT/agents will be 1,000× larger” market thesis:** the sourced populations and growth rates are heterogeneous; use observed workloads and labelled stress scenarios instead.
