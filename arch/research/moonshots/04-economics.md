# Economics, law and societies of agent-computers

Research date: **2026-10-27**. Lens: economic institutions for portable, stateful computers. Written against [the moonshots brief](BRIEF.md); only this report was added to the repository. No deployments, transactions, purchases, contacts or commits were made.

**Labels:** **F** = sourced fact, including attributed vendor claims; **M** = measurement or observation in an explicitly described experiment; **I** = inference, proposal or judgment; **E** = estimate or proposed experimental threshold. All web sources were inspected through browsing on **2026-10-27**; publication dates or documentation dates appear beside links where available. Proposed proofs and tweets describe future experiments, not completed products. “100×” describes ambition, not a measured improvement.

**F — Retrieval scope:** evidence includes publisher pages, repository text and indexed excerpts of primary sources. ## The opportunity: make economic work carry its evidence

**I — Bet on a computation receipt that someone else can execute.** A buyer receives an answer, the mandate under which it was produced, the paid inputs, an execution journal, and a compatible parked computer. The buyer can inspect the work, continue it, or submit it to an independent verifier without the original vendor's account. This becomes the common evidence layer for outsourced work, escrow, warranties and disputes. Stronger models increase the amount and consequence of delegated work, increasing the value of this layer.

**I — The larger vision is an economy of accountable continuations:** billions of programs can subcontract, move between suppliers, spend bounded funds, produce deliverables, pool resources, and transfer stewardship while retaining an intelligible history. Their economic identity is a principal plus a mandate plus a lineage of execution, not an arbitrary wallet address. The near-term customer buys fewer disputed invoices and safer delegation; the moonshot is a portable institutional substrate for machine commerce.

**F — This is a continuation of old work, not a newly discovered analogy.** Miller and Drexler's [*Markets and Computation: Agoric Open Systems* (1988)](https://papers.agoric.com/papers/markets-and-computation-agoric-open-systems/full-text/) proposed objects owning and trading resources, paying suppliers, and charging customers. Section 7 identifies weak immediate demand and accounting/negotiation overhead at small scale as obstacles. **I:** today's change is a plausible demand shock from useful generated programs, plus cheap state transport and HTTP payment infrastructure. Whether the transaction costs now fall far enough is the central experiment, not an established fact.

**F — Agoric also remains direct implementation lineage.** Its current [xsnap API](https://github.com/Agoric/agoric-sdk/blob/master/packages/xsnap/README.md) saves resumable XS JavaScript workers; [Zoe's offer-safety design (documentation dated 2022-08-25)](https://docs.agoric.com/guides/zoe/offer-safety) separates asset custody from contract logic. **I:** acknowledge this unusually close combination of JavaScript execution, snapshots, accounting and contracts. Sofort's candidate contribution is an independently usable browser/server/edge work artifact and its economic semantics, not “Agoric, invented again.”

## The economic constitution that every idea needs

**I — Copyable state and scarce rights require different machinery.** A snapshot can contain a budget display, receipt history, signed mandate and logical grant references. The spendable balance and current authorization must be enforced by a trusted broker or settlement ledger outside that snapshot. A copied private key remains the same private key; a signed balance becomes stale; a content hash identifies bytes, not exclusive ownership.

**I — Five invariants:**

1. **Conservation:** for a funded root mandate, `unallocated + allocated-unspent + reserved + spent = funding`, with disjoint categories, integer units and explicit refunds. Creating a child transfers allocation; it never mints money. Aggregate root caps include model calls, unsuccessful branches, verification and broker fees.
2. **Effect identity:** persist a logical economic operation and its request digest before dispatch. A retry uses that identity; an intentional new purchase gets another. Reusing an ID with changed amount/payee/request fails. A fork inherits completed history, not permission to redispatch a pending payment.
3. **Authority fencing:** migration or stewardship transfer increments a broker-side epoch and renews capabilities. Old copies can compute locally but cannot cause new brokered effects. Revocation is checked at the effect boundary. Already dispatched effects cannot simply be recalled.
4. **Evidence scope:** a signature authenticates a signer's assertion; a timestamp supports existence before a time; deterministic replay checks consistency with specified inputs. Neither proves those inputs were true, a model was correct, or an external service performed its promised real-world action.
5. **Liability and exit:** identify the actual principal, counterparty, mandate scope, expiry, dispute procedure and allocation of losses. A program's failure must not create an ownerless obligation. A new controller does not automatically acquire non-transferable licences or release the former controller's liabilities.

**F — Borrow the bookkeeping rather than inventing token balances.** TigerBeetle's [immutable transfers](https://docs.tigerbeetle.com/reference/transfer/) and [two-phase transfers](https://docs.tigerbeetle.com/coding/two-phase-transfers/) distinguish reservation from posting/voiding; corrections add records. **I:** implement the small prototype in a transactional SQLite-backed Durable Object, with balanced entries for each economic event. Cloudflare documents [transactional, strongly consistent storage (updated 2026-09-21)](https://developers.cloudflare.com/durable-objects/api/sqlite-storage-api/). This does not make a transaction spanning a payment provider atomic: use an outbox, stable operation IDs and reconciliation; hold uncertain amounts until resolved. A local timeout is not evidence that a remote charge failed.

### A small falsification experiment

**M — New local experiment:** `/tmp/moonshot-economics/ledger-probe.ts`, run with Node **v24.22.1**; results in `/tmp/moonshot-economics/results.json`. Reproduce with `node /tmp/moonshot-economics/ledger-probe.ts`. These scratch files are small and outside the repository.

- One hundred copied records each showed a budget of 10 units and spent 6: **600 units** of admitted spending.
- One shared authoritative ledger admitted **one** 6-unit reservation/post and rejected **99** others, leaving 4 units.
- One hundred retries of the same operation consumed 6 units once; a changed amount under that ID was rejected. An old ownership epoch was rejected. An unresolved 6-unit hold blocked another 6-unit request.
- **1,000 seeded traces × 100 operations** preserved the conservation assertion after each attempted reserve/post/void.

**I — Result:** a portable economic actor needs portable evidence and externally conserved authority. **M — Scope:** this is a single-process integer state-machine model, not a Wasm, distributed concurrency, durable crash recovery, payment or security test. It decides the architecture question; it does not qualify its implementation.

### What existing payments already solve

**F — Cloudflare Pay Per Crawl** launched in private beta on [2025-07-01](https://developers.cloudflare.com/changelog/post/2025-07-01-pay-per-crawl/). Its current [crawler flow (updated 2026-04-23)](https://developers.cloudflare.com/ai-crawl-control/features/pay-per-crawl/use-pay-per-crawl-as-ai-owner/crawl-pages/) uses `402`, `crawler-price`, signed price-acceptance headers and `crawler-charged`. This is a specific authenticated crawler/billing arrangement, not every possible HTTP 402 exchange.

**F — x402 v2** defines payment requirements, authorization, verification and settlement; its current [core specification](https://github.com/x402-foundation/x402/blob/main/specs/x402-specification-v2.md) includes authorization/upfront/escrow flows and explicitly excludes client budget management. Its [HTTP transport](https://github.com/x402-foundation/x402/blob/main/specs/transports-v2/http.md) is separate. **I:** do not pitch escrow or replay protection as missing from x402. The proposed addition is task-level acceptance and reproducible evidence linked to the purchase, beyond moving funds.

**F — Stripe and Tempo introduced MPP on [2026-03-18](https://stripe.com/blog/machine-payments-protocol), including Stripe PaymentIntents integration. Cloudflare's [Agents payment docs (updated 2026-08-05)](https://developers.cloudflare.com/agents/tools/payments/) support x402 and MPP; [MPP uses payment challenges, credentials and receipts](https://developers.cloudflare.com/agents/tools/payments/mpp/). AWS also documents [payment sessions with optional spending budgets](https://docs.aws.amazon.com/bedrock-agentcore/latest/devguide/payments-process-payment.html). **I:** use these as rails. “An agent bought an API call” is already a weak standalone demo.

**F — Signed agent mandates also have current prior art.** The inspected [AP2 specification](https://github.com/google-agentic-commerce/AP2/blob/main/docs/ap2/specification.md) defines Checkout and Payment Mandates bound to checkout data, with a trusted authorization surface and signed receipts. **I:** integrate existing mandate formats where applicable; do not re-pitch signed purchase authorization. The additional research questions are how authority changes when an actual continuation forks or transfers, and how its disclosed execution history supports a work-level dispute. A protocol mandate is not automatically a legally sufficient power of attorney.

## 1. Notarised computation: an invoice you can run

**I — One sentence:** sell work with a portable evidence package that a buyer-selected verifier can replay. **100× version:** every consequential machine-produced deliverable arrives with an executable account of its production, forming a common evidence language across vendors, auditors, procurement systems and dispute services.

**F — Lineage:** [RFC 3161 (August 2001)](https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc3161) specifies timestamp assertions about data's prior existence; [W3C PROV (2013-04-30)](https://www.w3.org/TR/prov-overview/) models provenance; the project's [landscape](../v8/02-landscape.md) documents durable journals and snapshot prior art. **I — What limited the old combination:** timestamps and provenance records do not execute the disputed procedure; workflow logs often depend on the producing service. A constrained tool-only runtime makes a compact independent replay artifact feasible. This is a product hypothesis about portability, not a claim that reproducible computation is new.

**I — Evidence contract:** include source/bootstrap/module hashes, initial deterministic environment, ordered tool requests/replies, output digest, mandate and broker signatures, payment receipt references, checkpoint roots and verifier instructions. Replay from the initial state verifies the whole disclosed trace. Starting from a signed intermediate checkpoint verifies only the suffix and trusts the checkpoint issuer for the prefix. A seller-supplied arbitrary heap cannot establish that the signed source produced it. Keep model inference as recorded external input: reproducing the orchestration does not reproduce or validate the model's hidden computation.

**I — Value and model scaling:** a procurement buyer pays to resolve “what work did we purchase?”; an agent vendor pays to ship defensible evidence; an auditor depends on evidence export beyond vendor retention. More capable models create longer, higher-value execution chains and more supplier boundaries. The demand survives improvements in answer quality because attribution, input authenticity and authorization remain separate questions.

**E — First proof, 8–10 working days:** take a bounded invoice-reconciliation agent with fixture invoices and an explicitly labelled sandbox paid-rate endpoint. Reuse a compatible QuickJS/Wasm module. Capture one genuine parked continuation with a closure; preserve the entire journal from a known initial state. Produce a signed manifest with a separately operated verifier key. A Node CLI and browser verifier recompute requests/output without outbound tools. Bind the final checkpoint hash to the replayed trace; in the same pinned host also compare checkpoint state where deterministic byte equality is qualified. Corrupt an invoice byte, substitute a tool response, and splice in an unrelated checkpoint: each must fail at its corresponding verification boundary. Record total artifact bytes and full verification time, not warm copy time. No customer data or live payment is needed.

**I — 60-second demo:** seller produces an invoice result; download the running-state package; destroy seller process; an independent verifier replays it with zero external calls; tamper with one receipt and get a precise mismatch; restore the verified final continuation elsewhere. Caption the local/hosted environments and signer trust. “Notarised” means a technical witness service in this prototype, not a legally commissioned notarial act.

**I — Draft tweet, publish only after the experiment:** “This agent's invoice comes with a runnable receipt: its program, recorded inputs, mandate and parked state. A separate verifier replays the work without calling the tools again. Change a receipt and verification fails. QuickJS/Wasm prototype; sandbox payments.”

**E — Kill test:** give three external integration engineers the capsule package and a source-plus-signed-journal baseline. If the capsule saves none of them meaningful verification/continuation integration work, and they prefer the smaller baseline, abandon the capsule-specific notary product. A hash chain alone is not a win.

## 2. Fork firms: an agent hires copies of its current self

**I — One sentence:** a working agent can capitalize narrowly mandated copies of its current continuation, buy useful subresults, and close each child's books. **100× version:** ephemeral firms assemble around problems, preserve their work in progress, choose between internal production and outside suppliers, and return residual funds and evidence to their owners.

**F — Lineage:** agoric systems explicitly contemplated service-trading objects; [Coase's *The Nature of the Firm* (November 1937)](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1468-0335.1937.tb00002.x) explains firms through the costs of using markets versus internal coordination. **I — Lesson:** do not hold an auction for every function call. Put tightly coupled work under a shared mandate, and use markets at supplier boundaries. Historical computational-market overhead remains a reason to reject this design for cheap homogeneous tasks. Agents now offer useful task decomposition; snapshots can preserve expensive shared preparation rather than reconstruct it in every child.

**F — Even funded forks are prior art:** [Spawn (February 1992), §I.C](https://www.waldspurger.org/carl/papers/spawn-ieee-tse-feb92.pdf), describes a sponsorship hierarchy and conservation of funding across process forks. **I:** the proposed experiment combines that principle with portable agent continuations and independently usable receipts; neither recursive funding nor budget conservation is a new invention.

**I — Mechanism:** at a quiescent boundary, fork the actual heap, then give each child a distinct task input and fresh broker grant. Transfer child budgets out of the parent's available allocation. Children cannot assign themselves funds. Work receipts distinguish purchased results from internal transfers. If all children have one human/company owner, “hiring itself” is cost allocation, not new external revenue. Clones share failure modes and are not independent reviewers.

**I — Value and model scaling:** a software team buys bounded parallel investigation or migration planning; a research operator buys exploration that preserves paid preparation. Stronger models can discover more useful decompositions and decide when an extra branch has positive expected value. They also make recursion more expensive when unconstrained. Use a broker-enforced depth/fan-out/total-spend cap and require a cost comparison to fresh tasks with shared inputs.

**E — First proof, 1–2 weeks:** a coordinator analyzes a fixture supplier catalogue, reaches a real tool await, and forks three continuations to investigate different products. Start with 100 internal test credits; transfer 20 to each child and retain 40 at the parent. Meter all inference/tool/verification expenses and return unused allocations. One child tries to exceed its grant; another retries a paid call after a simulated lost acknowledgment. Show that the parent-plus-child ledger remains conserved and the merchant records one charge for the retry. Publish cold-start tasks plus shared-source/history as the baseline; measure actual saved preparation, artifact transport and total quality-adjusted cost. Test credits and fixture merchant must be labelled.

**I — 75-second demo:** a coordinator “incorporates” three copies from one checkpoint; each resumes with its own assignment and purse; one gets its card declined; the winner returns a result and receipt; the firm dissolves and refunds leftovers. The chart on screen is the ledger's actual transfers, not an animated guess.

**I — Draft tweet:** “My agent hired three copies of its paused program. Each inherited its work, got a separate task and a capped slice of the same budget. One overspent and was refused. Results come with receipts; unused funds return. Internal test credits, real continuation forks.”

**E — Kill test:** on a preregistered task set, true continuation forks produce no preparation-cost or completion-time advantage over fresh tasks with shared context at equal result quality. Then this is an agent scheduler with accounting; demote the capsule story.

## 3. Executable work orders: escrow pays for an accepted result

**I — One sentence:** a buyer escrows payment against a signed work order whose deliverable, acceptance rule and dispute evidence are executable. **100× version:** agents buy entire chains of subcontracted work across unknown vendors, with payment released by agreed predicates and portable evidence rather than bilateral SaaS integrations.

**F — Lineage:** [Szabo's *Formalizing and Securing Relationships on Public Networks* (1997-09-01)](https://firstmonday.org/ojs/index.php/fm/article/view/548) connects contracting protocols to legal and security principles. [Grigg's *The Ricardian Contract* (2004)](https://iang.org/papers/ricardian_contract.html) binds human-readable contractual meaning and machine-readable terms through a signed document identifier. [Zoe](https://docs.agoric.com/guides/zoe/offer-enforcement) keeps custody outside contract logic. **I — What stalled generalization:** a safe exchange of digital assets does not determine whether research, a repair or an external action is good. Oracles and incomplete specifications remain the hard part. Replaying a constrained computation helps with a useful subset of that problem.

**I — Mechanism:** sign one bundle containing prose terms, typed parameters, acceptance-code hash, verifier version, deadline, evidence-access rights and dispute procedure; specify precedence when prose and code disagree. For the first product, accept objective transformations of committed inputs: a balanced reconciliation table, a schema migration against pinned fixtures, or a verified extraction with source offsets. Release against a verifier's receipt. A seller can exploit a weak acceptance test; model-written prose and model-written tests are not independent protection.

**I — Value and model scaling:** automation platforms and buyers of agent services pay for fewer integrations and disputes. Stronger models increase the range of work and the ability to draft adapters, but also increase test gaming. Keep acceptance deterministic for automatic settlement; subjective quality goes to a named adjudicator or human. This is above x402/MPP, whose funds-flow support should be reused, including existing escrow mechanisms where available.

**E — First proof, 7–10 working days:** two Workers represent buyer and seller; one DO maintains test-credit escrow and reservation entries. A Ricardian-style work order purchases reconciliation of a committed invoice batch. The seller supplies the replay package from idea 1. A verifier independently checks the agreed reconciliation predicates and trace before settlement. Run valid delivery, malformed output, late delivery and disagreement between deadline and delivery receipt. Freeze a disputed order rather than silently selecting the platform's interpretation. Crash after release request/before acknowledgment and reconcile via one operation ID. External payment wiring is optional; the work-order semantics are the experiment.

**I — 60-second demo:** buyer funds a work order, malicious seller submits a fluent but unbalanced answer and receives no release, a valid seller delivers verifiable work and is paid, independent viewer replays why. Explicitly label the acceptance rule's narrow scope.

**I — Draft tweet:** “An agent bought invoice reconciliation under an executable work order. The seller's answer sounded right but didn't balance, so escrow stayed locked. The corrected result replayed and passed the agreed test. Prototype with test credits; the acceptance code is public.”

**E — Kill test:** in a blind adversarial set of 20 outputs, the preregistered acceptance rule releases funds for even one materially invalid deliverable. Drop automatic settlement for that task class; evidence-assisted adjudication may still survive.

## 4. A spot exchange for work already in progress

**I — One sentence:** a capsule buys its next bounded execution slice from eligible hosts and moves when a better offer exceeds its migration cost. **100× version:** ongoing agent work becomes portable demand that can route among capacity suppliers, with an execution history independent of any supplier's account.

**F — Lineage:** [Spawn (February 1992)](https://www.waldspurger.org/carl/papers/spawn-ieee-tse-feb92.pdf) implemented a computational economy on workstations and studied allocation, price dynamics and scaling. [Vickrey (March 1961)](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1540-6261.1961.tb02789.x) supplies auction foundations. Current [EC2 Spot interruption notices](https://docs.aws.amazon.com/AWSEC2/latest/UserGuide/spot-instance-termination-notices.html) are best-effort, with a two-minute warning for stop/termination, not for immediate hibernation. **I — Historical limit:** allocating remote tasks is easier than transferring arbitrary work in progress safely. Portable checkpoints narrow the compatibility problem; they do not eliminate it or create a liquid capacity market.

**I — Mechanism:** auction a fixed, metered unit of eligible execution, with runtime hash, isolation requirement, locality/data constraints, memory/fuel ceiling and completion deadline. A buyer-announced maximum plus independently submitted host asks is sufficient for a prototype. A reverse second-price auction is defensible only under its restricted independent-cost, single-parameter assumptions; related hosts, fake bids, quality differences and correlated interruptions break the simple story. [Myerson–Satterthwaite (April 1983)](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/0022053183900480) is a reminder that private-information trade cannot generally promise every desirable efficiency/incentive/budget property simultaneously.

**I — Value and model scaling:** compute brokers and batch-agent operators pay if portable progress makes interrupted cheap capacity usable. Stronger models improve workload partitioning and the value of state already accumulated. But if the real bill is dominated by remote inference, shaving interpreter CPU has little value. A more credible first market is scarce tool access or data-local execution, not tiny glue CPU alone. Browser suppliers can offer reproducible computations on public data; they cannot be trusted with confidential plaintext or attest their own honesty.

**E — First proof, 1–2 weeks:** run a registry Worker, a clearing DO, and three real local hosts (Node, browser and workerd), explicitly displaying synthetic offered prices. Rebind the same pinned capsule at a tool boundary; force one host interruption; fence the old owner. Exclude destinations whose grants/data rules do not permit the move. Compare migration against finishing locally and ordinary replay recovery. Measure complete transfer, decompression, verification and resumption, with a multi-megabyte image. A hosted Worker adapter is a separate deployment gate.

**I/E — Migration decision:** move only if expected remaining savings exceed transfer/checkpoint/verification cost, expected interruption loss and an uncertainty margin. At **3 MB and 10 Mbit/s**, payload transfer alone is **2.4 s**; those are illustrative inputs, not new measurements. That is unattractive for milliseconds of glue and potentially useful for valuable long-lived state or data access.

**I — 60-second demo:** three actual hosts bid; one becomes unavailable; the paused computer moves to the next eligible host, continues its calculation and declines a cheaper host in a disallowed location. The screen labels prices as synthetic and runtime migration as real.

**I — Draft tweet:** “This paused program can change compute suppliers without discarding its work. Three local hosts offer execution; it moves after an interruption and refuses an ineligible destination. Portable QuickJS/Wasm state, fenced tool authority, synthetic auction prices.”

**E — Kill test:** after full migration and risk costs, no representative workload beats the best fixed eligible host by at least 20% at equal completion reliability. Demote the open spot exchange; migration can remain a resilience feature.

## 5. Licensed knowledge supply chains, with receipts attached to the answer

**I — One sentence:** agents buy data under explicit terms and carry access receipts and provenance into every derived work order. **100× version:** an answer can fund a network of specialist data suppliers, disclose which paid sources supported it, and be audited or recomputed without confusing access, redistribution and training rights.

**F — Lineage:** Cloudflare's [Pay Per Crawl request/charge protocol](https://developers.cloudflare.com/ai-crawl-control/features/pay-per-crawl/use-pay-per-crawl-as-ai-owner/crawl-pages/), Grigg's signed-contract identifiers and W3C PROV provide existing pieces. **I — What remains unsolved:** a charge proves neither content quality nor comprehensive usage rights. Once plaintext enters a buyer's memory, a copyable capsule cannot prevent copying it. The change is an economical way to retain the agreed terms, paid inputs and derivation trace with continuing work; this is evidence and enforcement at trusted boundaries, not universal DRM.

**I — Mechanism:** bind provider, content digest, terms digest, receipt, permitted use/retention and audit-access conditions to each tool result. Store large licensed bodies in access-controlled blobs, with the guest holding minimal windows or handles. Forking may require a new licence grant; sharing an old receipt is not automatically another right of use. Never advertise paid crawling as general permission to redistribute or train. Contradictory or unrecognized terms cause a human escalation or a different supplier, rather than a model inventing legal compatibility.

**I — Value and model scaling:** premium datasets and internal knowledge owners depend on attribution and usage accounting; enterprise research buyers pay for provenance on purchased inputs. More capable models should find more valuable specialized sources and produce more derivatives. The market disappears if free substitutes provide equal value; no payment header fixes that.

**E — First proof, 1–2 weeks:** three fixture publishers serve versioned documents and explicit demo licences through a paid Worker endpoint. Use an actual x402 or MPP sandbox adapter where accessible; label a mock adapter otherwise. A research capsule chooses two sources, parks, moves, and completes a sourced answer. Its verifier checks document digests, allowed derivations and receipt references. Fork it into a licence-ineligible redistribution task and show the trusted export broker refusing the output; do not claim it can stop an operator reading memory. Actual Cloudflare Pay Per Crawl billing integration is not a prerequisite and must not be implied by the demo.

**I — 60-second demo:** a question buys two useful documents; the answer unfolds into a source/payment/terms trail; a fork tries to publish an unlicensed full source and the broker refuses. Show that source truth remains outside replay's guarantee.

**I — Draft tweet:** “My research agent's answer includes the data receipts, content hashes and agreed terms behind it. Move the computation and the provenance comes too. A redistribution fork needs its own authority. Prototype publishers and sandbox payments; no claim to prevent copying plaintext.”

**E — Kill test:** in a blinded paid-versus-free comparison, purchased sources add no buyer-valued improvement sufficient to cover acquisition and governance cost. Drop the marketplace; retain provenance as a feature.

## 6. Underwrite the action, then use replay to settle the claim

**I — One sentence:** quote a bounded warranty for a specified agent action, attach its scope to the capsule, and evaluate a claim from committed inputs and signed receipts. **100× version:** a broad market prices narrow risks of delegated actions, making responsibility a purchasable component of an agent work order.

**F — Lineage:** AI performance insurance already exists: Munich Re describes [aiSure and its risk assessment](https://www.munichre.com/en/solutions/for-industry-clients/insure-ai.html); Mosaic's [2026-02-26 announcement](https://www.mosaicinsurance.com/resources/press-releases/~/mosaic-partners-with-munich-res-aisure-to-provide-pioneering-coverage-for-ai-vendors/) describes cover for defined performance failures and measurable performance data. **I — What remains difficult:** reproducible evidence can lower investigation cost but does not supply loss-frequency data, causal attribution or capital. An arbitrary agent cannot be actuarially priced merely by hashing its code. Existing insurers are partners or competitors, not proof of an empty market.

**I — Mechanism:** restrict the first promise to an observable loss, such as a duplicate charge by the defined broker, or a transformation failing a committed postcondition. Bind the coverage to program/build, tool versions, permitted action class, maximum exposure and period. Requiring the same broker can genuinely bound covered outflows; it cannot bound every downstream economic or reputational harm. Price by model/tool/policy cohort, not as if forks were independent draws. A model upgrade or shared tool failure can hit the entire book simultaneously.

**I — Value and model scaling:** agent vendors buy warranties that shorten a customer's decision to delegate; insurers buy evidence that reduces assessment and claims expense. Better models may reduce error rates while increasing exposure and task scope. That can expand insured value, but not necessarily premiums or profits. This idea ranks below infrastructure because distribution, licensed risk carriers and credible loss data matter more than the runtime.

**E — First proof, 1–2 weeks:** implement a **simulated warranty reserve**, not an insurance sale. Commit fixture work orders and a coverage rule; quote using openly declared scenario frequencies; inject defined broker failures; submit signed evidence; use an independent verifier to approve or reject payouts. Run a common-mode outage across all copies. Track peak reserve shortfall and false claims, rather than displaying invented actuarial accuracy.

**E — Illustrative arithmetic:** if a precisely defined event has assumed probability 0.001 and capped loss $100, expected indemnity is $0.10 before investigation, expenses, capital and margin. These inputs are assumptions, not a premium recommendation. Ten thousand highly correlated copies can create up to $1 million of nominal exposure; treating them as a diversified pool would be misleading.

**I — 60-second demo:** an agent chooses a safer bounded policy; a displayed simulated warranty covers a specified duplicate-charge failure; a fault produces a replayable claim and reserve debit; a non-covered bad answer is rejected with the exact exclusion. Show the correlated-failure scenario breaking a naive reserve.

**I — Draft tweet:** “Can a delegated action carry a warranty you can inspect? This prototype binds a narrow coverage rule to an agent's program and receipts, then replays a claim. Simulated reserves and injected failures—not a live insurance product. Correlated forks count as one risk cohort.”

**E — Kill test:** in a blinded claims set, the evidence cannot distinguish covered from excluded events better or more cheaply than ordinary structured broker logs. Then the capsule has no underwriting advantage; do not build a risk carrier around it.

## 7. Transferable mandates: sell, hand over, or inherit work in progress

**I — One sentence:** transfer stewardship of a running procedure through a signed handover that carries its state and evidence while reissuing its actual authority. **100× version:** useful agent operations can outlive a device, vendor, employee or owner and be assigned or succeeded under intelligible rules, instead of dying with an API account.

**F — Lineage and legal boundary:** US [15 USC §7001(h), E-SIGN (2000)](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/15/7001) recognizes electronic-agent involvement subject to legal attribution to the bound person. [UNCITRAL's Model Law on Automated Contracting, adopted 2024-07-11](https://uncitral.un.org/en/mlac), supplies model rules for automated contracting; a model law is not itself universally enacted law. German [BGB §§164–168](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/englisch_bgb/englisch_bgb.html) addresses agency/authority; [§1922](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/bgb/__1922.html) addresses succession to an estate. **I:** these are reasons to represent a real principal and explicit authority, not to declare a software process a legal person. Do not assume the rules for a human representative map directly to software.

**I — What remains difficult:** possession of a file does not convey title to data, a financial account, contractual obligations, a licence or power of attorney. A timeout does not establish death or lawful succession. The first useful version is a company handing a long-running operational task to another authorized operator. Selling a capsule may sell a licensed copy or an exclusive right to brokered continuation; it cannot make already disclosed information exclusive.

**I — Value and model scaling:** enterprises pay to preserve operations through staff/vendor changes; buyers of bespoke automations pay for handover without reverse-engineering hidden state; estate software could later depend on continuity following independently established succession. Stronger models accumulate more useful specialized procedures, relationships and pending work, increasing handover value. A durable file format still requires an engine-retention and migration policy; “runs for decades” cannot be inferred from today's Wasm support.

**E — First proof, 1–2 weeks:** two authenticated principals exchange a fixture procurement task paused before final action. The old principal signs an assignment proposal; the new principal and fixture counterparty accept; the registry records the transition, holds unsettled obligations and increments the epoch. Reissue eligible grants and leave non-transferable ones suspended. Restore the original copy and show a new purchase refused. A succession simulation requires an explicitly labelled executor authorization fixture, never just a dead-man timer. Use test credits and no actual inheritance or legal transfer.

**I — 60-second demo:** hand over a half-finished task, keep its closures and receipts, replace its spending mandate, and attempt an unauthorized action from the seller's retained copy. The transfer fails safely when a required counterparty has not consented.

**I — Draft tweet:** “I handed over a paused automation with its unfinished work and receipts. Its new owner got fresh authority; the retained copy could compute but couldn't spend through the broker. Test principals, simulated assignment. Owning bytes doesn't by itself transfer legal rights.”

**E — Kill test:** on three genuine enterprise handover scenarios, exporting application state plus ordinary credential rotation is no harder than transferring the capsule. Demote portable mandates to an export feature; avoid the much harder estate/personhood business.

## 8. Agent guilds and governed commons that clones cannot capture

**I — One sentence:** organizations pool agent budgets, tools and reusable work under a charter whose resource rules, sanctions and appeals are enforced by explicit institutions. **100× version:** durable machine-assisted cooperatives maintain shared knowledge and infrastructure, admit accountable participants, purchase work and adapt their rules without turning every interaction into a market.

**F — Lineage:** [Ostrom's Nobel lecture slides (2009-12-08)](https://www.nobelprize.org/uploads/2018/06/ostrom-lecture-slides.pdf) identify boundaries, locally fitted rules, monitoring, graduated sanctions, conflict resolution and nested organization. [Douceur's *The Sybil Attack* (2002)](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/wp-content/uploads/2002/01/IPTPS2002.pdf) explains why many apparent identities cannot establish independent participants. **I — Historical lesson:** rules need identifiable membership, monitoring and legitimate conflict resolution; a token or vote button does not supply them. Agents reduce monitoring/administrative work but make identity multiplication almost free. This worsens, rather than solves, the admission problem.

**I — Mechanism:** a guild of actual principals owns a paid-data/compute pool and a versioned charter. Agents are delegates; forks inherit lineage and share their principal's quotas and voting entitlement. Record reputation as scoped evidence—task class, policy/model version, independent customer, success/failure denominator, disputes and expiry—not a scalar that every copy inherits as fresh credit. Aggregate related-party transactions and charge a real membership/bond cost; a bond alone does not prove distinct ownership. “Three verifiers” means three approved independent principals/operators, not three forks of one model.

**I — Value and model scaling:** an open-source foundation, professional association or small enterprise consortium depends on pooled maintenance and purchasing. Useful agents can propose work, buy narrow specialist services and retain unfinished maintenance between funding cycles. Better models make monitoring and rule proposals cheaper; legitimate rule-making remains with members. Knowledge is non-rival, while inference capacity, source licences and member attention are scarce: govern each differently. A central firm may still coordinate better; test that alternative.

**E — First proof, 1–2 weeks:** three fixture member principals share a maintenance/data budget. A charter stored by hash defines quotas, evidence requirements, a quorum for policy changes, staged suspension and an appeal. Launch 100 real continuation branches for one member and show they obtain neither 100 votes nor 100 allocations. A member proposes work; two others approve a bounded work order; a purchased result enters the common library; its source licence determines whether it may be shared. A monitor flags misuse, suspends the relevant grant, and an appeal leaves an append-only correction. Reputation uses fixture outcomes labelled synthetic, never manufactured customer traction.

**I — 75-second demo:** an agent attempts a clone takeover; the principals' allocation and vote totals remain unchanged; members approve a useful shared repair; an incorrect sanction is appealed and reversed without erasing history. Measure the paid maintenance result as well as the governance spectacle.

**I — Draft tweet:** “A hundred forks tried to claim a hundred shares of this agent commons. They got one member's quota. The prototype ties votes, budgets and reputation to accountable principals, then uses agents to do and document shared maintenance. Fixture members; real forked continuations.”

**E — Kill test:** in a controlled repeated-task exercise, the charter consumes more human governance time per accepted deliverable than one accountable maintainer with quotas, without improving outcomes or member control. Keep pooled billing; drop the autonomous-society claim.

## Cross-cutting pressure tests

**I — Replay is a witness, not omniscience.** Its trust ladder is: artifact integrity; signer identity; replay consistency; authenticated external inputs; evidence of contemporaneous authorization; task-specific correctness; legal enforceability. Each step needs extra evidence. A perfectly replayed lie is still a lie. A browser-controlled signer cannot attest facts about its own honest execution. External tool receipts should bind the operation and response digests, not merely say “200 OK.”

**F — There is a legal evidence lineage worth integrating, without claiming its status.** EU [Regulation 2024/1183, 2024-04-11, Articles 45k–45l](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2024/1183/oj/eng) addresses electronic-ledger evidence and qualified-ledger presumptions for ordering and integrity, with qualified trust-service-provider requirements. **I:** a DO hash chain is not thereby a qualified ledger, and such presumptions do not establish the truth or quality of recorded work. An existing timestamp/trust-service provider is a later integration, not a title to put on a homemade signer.

**I — Do not rewind the money with the computer.** Restoring a pre-purchase snapshot must not restore its spending balance or erase an obligation. The authoritative broker journal remains monotonic even when guest execution branches. Disaster recovery/PITR for the ledger must reconcile external settlement history before reopening spending; otherwise a beautifully durable application can resurrect already-spent funds. A DO per firm is a feasible small prototype, not a claim of account-wide atomicity or billion-agent throughput. Cross-firm reservations require coordinated settlement.

**I — The host is part of the evidence trust model.** Keep secrets outside snapshots; verify content, provenance and compatibility before restoration; treat browser-provided heaps as hostile; cap raw and compressed bytes and work. Frees need not erase old plaintext from a linear-memory image. Sensitive replay packages need encryption, controlled disclosure and an agreed retention policy; a redacted artifact proves only what the disclosed evidence supports. An honest-host replay prototype does not provide confidential computing, zero-knowledge proofs or hardware attestation.

**I — Improved models remove neither scarcity nor institutional choices.** They may reduce error rates and automate integration, shrinking some notary revenue. They also increase suppliers, action volume and value at risk. A truly perfect single trusted agent would weaken the case for third-party verification; independent ownership, adversarial incentives and irreversible actions are the reasons the stronger proposals survive. Measure those needs with buyers rather than assuming errors stay constant forever.

**E — Billion-agent arithmetic is a design warning:** at an illustrative 3 MB each, a billion parked images require **3 PB** before history, replicas and journals. Ten checkpoints mean **30 PB** before deduplication. They cannot all be active and independent payers merely because the idle files fit in object storage. Store one immutable shared image where legitimate, attach small lineage/authority records, wake selectively, batch verification and settle aggregate invoices. These are scaling directions, not demonstrated capacity or a current hosting quote.

**I — A sensible first product boundary:** publish a permissively usable receipt/envelope format and a small verifier; build one seller/buyer integration around it. Existing DOs, R2, queues, workflow recovery and x402/MPP supply infrastructure. Do not first build a currency, global court, risk carrier, cloud scheduler or new legal person. The eight ideas are competing paths and possible extensions, not eight dependencies for launch.

## Ranking and the top three

**I — Full ranking, balancing ambition, paid value, capsule necessity and a falsifiable two-week proof:**

1. **Notarised computation:** best independent artifact, clearest evidence value, credible route into buyer/seller integrations. Main risk: a signed journal is sufficient.
2. **Fork firms:** strongest visual economic action and unusually direct use of continuation state. Main risk: fresh parallel tasks are simpler and equally effective.
3. **Executable work orders:** strongest near-term purchase/settlement use case; it turns receipts into consequential behavior. Main risk: acceptance predicates cannot capture buyer value.
4. **Licensed knowledge supply chains:** plausible data-vendor demand and established payment rails; capsule advantage depends on ongoing derived work.
5. **Transferable mandates:** important continuity problem and necessary authority semantics, but many cases are ordinary export and credential rotation.
6. **Action underwriting:** potentially very valuable; credible pricing and distribution lie well beyond a two-week software demo.
7. **Spot exchange:** technically attractive, economically weak for tool-dominated glue; constrained markets may work before an open compute market.
8. **Guild commons:** largest societal ambition, weakest immediate buyer and hardest nontechnical validation. Worth a bounded mechanism experiment, not the flagship.

**I — #1 case:** the portable receipt is the most reusable institutional primitive here. A small supplier can hand a large buyer something independently inspectable without requiring a new cloud account, common orchestration platform or legal reform. Replaying a real invoice transformation, rejecting a manipulated input and continuing a checked checkpoint is visible and bounded. It becomes more valuable as agents buy and sell longer procedures. Its kill test is also unusually clean: if source plus a signed journal is enough, the capsule must earn its place rather than becoming a fetish.

**I — #2 case:** a fork firm compresses the whole thesis into an understandable action: one program decides to create subcontractors, divides a conserved purse, receives useful work and closes the accounts. The scarce resource is genuinely the budget; the inherited asset is genuinely execution state. It tests whether generated programs can use institutional structure to buy better outcomes, while exposing the overhead, correlated failures and bogus internal-revenue claims that could kill the thesis. It is the best second demo once receipt and grant semantics work.

**I — #3 case:** work orders establish an obvious reason to care about evidence: money moves only after agreed checks. This can begin with internal credits and a narrow objective deliverable, then attach real rails through an existing provider. It has a more immediate commercial surface than selling a general runtime, but should follow the receipt experiment so the team does not mistake a JSON-schema check for trustworthy procurement. The difficult acceptance/oracle problem is explicit rather than hidden in a claim of “trustless agent work.”

**I — The ONE I would bet on:** **notarised computation, demonstrated as a replayable invoice-reconciliation receipt**, with one real continuation exported and resumed after independent checking. Week one proves the verifier and evidence boundary; week two adds a buyer-selected verifier and a small test-credit work order. The launch artifact is a downloadable work receipt plus a no-account replay path. The firm, insurance and governance visions become extensions only after a third party finds that artifact useful.

## Five-line TL;DR

1. **I:** Build economic actors from portable state plus externally conserved authority; cloning a computer must not clone its money or mandate.
2. **F/I:** Agoric, accounting, contract protocols and current HTTP payment rails supply substantial prior art; evidence portability is the proposed addition.
3. **I:** The strongest three bets are runnable computation receipts, agents hiring continuation forks, and escrowed executable work orders.
4. **I:** Replay establishes consistency with disclosed inputs; truth, independent identity, acceptance quality and legal attribution need separate evidence.
5. **I/E:** Bet on a two-week notarised-computation demo, and kill the capsule-specific pitch if a source-plus-signed-journal baseline serves buyers equally well.
