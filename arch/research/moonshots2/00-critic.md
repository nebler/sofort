# Round 1 critic: verification survives; the capsule thesis remains unproved

Review date: **2026-10-27**. Read against [the round-2 brief](BRIEF.md), all seven round-1 reports ([01](../moonshots/01-archaeology.md), [02](../moonshots/02-biology.md), [03](../moonshots/03-physics.md), [04](../moonshots/04-economics.md), [05](../moonshots/05-planet.md), [06](../moonshots/06-selfgrowing.md), [07](../moonshots/07-nextmodel.md)), and the [capsule landscape](../v8/02-landscape.md). This is a critique of the existing bets, not another idea list. Narrowings below retain mechanisms already proposed in round 1.

**Labels:** **F** = sourced fact, including an explicitly attributed first-party finding; **I** = inference or judgment; **E** = illustrative estimate or proposed experimental threshold. There are **no new runtime measurements**. External sources were checked on **2026-10-27**; publication dates are given where established. Every 2030 scenario is **E**, not a demand forecast, capacity commitment, or prediction of future prices.

## Judgment

**I — Round 1 found a real family of problems, then overgeneralized its cause.** Accountability, independent evidence, changing environments, and state continuity remain valuable with excellent models. Repeatedly searching for mistakes in model-written code becomes less valuable as those mistakes disappear. These are different markets. “Models generate more, therefore verification becomes scarcer” does not establish either rising verification expenditure or a defensible product.

**I — The biggest unsupported step is from a surviving need to a necessary representation.** Tests, signed records, event histories, explicit application checkpoints, and durable workflows can serve most of the surviving needs. None of the seven reports demonstrates a capsule advantage on an independently selected customer workload. The reports generally acknowledge this and propose fair comparisons; the critic's objection is that their optimistic rankings precede those comparisons.

**I — Best survivors:** a **narrow Oracle Foundry with Failure Seed Bank as its feedback loop**, and **notarised computation narrowed to cross-party evidence for a specified deliverable**. Neither earns a mandatory heap snapshot. The counterfactual debugger is the strongest technical entry feature, especially for the Foundry, but exact replay has a stronger case than prediction of hypothetical production futures.

**I — Direct casualties:** “one tab → eight branches → one file” as a general product thesis, and **runtime fault-grown Node** as a durable response to missing implementation labor. Their useful infrastructure or qualification work can remain; the proposed economic reasons for doing it at runtime weaken sharply under the stipulated model.

## 1. What the superhuman-model test actually removes

**I — Apply the premise generously.** At **10×**, routine adapters and migrations are already highly reliable, ordinary incidents can be diagnosed directly, and the model can construct good tests. At **100×**, fully grant near-zero implementation errors, perfect API knowledge (including existing quirks), and reliable long autonomous work. API trivia and unfamiliar documentation cannot protect a bet. Do not smuggle today's bad coding, context limits, or weak planning back into the argument. Also give the competing CI system and incumbent platform access to this model.

**I — Reasons assurance can survive that premise:**

- **Accountability and liability:** someone must establish who authorized an action, which version ran, and who bears an unresolved obligation. Confidence in a model is not an attributable historical record.
- **Adversarial counterparties:** a competent supplier can lie, omit an input, or optimize a weak acceptance rule. Better intelligence does not align its incentives with the buyer's. Independence belongs to principals and evidence sources, not merely different model names.
- **An unknown, changing world:** actual stock, hardware faults, new deployment state, other actors' actions, and whether a timed-out request committed require observations. Perfect API knowledge is not knowledge of the next response. The world may be nondeterministic; even deterministic private state can be unavailable to the model.
- **Irreversible effects:** spending, deletion, disclosure, and physical action require authority, preconditions, and reconciliation. A correct model still needs a system that implements these semantics.
- **Regulation and other parties needing proof:** an auditor, insurer, customer, or regulator may need inspectable evidence under its own trust policy. This is domain-specific; it is not a universal requirement to replay every action.
- **Specification ambiguity:** a model cannot verify a requirement nobody supplied. It can elicit preferences or propose alternatives, but an authorized owner must settle contested intent. Running more branches cannot discover which refund policy the business meant to adopt.

**I — Reasons a separate checking product can shrink or vanish:**

- The model simply produces a correct implementation; repeated candidate search adds no outcome value.
- The model performs proof, test generation, and checking inside the normal engineering workflow. Verification survives as a function while the external product disappears.
- Users stop checking low-stakes outputs when checking costs more than occasional correction.
- A platform enforces a small invariant once, instead of repeatedly testing each compliant operation.
- A standard signed transcript establishes the relevant fact more cheaply than executable replay.
- The same stronger model makes explicit state serialization, ordinary reproducers, and source replay cheap, eroding the capsule's claimed engineering advantage.

**I — There are therefore three separate tests:** does assurance remain necessary; does an independent supplier earn payment for it; does retaining a live heap improve it? Round 1 often answers the first and implicitly awards the other two.

### The economic condition, not the slogan

**I — For a bounded class of actions, a first approximation is:**

`value of added checking = N × p_bad × loss_if_bad × fraction_prevented + evidence/recovery value − total checking cost`

**I:** each term changes with capability. Better models lower `p_bad`; better checking also lowers its own cost; autonomy may increase action volume `N` and loss exposure. If volume rises 100× while error probability falls 100× and stakes stay fixed, expected error loss is unchanged. It does not automatically rise. Correlated failures and tail losses need more than this simple expectation, but do not rescue an unmeasured growth assumption.

**I — Unknown specifications are not automatically a market for verification.** They can instead make verification impossible or turn the business into expensive requirements consulting. A payer, an authorized interpretation, and an observable acceptance condition must exist before the proposed gate can sell a verdict.

## 2. What history and 2024–2026 evidence actually support

### Compilers removed one inspection burden, not all testing

**F:** CompCert proves preservation of specified source-language behavior through compilation. Its documentation explicitly separates miscompilation from bugs already in the source and discusses extra assurance for critical software. **I:** this is the right analogy: a trustworthy generator can remove translation-level review without establishing that the source expresses the right requirement or works with its environment. Most developers stopped routinely reviewing generated assembly; they did not thereby establish application correctness. Conversely, an application team does not buy a new independent compiler audit for every ordinary build. [CompCert manual, current](https://compcert.org/man/manual001.html).

**F:** a NIST summary published **2010-11-09** cites its earlier **2002** study, stating that testing consumed 50% of development budgets and reporting a $59.5 billion annual US cost from software flaws. **I:** this dated summary establishes that testing remained economically important decades into widespread compiler use. It is **not** a longitudinal estimate showing that compiler improvements caused testing expenditure to increase, and neither the budget share nor the old national estimate is a current market measurement. [NIST summary](https://www.nist.gov/news-events/news/2010/11/updated-nist-software-uses-combination-testing-catch-bugs-fast-and-easy).

### Cloud automation preserved operational assurance, but the spend proxy is imperfect

**F:** Datadog reported **$2.68 billion** revenue for **2024**, up **26%**, and **$3.43 billion** for **2025**, up **28%**. **I:** customers continued paying substantially for operating visibility as cloud tooling improved. This is one vendor's revenue across observability and adjacent products, not a measurement of correctness verification, its share of software spending, or a causal effect of automation. [FY2024 results, released 2025](https://investors.datadoghq.com/news-releases/news-release-details/datadog-announces-fourth-quarter-and-fiscal-year-2024-financial/), [FY2025 results, released 2026](https://investors.datadoghq.com/node/16286/pdf).

**F:** Amazon's **2019-12-05** rollback-safety account explains how individually working versions can still be incompatible during upgrades or rollback. FoundationDB's **SIGMOD 2021** paper describes systematic deterministic fault simulation. **I:** distributed state and deployment transitions remain reasons for assurance even with good individual components. Neither source proves that a perfect model needs thousands of rehearsals for every write. [Amazon deployment account](https://builder.aws.com/content/3F04j2yRAAMBuPSPs50xwXZqg01/ensuring-rollback-safety-during-deployments), [FoundationDB paper](https://www.foundationdb.org/files/fdb-paper.pdf).

### Auditors and aviation assurance survive for specific reasons

**F:** PCAOB **AS 1105** distinguishes evidence quality from quantity, favors independent/direct evidence, and says better evidence can reduce the amount of additional corroboration needed. Its current text also addresses electronic external information received through a company. **I:** better accountants do not eliminate other parties' need to establish existence, completeness, rights, and authorization. But the standard also contradicts an unconditional “better systems imply more checking” thesis: improved evidence can reduce checking. Replaying arithmetic is only one audit procedure; it does not confirm that the recorded asset exists. [AS 1105, current, including 2025 guidance links](https://pcaobus.org/oversight/standards/auditing-standards/details/AS1105).

**F:** the PCAOB reported operating expenses of **$370.5 million in 2024**, versus **$332.2 million in 2023**, with personnel and systems-related drivers. **I:** independent audit oversight demonstrably remained funded. These are oversight expenses, not total audit fees or proof that improving accountants caused higher expenditure. [PCAOB 2024 annual report, published 2025](https://assets.pcaobus.org/pcaob-dev/docs/default-source/about/administration/documents/annual_reports/2024-annual-report.pdf).

**F:** FAA **AC 20-115D**, dated **2017-07-21** and still listed on its current software guidance page, recognizes DO-178C-related assurance and life-cycle evidence, including supplements for formal methods and tool qualification. **I:** aviation is evidence that catastrophic consequences sustain assurance processes despite mature tools. It is also a warning against selling simulation as certification. Better formal tools can change how objectives are satisfied; they do not mandate Sofort's runtime or a human rereading every generated line. [FAA circular](https://www.faa.gov/documentLibrary/media/Advisory_Circular/AC_20-115D.pdf), [current FAA index](https://www.faa.gov/aircraft/air_cert/design_approvals/air_software/software_regs).

### AI evidence shows persistent evaluation activity, not inevitable verification revenue

**F:** LangChain's **2024** practitioner survey reported **39.8% offline evaluation** and **32.5% online evaluation**. Its later survey, collected **November 18–December 2, 2025**, reported **52.4%** and **37.3%**, alongside **89% observability**. **I:** evaluation adoption increased in these self-selected samples while models improved. This is not a panel, a representative industry census, or a dollar-spend series. LangChain sells related tooling, which matters when interpreting the sample. [2024 survey](https://www.langchain.com/stateofaiagents), [later survey and methodology, accessed 2026](https://www.langchain.com/state-of-agent-engineering).

**F:** DORA's **2024** research associated more AI adoption with lower delivery stability and a small throughput decrease. Its **2025** research subsequently found improved throughput while stability remained dependent on organizational foundations. **I:** do not freeze a 2024 correlation into a law of future models. Present-day delivery problems show integration matters; they cannot settle the superhuman-model counterfactual. [DORA 2024](https://dora.dev/research/2024/dora-report/), [DORA's 2025 retrospective, 2026-01-07](https://dora.dev/insights/dora-2025-year-in-review/).

**F:** Anthropic's **2026-01-09** evaluation account describes dedicated evaluation infrastructure with domain/product teams contributing tasks, plus code, model, and human grading. NIST's **2026-03-06** monitoring publication emphasizes deployment conditions that controlled predeployment testing can miss. **I:** both support continued investment in measurement and an ownership problem around ground truth. They do not quantify an eval/guardrail spending curve or show a new vendor must own the infrastructure. [Anthropic account](https://www.anthropic.com/research/demystifying-evals-for-ai-agents), [NIST deployment-monitoring publication](https://www.nist.gov/publications/challenges-monitoring-deployed-ai-systems-center-ai-standards-and-innovation).

**F:** OpenAI's **2026-02-23** reassessment found material test/task problems in **59.4% of an audited 138-task difficult subset** of SWE-bench Verified, and reported contamination concerns. This is not a 59.4% estimate for all software tasks or even an unbiased sample of its full benchmark. **I:** increasingly capable models can expose defective graders. A gate can be the problem; “independent” and “held-out” are not synonyms for correct. [Benchmark reassessment](https://openai.com/index/why-we-no-longer-evaluate-swe-bench-verified/).

**I — Evidence limit:** the sources inspected do **not** establish a comparable, independent 2024–2026 dollar series for AI-specific evaluation and guardrail spending. Adoption, published engineering investment, broad observability revenue, regulation, and funding are different measures. The defensible historical conclusion is **assurance persists and changes form**. “Its spend must outgrow generation” remains an unproved economic hypothesis.

## 3. Scale assumptions used below

**F:** current R2 Standard rates are **$0.015/GB-month**, **$4.50/million Class A operations**, and **$0.36/million Class B operations**. Workers Standard marginal rates include **$0.02/million CPU-ms** and **$0.30/million requests**. These are October 2026 prices, not 2030 quotes. [R2 pricing, updated 2026-10-26](https://developers.cloudflare.com/r2/pricing/), [Workers pricing, current](https://developers.cloudflare.com/workers/platform/pricing/).

**E — Common arithmetic:** decimal units; no allowance, tax, discount, redundancy, or deduplication assumed. One million 3 MB snapshots occupy **3 TB**, costing **$45/month** for one retained cohort. A million **50 CPU-ms** replays cost **$1 in guest CPU**; that tiny number excludes reference execution, instrumentation, state restoration, compression, scheduling, logs, and tools. It is a lower bound for deliberately cheap suffixes, not a total verification quote.

**E — Unpriced work gets an explicit assumption:** use **$0.10–$1 per bounded model attempt** and **$200,000/year per fully loaded engineering/operations role**, about **$16,700/month**. These are scenario inputs, not researched current model tariffs or labor averages. Native reference fleets, external data, and audit partners are additional where named. A 10× increase in model-attempt cost multiplies that line tenfold. Cheap R2 storage does not establish cheap product operations.

**I — Execution shape:** Workers/TypeScript handle bounded tasks; R2 holds immutable artifacts; DOs shard ownership and metadata; Queues/Workflows orchestrate jobs. Native or heavyweight references run separately. No global DO, permanent browser uptime, automatic geographic pinning, or copying of remote authority is assumed. A copied heap is not a copied world, payment balance, or permission.

**I — Score interpretation:** each verdict gives **model-proofness / need / scalability**, each **0–5**, higher better. Model-proofness asks how much of the **named bet**, rather than a generic adjacent market, remains under the 100× premise. Need asks for incremental value over the best simple alternative. Scalability includes the domain and operational work, not merely whether cloud compute can be purchased. Scores are judgments, not additive investment returns.

## 4. Individual verdicts

### 4.1 Rebuild occupied software — 01

**Superhuman-model test — I:** at **10×**, migration authoring and recovery improve; the need to preserve customer state remains. At **100×**, many migrations become ordinary correct transformations, so a population of competing migration futures shrinks. The durable problem is coordinating state, pending requests, version transitions, and external effects while requirements change. A model can generate the protocol, but it cannot retroactively change what an old request already did or decide an unspecified business policy. Independent evidence may matter to the owner; regulation is not a general prerequisite for this market.

**I — Important counterargument:** near-perfect agents can maintain explicit schemas and backward-compatible boundaries from the beginning. The estate need not contain millions of mysterious heaps. Models creating more bespoke software might increase maintenance demand, but good models can also reduce bespoke divergence through shared components. Round 1 assumes the former wins without evidence. Amazon's upgrade/rollback example supports transition management; it does not establish that opaque persistent state is the best representation.

**2030 scale — E:** an application-platform operator maintains **10,000 tenant variants**, with **four changes per tenant/month**: **40,000 migration jobs**. Twenty retained-state/schedule trials per job give **800,000 checks**. Ten 3 MB checkpoints per tenant occupy **300 GB**, about **$4.50/month** before journals. One model attempt per job costs **$4,000–$40,000/month**. At only **1% manual exceptions**, with **30 minutes each**, review already consumes **200 hours/month**. Contracts, customer support, rollback qualification, and the application platform dominate raw storage.

**I — Path from two weeks:** the proposed 30-state upgrade clinic can demonstrate one bounded change. It cannot establish safe arbitrary heap/code evolution. Next evidence must cover multiple independently chosen changes, intentionally changed semantics, old pending calls, and a customer who values uninterrupted progress. Scale through common versioned contracts; per-tenant handwritten migrations destroy the premise. A solo prototype is plausible; operating the estate requires the platform owner and ongoing support.

**Do we need it? — I:** strongest alternative: explicit state + expand/contract schema migration + versioned workflow handlers + canary/rollback. A better model can write these. Capsules win only when they preserve valuable in-flight computation that this approach cannot cheaply reconstruct. Round 1's unsupported arbitrary-frame upgrades are correctly excluded; that exclusion also leaves a smaller advantage over conventional workflows. Its 90%-of-30 threshold is a feasibility threshold, not an acceptable production error rate.

**Verdict — survives only narrowed:** controlled evolution of valuable, difficult-to-replay in-flight state under declared boundaries. **Scores: 3 / 4 / 3.** Kill the capsule requirement if explicit state and versioned workflows match downtime, lost progress, and total maintenance effort across several real changes.

### 4.2 Executable citations — 01

**Superhuman-model test — I:** at **10×**, models reconstruct analyses and explain assumptions better, reducing the friction this product removes. At **100×**, checking ordinary arithmetic or rerunning simple code often disappears into the reader's agent. Human inspection of routine claims shrinks. What survives is a reader's ability to inspect **which inputs, assumptions, and versions were actually used**, especially in contested research or commercial analysis. Better reasoning cannot recover private historical inputs that were never preserved.

**I:** other parties needing evidence and specification disagreement are stronger reasons than residual coding errors. Adversarial selection of data can matter; a perfectly replayable cherry-picked analysis remains misleading. The claim's scope, source authenticity, and omitted evidence may matter more than its saved continuation. A capsule cannot settle whether a statistical assumption describes reality.

**2030 scale — E:** a technical publishing/review service retains **one million claims** and serves **100,000 challenged forks/month**. At 3 MB each, the saved-state floor is **3 TB/$45 per month**; one full-image read per fork moves **300 GB/month**. If every publication needs just **two minutes of manual packaging**, one million publications consume **33,333 hours**. Thus the viable service must extract manifests automatically from a small supported toolchain, while expert review remains selective. Source data, licensed references, and long-term engine support are outside the $45 figure.

**I — Path from two weeks:** the five-reader challenge comparison is a reasonable usability pilot. Use analyses selected before deciding to preserve a cyclic graph; otherwise the proof manufactures the capsule's advantage. Then test real disagreement between author and reviewer, durable source availability, and author effort. A solo demo can lead to a supported publishing integration; it does not lead directly to a universal literature format.

**Do we need it? — I:** alternative: pinned source + input hashes + environment manifest + a notebook/Binder link + cached intermediate data. Binder already makes repositories executable and shareable. A good reader's model makes this baseline stronger. The capsule must reduce time to a **correct substantive challenge**, including author packaging and dependency maintenance, rather than just time to the first animated result. [Binder documentation, current](https://mybinder.readthedocs.io/en/latest/introduction.html).

**Verdict — survives only narrowed:** expensive intermediate computational analyses where exact historical assumptions matter. **Scores: 3 / 2 / 4.** Keep citation/provenance independently of snapshots. A public footnote-fork demo alone does not justify a standalone product.

### 4.3 Immune repair colonies — 02

**Superhuman-model test — I:** at **10×**, ordinary schema drift and parser errors often need one repair, making 16 candidates wasteful. At **100×**, perfect API knowledge and correct adapters remove much of the proposed incident set. A reusable library of generic “memory cells” can also be regenerated or replaced by the model's knowledge. Those parts shrink sharply.

**I:** capture/recovery survives for a newly occurring provider failure or change, actual partial completion, a rare tenant-specific state, and an ambiguous remote outcome. Existing undocumented quirks do not qualify as unknowable under the premise; unobserved events after the agent's last observation can. A better model still benefits from observations of that incident. But it can often choose a correct repair directly. The durable asset is the **incident state and effect history**, not a colony of weak alternatives. Accountability can require a repair record; adversarial inputs require enforcement. Neither requires biological selection. For an unspecified transformation, no amount of independent replay establishes the desired record semantics.

**2030 scale — E:** an integration vendor has **one million workflows**; assume **0.1%/month** trigger a distinct repair incident: **1,000 incidents**. Sixteen proposals produce **16,000 attempts**, costing **$1,600–$16,000/month** under the shared assumption. One hundred replay cases per proposal mean **1.6 million checks**, only **$1.60 of 50 ms guest CPU**, excluding references and I/O. One 3 MB state per workflow is **$45/month**; 30 versions are **$1,350**. If **10%** of incidents require **two hours** of specialist judgment, that is **200 hours/month**. Two to four sustained product/operations roles add roughly **$33,000–$67,000/month** before other infrastructure.

**I — Path from two weeks:** the ingestion proof can establish retained-state value on six fault classes. It must then survive independently sourced incidents and show transfer **between compatible profiles**, without moving raw customer heaps across tenants. One accepted decoder does not generalize to a provider's changed commercial meaning. Reuse repairs by contract/version, not incident count.

**Do we need it? — I:** best alternative: Temporal/Workflows + retries and reconciliation + JSON/application checkpoints + the strongest repair model + ordinary regression tests. Temporal already retains event history for recovery; record/replay is not unique to a capsule. The proposed colony needs a valuable internal state that this alternative loses, and a repair-selection gain beyond a single strong proposal. [Temporal event-history contract, current](https://docs.temporal.io/workflow-execution/event).

**Verdict — survives only narrowed:** executable incident capture and carefully bounded recovery for hard-to-reconstruct state. **Scores: 2 / 3 / 3.** Colony search is optional and likely shrinks. Kill it if one strong repair plus explicit state matches end-to-end results within the report's own 20% threshold.

### 4.4 Failure-search gate — 03

**Superhuman-model test — I:** at **10×**, adversarial tests still find integration races and stale assumptions. At **100×**, correct code can already implement retries, revocation, fencing, and authorization without rediscovering each invariant in 10,000 simulations. The cases “model forgot to check expiry” and “model used the wrong account” should become rare under the premise. Do not preserve them by assumption.

**I:** changing world state, hostile counterparties, and irreversible actions sustain **effect enforcement and revalidation**. Some adversarial testing survives for imperfect tool contracts or a newly deployed combined system. But more simulation does not reveal genuinely unobservable state, and a correct broker may prevent a forbidden effect more strongly than testing it. A simulator authored from the same mistaken specification is no independent defense. The most model-proof part is the broker's authority boundary; that part is also a familiar IAM/transactional-systems function.

**2030 scale — E:** an enterprise action platform handles **one million proposed writes/day**, approximately **30 million/month**. It enforces cheap authorization/version checks on each write, but rehearses only the **0.1%** introducing a new policy or integration: **30,000 qualification jobs/month**. At **1,000 cheap suffixes/job**, that is **30 million replays**, **$30 of 50 ms guest CPU**, plus all orchestration/reference/storage costs. One bounded model planning/challenge attempt per job costs **$3,000–$30,000**. Rehearsing every write instead produces **30 billion suffixes/month** and **$30,000 guest CPU**; putting even a $0.10 fresh model call in every suffix would cost **$3 billion**. Selectivity is fundamental, not an optimization to add later.

**I — Path from two weeks:** one owned configuration connector can prove a bounded effect protocol and discover a seeded race. It does not prove a simulator predicts a third-party API. Expansion requires independently validated connector contracts, captured incidents, and evidence that a testing verdict predicts real deployment behavior. That is a domain program, not scaling the branch scheduler. Qualification should generally happen per policy/version; current conditions are rechecked at commit.

**Do we need it? — I:** alternative: a transactional effect broker, policy-as-code, property-based fault testing, and conventional release gates. FoundationDB and Antithesis are serious existing simulation approaches; Antithesis already documents fault control and multiverse debugging. A narrow tool ABI can ease adoption, but is not a new category. Test guided search against the strongest such baseline at equal total budget. [Antithesis documentation, current](https://antithesis.com/docs/setup/overview/).

**Verdict — survives only narrowed:** selective adversarial qualification of new, high-consequence policies with calibrated contracts; cheap enforcement for routine effects. **Scores: 3 / 4 / 3.** A universal per-write multiverse gate dies. Keep the distinction between a tested policy and a broker-enforced invariant visible.

### 4.5 Counterfactual debugger — 03

**Superhuman-model test — I:** at **10×**, a model can often diagnose from good logs, reducing interactive investigation time. At **100×**, the remaining value shifts to providing the model or another operator with **otherwise unavailable execution evidence**. Rare schedules, private observed data, and historical versions cannot be inferred with certainty from perfect API knowledge. An incident artifact can remain useful even if its consumer is entirely automated.

**I:** exact reproduction supports accountability, disputes, and regression. Counterfactual production claims remain much weaker: changing one request can invalidate every recorded reply after it. A perfect model does not know how an external actor would actually respond in a world that never happened. A correct debugger must stop at that knowledge boundary or label a simulator. More capable diagnosis is a reason to need fewer UI manipulations, not a reason evidence capture disappears.

**2030 scale — E:** an agent platform investigates **10,000 incidents/month**, with **20 interventions each**: **200,000 replays**. At **200 CPU-ms/replay**, the guest CPU floor is **$0.80/month**. Capturing **10 MB/incident** adds **100 GB/month**; a rolling year is about **1.2 TB/$18 per month**, excluding indexes, replicas, and large referenced payloads. Model investigation at one bounded attempt per incident costs **$1,000–$10,000/month**. Two capture/integration/operations roles already cost roughly **$33,000/month**. Instrumentation, retention rights, and data handling determine adoption and margin.

**I — Path from two weeks:** the toy order-coordinator replay is a credible first mechanism. The next step is one opt-in production workflow with measured capture overhead and a blinded diagnosis task. Capture must preserve useful evidence without requiring an application rewrite. Do not count artificial closure complexity as customer value. The product can grow as an incident-export/debugger integration, with heavier simulations added only where validated.

**Do we need it? — I:** alternative: structured traces + source/version hashes + recorded tool responses + an ordinary reproducer, or existing record/replay tooling. The capsule wins if it saves the expensive prefix or a genuinely difficult-to-export local state and makes reproduction measurably easier. It does not win because the UI displays a tree. Measure the investigator's correct conclusion and elapsed total work, not number of branches explored.

**Verdict — survives only narrowed:** exact replay and bounded, explicitly modeled interventions. **Scores: 4 / 4 / 4.** This is stronger than the universal gate because it begins with observed evidence rather than a claim to know future reality. Predictive counterfactuals remain optional and domain-bound.

### 4.6 Notarised computation — 04

**Superhuman-model test — I:** at **10×**, routine correctness disputes shrink. At **100×**, a buyer still may need to establish what was purchased, which inputs were used, what was authorized, and what the supplier actually attested. Two highly capable parties can have conflicting interests. Historical evidence and accountability survive near-perfect computation. This is the clearest separation between being correct and convincing a party with a different trust policy.

**I:** however, a perfect invoice reconciler seldom needs its entire interpreter heap replayed. A signed input/output manifest and source/tool receipts may suffice. The seller can fabricate or omit an input and then honestly replay the fabrication. Independent signatures prove attribution/integrity, not truth, completeness, faithful execution on a hostile host, or legal notarial status. A buyer-selected verifier helps only if it has an independently grounded acceptance rule or input source. Regulation is a possible domain requirement, not evidence that every invoice needs this package.

**2030 scale — E:** an existing procurement/audit platform receives **10 million work receipts/month**. A **10 KB signed envelope** per receipt is **100 GB/$1.50 per month per retained cohort**. A mandatory **3 MB capsule** makes the same cohort **30 TB/$450**. Replaying a **1% sample**, **100,000 jobs** at **500 CPU-ms**, adds only **$1 guest CPU**, but external verification and evidence retrieval can dominate. Human review of **0.01% of receipts** for **20 minutes each** already takes **333 hours/month**. Two to five technical/operations roles cost **$33,000–$83,000/month**, before domain auditors or trust-service partners. At **1% manual review**, this becomes over **33,000 hours**; a mass-market product must automate most acceptance and escalate selectively.

**I — Path from two weeks:** tamper detection and offline replay are feasible demonstrations of integrity and consistency. The essential next test is an actual buyer/verifier resolving a realistic dispute with less total work than the baseline. It must reject an internally consistent package using an unauthorized or missing input. A solo builder can supply an envelope/verifier and one integration. Broad institutional acceptance requires distribution, evidence policy, and recognized counterparties; implementing R2 signing does not supply them.

**Do we need it? — I:** alternative: signed source + signed input/output transcript + hashes + ordinary audit logs, with on-demand reproduction. Sigstore already supplies artifact identity, signatures, and transparency tooling; inventing a signer is not differentiation. Executable replay earns payment only when it resolves an acceptance question that simpler records leave costly or unresolved. [Sigstore documentation, current](https://docs.sigstore.dev/).

**Verdict — survives only narrowed:** cross-party evidence for specific, consequential deliverables; capsules optional. **Scores: 4 / 3 / 4.** The institutional need is strong; the extra need for executable notarisation is unproved. Prefer this to a universal “every answer carries a proof” claim.

### 4.7 One tab → eight branches → one file — 05

**Superhuman-model test — I:** at **10×**, parallel independent tool searches can still reduce latency; eight speculative reasoning attempts often lose value. At **100×**, one agent can perform most of the proposed code repair and synthetic incident diagnosis correctly. Models and providers may internally parallelize search. Exposing eight user-level actors or eight copies of the JS heap need not improve anything.

**I:** irreducible parallel data acquisition can survive, but ordinary async tasks already provide it. Cross-device continuity and ownership also survive, but are separate from swarming. Accountability is served by a parent task and its result records, not the branch count. No regulatory or liability argument specifically supports eight agents. Correct joining is useful infrastructure; it does not create independent evidence or justify a product category.

**2030 scale — E:** a cloud/agent SDK handles **one million jobs/day**. Eight branches each imply **240 million branch attempts/month**, versus **30 million** for one attempt. If each branch needs one bounded model attempt at $0.10–$1, the bills are **$24 million–$240 million**, versus **$3 million–$30 million**. The incremental **$21 million–$210 million** must buy improved outcomes or valuable latency. Sending a 3 MB base independently to each branch moves **720 TB/month**. Seven days of one retained base per job occupy **21 TB/$315 per month**; guest CPU at 50 ms per branch is only **$240/month**. Saving the heap copy is not saving the model's weights, prompt processing, or reasoning state.

**I — Path from two weeks:** the proposed demonstration can prove suspend/fork/join/handoff with actual branches. It does not validate customer demand. The next test must choose workloads before selecting a fan-out, compare the same strongest model with equal total budget, and allow the scheduler to choose **one**. A scalable implementation is plausible as an SDK feature; a separate company justified by this topology is not.

**Do we need it? — I:** alternative: one strong agent, `Promise.all`/task queues for independent tool work, shared immutable inputs, and a normal final state export. Anthropic's **2025-06-13** production account reports useful multi-agent research but about **15× chat token use**, with gains strongly related to token budget and task parallelism. That supports selective parallel research, not unconditional swarming or continuation cloning. [Anthropic production account](https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/multi-agent-research-system).

**Verdict — dies as the top product bet. Scores: 1 / 2 / 4.** Retain fork/join and handoff as capabilities for another product. If independently selected workloads meet the report's equal-budget +10-point success or 2× latency threshold, sell that demonstrated workload benefit, not “eight branches.”

### 4.8 Fault-grown Node — 06

**Superhuman-model test — I:** at **10×**, the missing polyfill is synthesized faster. At **100×**, perfect API knowledge and excellent code generation make building the needed compatibility layer **ahead of execution** straightforward. Repeatedly stopping a user's program to discover a known API is an unnecessary deployment strategy. The synthesis spectacle loses its economic reason to occur at runtime.

**I:** independent qualification can remain useful, but missing capabilities are not missing code. A model cannot synthesize a real OS process, native addon, or unavailable host primitive inside an executor that forbids it. It can refactor the application or select a different runtime; those alternatives improve with the same model. Demand for actual compatibility and support survives, while the “fault causes a colony of implementation attempts” story shrinks.

**F/local:** report 06 attributes most newly passing GrowOS corpus programs to one mmap handler and documents helper work, environment overfitting, and limits of held-out evidence. **I:** that is evidence of leverage at one gap, not evidence of a scalable demand-paging model for semantics. It is particularly weak support for extrapolating across the long tail of npm native/process dependencies. [Round-1 account and its underlying experiment links](../moonshots/06-selfgrowing.md).

**2030 scale — E:** a compatibility distributor qualifies **1,000 package/version combinations/month**, with **20 candidate attempts each**: **20,000 attempts/$2,000–$20,000**. Assume **$1,000–$10,000/month** for a controlled native reference fleet and at least **two specialist maintainers/$33,000 per month**. The illustrative operating floor is therefore roughly **$36,000–$63,000/month**, before customer support. Programs consume immutable qualified modules; each real request should not fund another synthesis job. The largest technical cost is joint semantics and version/security maintenance, not module generation.

**I — Path from two weeks:** a grown StringDecoder and unseen-package reuse can establish one bounded mechanism. Before generalization, census the packages users actually need, distinguish shimmable code from absent host semantics, and demonstrate incremental supported applications. Cloudflare's built-in Node compatibility and `unenv` are direct alternatives with existing distribution. [Workers Node support, current](https://developers.cloudflare.com/workers/runtime-apis/nodejs/), [unenv source, current](https://github.com/unjs/unenv).

**Do we need it? — I:** alternative: native Workers support + ordinary polyfill bundling + build-time model adaptation + CI against Node; use a Node/container tool when the host contract requires it. A perfect coder strengthens every one of these options. A universal Node implementation is a finite, shared asset, so future synthesis cost can collapse rather than create recurring per-program revenue.

**Verdict — dies as a durable runtime-growth product. Scores: 1 / 2 / 2.** The replacement is already in report 06: offline qualification/distribution of a narrowly supported compatibility profile, possibly its behavioral-map/oracle factory. Score that as part of Oracle Foundry, not a rescue of runtime fault growth.

### 4.9 Oracle Foundry — 07

**Superhuman-model test — I:** at **10×**, it becomes easier to generate both implementations and effective discriminating tests. At **100×**, testing pure implementations of fully specified public APIs has much less incremental value: a correct implementation or checkable proof can arrive with the code. A universal outsourced grader is also exposed to bundling. Round 1's unqualified **5/5** model-resilience rating is too high.

**I:** the narrower need survives where acceptance depends on a **private customer contract, current target environment, actual compatibility requirement, independent counterparty, or newly observed failure**. Knowing every API does not establish which behavior a customer requires preserving, the outcome of an unobserved deployment failure, or which principal can authorize a semantics change. The Foundry earns its role through access, evidence ownership, and adjudication. It cannot infer omitted preferences by running more tests. A mistaken reference can preserve an unwanted bug; compatibility and desired improvement need separate contracts.

**2030 scale — E:** a managed qualification operator serves **100 customers**, each with **10 relevant releases/month**: **1,000 qualification jobs**. At **10,000 stateful test sequences/job**, **10 million sequences** flow through versioned reference/candidate runners. Fifty-ms guest checks cost **$10 CPU**, not the total reference bill. Ten thousand bounded proposal/test-generation attempts cost **$1,000–$10,000**. Two domain engineers and one operations/integration role cost **$50,000/month**; assume another **$1,000–$10,000** for reference infrastructure. That gives roughly **$52,000–$70,000/month** before sales, support, audit partners, and margin. The reference and contract should amortize across many releases or customers. If each new customer needs a new semantics expert, this scales as consulting.

**I — Path from two weeks:** the 20-attempt/three-contract proof can demonstrate rejection of known bad candidates and reproducible evidence. It should then test genuinely unseen model outputs and at least one **customer-owned** contract. The progression is one reference-backed API family → a maintained CI acceptance integration → fresh incident ingestion → additional families only where reference reuse is economical. A solo founder can begin this; a general acceptance network is a later institutional ambition, not the direct result of running more tests.

**Do we need it? — I:** alternative: existing reference-backed CI, property/differential testing, mutation testing, and a better coding agent. These already separate production from acceptance. The Foundry must reduce **wrong acceptance at the same false-rejection and total human/oracle budget**, or materially shorten a release/dispute. Rejecting deliberately planted mutants proves grader plumbing, not customer value. Benchmark flaws documented in 2026 are a reason to maintain and challenge the oracle, not permission to declare one's own oracle authoritative.

**Verdict — survives only narrowed:** maintained, independently grounded qualification for one difficult contract family. **Scores: 3 / 4 / 3.** Best builder fit and most credible accumulation loop, but customer data rights and a trusted reference must be acquired. A public synthetic test collection is not that asset. Capsules remain optional acceleration/export machinery.

### 4.10 Failure Seed Bank — 07

**Superhuman-model test — I:** at **10×**, old bugs can be repaired quickly; replay helps prevent regression and compare upgrades. At **100×**, many frozen model-error cases become trivial and cease to discriminate. Public cases can also enter training. An ever-growing archive does not imply an ever-growing reliability advantage.

**I:** observed private incidents, environmental changes, rare schedules, and target-specific semantics can remain useful evidence. The model cannot reconstruct an unrecorded historical request outcome from API knowledge. Accountability can require retention even after every current model passes. However, requirements change: preserving a former expected output forever can incorrectly reject an intentional improvement. Retire or reclassify cases with their contract versions. Fresh failure rights and case maintenance matter more than the number of dormant capsules.

**2030 scale — E:** a qualification/agent platform retains **one million minimized incident packages**, assumed **10 MB each including journal and referenced fixture data**: **10 TB/$150/month** for one retained version. Ten thousand new cases arrive monthly. Ten release campaigns each select **100,000 relevant cases**, giving **one million replays**; at 200 ms each, the guest CPU floor is **$4**. Mutating **10,000 cases** with one bounded model attempt each costs **$1,000–$10,000**. Even **0.5%** of new cases needing **30 minutes** of adjudication consume **25 hours**. One to two archive/integration roles add **$17,000–$33,000/month**; secure capture and source storage add more. Select by tenant, contract, and failure mechanism rather than rerunning every case against every change.

**I — Path from two weeks:** twelve synthetic cases can prove capture, reduction, version comparison, and replay. They cannot establish transfer to future incidents. Next collect an opt-in stream, freeze a temporal evaluation split, and ask whether bank-guided qualification predicts **later** failures better than an ordinary regression suite. Keep invalid or unminimizable captures in the denominator. Cross-tenant sharing requires explicit rights; stripping visible identifiers does not establish safe or semantically faithful heap redaction.

**Do we need it? — I:** alternative: a minimized regression corpus of source, input, expected outcome, and event schedule, attached to existing CI/observability. This is a very strong alternative, not a straw man. Store a snapshot only when a measured expensive prefix or hard-to-export state justifies it. The bank's value can survive while the capsule disappears.

**Verdict — survives only narrowed:** a fresh, versioned incident corpus inside the Foundry or debugger. **Scores: 3 / 4 / 4.** It is an accumulation loop and release feature, not yet an independent moonshot business. Failure to predict new incidents still leaves useful regression coverage, but kills the stronger learning claim.

## 5. What round 1 has not established

**I — Surviving assurance does not establish permanent human review.** A very strong model can conduct an independent audit on behalf of a different principal. Independence can remain institutional while checking becomes automated and cheap. That world can have more checked work and less spend per work item, fewer reviewers, and less revenue for a new general evaluation vendor.

**I — More capable adversaries do not automatically justify the same product.** A malicious supplier can manipulate inputs or omit side effects; replaying its chosen transcript cannot detect that without independent observations. A model can also produce code whose finite tests all pass. Correct acceptance may require a stronger formal contract or trusted execution/input path, rather than additional forks. Those are requirements to confront, not unbuilt features to wave into the business plan.

**I — Broker correctness is being credited to the capsule in several proposals.** Conserved budgets, stable logical operation IDs, stale-owner rejection, and reconciliation live outside the heap. They can serve ordinary workflows. If these mechanisms create all the observed safety improvement, report a successful broker, not proof of a portable-computer thesis.

**I — Capsule convenience has an opposing maintenance bill.** The reports attribute 1.5–6.4 MB populated images and 5–11× glue overhead to prior project measurements, plus engine/ABI pinning and retained sensitive bytes. These were not rerun here. Stronger models make explicit serializers and state migrations cheaper, while opaque heaps still require engine retention and difficult upgrades. The supposed removal of serialization work may merely move it to runtime compatibility. [Round-1 substrate evidence](../moonshots/01-archaeology.md), [prior landscape](../v8/02-landscape.md).

**I — Small pilots cannot demonstrate the advertised reliability tail.** Zero failures in 30 cases is not evidence of near-zero production risk. Under an ideal independent random-sampling model, zero failures in 30 trials gives an approximate 95% upper bound near **10%**; selected graph variants are not such a sample anyway. Replaying 10,000 schedules from one inaccurate simulator does not supply 10,000 independent reality checks. Predeclared defect sets are legitimate mechanism tests, but should not become deployment-confidence claims.

**I — The best baseline must receive the same stronger model.** It gets the same data, prior traces, model budget, tools, parallelism, and opportunity to write serializers or reproducers. Charge proposed systems for capture, onboarding, oracle maintenance, false rejections, and manual escalations. Charge the baseline for its actual implementation work too. Otherwise the experiment compares unequal engineering effort rather than representations.

## 6. Overall verdict and what round 2 must answer

**I — Verdict on “verification is the scarce resource”: survives only narrowed.** When implementation becomes reliable, the persistent scarcity is often **authoritative specifications, observations of changing systems, permission to act, and evidence accepted by another principal**. Verification is one way to use those scarce inputs. Some checking becomes a commodity or vanishes; some is incorporated into models and platforms. Historical assurance persistence does not prove that its costs rise monotonically, that it is the dominant bottleneck in 2030, or that it needs portable heaps.

**I — First choice: Oracle Foundry + Failure Seed Bank, restricted to a real reference-backed contract family.** It best fits the builder's existing experience and has a plausible two-week falsification. Its success must be an acceptance improvement on actual unseen work, with affordable reference maintenance. If that works without capsules, the product has succeeded and the capsule thesis has failed. Accept that result.

**I — Second choice: notarised computation for a specified cross-party deliverable.** Independence and historical evidence are more model-proof than error correction. But start with the buyer's actual acceptance/dispute need and beat a signed transcript. If no external verifier values the runnable package, retain ordinary receipts and stop. The counterfactual debugger is the preferred technical interface/entry feature for the first choice, with exact replay clearly separated from modeled futures.

**I — Round 2 must answer these questions before promoting another top bet:**

1. **Which failure remains with the stipulated 100× model?** Name an actual mechanism: unavailable current state, conflicting principals, an unspecified preference, or a required evidence procedure. “It can still hallucinate” does not pass this test.
2. **Who has the authoritative answer, and why can the model or platform not simply obtain it?** Identify the reference owner, data rights, calibration process, adjudicator, and update cost. A private synthetic test is not automatically an independent oracle.
3. **Which buyer pays for which changed decision?** A shorter incident, avoided wrong acceptance, cheaper dispute, or preserved in-flight work must replace a current cost. Name the budget owner and the simplest existing purchase/process it replaces.
4. **Does the need grow faster than the unit cost falls?** Separate number of jobs, stakes, error probability, sampling rate, and per-job assurance cost. Show both a growth scenario and a credible collapse scenario. Do not use Jevons as a substitute for the calculation.
5. **Why does this require a capsule?** Measure common-prefix cost, irreducible state, total artifacts, capture overhead, engine maintenance, and handoff effort against source + event log + explicit checkpoints. Let the experiment conclude that a normal representation wins.
6. **What is checked once, what is checked on each release, and what is enforced at every effect?** Universal per-action simulation is a different economic proposition from occasional qualification plus a cheap broker. Specify the latency budget and failure behavior.
7. **Can the acceptance procedure itself be falsified?** Include correct controls, unseen wrong outputs, environment variation, disagreement resolution, and a fresh temporal holdout. Distinguish preserving compatibility from intentionally changing semantics. Count false rejections and reviewer time.
8. **What makes the two-week result predict a scalable product?** Require a reachable adopter, a repeatable contract family, explicit operational staffing, and evidence that the next ten integrations reuse the first. A successful 75-second mechanism demo does not supply those answers.

**I — Decision rule for round 2:** prefer a narrowly demonstrated relationship to independent reality over a larger population of generated programs. Keep the ambition, but withdraw the assumption that today's model mistakes remain the customer's future bottleneck.
