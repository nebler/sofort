# The problems that get worse as models get better

Research date: **2026-10-27**. Lens: second-order costs of capable, cheap, abundant agents. Read the round-two brief, all seven round-one shortlists/TL;DRs, and the V8 capsule landscape. Only this report is changed; no experiments, deployments, contacts, or commits were made.

**Labels:** **F** = sourced fact, including an attributed vendor measurement or projection; **M** = locally measured; **I** = inference or product judgment; **E** = estimate, illustrative arithmetic, or proposed target. There are **no new M claims** here. Growth evidence is from sources published in **2024–2026**; older sources are explicitly systems lineage. All web sources were inspected on **2026-10-27**. Proposed 2030 scales are design scenarios, not forecasts.

## Thesis: perfect workers still compete for a finite world

**I — The strongest opportunity is managing demand and lifetime at shared resource boundaries.** A perfect agent still cannot read an origin at infinite QPS, spend the same dollar twice, make a dependent round trip instantaneous, or keep every temporary computer alive forever without cost. Better models make more tasks worth doing. That can expand the number of consumers of a resource faster than intelligence reduces resource use per task.

**I — This is a different bet from round one's acceptance thesis.** The proposals below do not require models to keep producing incorrect programs. Their core jobs are allocation, locality, conservation, and reclamation. Tests remain necessary to establish that an implementation works, but selling a test oracle is not the business premise.

**F — A demand expansion is visible, but it is not proof of inevitable Jevons effects everywhere.** Google reported monthly processed tokens across its surfaces rising from **9.7 trillion in May 2024**, to **480 trillion in May 2025**, to **over 3.2 quadrillion in May 2026**: approximately **330× over two years**, or **6.7× in the second year**. This includes changing products, models, modalities, and token accounting; it is neither an agent-count series nor a FLOP/energy series. [Google I/O, 2026-05-19][Google26]. Stanford's 2025 AI Index reports that the price of achieving a fixed GPT-3.5-level MMLU threshold fell over **280× between November 2022 and October 2024**. That is a benchmark-specific price comparison, not a universal inference-cost curve. [2025 AI Index][AIIndex].

**I — The causal test for every row:**

```text
resource demand = tasks attempted × branches/task × actions/branch × resource/action
live state      ≈ creation rate × average retained lifetime × bytes/state
chain latency   ≥ sequential remote dependencies × propagation RTT
approval load   = attempted actions × fraction requiring a human decision
```

**I — Better models can lower any per-task term.** A problem worsens only when expansion elsewhere outweighs that saving. The report therefore distinguishes observed aggregate growth, proxies, and conditional mechanisms. In particular, human approvals and synthetic-data damage are weaker model-proof bets than physical contention and resource lifetime.

**F/E — The speed-of-light floor is literal.** NIST gives the exact vacuum speed as **299,792,458 m/s**. [SI reference, current source][NIST]. For endpoints **6,000 km apart**, a request and dependent reply require at least **~40 ms even in vacuum**; 100 sequential exchanges require **~4 s**, before slower propagation in fibre, routing distance, queues, or service time. This is illustrative arithmetic, not a measured network path. Faster inference cannot reduce that floor; fewer round trips or different placement can.

### What the traffic and energy headlines do—and do not—establish

- **F — Bot share is not agent share.** Thales/Imperva reports **49.6% bot traffic in 2023**, **51% in 2024**, and **53% in 2025** across its observed traffic. Those are shares with changing denominators, not a 2024–2026 autonomous-agent volume curve. [2024-04-16][Thales24]; [2025-04-15][Thales25]; [2026-04-29][Thales26]. Cloudflare's 2024 report instead found **31.2% of application traffic** was automated. Different coverage/classification prevents splicing these into one curve. [2024-07-11][CF24].
- **F — Training and interactive demand differ.** Cloudflare's July 2026 report says AI-training purpose reached **52% of classified crawler requests in June 2026**, from **22% in spring 2025**: **+30 percentage points**, or **2.36× share**, not 2.36× request volume. It also explicitly says it is investing in freshness signals and discovery to reduce indiscriminate crawling. [2026-07-01][CF26]. **I:** a new “freshness hints for crawlers” product would collide with that roadmap.
- **F — Crawl-to-refer ratios are an economic signal, not origin utilization.** Cloudflare measured roughly **38,066 Anthropic crawls per attributed referral in July 2025**, down from **286,930 in January**; OpenAI's July ratio was roughly **1,091:1**. Missing native-app `Referer` headers can overstate the ratios. Neither proves bandwidth costs rose by that multiple or that readers disappeared entirely. [Cloudflare, 2025-08-29][Crawl25]; [metric caveat, 2025-07-01][CrawlMethod].
- **F — Power constraints are physical and local.** IEA's 2025 analysis put about **20% of planned data-centre projects at risk of delays** from grid constraints and cited **4–8 years** for new transmission in advanced economies. Its 2026 update reports efficiency per AI task improving by at least an order of magnitude annually in recent years, even as aggregate demand grows. [2025-04-10][IEA25]; [2026-04-16][IEA26]. **I:** this supports demand reduction and schedulable work, not the claim that every task gets more expensive.
- **F — More activity need not imply worse delivery.** DORA's 2025 report finds a positive relationship between AI adoption and throughput/product performance, while the relationship with delivery stability remains negative. This is observational. [2025][DORA25]. **I:** do not extrapolate today's quality failures into a permanent need for human review.

## Eight product ideas

**I — These are candidate products, not eight platforms to build.** Numbers marked E below are proposed experiment thresholds and design scenarios. The winning design may use ordinary application state rather than capsules; none depends on inventing snapshotting.

### A. Origin Work Protocol: let the scarce server schedule the agents

1. **One sentence + 100× version — I:** An origin publishes bounded query jobs, freshness contracts, and capacity reservations so agents request useful results instead of independently crawling and polling. The **100× version** is a network where millions of consumers subscribe to owner-defined computations and changes, with shared work performed once per authorized equivalence class.
2. **Worsening problem + payer — F/I:** Map #1/#8/#9 supplies observed traffic expansion and duplicated work. The first payer is a retailer, API operator, or data service with costly dynamic reads that generic page caching does not combine. Sell fewer backend executions and predictable useful throughput under agent load. Ordinary correct agents create the problem; blocking malicious bots is adjacent.
3. **Superhuman-model test — I:** **Matters more if aggregate demand grows.** A brilliant caller cannot unilaterally deduplicate work across competitors or decide another owner's capacity allocation. An owner can. It vanishes as a separate product if existing cache, query API, and subscriptions achieve the same savings with little integration.
4. **2030 scale — E:** A CDN/operator hosts manifests for **100,000 participating origins** and serves **1B logical queries/day**. Jobs carry code/query hash, data epoch, freshness bound, authorization scope, output cap, price/budget, and cancellation lease. The dramatic win is origin work growing with distinct authorized queries and data changes rather than consumer count; it is not a guaranteed 100× saving for personalized queries.
5. **Lineage — F/I:** **Active Cache, Middleware 1998**, let servers attach programs executed by proxies to serve dynamic content. [Cao, Zhang, Beach][ActiveCache]. **I:** its distribution, trust, accounting, and dynamic invalidation burden limited the general product shape; the idea itself did not disappear—CDNs and edge compute succeeded. Today's difference is Cloudflare's installed execution/enforcement plane and model-generated client plans. Start with owner-published templates, not arbitrary visitors running against a production database.
6. **Value — I:** Subscription plus metered completed owner-approved jobs; optionally share independently established backend savings. A data seller can license a bounded derived result, but recurring access/payment is an integration with existing rails. An independent builder starts with a Worker adapter for one origin, not a marketplace that requires the entire internet to agree.
7. **First proof, 1–2 weeks — E:** Build one catalog/search origin with three templates, two authorization classes, explicit epochs, and a DO scheduler. Replay **1,000 logical consumers** with controlled repeated and unique queries. Compare tuned HTTP caching/ETags + request coalescing, batching, and a change feed against the protocol. Target **≥10× fewer backend executions** in the repeated cohort at equal freshness and **≥2× fewer** on a held-out mixed trace; report unique-query regressions, p95, admission overhead, stale responses, and total cost. Wrong-scope sharing must be zero. Publish hit-rate curves rather than a cherry-picked 1,000:1 demo.
8. **Demo + draft tweet — I:** Show a synchronized agent surge, a bounded backend work graph, a data update, and results fanning out only to entitled consumers. Draft: “1,000 agents ask the same live catalog question. The origin computes once per data epoch and permission class. We measured backend calls, freshness, and p95 against caching + ETags. Here's where sharing helps—and where it doesn't.”
9. **Kill test — E/I:** Stop if realistic queries rarely share, standard subscriptions win, the overhead erases net savings, or no origin owner will provide a query/data-epoch contract. Without a non-cacheable workload and willing owner, this is an elaborate cache. Cloudflare's freshness/discovery roadmap is a major bundling threat.

### B. Lease OS: garbage collection for generated computers

1. **One sentence + 100× version — I:** Every generated app/task receives an owner, renewable lifetime, retained roots, and a reclamation plan covering its state and external resources. The **100× version** makes creation and retirement equally automatic across billions of temporary computers: memories, files, deployments, timers, credentials, and retained evidence expire with their purpose.
2. **Worsening problem + payer — F/I:** Map #2/#3: creation/activity is rising; the orphan-state population is unsized. The initial buyer is an agent host operating many short-lived apps. Sell an attributable inventory and fewer idle resources, unexpected wakes, and stale grants. **E:** at **1M new tasks/day**, 30-day retention gives **30M task roots** before history; 1 MB each is **30 TB**. This is an illustrative stock calculation, not observed adoption.
3. **Superhuman-model test — I:** **Still matters.** Perfect code does not settle whether an owner still wants an old task or whether a disconnected descendant may remain funded. Better agents can implement cleanup; the reusable value is complete resource ownership, enforceable expiry, and cancellation semantics across independent services.
4. **2030 scale — E:** An agent platform tracks **1B dormant task roots**, but only their live leases authorize activity. Shared base images are content-addressed; descendants retain dependencies explicitly. A task can be frozen as a receipt, kept as user-owned state, or retired. Retiring authority precedes stopping wakes and deleting data. Retained audit evidence has a separate root and deadline.
5. **Lineage — F/I:** **Gray/Cheriton leases, SOSP 1989**, bound rights under partial failure; **Nix, LISA 2004**, made closures, versions, and safe GC explicit. [leases][Leases], [Nix][Nix]. **I:** neither stalled as a mechanism. Their scope did not provide an ownership graph spanning generated SaaS resources, remote tool sessions, and opaque agent heaps. The tool-channel boundary can make that graph enumerable now—only for resources actually allocated through it.
6. **Value — I/E:** Charge by managed roots/resources and reclaim operations, not “AI tokens analyzed.” **F:** R2 standard storage is **$0.015/GB-month**, writes **$4.50/M**, reads **$0.36/M**, with free direct egress. [updated 2026-10-01][R2]. **E:** the illustrative 30 TB costs about **$450/month** in raw storage, whereas writing one checkpoint/minute for **1M active tasks** creates **43.2B writes/month**, about **$194,400** before free tier, compute, and other operations. A collector must avoid creating more bookkeeping than it saves. Idle browsers, live services, and credentials can justify the product where bytes alone do not.
7. **First proof, 1–2 weeks — E:** Wrap five resource adapters: R2 images, DO task state, scheduled wakeups, a simulated browser session, and broker grants. Create **10,000 short-lived task trees**, inject crashes at each allocation/record/retirement boundary, and retain designated roots. Compare native TTL rules + explicit Code Mode disposal/expiry. Target **zero deletion of retained roots**, **zero authorized wake after expiry**, and **≥95% reclamation of eligible resources within two sweeps**; itemize unknown external outcomes and adapter coverage. Use coarse leases, not per-file heartbeat writes.
8. **Demo + draft tweet — I:** Generate a large fleet; cancel its project; show inherited files, grants, wakes, and sessions closing while a pinned receipt remains. Draft: “We taught an agent platform how to forget. Cancel one project: its timers stop, grants expire, sessions close, and unreachable state is collected. The retained result still opens. Crash tests and the resource inventory are public.”
9. **Kill test — I:** Kill the platform thesis if native TTL + existing workflow cleanup covers the buyer's meaningful spend, or integrations cannot enumerate most costly resources. Kill the capsule-specific story if explicit JSON task roots suffice. A scan that merely recommends deletions is a weak cloud-management feature, not this bet.

### C. Round-trip compiler: move the next hundred instructions to the data

1. **One sentence + 100× version — I:** Lower discovered, deterministic stretches of agent work into bounded programs placed beside the tool/data owner, returning to the model only at genuine reasoning or authority boundaries. The **100× version** optimizes a task's distributed critical path across cooperating domains, rather than accelerating one model call.
2. **Worsening problem + payer — F/I:** Map #5/#9 describes increasing feasible task difficulty and expensive intermediate movement; it does not establish measured chain-depth growth. Buyers are agent platforms with traceable cross-region tool latency. Charge for latency/transfer reductions on useful completed tasks, not tokens claimed to have been avoided.
3. **Superhuman-model test — I:** **Still matters physically; compiler differentiation may shrink.** A stronger model can generate an excellent plan in one shot, but that plan still needs placement, transport, resource caps, and authority. Models or platforms may absorb the compiler front end entirely. Truly new reasoning after every remote observation cannot be pipelined away by wishful static analysis.
4. **2030 scale — E:** A host routes **100M daily workflows** as dependency graphs: local tool loops execute in owner-approved Workers/Wasm environments, while an external broker authorizes writes. Capsules park only where they save substantial reconstruction; data that cannot leave stays local. A chain crossing five independent owners still incurs real transfers and policy checks.
5. **Lineage — F/I:** **Cap'n Proto's 2013 promise pipelining** sends dependent calls before intermediate results return; **Active Names, USITS 1999**, composed mobile programs with location-aware services. [pipelining][CapsProto], [Active Names][ActiveNames]. **I:** these did not erase cross-owner deployment and data-dependent local logic. Now models can produce and explain the subplan, and Wasm offers a constrained execution unit. **F:** Cap'n Web already brings pipelining to TypeScript. [2025-09-22][Capnweb].
6. **Value — I:** A library plus managed placement/trace service for hosts, initially one customer with two cooperating tool endpoints. Cloudflare is unusually well placed to integrate this, making a stand-alone generic RPC business unattractive. scriptc is an optional native backend for an existing host; do not imply its native binary is a Worker-compatible Wasm binary.
7. **First proof, 1–2 weeks — E:** Run **30 held-out read-only tasks** with ordinary tool calls, hand-written Code Mode, Cap'n Web batching/pipelining, and the proposed lowering/placement. Use measured or deliberately injected **20/80/160 ms RTT** cohorts, clearly distinguished. Include payload, cold start, and owner validation. Go only if the new placement beats **best existing baseline by ≥2× p95** on at least one real target workload, with equal outputs and no more authority; show when it loses.
8. **Demo + draft tweet — I:** Reveal the critical-path trace as one hundred remote steps collapse into a local loop and a small result. Draft: “Faster models expose slower networks. We moved a deterministic tool loop beside its data and compared it with Code Mode + Cap'n Web. The trace shows the RTTs removed, bytes moved, and the boundaries that still require a trip.”
9. **Kill test — I:** Stop if Code Mode + ordinary server-side endpoints + existing placement does the job, the owner will not execute the subplan, or each step genuinely requires central inference. A synthetic sleep benchmark proves arithmetic, not a new product.

### D. Conserved mandates: cloning a worker cannot clone its rights

1. **One sentence + 100× version — I:** A tool broker gives descendants attenuated rights against one externally conserved task budget, with revocation and fenced ownership across copies. The **100× version** is a resource constitution for delegated work: millions of independently hosted descendants can act, but their combined money, quotas, and effect rights remain bounded by the principal's grant.
2. **Worsening problem + payer — F/I:** Map #4/#8/#10: identities and delegation outnumber principals, while humans cannot approve every branch. First buyers are agent platforms with multi-provider subcontracting or merchant/tool workflows. Sell enforceable limits and attribution; do not count human permissions as something a model's intelligence can infer into existence.
3. **Superhuman-model test — I:** **Matters even for honest, perfect agents.** Multiple authorized workers can concurrently consume a finite quota. A grant's scope and whether the principal delegated it are institutional state. A model can propose the grant; it cannot make duplicated tokens conserve a shared purse without a coordinating protocol.
4. **2030 scale — E:** A platform handles **10M active mandate roots**, each sharded independently. Root brokers reserve sub-budgets into bounded child escrows so calls do not all pass through one global sequencer. A signed request identifies operator, principal/root, descendant, intended resource, amount, expiry, and operation ID. Sensitive writes remain on connectors with defined uncertainty recovery.
5. **Lineage — F/I:** **Macaroons, NDSS 2014**, provide efficient decentralized delegation with contextual attenuation. [original paper][Macaroons]. **I:** attenuation succeeded but does not by itself prevent two copies spending the same global balance. Add escrow/reservation and versioned ownership at the shared resource. The modern difference is cheap execution cloning and widespread agent-tool traffic, not a newly invented capability model. jitk's sole tool channel is a particularly clean place to test mandatory mediation.
6. **Value — I:** Charge the host/tool operator for active roots and authorized operations. A human sets a bounded policy once; only a new goal, exhausted budget, or unresolved preference conflict escalates. A signed agent's platform identity is one input, not proof that a named consumer authorized every proposed purchase.
7. **First proof, 1–2 weeks — E:** Use TS broker + per-root DO, capsule clones, and a fake metered tool. Give one root **1,000 credits** and fork **100 descendants** across two adapters. Inject retries, stale restores, cancellation, and crashes before/after reserve/dispatch/record. Require **aggregate authorized spend ≤1,000**, no post-revocation new reservation, and no stale-owner dispatch. Retain uncertain external operations as unresolved rather than silently refunding/retrying them. Compare ordinary OAuth scopes + a transactional shared balance; the fork/handoff integration must earn its complexity.
8. **Demo + draft tweet — I:** One agent becomes a swarm; the shared meter never grows; an old downloaded image cannot resurrect authority. Draft: “Copy the agent 100 times. Its budget stays 1,000 credits. Rights live in a broker, not the snapshot; stale copies cannot spend. We publish the race/crash cases and the external-effect limits.”
9. **Kill test — I:** Demote to a library if existing IAM plus a simple budget table covers target users. Kill claims of global exactly-once writes without external idempotency support. If the next protocol/platform already gives interoperable descendant budgets and portable revocation, integrate it instead of creating another identity registry.

### E. Inference refinery: spend intelligence once, run the routine many times

1. **One sentence + 100× version — I:** Convert repeated, stable agent work into versioned deterministic programs, retaining inference only for novelty. The **100× version** lets a fleet accumulate a shared repertoire of executable skills whose marginal repeated execution approaches ordinary compute rather than repeated reasoning.
2. **Worsening problem + payer — F/I:** Map #6/#9: demand can outrun efficiency; repeated interpretation is a removable component. The payer is an agent host with repeated transformations, extraction, dispatch, or queries—not a buyer of a universal model replacement. The IEA evidence concerns all compute/AI workloads; this product addresses only the fraction that can be expressed as stable code.
3. **Superhuman-model test — I:** **Matters if model inference retains material cost versus code.** A perfect model can produce the routine more reliably, which improves conversion. If hardware/model economics erase the cost gap, or the workload remains mostly novel, this product shrinks. It does not depend on a permanent gap in correctness.
4. **2030 scale — E:** A host runs **10B routine invocations/day** using content-addressed skills, with a minority returning to a model for a changed contract/input class. A skill declares input domain, policy, dependencies, and exact version. Sharing uses explicit semantics and permission scope, never fuzzy caching of arbitrary answers.
5. **Lineage — F/I:** **Futamura, 1971**, specialized a computation by fixing known inputs and leaving the residual program. [original paper][Futamura]. **I:** partial evaluation became important compiler practice; universal application specialization was limited by dynamic environments and staging difficulty. Models now supply candidate routines and identify reusable structure. This is not automatic proof that a generated program preserves arbitrary model behavior.
6. **Value — I/E:** Sell net saved serving cost after generation, compilation, invalidation, and fallbacks. Use Workers JS/Wasm first; scriptc for its actually supported native subset on a compatible host. **E:** if conversion costs C and each reuse saves s, break-even requires more than C/s reuses; report that threshold for every skill class.
7. **First proof, 1–2 weeks — E:** Select **20 repeated workloads** with stable, explicit output contracts and **1,000 held-out inputs each**. Compare prompt caching, batching, a cheap adequate model, hand-written templates, and generated JS before native compilation. Target **≥10× net cost reduction** on at least five genuinely recurring workloads, with equal accepted behavior and recorded fallback rates. Measure after the best model-price baseline, not a deliberately expensive frontier model.
8. **Demo + draft tweet — I:** A flow visibly stops calling a model on recurring inputs while a changed contract returns to inference. Draft: “The agent wrote this routine once. We then ran 20,000 held-out cases against cached prompts, a cheap model, and plain JS. Here are total dollars, latency, fallback rate, and the reuse count needed to break even.”
9. **Kill test — I:** Kill if prompt caching/cheap models win, reuse is too low, or a simple hand-built template dominates. Native compilation is not automatically worthwhile for network-bound glue. No claim that compilation replaces the largest training/inference power loads follows from a small extraction demo.

### F. Runtime closure commons: patch the shared substrate, retire the copies

1. **One sentence + 100× version — I:** Package generated apps as explicit immutable dependency/runtime closures, deduplicate them, and propagate supported substrate updates across their dependent fleet. The **100× version** lets a billion bespoke programs share a small maintained compatibility layer instead of a billion unrelated mutable environments.
2. **Worsening problem + payer — F/I:** Map #2/#7 gives growth in code/build/package demand. **F:** Sonatype counted more than **454,600 new malicious packages in 2025**, bringing its cumulative detected/blocked count above **1.233M**; this is vendor detection telemetry, not every package or an AI-attributable series. [2026 report][Malware26]. **I:** even perfect app code inherits the operational costs of shared dependencies. Buyers are sandbox and internal-app platforms with repetitive closures.
3. **Superhuman-model test — I:** **Still relevant, with a strong counterforce.** Better agents can produce fewer dependencies and perform migrations, reducing pain per app; more apps and independent versions can increase total pain. A reusable ABI/closure registry remains useful without model mistakes. A compatibility oracle can help implementation but is not the reason customers must operate the fleet.
4. **2030 scale — E:** A host manages **100M deployed app revisions** over **10,000 maintained closure families**. A new base revision triggers migration of eligible tasks, explicit pinning of incompatible ones, and retirement of unreferenced bases. Sharing reduces bytes and rebuilds; it does not promise one replacement can safely patch every version.
5. **Lineage — F/I:** **Exokernel, SOSP 1995**, separated resource protection from application-level OS management; **Nix 2004** made deployment closures and variants explicit. [Exokernel][Exo], [Nix][Nix]. **I:** both are established, not forgotten inventions. The proposed change is agent-grown minimal compatibility tied to a large generated-app population. GrowOS contributes the demand-driven growth loop; a fixed tiny trusted host remains outside that loop.
6. **Value — I:** Platform subscription for managed closure families, patch fan-out, and storage/build savings. Initial public artifact should be a registry and two narrow adapters, not a new full npm runtime. Existing Nix, package mirrors, lockfiles, bundlers, and platform bases are formidable substitutes.
7. **First proof, 1–2 weeks — E:** Choose **20 constrained TS programs**, three dependency families, and one explicitly compatible substrate fix. Compare normal bundled dependencies + warm package cache with shared immutable bases. Measure total downloaded/stored bytes, start time, rebuild CPU, upgrade labor, and retained old states. Require **≥5× fleet byte reduction** or **≥2× end-to-end rebuild reduction** beyond baseline, with successful rollback and clearly rejected incompatible images. Grow one missing nontrusted API only if required by the corpus.
8. **Demo + draft tweet — I:** Update one supported runtime base, wake old occupied tasks, show the incompatible ones remaining pinned, and collect the unused predecessor. Draft: “20 generated apps, three immutable runtime families. One supported fix reaches the compatible fleet; old pinned tasks still run, and dead bases are collected. We compare total bytes and rebuilds with ordinary package caching.”
9. **Kill test — I:** Stop if standard bundling/package caches win, base families fragment almost one-per-app, or migrations become a custom service per tenant. Do not grow the trusted jitk host to chase every npm package; that gives away the most useful boundary.

### G. Observation capsules: preserve the world a correct program actually saw

1. **One sentence + 100× version — I:** Package a constrained execution with pinned code/engine, tool observations, data identities, and explicitly absent secrets so another host can reproduce its recorded result. The **100× version** is a portable interchange for computational history that survives the original model provider and orchestration service.
2. **Worsening problem + payer — F/I:** Map #11 supplies execution-volume growth, not a measured increase in irreproducibility. The payer is an integration platform needing supportable records of consequential runs or a research team shipping computational artifacts. Sell reconstruction time and portable ownership, not an assertion that the original result is true.
3. **Superhuman-model test — I:** **Still matters when exact past observations matter.** A perfect model can explain a complete history; it cannot recover missing old inputs. If customers only want a current answer and never need historical reconstruction, this is unnecessary storage.
4. **2030 scale — E:** Operators archive **100M compact run manifests/day**; heavy bodies/images are separately retained by policy. An offline reader can replay covered computation but cannot silently issue real effects or pretend a deleted external response is recoverable. A fresh fork receives fresh authority and explicitly becomes a new experiment.
5. **Lineage — F/I:** **ReVirt, OSDI 2002**, captured nondeterminism for replay rather than relying on incomplete application logs. [paper][ReVirt]. **I:** whole-machine capture carried implementation/architecture costs, while many workflow engines succeeded with narrower journals. The tool-only ABI reduces scope, and portable interpreter images can bound recovery work. It does not capture an external service, socket, GPU, or entire browser environment.
6. **Value — I:** Per retained run/body and support integrations; portable export is the main addition beyond observability. Respect deletion/retention boundaries: the durable receipt can attest a body hash without retaining a readable body, but that lowers subsequent replay capability. This is a product choice, not a cryptographic workaround.
7. **First proof, 1–2 weeks — E:** Capture **30 read-only tool workflows** with clock/random inputs and a changed upstream response. Replay offline in a browser Worker and a separate Node host using an existing engine. Compare signed source+inputs+journal and existing Code Mode logs. Require **100% exact covered-output reproduction**, zero live network calls during replay, and at least **2× lower reconstruction time** on costly-prefix cases to justify the image. Report the full dependency bytes.
8. **Demo + draft tweet — I:** Remove the original account and alter the live API; open the old recorded computation offline, then label a live fork separately. Draft: “This agent result still replays after its API changes. The download includes pinned code, runtime, and recorded observations. Replay makes no network calls; a fresh rerun is visibly different. Here's the source+journal comparison.”
9. **Kill test — I:** Kill the capsule requirement if ordinary journals serve users equally well. Kill a standalone business if the artifact never enters a support, ownership, or research workflow. Model nondeterminism must be recorded as an observation or regenerated as a new run—not described as magically reproducible.

### H. Fresh evidence exchange: buy an observation rather than another paraphrase

1. **One sentence + 100× version — I:** Data owners publish signed, time/version-scoped observations and derived query results with explicit reuse terms, letting agents distinguish original evidence from a chain of copies. The **100× version** is a market for fresh facts and licensed computations where provenance survives downstream composition.
2. **Worsening problem + payer — F/I:** Map #12's synthetic-data papers establish conditional risks, not a measured contamination market. Crawl/referral economics establishes data-owner incentives without proving this product will sell. First buyer: a data vendor whose changing catalog/availability facts are expensive to source correctly; start with direct agent customers, not model training labs.
3. **Superhuman-model test — I:** **Freshness survives; model-collapse insurance may vanish.** Excellent reasoning cannot observe a private inventory update without a data path. It may generate excellent synthetic training data, which removes the weaker sales pitch. A provenance signature asserts origin/key and bytes, not truth, human authorship, independent sources, or legal permission inferred from access.
4. **2030 scale — E:** **10,000 data owners** serve **100M paid or contract-metered observations/day**. Each response carries identity, source version/time, query hash, output digest, and a machine-readable licence reference. Derived artifacts retain source relationships; recipient permissions are separately enforced. Secrets and private data do not become exportable merely because the computation is portable.
5. **Lineage — F/I:** **W3C PROV, 2013**, standardizes entities, activities, agents, and provenance interchange. [2013-04-30][PROV]. **I:** provenance standards did not solve capture incentives, trust, or payments by themselves. Origin/CDN capture can reduce missing provenance, while current machine-payment/access mechanisms reduce integration work. This is an incentive/integration proposal, not invention of signed provenance.
6. **Value — I:** Owners charge per authorized fresh query or contract and pay for attribution/delivery. Use existing Pay Per Crawl/payment services where appropriate. A neutral builder's advantage must be integration and usable provenance downstream; Cloudflare can bundle much of the network part.
7. **First proof, 1–2 weeks — E:** One consenting/synthetic owner publishes a changing dataset through a Worker; two independently configured clients buy/read scoped derived results and create a provenance-linked artifact. Test stale versions, replayed receipts, forbidden redistribution, and intentionally false but correctly signed owner data. Require the UI to distinguish each case; prove **zero entitlement bypass in the bounded suite**, not truth detection. Compare plain signed JSON + existing billing.
8. **Demo + draft tweet — I:** Show a copied claim and a fresh signed observation side by side; change the underlying catalog and expose the old version. Draft: “The signature doesn't prove a fact is true. It tells you which owner returned which bytes, for which query and data version. We built a fresh-data endpoint whose provenance survives a derived result, with reuse terms attached.”
9. **Kill test — I:** Stop if owners/clients will not preserve provenance or plain signed JSON solves the buyer's problem. Do not sell a universal detector of AI content or promise to prevent model collapse. This is the least compelling independent bet here.

## Shared evaluation and where the unfair advantage actually lies

**I — Measure completed useful work at equal freshness, scope, and semantics.** Each experiment must report baseline tuning, warm/cold paths, per-tenant isolation, total bytes and operations, p50/p95, and failure/cancellation behavior. Do not obtain a saving by serving stale data, dropping work, hiding the origin cost, or retaining unauthorized state. A superhuman model is allowed to generate the best simple baseline too.

**I — Three separable advantages:**

1. **Cloudflare's network can see/control requests before an origin spends work.** That is especially valuable for **A**, then **D/H**. A small builder can supply adapters and an open contract, but cannot assume access to cross-customer traffic or a signed-agent policy exception. One willing origin is the first distribution proof.
2. **The builder's tool-only execution boundary can enumerate and mediate resources.** That is strongest for **B/D/G**. It makes expiry, retained roots, budgets, observations, and pending effects explicit. An opaque arbitrary Linux process is harder to inventory. Effects outside the broker invalidate completeness claims.
3. **GrowOS + scriptc can manufacture smaller reusable implementations.** That favors **E/F**, provided actual supported workloads show net savings. Better models improve the factory, while the accepted repertoire and customer integration accumulate. Do not reuse the previous round's oracle narrative as the only reason this should matter.

**I — The old ideas often succeeded; their scope is the opening.** HTTP caches, leases, capability delegation, replay, Nix closures, and compiler specialization are not undiscovered. Current agent economics can expand their addressable unit from a file/process/request to an independently owned task and its descendants. If a short composition of existing primitives solves the problem, ship that composition; a new runtime is not automatically justified.

**I — Human attention belongs inside mandates, not in the top-level product list.** Horvitz's **1999 mixed-initiative interfaces** already treated automation and human control as a joint design problem. [CHI 1999][Horvitz]. The robust use is to ask for preferences/authority at the right boundary and amortize the decision. A permanently growing queue of approvals is neither a model-proof prediction nor a good product objective.

**I — Coordination belongs inside origins and budgets before it becomes a market.** Cross-owner contention needs enforceable resource semantics and credible principals. A fully decentralized market adds identity, pricing, clearing, strategic behavior, and distribution before proving useful work. Start with cooperative scheduling and reserved quotas; charge for an observed operational result.

## Ranking the eight products

**I — Ordinal judgment, not fabricated market scores.**

| Rank | Idea | Why it stays / what could eliminate it |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **1** | **A — Origin Work Protocol** | Strong observed demand, real resource boundary, strongest Cloudflare distribution fit. Eliminate if tuned caching/change feeds achieve the same result or query overlap vanishes. |
| **2** | **B — Lease OS** | Strongest builder-owned mechanism and clear need to retire resources; can start on one host. Eliminate if native lifetimes already reclaim meaningful costs or adapter coverage remains poor. |
| **3** | **D — Conserved mandates** | Excellent models still need finite budgets and externally granted authority. Crowded IAM/accounting space; must win on descendants, portability, and revocation rather than another token format. |
| 4 | C — Round-trip compiler | Physical limit, clear trace demo, useful adjacency. Code Mode/Cap'n Web/ordinary placement sharply narrow novelty. |
| 5 | E — Inference refinery | Better models improve synthesis; serving economics can be measured. Cheap models, caching, and low reuse may erase the savings. |
| 6 | F — Runtime closure commons | Most direct GrowOS/scriptc extension to software proliferation. Fragmentation, compatibility, and existing deployment tools may dominate. |
| 7 | G — Observation capsules | Exact observations survive perfect reasoning; good artifact. Much of the benefit may already be source plus journal; distribution is weak. |
| 8 | H — Fresh evidence exchange | Freshness remains scarce, but provenance/payment bundling and participation are hard. “Prevent model collapse” is an insufficient business premise. |

## Ranked top three

**1. A — Origin Work Protocol. I:** Start with a real owner-controlled dynamic query workload. The important artifact is a version/freshness/scope contract that lets independent agents share backend work without sharing unauthorized answers. The next model can improve plans, but cannot create another owner's backend capacity or coordinate all other callers by itself. This has the largest network-position advantage, and the cache/change-feed comparison can kill it quickly. A solo builder's risk is distribution: find one origin with measured pain before building a marketplace or asking Cloudflare to adopt a standard.

**2. B — Lease OS. I:** This is the strongest alternative if origin participation is inaccessible. It turns the builder's small owned computers and single tool channel into a complete lifecycle contract. Millions of excellent disposable apps still need an answer to “who owns this, why is it alive, what may wake it, and what dies with it?” Start where abandoned sessions and schedules cost real money or authority; raw R2 bytes may be too cheap. Its distinguishing test is cross-resource completeness under crashes, beyond existing expiry hooks—not a prettier list of snapshots.

**3. D — Conserved mandates. I:** This is necessary infrastructure for both leaders and may become a product with the right agent-platform buyer. The primitive is a finite grant shared across descendants, independently enforced from copied execution state. Treat operator authentication, user authority, resource accounting, and external-effect uncertainty separately. It remains relevant even if every generated plan is correct. Its commercial weakness is strong existing IAM/payment platforms; a minimal broker integration with one real workflow is a better opening than a universal identity network.

## The ONE bet

**I — Bet on A: make one origin withstand useful agent demand by computing shared work once.** The first vertical should be a changing catalog or expensive read API with real query repetition and distinct permission/freshness classes. Build only the manifest, scheduler, owner-approved query executor, and result-sharing boundary. Use existing Workers and DOs; add capsules only when parked work makes a measurable difference. D's quota conservation is a small required component; B supplies explicit lifetime for reservations and parked jobs.

**E/I — Day-14 decision:** continue only if the protocol beats the best cache/coalescing/change-feed baseline on a held-out mixed workload by at least **2× backend work**, preserves the same freshness/entitlements, and one intended origin operator finds that result operationally useful. A staged burst of identical requests is not enough. If there is no willing origin or sharing disappears under realistic personalization, stop A and run B's crash/reclamation experiment; do not retreat to selling “verification for smarter agents.”

## Five-line TL;DR

1. **F/I:** Interactive crawl traffic, package consumption, software activity, and AI power demand are growing; all-bot shares and capability horizons need careful denominators.
2. **I:** Perfect agents still face finite origins, propagation delay, shared budgets, and retained-state costs; better reasoning cannot abolish those boundaries.
3. **I:** Top three: owner-scheduled shared origin work, lifecycle/garbage collection for generated computers, and conserved mandates across descendants.
4. **I:** Cloudflare's request position favors the first; the builder's tool-only kernel and portable state favor the second and third. Basic snapshots, Code Mode, and signed bots already exist.
5. **I/E:** Build the origin experiment first; demand a ≥2× held-out backend-work win over tuned existing tools, then stop if the owner, sharing, or economics is missing.
